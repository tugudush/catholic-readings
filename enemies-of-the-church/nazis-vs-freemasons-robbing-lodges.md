## Overview

This documentary excerpt details the dramatic history, looting, and eventual repatriation of Freemasonry archives during and after World War II. Driven by conspiracy theories and ideological obsessions, both the Nazi regime and the Vichy French government systematically looted Masonic lodges, compiling massive databases to persecute members. Following Germany's defeat, the Soviet Union secretly seized these archives as spoils of war, hiding them in Moscow for over 50 years. The video chronicles how these archives were eventually rediscovered in the 1990s and returned to France and Belgium.

---

## Key Scenes

- **The Fall of Paris and First Raids (1940):** German troops occupy Paris and immediately target Masonic lodges, driven by Nazi ideologue Alfred Rosenberg's conspiracy theories about "Judeo-Masonic" occult power.
- **The Vichy Persecution:** Marshal Pétain's Vichy government outlaws secret societies and appoints anti-Masonic historian Bernard Faÿ to compile detailed spy files on over 150,000 French citizens.
- **The Soviet "Special Archive" in Moscow:** The Red Army discovers the looted archives hidden by the Nazis in Silesian castles. Under Lavrentiy Beria, the NKVD secretly transports 28 train cars of documents to Moscow to analyze Western political networks.
- **The Diplomatic Battle and Return (1999):** Following the fall of the Soviet Union, American historian Patricia Grimsted discovers the files. French President Jacques Chirac successfully negotiates their return with Boris Yeltsin, leading to the dramatic unpacking of historic treasures in Paris.

## Notable Details

- **Occult Beliefs:** Alfred Rosenberg and Heinrich Himmler genuinely believed Masonic archives contained active alchemical secrets, including the formula for the Philosopher's Stone (turning lead into gold).
- **Vichy's Absurdity:** The Vichy regime was so thorough in its paranoia that it kept updated active tracking files on thousands of Freemasons who had already been dead for years.
- **The "Six Kilometers" of Files:** The scale of the looted archives was staggering; the French police documents stolen by the Germans and taken by the Soviets measured roughly six kilometers (3.7 miles) if lined up end-to-end.
- **The Price of Repatriation:** To secure the return of their stolen heritage, the French government paid the Russian Federation 4 million French Francs, officially billed as "fees" to cover the cost of microfilming the papers for Russia's records.
- **A Literary Discovery:** Among the returned boxes was the original registry of _Les Neuf Sœurs_ (The Nine Sisters), the highly influential 18th-century Paris lodge whose members included Voltaire and Benjamin Franklin.
