# Our Lady of the Revelation, Tre Fontane (1947)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The alleged apparition of Tre Fontane ("Three Fountains") in Rome — at the traditional site of the martyrdom of Saint Paul the Apostle — is a case whose ecclesial status is delicately balanced between an initial negative declaration and a subsequently tolerated cult:
- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ.
- The Holy Office in 1948 declared that the supernatural character of the events was **not established** (*non constat de supernaturalitate*), yet the site was never formally condemned, public devotion was tolerated, and a church was later built and consecrated at the place. The devotion thus occupies an intermediate position and remains, strictly speaking, unapproved.

## The Vision of Bruno Cornacchiola
On **April 12, 1947**, **Bruno Cornacchiola** (1913–2001), an Italian worker who had become a fierce anti-Catholic preacher of a Seventh-day Adventist community, was at Tre Fontane with his three young children when he claimed to see the Blessed Virgin Mary.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The Context**: Cornacchiola had publicly vowed to destroy the "idolatry" of the Catholic devotion to Mary; his wife, Iolanda, a Catholic, prayed for his conversion. The vision occurred while he was preparing a polemical talk against the Church.
2. **The Vision**: On the afternoon of April 12, 1947, as he searched for his children near the Eucalyptus avenue of Tre Fontane, Cornacchiola saw a dazzling light and a Lady of indescribable beauty, clothed in white with a green mantle, holding a small book; she addressed him, and he felt himself called to repentance. His children saw the same Lady.
3. **The Conversion**: Cornacchiola converted on the spot, was reconciled with the Church, and devoted the rest of his life to the apostolate of the "Madonna della Rivelazione" (Our Lady of the Revelation). He died in 2001.
4. **The Message**: The alleged message called for conversion, prayer, and penance, and requested the building of a church at the site dedicated to the Mother of God.
5. **The Shrine**: A church — the Basilica-church of the Madonna della Rivelazione — was built at Tre Fontane and consecrated, and the site has been a place of pilgrimage and prayer for the sick ever since.

## Ecclesiastical Status
- **The 1948 Declaration**: On **May 2, 1948**, the Holy Office declared that the supernatural character of the events was **not established** (*non constat de supernaturalitate*). This negative finding meant the Church did not authenticate the apparition.
- **The Tolerated Cult**: Despite the negative declaration, the Church did not forbid private devotion; the shrine was erected with ecclesiastical approval for worship, and Masses and pilgrimages have continued at Tre Fontane for decades, with several popes and cardinals visiting the site.
- **No Formal Approval**: No subsequent decree of the Holy See or of the Diocese of Rome has ever formally approved the apparition as supernatural. The case thus remains, canonically, an unapproved phenomenon whose devotional fruits the Church has tolerated without authenticating its origin.

## Pastoral Guidance
The faithful may visit the shrine of Tre Fontane and benefit from its sacramental life and ministry to the sick, while remaining free of any obligation to believe in the supernatural origin of the 1947 events. The distinction observed by the Church — permitting worship at the place while withholding judgment on the phenomenon — is itself a model of pastoral prudence in the discernment of private revelation.
