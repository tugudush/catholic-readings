# Our Lady of Medjugorje (1981– )

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The spiritual experience of Medjugorje, in Bosnia-Herzegovina, is the most widely followed alleged apparition of the modern era and the paradigmatic case of the Church's nuanced contemporary discernment:

- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ, and belief in them is not required.
- On **19 September 2024**, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (DDF) issued the note **_"The Queen of Peace": Note About the Spiritual Experience Connected with Medjugorje_**, which concluded a "long and complex history" under the framework of the new _Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena_ (17 May 2024).
- The note granted a _**nihil obstat**_ — authorizing the faithful to adhere to the devotion prudently and to make pilgrimages — **without declaring the events to be of supernatural origin**. Medjugorje is therefore **neither approved nor rejected**; it remains under the Church's ongoing pastoral vigilance.

## The Alleged Apparitions

On **June 24, 1981**, six children and teenagers of the village of Medjugorje reported seeing the Blessed Virgin Mary on a hillside. The seers were Ivan Dragičević, Ivanka Ivanković, Mirjana Dragičević, Vicka Ivanković, Marija Pavlović, and Jakov Čolo.

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The "Queen of Peace"**: Mary identified herself as the "**Queen of Peace**" (_Kraljica Mira_), and the core of the alleged messages is a call to **conversion, prayer, fasting, penance, and peace** — a message that echoes Fatima and Lourdes.
2. **The Frequency**: Several of the visionaries have claimed daily apparitions continuing for decades, a claim without parallel in the history of approved apparitions and itself a factor in ecclesiastical caution.
3. **The Secrets**: The seers have reported receiving a series of "secrets" concerning the future of the world and of the Church, to be revealed at appointed times — a feature that has fueled both devotion and eschatological speculation.
4. **The Fruits**: The site has drawn tens of millions of pilgrims and is marked by abundant reports of conversions, confessions, vocations, and prayer — the "fruits" that the Church has verified and affirmed.
5. **The Difficulties**: The local bishop of Mostar, Bishop Pavao Žanić, repeatedly declared the events _non constat de supernaturalitate_ ("not established as supernatural") and opposed the devotion; some alleged messages were later acknowledged by the DDF to contain contradictions or to be "connected with the desires or interests of the alleged visionaries."

## The 2024 Dicastery Note and Current Status

- **The _Nihil obstat_**: The 2024 note concludes that, in the midst of the experience, the Holy Spirit has acted for the good of the People of God, and it recognizes the spiritual fruits. The faithful "are authorized to give to it their adherence in a prudent manner," and pilgrimages to Medjugorje may be organized by dioceses and parishes. This corresponds to the outcome category of the 2024 Norms that imposes "no obstacles" (_nihil obstat_).
- **No Judgment on Supernatural Character**: The Dicastery expressly refrains from any declaration that the events are of supernatural origin (_constat de supernaturalitate_), and it does not imply any judgment about the moral life of the alleged visionaries.
- **Caveats on the Messages**: The note acknowledges that some messages contain "contradictions" or imprecise, theologically incorrect mystical language (citing the expression "my Son, one and triune, loves you," 2 November 2017, as an example to be understood in a Trinitarian-orthodox sense).
- **Ongoing Vigilance**: Under the 2024 Norms, the Diocesan Bishop continues to exercise vigilance over the phenomenon, watching over the fruits and the limits of any permitted veneration.

## Pastoral Guidance

Catholics may participate in the devotional life of Medjugorje with prudence and obedience, receiving the spiritual goods offered (prayer, the Rosary, fasting, the sacraments) while remaining free of any obligation to believe in the supernatural origin of the events. The Church's discipline requires that the alleged "messages" never be presented as adding to the deposit of faith, and that all devotion remain subject to the local Ordinary and the norms of the Holy See. The case remains open: Medjugorje is a devotion in progress, neither canonically approved nor forbidden.
