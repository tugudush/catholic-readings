# Our Lady of America (1954–1960)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The alleged revelations of "Our Lady of America, the Immaculate Virgin" were received by an American religious sister in the 1950s and remain among the most prominent **unapproved** American private revelations:

- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ; the Church has **never approved** them, and belief in them is not required.
- The case is distinguished by the fact that it was never formally condemned either, and devotion — centered on a proposed national shrine — continues privately among some of the faithful. It is therefore classified among ongoing, unapproved phenomena under the Church's vigilance.

## The Alleged Revelations to Sister Mary Ephrem

Between **1954 and 1960**, Sister **Mary Ephrem Neuzil** (1899–1985), a member of the Congregation of the Sisters of the Precious Blood (a community then based in Ohio), reported a series of apparitions and locutions of the Blessed Virgin Mary, under the direction of her spiritual director, Father Philip Furlong, and with the knowledge of Archbishop Paul F. Leibold, a longtime supporter.

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The Location**: The revelations occurred in Ohio — at the community's motherhouse near Dayton and, in the reported accounts, at New Riegel and in the Diocese of Toledo — and were directed toward the United States as a nation.
2. **The Title and Message**: Mary appeared as the "**Immaculate Virgin**" and "**Our Lady of America, the Immaculate Virgin**," calling for a renewal of devotion to the Immaculate Conception, for prayer, penance, and the sanctification of the family. The central request was the building of a **national shrine to the Immaculate Conception** as a sign of the nation's consecration to Mary.
3. **The Written Account**: Sister Mary Ephrem left extensive written accounts of the messages, which have been circulated privately and, later, by devotional groups. She died in 1985.
4. **The Absence of Approval**: Despite the support of individual clergy, no competent ecclesiastical authority ever approved the revelations or authorized the proposed shrine. The Archdiocese of Cincinnati and the relevant dioceses took no act of approbation, and the Holy See has made no pronouncement.
5. **The Devotional Continuation**: Devotion to "Our Lady of America" continues among some Catholics, with prayer cards and a proposed shrine project, but it lacks any episcopal or pontifical recognition.

## Current Status

- **No Approval and No Condemnation**: The Church has issued no decree either of approbation or of condemnation. The phenomenon rests, in canon law and pastoral practice, in a state of non-recognition: the faithful are free to weigh it prudently, but it has no standing as an approved devotion.
- **Pastoral Guidance**: Under the Church's norms (including the 2024 _Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena_), private revelations of this kind may not be presented as binding, may not add to the deposit of faith, and must remain subject to the local Ordinary. Any promotion of the devotion as if it were approved would be pastorally imprudent.
- **Ongoing Vigilance**: Because no formal judgment has been rendered, the case remains, in the broad sense, under the Church's ongoing discernment.
