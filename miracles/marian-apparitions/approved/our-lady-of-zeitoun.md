# Our Lady of Zeitoun (1968–1971)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The luminous apparitions of Our Lady of Zeitoun, in a suburb of Cairo, Egypt, are among the most witnessed phenomena in modern religious history, observed by hundreds of thousands of people, including many non-Christians:
- The events were formally recognized by the **Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria**; the Catholic Church never issued a formal decree either of approval or of condemnation, but permitted devotion and did not dispute the phenomenon. They are therefore received by Catholic faithful with respect and prudence, subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ (CCC 67).
- Zeitoun is treated in Catholic compendiums among the widely accepted manifestations of the Blessed Virgin, with the precise ecclesial status of the recognition clearly distinguished.

## The Luminous Apparitions over the Church
Between **April 2, 1968** and **May 1971**, the Blessed Virgin Mary was reported to appear repeatedly, at night, as a luminous figure above the dome of the Coptic Orthodox Church of Saint Mary in Zeitoun, Cairo.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The First Apparition**: On the night of April 2, 1968, Muslim workmen employed at a nearby municipal depot saw a luminous woman on the roof of the church of Saint Mary; the figure was soon identified as the Virgin Mary. The news spread immediately, and crowds gathered.
2. **The Manifestations**: Over the following months and years, the figure appeared on many occasions — sometimes for minutes, sometimes for hours — gliding over the dome, bowing, and appearing with doves of light. The phenomena were witnessed by hundreds of thousands, including Egyptian officials, journalists, and foreign observers, and were photographed and filmed.
3. **The Witnesses**: The apparitions were seen not only by Christians but by Muslims, who venerate Mary as the mother of the prophet Jesus; the figure was observed by groups of people who had not previously believed, and the phenomenon was reported in the Egyptian press and on national television.
4. **The Response of the Coptic Church**: The Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria, after an investigation under Pope Cyril VI, formally declared the apparitions authentic in 1968–1969, and three churches were later built at the site in thanksgiving. Many Coptic and other witnesses reported healings and graces.
5. **The Nature of the Vision**: The manifestations at Zeitoun were predominantly silent and luminous — no verbal message was reported, in contrast to the apparitions at Lourdes, Fatima, or Kibeho; the silent presence of the Virgin was itself understood as a sign of peace and consolation at a time of regional turmoil.

## Ecclesiastical Status
- **Coptic Orthodox Recognition**: The Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria formally accepted the authenticity of the apparitions, and the site became one of the most visited Marian shrines in the Middle East.
- **Catholic Posture**: The Catholic Church issued no formal decree on Zeitoun. The local Latin Catholic authorities did not censure the devotion, and Catholic witnesses and hierarchs have spoken of the events with respect. Because the Church of Rome has made no pronouncement, Catholic participation rests on prudence and on the general norms governing private revelation.
- **A Sign of Ecumenism**: The events at Zeitoun, occurring in a predominantly Muslim country and recognized by the Coptic Orthodox Church, have been received as a sign of Mary's motherly presence and as an occasion of interreligious respect and ecumenical encounter.

## The Message and Devotion
The message of Zeitoun, delivered not in words but in a luminous maternal presence, is understood as a call to **peace, prayer, and confidence in the Mother of God** at a time of tension in the Middle East. The devotion honors the silent Queen of Peace whose presence drew thousands to prayer and to the churches of the region, and the shrine remains a place of pilgrimage and interreligious encounter.
