# Our Lady of Laus (1664–1718)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The apparitions of Our Lady of Laus, in the French Alps of the Hautes-Alpes region, constitute one of the longest series of approved apparitions in the history of the Church, unfolding over more than half a century to a single seer:
- As private revelation (CCC 67), the Laus events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ and are proposed to the faithful as "worthy of belief" by the competent ecclesiastical authority.
- Laus was the first French apparition to receive formal episcopal approval since the nineteenth-century approvals of Lourdes and La Salette, and it is distinguished by its uniquely **sacramental** character: the shrine's mission is centered on the Sacrament of Penance.

## The Apparitions to Benoîte Rencurel
Between **1664 and 1718**, the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared on an "incalculable" number of occasions to **Venerable Benoîte (Benedicta) Rencurel** (1647–1718), a shepherdess of the village of Saint-Étienne-le-Laus, in the Diocese of Embrun (today the Diocese of Gap).

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The First Apparition**: In May 1664, while tending sheep in a narrow valley near Laus, the young Benoîte, who was unable to read and had only recently been taught her catechism, saw a beautiful Lady. The apparition identified herself as **"Dame Marie"** ("I am Lady Marie"), and directed her to a place that, in the Lady's words, would become a place of conversion for sinners.
2. **The Many Apparitions**: The appearances continued for the rest of Benoîte's life — more than fifty years — during which Our Lady instructed her, called her to penance and to the recitation of the Rosary, and entrusted her with a mission for the conversion of sinners.
3. **The Sanction of the Vicar General**: The Vicar General of the Diocese of Embrun formally affirmed the truth of the events (*omnia veridica esse* — "all is true") after investigation, and pilgrimages with notable healings (*mirae sanationes*) began at the site.
4. **The Sanctuary of Reconciliation**: A church and a priests' house were built at Laus expressly so that pilgrims could receive the Sacrament of Reconciliation; this sacramental orientation — reconciliation as the heart of the message — is the defining mark of the Laus devotion.
5. **Later Trials**: Benoîte endured later skepticism and accusations amid the doctrinal and political tensions of the age (including Jansenist-influenced controversy), at times being restricted from Holy Communion. When the sanctuary was entrusted to the Missionary Fathers of Notre-Dame de la Sainte Garde, the suspicions ceased, and she spent her final years in peace, dying in 1718.

## Ecclesiastical Approval
- **Early Approbation**: The episcopal vicar's affirmation and the centuries-long cult at the sanctuary constitute the ancient foundation of the shrine's standing.
- **The 2008 Decree**: On **May 4, 2008**, Bishop **Jean-Michel di Falco** of the Diocese of Gap declared the apparitions of Our Lady of Laus authentic and "worthy of belief," formally approving the devotion. This made Laus the first French apparition formally approved in the modern era after Lourdes and La Salette, and the longest-approved series of apparitions in the Church's history.
- **Venerable Benoîte Rencurel**: The seer's cause of canonization was opened; she was declared **Venerable** by Pope Benedict XVI (decree of 2010), recognizing her heroic virtue.

## The Message and Devotion
The message of Laus is a sustained call to **conversion and sacramental reconciliation**: sinners are invited to return to God through the ministry of the Church, above all the Sacrament of Penance, under the maternal guidance of the Blessed Virgin, who identified herself as "Dame Marie." The shrine of Notre-Dame du Laus remains a center of pilgrimages and of the ministry of confession, and its mission is summarized in the title given to the sanctuary: a house of reconciliation and grace.
