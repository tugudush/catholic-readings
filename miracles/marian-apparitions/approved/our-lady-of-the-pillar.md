# Our Lady of the Pillar (c. 40 A.D.)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The apparition of Our Lady of the Pillar at Zaragoza is the oldest Marian apparition claimed in Catholic tradition, occurring — according to the received tradition — in the lifetime of the Blessed Virgin Mary herself, c. 40 A.D., when Saint James the Greater was evangelizing Spain:

- As with all private revelation (CCC 67), the event is subordinate to the definitive public Revelation of Jesus Christ, which closed with the death of the last Apostle. The Pillar tradition is thus received by the Church as a venerable and pious tradition rather than an article of faith.
- The tradition rests on immemorial cult and the continuous veneration of the shrine at Zaragoza, which has been endorsed and enriched by the papacy across the centuries without ever being proposed as an object of dogmatic faith.

## The Apparition at Zaragoza

According to the ancient tradition preserved in the _Sancta Capilla_ (Holy Chapel) of Zaragoza, the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared to the Apostle Saint James the Greater while he was praying by the banks of the Ebro River with a small band of disciples, discouraged by the meager fruits of his preaching in Spain.

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The Appearance**: Mary appeared standing upon a pillar (Spanish, _el Pilar_) of jasper, accompanied by angels, while yet living on earth in the first century. She asked Saint James to build a church on that spot, promising that the faith he preached would never die in Spain.
2. **The Pillar**: The column upon which she stood was left behind as a relic; it is preserved today in the Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar, encased in silver and bronze, and is venerated as the very pillar of the first apparition.
3. **The First Chapel**: Saint James and his companions constructed a small chapel around the pillar, the first chapel in history dedicated to the Blessed Virgin — the origin of the shrine that has been a center of Spanish Marian devotion ever since.
4. **The Wooden Statue**: A small wooden image of Mary holding the Child Jesus, carved at the time of the apparition according to tradition, stands atop the pillar and is the object of the famous devotion.

## Ecclesiastical Approval and Authority

- **Immemorial Cult and Papal Endorsement**: The devotion has been endorsed by numerous Roman Pontiffs. In 1730, Pope Innocent XIII granted a proper Office and Mass for the feast of Our Lady of the Pillar, which is celebrated on October 12. Later popes, including Saint John Paul II, made pilgrimage to the shrine; the _Santa Capilla_ and Basilica have been enriched with papal privileges, and the feast was long observed throughout the Spanish-speaking world.
- **The Coronation (1905)**: On May 20, 1905, the image was solemnly canonically crowned with the authorization of Pope Saint Pius X, an act that recognizes the antiquity and authenticity of the cult.
- **Patronage**: Our Lady of the Pillar is the Patroness of Spain and of the Hispanic world (_Hispanidad_), a patronage solemnly proclaimed in subsequent papal acts and reflected in the October 12 celebrations.

## The Message and Devotion

The essential message of the Pillar tradition is one of **continuity of faith**: Mary's promise that the faith would never fail in Spain, the stability of the pillar itself, and the call to persevere in evangelization and devotion to her Son. The shrine remains one of the great pilgrimage centers of Europe, and the feast of October 12 — shared with the discovery of the Americas — ties the devotion to the evangelization of the New World.
