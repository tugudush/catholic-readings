# Our Lady of La Salette (1846)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The apparition of Our Lady of La Salette has been declared worthy of belief by the competent ecclesiastical authority, placing it under the Catholic theology of **private revelation**:

- As stated in the Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC 67), private revelations do not form part of the deposit of faith. They are meant to guide Christian living in specific historical epochs.
- The Church permits public devotion but requires prudent discernment to distinguish the central message from any subsequent devotional exaggerations or unauthorized publications.

## The Apparition at La Salette

On September 19, 1846, at approximately 3:00 p.m., the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared on a high mountain pasture near the village of La Salette-Fallavaux, France.

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The Witnesses**: Two young peasant shepherds, Mélanie Calvat (age 14) and Maximin Giraud (age 11), witnessed the apparition.
2. **The Phenomenon**: They saw a "beautiful lady" sitting on a stone, weeping intensely with her face in her hands, surrounded by a brilliant light. She wore a crucifix featuring a hammer and pincers (representing the tools of the Passion and the choices of mankind) and a heavy chain.
3. **The Message**: Mary spoke to the children in French and then in their local patois (Occitan dialect). Her message was one of profound sorrow over the widespread impiety, despoiling of the Sabbath (working on Sundays), blasphemy, and disregard for the Lenten fast.
4. **The Warning and Promises**: She warned of a coming famine, crop failures, and deep suffering if people did not amend their lives, but promised abundance and divine mercy if they returned to prayer and conversion.
5. **The Secrets**: She entrusted a secret message to each child individually, which they were not to disclose until a later time.

## The Alleged "Two Worm-Ridden Popes" Passage

The phrase about "two worm-ridden popes" belongs to a later and disputed layer of La Salette tradition. It should not be presented as part of the approved public message or as a verified transcript of the words spoken during the apparition.

### The Text and Its Provenance

The passage is commonly attributed to a letter Mélanie Calvat wrote to Abbé Isidore Roubaud at Galatina, Italy, on September 30, 1894:

> "Je n'ai pas vu, je ne vois pas de Grand Pape et de Grand Monarque avant une grandissime tribulation, épouvantable, terrible et générale pour toute la chrétienté. Mais avant ce temps il y aura deux fois une paix de peu de durée, deux Papes vermoulus, plats, douteux."

A close translation is:

> "I did not see, I do not see a Great Pope and a Great Monarch before a very great, frightful, terrible, and general tribulation for all Christendom. But before this time there will twice be a peace of short duration: two worm-eaten, flat, doubtful popes."

The French word _vermoulus_ literally means "worm-eaten" or "decayed" and can also have the figurative sense of something rotten or unsound. _Plats_ may be translated as "flat," "mediocre," or "servile," depending on the interpretation. The sentence itself does not name the popes, call them antipopes, or explicitly say that they will reign simultaneously or consecutively.

The two brief written secrets that Mélanie and Maximin sent to Pope Pius IX in 1851 do not contain this phrase in their published transcriptions. The 1894 letter is therefore a later testimony attributed to Mélanie, not one of the original 1851 submissions. The history of these texts and their transmission is discussed in René Laurentin and Michel Corteville's [_Découverte du secret de La Salette_](https://www.fayard.fr/livre/decouverte-du-secret-de-la-salette-9782213612836/), published in 2002.

### Later Interpretations

Some interpreters have understood the phrase as a symbolic warning about papal weakness, corruption, or failure to defend the Catholic faith. Others have treated it as a prediction of two successive antipopes. Older polemical interpretations have proposed Paul VI and John Paul II; more recent writers have proposed Pope Francis and Pope Leo XIV. These identifications are retrospective opinions and are not stated in the passage itself.

Father Gregory Hesse later claimed that the author and former priest Malachi Martin had confirmed the existence of a censored La Salette line. Hesse nevertheless acknowledged that he had not personally seen the alleged original document. This makes the claim secondary oral testimony, not documentary proof.

### Catholic Status of the Passage

The competent ecclesiastical authority approved the apparition and authorized devotion to Our Lady of La Salette in 1851. The official [history of the La Salette sanctuary](https://lasalette.cef.fr/en/history/) presents the central message as a call to conversion. That approval does not authenticate every later text, expansion, or interpretation attributed to Mélanie.

The Holy Office's decree of December 21, 1915, ordered the faithful not to discuss or publish the alleged "Secret de La Salette" in its various forms, including attempts to apply it to present or future events. The decree explicitly stated that it did not prohibit devotion to the Blessed Virgin under the title of Our Lady of La Salette. In 1923, the Holy Office also condemned a reprint of Mélanie's expanded 1879 text and ordered its copies withdrawn; see the [1915 decree](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0d4de0da-b7b2-4738-a98f-8f233b9bc355/ref/page8) and the [1923 decree](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/bd958b08-5143-4968-a772-1ee5963d3835/ref/page46) in _Acta Apostolicae Sedis_.

In accord with [CCC 67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67), this passage is not part of the deposit of faith and cannot be used to establish the identity of a pope, declare a claimant an antipope, or replace the Church's judgment. It is best catalogued as a disputed later La Salette text whose spiritual themes must remain subordinate to the approved call to prayer, penance, and conversion.

## Ecclesiastical Investigation and Controversy

Due to the dramatic warnings in the message and the subsequent behaviors of the seers in their adult lives, La Salette attracted significant controversy. However, the Church carefully separated the core apparition from later disputes:

- **Diocesan Approval**: After a rigorous investigation by a canonical commission, Bishop Philibert de Bruillard of Grenoble published a pastoral letter on November 16, 1851, declaring:
  > "We judge that the apparition of the Blessed Virgin to two shepherds... on September 19, 1846... carries in itself all the characteristics of truth, and that the faithful are justified in believing it certain and doubtless."
- **Inception of the Shrine**: On May 25, 1852, Bishop de Bruillard laid the foundation stone for a major church at the site, which was later elevated to the status of a basilica. The congregation of the Missionaries of Our Lady of La Salette was founded to serve the shrine and spread the message of reconciliation.
- **Vatican Disciplinary Actions**: Due to unauthorized and sensationalist publications about the "Secret of La Salette," the Holy Office issued a decree on December 21, 1915, ordering the faithful to cease publishing or discussing the alleged secret in its various forms, while explicitly stating that this disciplinary measure did not prohibit devotion to "Our Lady, Reconciler of Sinners."
