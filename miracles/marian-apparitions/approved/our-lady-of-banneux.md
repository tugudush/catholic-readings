# Our Lady of Banneux (1933)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The apparitions of Our Lady of Banneux, in the province of Liège, Belgium, occurred in the winter of 1933 to an eleven-year-old girl and were formally approved by the Church within the same generation:
- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ but were judged "worthy of belief" by the competent authority.
- Banneux, with the contemporary apparitions of Beauraing, forms part of the Belgian series of 1932–1933 and is distinctive for its explicit title, "Virgin of the Poor," and for the healing spring that Mary indicated.

## The Apparitions to Mariette Beco
Between **January 15 and March 2, 1933**, the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared eight times to **Mariette Beco (11)** in the garden of her family's home in Banneux, a hamlet of the commune of Louveigné, Belgium.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The First Apparition**: On the evening of January 15, 1933, while looking out her window, Mariette saw a beautiful Lady standing in the garden, clothed in white with a blue sash and a rosary over her arm, who beckoned to her. The Lady's feet did not touch the ground, and a brilliant light surrounded her.
2. **The Title "Virgin of the Poor"**: In answer to Mariette's question, the Lady identified herself: "I am the Virgin of the Poor" (*Je suis la Vierge des Pauvres*).
3. **The Spring and the Message**: On January 18, the Lady led Mariette to a small spring and told her it was reserved for her; later she explained, "This spring is reserved for all nations, for the relief of the sick." On subsequent apparitions she asked for prayer, for a small chapel, and gave her final message: "I have come to relieve suffering; pray, and I will pray for you." She also added: "Believe in me, and I will believe in you."
4. **The Request for a Chapel**: Mary asked that a chapel be built at the site of the spring; a chapel was soon erected and became the heart of the shrine of Banneux.
5. **The Witnesses**: Mariette, examined repeatedly and separated from others, gave consistent testimony. The site drew large crowds, and numerous healings were reported in connection with the spring, which was medically studied and found to be ordinary water — the cures being attributed to faith and the intercession of the Virgin of the Poor.

## Ecclesiastical Approval
- **Canonical Approval (1949)**: On **August 22, 1949**, Bishop Louis-Joseph Kerkhofs of Liège declared the apparitions of Banneux authentic and authorized public devotion to Our Lady, Virgin of the Poor.
- **The Basilica**: A shrine complex was built at Banneux; in 1985 the church was elevated to the rank of a **minor basilica** by Pope Saint John Paul II, who had made the shrine a place of personal devotion and visited it in spirit through his apostolic ministry.
- **A European and World Shrine**: Banneux became a major pilgrimage center, especially for the sick, and was a station of the Marian Year and of subsequent papal initiatives for the suffering.

## The Message and Devotion
The message of Banneux is one of **trust in the maternal care of the Virgin of the Poor for the sick and the suffering of all nations**. The "spring reserved for all nations, for the relief of the sick" is understood as a sign of the healing and consoling presence of Mary, who calls all to prayer and to confidence: "Believe in me, and I will believe in you." The shrine of Banneux remains a center of pilgrimage for the sick and for all who seek the intercession of the Mother of the poor.
