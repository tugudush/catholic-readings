# Our Lady of Pontmain (1871)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The apparition of Our Lady of Pontmain, in the French department of Mayenne, is one of the most fully documented and quickly approved Marian apparitions of the nineteenth century:
- As private revelation (CCC 67), the event is subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ, but it was judged "worthy of belief" by the competent ecclesiastical authority within a year of its occurrence.
- Pontmain occurred during the Franco-Prussian War (1870–1871), at a moment when Prussian armies were advancing through France, and the apparition is remembered for the written message of hope that appeared in the heavens.

## The Apparition of January 17, 1871
On the evening of **January 17, 1871**, in the village of Pontmain, the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared in the sky above the house of the Barbedette family to two boys, **Eugène Barbedette (age 12)** and **Joseph Barbedette (age 10)**, who were the first to see her; a short time later two girls, Françoise Richer and Jeanne-Marie Lebossé, also saw the vision.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The Appearance**: Mary appeared dressed in a dark blue robe covered with golden stars, with a crown of gold, her hands joined in prayer. She wore golden slippers and a black veil, and her face bore an expression of profound gentleness.
2. **The Written Message**: As the crowd — which grew to several hundred villagers — prayed the Rosary and the litanies, golden letters appeared beneath Mary's feet, forming three phrases in French:
   > "Mais priez, mes enfants, Dieu vous exaucera en peu de temps. Mon Fils se laisse toucher." — "But pray, my children; God will answer you in a little while. My Son allows Himself to be moved [touched]."
3. **The Sign of the Cross**: As the apparition drew to a close, a small red cross appeared above each of Mary's hands, and a white cross appeared above her heart; she slowly made the Sign of the Cross, and the vision faded as she smiled and blessed the crowd.
4. **The Context of War**: The apparition coincided with the darkest hour of the war for the region. The very night of the apparition, the Prussian general in command — who was preparing to attack nearby Laval — received orders to halt; the village and the region were spared, which the faithful have always attributed to Our Lady's intercession.
5. **The Witnesses**: The children, who had no prior acquaintance with such phenomena and were examined separately and repeatedly, gave consistent accounts. The parish priest, the Abbé Guérin, was among the earliest to interrogate them and testified to their sincerity.

## Ecclesiastical Approval
- **Canonical Approval (1872)**: On **February 2, 1872**, Bishop Casimir Wicart of Laval declared the apparition authentic and authorized public devotion, concluding that the events "bear the character of the miraculous." This was one of the swiftest formal approvals in the history of apparitions.
- **The Basilica**: A shrine church was built on the site of the apparition and was consecrated; in 1920, the sanctuary was raised to the rank of a **minor basilica** by Pope Benedict XV.
- **Liturgical Recognition**: The feast of Our Lady of Pontmain, under the title **Our Lady of Hope** (*Notre-Dame de l'Espérance*), is celebrated locally, and the devotion received continued papal favor in the following decades.

## The Message and Devotion
The message of Pontmain is one of **prayer and hope**: the call to pray, the promise of a speedy answer, and the assurance that the Son of God "allows Himself to be moved" by the intercession of His Mother and the prayers of His people. The devotion to **Our Lady of Hope** has spread far beyond the Mayenne, and the basilica of Pontmain remains a place of pilgrimage for healing, peace, and confidence in divine providence.
