# Our Lady of the Snows (c. 352–366 A.D.)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The tradition of Our Lady of the Snows is one of the most ancient Marian commemorations of the Roman Church, associated with the foundation of the Liberian Basilica (the Basilica of Saint Mary Major) on the Esquiline Hill in Rome:
- The event belongs to the class of **private revelation** in the broad sense, received by the Church as a pious and venerable tradition rather than as an article of faith (CCC 67).
- Its enduring significance lies in the fact that it gave rise to one of the four patriarchal basilicas of Rome and to the dedication of the Western Church's principal Marian sanctuary, whose foundation is commemorated liturgically on August 5.

## The Miraculous Snowfall
According to the tradition preserved since late antiquity, during the pontificate of Pope Liberius (352–366), a noble Roman couple — John and his wife, who were without heirs — resolved to dedicate their wealth to the Blessed Virgin. Mary appeared to them and to Pope Liberius in a dream, directing that a church be built on the Esquiline Hill at the place where snow would fall.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The Sign**: On the night of August 5, during the height of the Roman summer, snow fell upon the Esquiline Hill in the shape of the ground plan of a church. Pope Liberius and the patrician couple marked out the perimeter and raised the basilica.
2. **The Liberian Basilica**: The church built on this site became the Basilica of Saint Mary Major, so named because it is the principal church in the world dedicated to the Blessed Virgin Mother of God. It houses the ancient icon of the *Salus Populi Romani* ("Salvation of the Roman People") and the relic of the Holy Crib of the Nativity.
3. **The Name "Our Lady of the Snows"**: The title derives from the miraculous snowfall; the dedication is also known as *Sancta Maria ad Nives* ("Saint Mary of the Snows") and, since the reforms of the liturgical calendar, as the Dedication of the Basilica of Saint Mary Major.

## Ecclesiastical Authority
- **Papal Recognition**: The feast of the Dedication of Saint Mary Major is inscribed in the universal Roman Calendar on August 5, with a proper liturgy commemorating the foundation. Pope Francis has repeatedly honored the *Salus Populi Romani* icon preserved in the basilica, venerating it before and after his apostolic journeys, following the custom of his predecessors.
- **The *Salus Populi Romani* (1613–1614)**: Although distinct from the snowfall legend, the venerated icon of Mary *Salus Populi Romani* — long housed in the basilica — received its solemn crown in 1838 and is the object of intense Roman devotion, officially venerated by the Roman Pontiffs.
- **The Basilica as a Patriarchal See**: Saint Mary Major is one of the four papal (patriarchal) basilicas of Rome and enjoys the privilege of the *Portiuncula*-style plenary indulgences under the usual conditions, attesting to the Church's continued favor for the shrine.

## The Message and Devotion
The message of the tradition is one of **generous dedication to God and to the Mother of God**: the couple's willingness to surrender their inheritance, the divine sign of the snow, and the subsequent centuries of Marian worship at the Esquiline. The Basilica of Saint Mary Major remains a center of devotion, the station church for the Roman stational liturgy of the August 5 feast, and the custodian of the *Salus Populi Romani*, venerated as the protectress of the city of Rome.
