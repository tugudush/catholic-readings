# Our Lady of Good Help (Champion, 1859)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The apparition of Our Lady of Good Help at Champion, Wisconsin, holds the singular distinction of being the **first and only apparition formally approved by a bishop of the United States**:
- As with all private revelations (CCC 67), the event is subordinate to the definitive public Revelation of Christ and is proposed to the faithful as "worthy of belief."
- The 2010 approval by the local Ordinary made Champion the first site in the United States whose apparitions received the formal approbation of the Church, a landmark in American Catholic history.

## The Apparitions to Adele Brise
In **October 1859**, the Blessed Virgin Mary appeared three times to **Adèle (Adele) Brise** (1831–1896), a young Belgian immigrant of the Diocese of Green Bay, Wisconsin, in the vicinity of the present-day Shrine of Our Lady of Good Help in the town of Champion (near Green Bay).

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The First Encounter**: While walking to Mass on a Sunday morning in October 1859, Adèle saw a woman clothed in dazzling white, with a yellow sash around her waist, standing between two trees in a burst of light. She took the vision to be a soul in need of prayers, and said nothing of it.
2. **The Second Apparition**: On the following Sunday, the same Lady appeared again while Adèle was walking with two companions; the companions saw nothing, and the Lady vanished when Adèle's companions drew near.
3. **The Third Apparition and the Message**: On the third Sunday, the Lady appeared while Adèle was making her confession and receiving Communion; afterward the vision returned in the same place. When Adèle asked who she was, the Lady replied:
   > "I am the Queen of Heaven who prays for the conversion of sinners. I want you to do the same. ... Gather the children in this wild country and teach them what they should know for salvation."
   She also asked for penance and prayer, and urged that the children be instructed in the faith, that the Gospel be preached, and that a chapel be built in her honor.
4. **The Response**: With the approval of her confessor and of the parish priest, Adèle undertook a life of catechesis, traveling to teach children their faith in the scattered settlements of northeastern Wisconsin — a mission she pursued for the rest of her life. A small chapel was built at Champion, the nucleus of the present shrine.
5. **The Preservation of 1871**: During the great **Peshtigo Fire of October 8, 1871** — the deadliest fire in American history — which destroyed surrounding towns and claimed over a thousand lives, the grounds of the shrine and those who took refuge there were preserved from the flames, an event long regarded as miraculous protection by Our Lady of Good Help.

## Ecclesiastical Approval
- **The 2010 Decree**: On **December 8, 2010**, Bishop **David L. Ricken** of Green Bay, after a thorough canonical investigation, formally declared the apparitions of 1859 "**worthy of belief**" and approved the devotion, making Champion the first Church-approved apparition site in the United States.
- **The Shrine**: The Shrine of Our Lady of Good Help at Champion was designated a diocesan shrine, and on December 8, 2016, it was elevated by the United States Conference of Catholic Bishops to the status of a **National Shrine** — the National Shrine of Our Lady of Good Help.
- **The Seer**: Adèle Brise is honored as a Servant of God; her cause of beatification has been opened, and she is remembered as the catechist of the frontier who answered Mary's call to teach the children.

## The Message and Devotion
The message of Champion is a call to **catechesis, conversion, and prayer**: the Queen of Heaven's request to "gather the children" and teach them what they need for salvation, together with the summons to penance and the conversion of sinners. The devotion unites the classic Marian themes of the Rosary and penance with a specifically catechetical mission, and the National Shrine continues to draw pilgrims from across North America.
