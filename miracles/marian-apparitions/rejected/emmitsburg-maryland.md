# Our Lady of Emmitsburg, Maryland (1993– )

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The alleged apparitions of Emmitsburg, Maryland, associated with the seer Gianna Talone Sullivan, were among the most prominent American private-revelation cases of the 1990s, and they were **rejected** by the competent ecclesiastical authority:

- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ; the Church's pastors have the authority to judge and prohibit them.
- The Archdiocese of Baltimore investigated the events and determined that they could not be established as supernatural, and prohibited public devotion at the site; the rejection has been maintained by successive archbishops.

## The Alleged Apparitions to Gianna Talone Sullivan

Beginning in **1993**, **Gianna Talone Sullivan** (born 1959), a Catholic woman of Scottsdale, Arizona, and later of Emmitsburg, Maryland, claimed to receive apparitions and locutions of the Blessed Virgin Mary and of Jesus Christ. The events associated with the devotion centered on a property near the historic Saint Elizabeth Ann Seton shrine in Emmitsburg.

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The Messages**: The alleged messages — gathered in a long series of published volumes — emphasized prayer, penance, the Rosary, and devotion to the Divine Will, together with apocalyptic warnings and calls for the founding of a new religious community and a retreat center.
2. **The Move to Emmitsburg**: In the mid-1990s, the seer and her supporters relocated to Emmitsburg, Maryland, where a "Saint Joseph's House of Prayer" and plans for a large devotional complex were promoted, drawing pilgrims from across the country.
3. **The Publications**: The messages were widely circulated in books and newsletters, giving the devotion a national reach and prompting sustained episcopal scrutiny.
4. **The Seer**: Talone Sullivan has maintained her claims and continues a ministry of prayer and spiritual direction; the case has been the object of repeated ecclesiastical statements.

## The Rejection by the Church

- **The 1999 Declaration**: In **1999**, Cardinal **William H. Keeler**, Archbishop of Baltimore, issued a statement declaring that the alleged apparitions at Emmitsburg could **not be established as of supernatural origin** (_non constat de supernaturalitate_), and prohibited public promotion of the alleged messages at the site.
- **The 2010 Confirmation**: On **April 27, 2010**, Archbishop **Edwin F. O'Brien** of Baltimore reaffirmed the negative judgment, declaring that "no credence should be given" to the alleged apparitions, that the events "did not bear the marks of the supernatural," and that public devotion at the site was not permitted. He explicitly invoked the norms of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith on the discernment of apparitions.
- **No Approval**: No approbation was ever issued by the Holy See, and the negative judgment has been maintained by the archbishops of Baltimore.

## Current Status

- **Formally Rejected**: The competent local Ordinary twice declared the events not established as supernatural and prohibited public promotion and devotion — a formal and reiterated rejection.
- **Obedience Required**: The faithful are bound to refrain from promoting the rejected messages and gatherings and to prefer the Church's approved devotions; individual conviction cannot override the pastoral judgment of the legitimate pastor.
