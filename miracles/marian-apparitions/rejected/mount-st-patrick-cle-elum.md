# Our Lady of Mount St. Patrick, Cle Elum (1975– )

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The alleged apparitions of Mount St. Patrick, near Cle Elum in central Washington State, are among the more recent American cases formally **rejected** by the competent ecclesiastical authority:

- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ; the Church's pastors have the authority to judge and prohibit them.
- The Diocese of Yakima investigated the events and determined that they could not be established as supernatural, prohibiting public devotion and warning the faithful against the claims.

## The Alleged Apparitions to "Mama Mary"

Beginning in **1975**, an alleged series of apparitions and locutions was reported at Mount St. Patrick, a private property in the Cascade foothills near Cle Elum, Washington, in the Diocese of Yakima. The central figure was a woman known as **"Mary Rose"**, who claimed to receive messages from the Blessed Virgin Mary under the title "**Mary, Refuge of Sinners**" (_Refugium Peccatorum_).

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The Site**: The devotion centered on a mountainside property where pilgrims gathered, reportedly experiencing visions, lights, and healings, and where devotional structures and stations were erected over the years.
2. **The Messages**: The alleged messages combined calls to prayer, penance, and the Rosary with apocalyptic warnings and instructions for the site, and urged a crusade of prayer for the conversion of the world.
3. **The Movement**: The "Mount St. Patrick" movement developed a following in the Pacific Northwest and beyond, with organized pilgrimages and published materials, and has continued in some form into the twenty-first century.
4. **The Investigation**: The Diocese of Yakima conducted a canonical investigation of the alleged apparitions, examining the seer, the messages, and the fruits of the movement.

## The Rejection by the Church

- **The 1984 Declaration**: In **1984**, Bishop **William S. Skylstad** of Yakima issued a statement declaring that the alleged apparitions at Mount St. Patrick could **not be established as of supernatural origin** (_non constat de supernaturalitate_), and that "no credence should be given" to the claims. Public devotion at the site was prohibited.
- **The Prohibition of Publicity**: The diocese prohibited the promotion of the alleged messages and the organization of pilgrimages as an approved devotion, and warned the faithful against participation, while leaving the private judgment of individuals subject to the pastoral directives of the Church.
- **No Approval**: No approbation was ever issued by the Holy See, and the negative judgment has not been reversed.

## Current Status

- **Formally Rejected**: The competent local Ordinary determined that the events were not established as supernatural and prohibited public promotion and devotion — a formal rejection of the alleged apparitions.
- **Obedience Required**: The faithful are bound to refrain from promoting the rejected devotion and to prefer the Church's approved means of grace; the case is cited among the American examples of episcopal discernment.
