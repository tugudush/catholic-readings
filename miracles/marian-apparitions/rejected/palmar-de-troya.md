# Our Lady of Palmar de Troya (1968– )

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The alleged apparitions of Palmar de Troya, in the Province of Seville, Spain, are among the most serious cases of **rejected** private revelation, because they gave rise not merely to an unapproved devotion but to a schismatic sect:
- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ; the Church judged them and their fruits decisively negative.
- The movement founded upon the alleged apparitions — the "Palmarian Catholic Church" — rejected the authority of the Roman Pontiff, created its own parallel hierarchy, and incurred canonical censures. The alleged apparitions are therefore formally rejected, and adherence to them is incompatible with Catholic communion.

## The Alleged Apparitions to Clemente Domínguez
Beginning in **1968**, **Clemente Domínguez y Gómez** (1946–2005), a young man of Palmar de Troya, together with companions, claimed to receive apparitions of the Blessed Virgin Mary at a site in the Andalusian countryside, in the Archdiocese of Seville.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The Early Messages**: The alleged apparitions, delivered with great frequency, combined calls to penance with increasingly apocalyptic and polemical messages, including denunciations of the post-conciliar Church and of individual Church figures.
2. **The Escalation**: Domínguez and his associate Manuel Corral claimed revelations instructing them to reject the reforms of the Second Vatican Council and eventually to reject the legitimacy of the popes from Pope Paul VI onward.
3. **The Schism (1976–1978)**: In 1976, Domínguez was "consecrated" bishop by Archbishop Pierre Martin Ngo Dinh Thuc, who had himself been drawn into the movement (an act later declared null and illicit). In **1978**, after the death of Pope Paul VI, Domínguez proclaimed himself **Pope Gregory XVII** of a "Palmarian Catholic Church," a schismatic body that has continued under successive self-styled "popes."
4. **The Church's Judgment**: The alleged apparitions were never approved; the movement was formally condemned, its hierarchy's ordinations and consecrations were declared null and illicit, and its members incurred the censures of canon law, including excommunication for the self-styled "pope."

## The Rejection by the Church
- **No Approval**: No decree of approbation was ever issued for the alleged apparitions of Palmar de Troya; the Archdiocese of Seville and the Holy See consistently withheld recognition.
- **Canonical Sanctions**: The "consecrations" of Domínguez and his associates were declared invalid and illicit by the Holy See; Domínguez and the leaders of the Palmarian sect incurred **latae sententiae excommunication**. The sect's parallel hierarchy and its rejection of the legitimate Roman Pontiff constitute a formal schism.
- **Formal Rejection**: The alleged apparitions are judged to be of non-supernatural origin and their promotion gravely harmful; adherence to the Palmarian sect is incompatible with full communion with the Catholic Church.

## Current Status
- **Formally Rejected and Schismatic**: Palmar de Troya is the extreme case in which unapproved apparitions produced a full schism; the Church's rejection extends both to the alleged phenomena and to the sect they generated.
- **Warning to the Faithful**: The faithful are warned against the sect's publications and gatherings; return to Catholic communion is urged upon those who have adhered to it, through repentance and reconciliation with the legitimate pastors of the Church.
