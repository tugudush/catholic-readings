# Our Lady of All Nations, Amsterdam (1945–1959)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The alleged apparitions of the "Lady of All Nations" in Amsterdam, Netherlands, received a formal negative judgment from the highest doctrinal authority of the Church, which was publicly confirmed in 2024:

- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ; the Church's pastors have the authority to judge and prohibit them.
- On **11 July 2024**, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (DDF) published a press release disclosing that the Ordinary Session of the then Sacred Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith of **27 March 1974** had decided, unanimously (_omnes_), that the events "**constat de non supernaturalitate**" — it is established that they are **not of supernatural origin** — and that further investigation was declined. The decisions were approved by Pope Paul VI on **5 April 1974**. The alleged apparitions are therefore formally **rejected** by the Holy See.

## The Alleged Apparitions to Ida Peerdeman

Between **1945** and **1959**, **Ida Peerdeman** (1905–1996), a Catholic woman of Amsterdam, claimed to receive apparitions and locutions of the Blessed Virgin Mary, who identified herself as the "**Lady of All Nations**" (_Vrouwe van alle Volkeren_) and, in the final messages, as "the Co-Redemptrix, Mediatrix, and Advocate."

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The First Apparitions**: Beginning on March 25, 1945 — while the Netherlands was still under German occupation — Peerdeman reported seeing a Lady of light who asked for prayer and identified herself as "Mary, Mother of God."
2. **The Title and the Prayer**: The Lady gave a prayer invoking her as "Lady of All Nations who once was Mary," and the messages increasingly urged the Church to recognize the title and to promote the devotion worldwide.
3. **The Controversial Doctrine**: The later messages — including the term "Co-Redemptrix" — became the focus of theological controversy, as they were judged to employ imprecise and theologically problematic language regarding Mary's cooperation in redemption.
4. **The Image**: In 1951, an image of the "Lady of All Nations" — a woman standing on a globe before the Cross, with rays of light — was produced from the seer's descriptions and became the devotional object of the movement.
5. **The Movement**: The devotion spread internationally, with prayer groups, publications, and the construction of a chapel in Amsterdam; it was repeatedly submitted to ecclesiastical scrutiny.

## The Rejection by the Church

- **The 1974 Decision**: The Ordinary Session of the CDF of **27 March 1974** voted unanimously: (1) on the doctrinal judgment, _"constat de non supernaturalitate"_ — established as **not supernatural**; and (2) on further investigation, _negative_. Pope Paul VI approved the decisions on **5 April 1974**.
- **The 2002 Local Approval and Its Aftermath**: In 2002, Bishop Josef M. Punt of Haarlem-Amsterdam issued a decree approving the "Lady of All Nations" devotion locally — an act that created confusion, since it appeared to contradict the earlier Roman judgment.
- **The 2024 DDF Disclosure**: On **11 July 2024**, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, noting the "persistent doubts raised about the alleged apparitions," publicly disclosed the 1974 decisions precisely in order that "the holy People of God and its Pastors may draw the appropriate conclusions." This communication restored clarity: the Church's definitive doctrinal judgment is that the events were **not supernatural**, and the 2002 local act does not overturn it.

## Current Status

- **Formally Rejected by the Holy See**: The unanimous 1974 judgment of _constat de non supernaturalitate_, approved by Pope Paul VI and publicly confirmed by the DDF in 2024, constitutes a formal rejection of the alleged apparitions by the supreme doctrinal authority.
- **Obedience Required**: The faithful are bound to respect the Church's judgment; the promotion of the "Lady of All Nations" devotion as an approved apparition is not permitted, and the faithful are to draw the appropriate pastoral conclusions from the Dicastery's communication.
- **A Case of Clarified History**: Amsterdam illustrates both the Church's confidentiality regarding negative judgments in past decades and the contemporary policy, under the new norms, of transparency in communicating discernment outcomes.
