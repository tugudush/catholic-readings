# Bayside: "Our Lady of the Roses" (1970–1995)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation
The alleged apparitions of Bayside, in the New York City borough of Queens, are among the most prominent cases of **formally rejected** private revelation in the United States:
- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ, and the Church has the authority to judge and, where necessary, to prohibit them.
- The Diocese of Brooklyn formally investigated and condemned the "Bayside apparitions" and their promotion, and successive local Ordinaries have repeated the prohibition. The case is therefore classified among **rejected** phenomena: the faithful are not permitted to adhere to them.

## The Alleged Apparitions to Veronica Lueken
Beginning in **1970** and continuing until her death in **1995**, **Veronica Lueken** (1923–1995), a Catholic housewife of Bayside, Queens, claimed to receive apparitions of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Jesus Christ, and a host of saints and angels at a site near the old Saint Robert Bellarmine church property in Bayside.

### Key Historical Details:
1. **The "Our Lady of the Roses" Messages**: Lueken's messages, delivered at "rosary vigils" that drew thousands, combined the traditional call to prayer, penance, and the Rosary with apocalyptic warnings, predictions of world catastrophe, and polemical attacks on the hierarchy, the liturgy, and the Second Vatican Council.
2. **The "Warnings" and Predictions**: The messages included specific predictions and a "three days of darkness" motif, and claimed to deliver divine condemnations of named Church figures — elements that escalated the conflict with ecclesiastical authority.
3. **The Vigils**: The Bayside vigils became a fixture of American traditionalist popular piety, with a large following, published "blue books" of messages, and media attention through the 1970s and 1980s.
4. **The Seer**: Lueken died in 1995; the movement continued after her death under the name of "Our Lady of the Roses," with followers maintaining the vigils and the publications.

## The Rejection by the Church
- **The 1986 Condemnation**: On **May 5, 1986**, Bishop **Francis J. Mugavero** of Brooklyn issued a formal statement declaring that the alleged apparitions of Bayside were **"not of supernatural origin"** and that "**no credence should be given**" to the messages. He prohibited the promotion of the devotion within the diocese and warned the faithful against participation.
- **The 1987 Publication Ban**: On **January 5, 1987**, the Diocese of Brooklyn formally prohibited the publication and distribution of the Bayside messages ("blue books") within the diocese, an act of ecclesiastical discipline reaffirming the earlier condemnation.
- **Successive Confirmations**: Subsequent bishops of Brooklyn and the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith were informed of and did not disturb the local prohibitions; the Archdiocese of New York also warned the faithful. The movement was never approved by any ecclesiastical authority.

## Current Status
- **Formally Rejected**: The competent local Ordinary has definitively judged the events not to be of supernatural origin and has prohibited adherence to and promotion of the "Our Lady of the Roses" messages. This constitutes a formal rejection of the alleged apparitions.
- **Obedience Required**: Under the Church's discipline, the faithful are bound to refrain from participating in the prohibited vigils and from spreading the condemned messages; private judgment may not override the pastoral judgment of the legitimate pastor.
- **A Warning Case**: Bayside is frequently cited as a cautionary example of how apocalyptic speculation, polemics against the hierarchy, and unapproved "revelations" converge in a manner that the Church must prudently and firmly reject.
