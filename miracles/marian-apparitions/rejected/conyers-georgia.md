# Our Lady of Conyers, Georgia (1987–1998)

## Theological Introduction to Private Revelation

The alleged apparitions of Conyers, in Rockdale County, Georgia, were among the most widely followed American Marian cases of the late twentieth century, and they were **rejected** by the competent ecclesiastical authority:

- As private revelation (CCC 67), the events are subordinate to the public Revelation of Christ; the Church's pastors have the authority to judge and prohibit them.
- The Archdiocese of Atlanta investigated the events, determined that they could not be established as supernatural, and directed the faithful away from them; no approval was ever granted.

## The Alleged Apparitions to Nancy Fowler

Beginning in **1987** and continuing until **1998**, **Nancy Fowler** (born 1948), a Catholic woman of Conyers, Georgia, claimed to receive apparitions of the Blessed Virgin Mary and, reportedly, of Jesus Christ at her farm, which she named "Heaven's Gate."

### Key Historical Details:

1. **The Apparitions**: Fowler reported visions of Mary, typically on the 13th of the month — echoing the Fatima pattern — accompanied by messages of prayer, penance, and the Rosary, and by reported healings and "luminous" phenomena at her farm.
2. **The Crowds**: At the height of the devotion in the early 1990s, tens of thousands of pilgrims gathered at the farm on the monthly dates, making Conyers one of the most frequented alleged apparition sites in the United States.
3. **The Seer**: Fowler, examined by the Church, maintained her claims until her reported final apparitions in 1998, after which the public gatherings ceased. She lived quietly thereafter, and died in 2018.
4. **The Ecclesiastical Investigation**: The Archdiocese of Atlanta conducted a canonical investigation, including interviews with the seer, examination of the messages, and observation of the gatherings, over the course of the events.

## The Rejection by the Church

- **The 1997 Declaration**: On **November 5, 1997**, the Archdiocese of Atlanta, in a statement issued with the knowledge of the Holy See, declared that the alleged apparitions at Conyers could **not be established as of supernatural origin** (_non constat de supernaturalitate_), and that "no credence should be given" to them. The faithful were advised not to participate in the gatherings and were reminded that participation was not required by Catholic faith.
- **The 1998 Pastoral Letter**: On **May 8, 1998**, the Archdiocese issued a further pastoral communication reiterating the negative judgment, prohibiting the promotion of the alleged apparitions, and calling the faithful to the ordinary means of grace — the sacraments, the liturgy, and the Church's approved devotions.
- **No Subsequent Approval**: The negative judgment was never reversed, and no approbation was ever issued by the Holy See.

## Current Status

- **Formally Rejected**: The competent local Ordinary determined that the events were not established as supernatural and prohibited their promotion — a formal rejection of the alleged apparitions.
- **Obedience Required**: The faithful are bound to refrain from promoting or participating in the rejected gatherings and to prefer the Church's approved means of devotion; the case is cited as an example of a large-scale devotion judged negatively by the Church.
