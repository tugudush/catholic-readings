# List of Eucharistic Miracles

## Theological Introduction

The Church distinguishes two realities that are both called "Eucharistic miracles":

- **The Eucharistic mystery itself** — the Real Presence of Christ under the species of bread and wine, effected by the words of consecration. This belongs to the deposit of faith. The Council of Trent (Session XIII, canon 1) defined that Christ is present "truly, really, and substantially" in the Most Holy Sacrament, and canon 2 defined **transubstantiation** — the conversion of the whole substance of bread into His Body and the whole substance of wine into His Blood, while the species (accidents) of bread and wine remain. In this sense, every validly celebrated Mass is already a Eucharistic miracle.
- **Extraordinary Eucharistic phenomena** — visible signs in which the accidents themselves are miraculously altered: a Host appearing as flesh, Precious Blood appearing on the Host or on sacred linens, Hosts preserved incorrupt for centuries, Hosts surviving fire, or images appearing on the sacred species.

Extraordinary Eucharistic miracles are not part of the deposit of faith. When investigated and found credible by ecclesiastical authority, they are treated as **private revelations or extraordinary signs** (cf. CCC 67): they may be accepted prudently, but no Catholic is bound to believe in any particular miracle with the assent of faith. As Saint Thomas Aquinas teaches, in such apparitions one does not see Christ's proper species, but "a species formed miraculously either in the eyes of the beholders or in the sacramental dimensions themselves" (_Summa Theologiae_ III, q. 76, a. 8, ad 2). The miracle, if genuine, neither creates nor intensifies Christ's presence; rather, it serves as a **motive of credibility** (CCC 156) — a reasonable sign confirming the faith of believers and inviting unbelievers to the truth of the Real Presence.

## The Canonical Catalogue

The most widely recognized compilation of Eucharistic miracles is the international exhibition **"The Eucharistic Miracles of the World,"** designed and created by **Saint Carlo Acutis** (canonized 7 September 2025), which has been displayed at the Vatican and in churches worldwide. It catalogues over 150 miracles in 166 exhibition panels, organized by country, together with sections on saints and mystics, the Blessed Virgin Mary, and miraculous communions. The Real Presence Eucharistic Education and Adoration Association maintains an even longer catalogue numbering several hundred reported cases.

The listings below follow the Acutis catalogue, which remains the standard reference. Unless otherwise indicated, the medieval and early-modern entries are **bleeding-Host miracles** — cases in which Precious Blood appeared on the consecrated Host or on the corporal, the altar linen, or a sacred vessel. Detailed studies of two of the greatest miracles are linked at the end of this document.

## The Church's Discernment

The Church does not formally "approve" a Eucharistic miracle as it might declare an apparition "worthy of belief"; rather, bishops and the Holy See investigate each claim — historically and, where possible, scientifically — and permit or foster the cult where credible. Modern discernment routinely concludes that some reported "bleeding" or discolored Hosts have natural causes, typically the red-pigmented bacterium _Serratia marcescens_ or fungal growth:

- **Dallas, Texas (2006)**: a reddened Host was determined by the diocese to be fungal mycelia and bacteria.
- **Salt Lake City (2015)**: a "bleeding" Host was identified as mold.
- **Indianapolis (2025)**: laboratory results indicated the discolored Host was not miraculous.
- **Aalst, Belgium (2016)**: a reddened Host in a private home was attributed to possible microbial growth.

A negative or skeptical finding touches only the particular claim and never the dogma of the Real Presence: the truth of transubstantiation rests on the word of Christ and the teaching of the Church, not on any empirical sign.
