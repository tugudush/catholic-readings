# Church Assessment & Warnings — _The Book of Truth_ (Maria Divine Mercy)

> ⚠️ **Summary.** _The Book of Truth_ has **no ecclesiastical approval**. It is not an approved private revelation, and no Catholic is obliged or encouraged to accept it. Beyond mere non-approval, several diocesan authorities have **prohibited or restricted its dissemination**, finding elements of the texts contrary to Catholic faith and theology. The most serious doctrinal problem is its teaching of a **literal earthly millennium** (millenarianism), which the Church has explicitly rejected (CCC 676). The messages' treatment of the papacy also places adherents at risk of **schism** (CIC 751; 1364 §1).

---

## 1. The Church's Framework for Judging Alleged Revelations

Alleged private revelations are not self-authenticating. The competence to examine them belongs first to the **diocesan bishop** in whose territory they are alleged to occur, and ultimately to the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (formerly the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith).

- **Private revelation is never part of the deposit of faith.** The Catechism teaches that "no new public revelation is to be expected before the glorious manifestation of our Lord" and that "Christian faith cannot accept 'revelations' that claim to surpass or correct the Revelation of which Christ is the fulfilment" (CCC 66; cf. CCC 67). Private revelations "do not belong … to the deposit of faith"; their credibility is at most a human one, and belief in them is "not … required" (CCC 67).
- **Vatican II** states that the economy of Revelation is complete in Christ: "no new public revelation is to be expected" (_Dei Verbum_ 4; cf. _Dei Verbum_ 2).
- **The 1978 CDF norms** — _Norms regarding the manner of proceeding in the discernment of presumed apparitions or revelations_ (published 2012) — set out the criteria a bishop should apply: the truth of the alleged facts, doctrinal orthodoxy, moral and spiritual integrity, the healthiness of the devotion and its spiritual fruits, and any supernatural character.
- **The 2024 DDF Norms** — _Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena_ (17 May 2024) — reorganize the process and define the possible conclusions, which range from _Nihil obstat_ (nothing stands in the way) through intermediate prudential measures to **Prohibetur et obstruatur** (prohibited and obstructed) and **Declaratio de non supernaturalitate** (declaration of non-supernaturality).
- **Canon law** assigns bishops the duty of safeguarding the integrity of the faith and morals in their churches (CIC 823 §1), and requires the faithful to follow the teaching of the Church's pastors (CIC 212 §1).

Because "Maria Divine Mercy" is an anonymous figure whose alleged revelations were distributed globally over the internet, **no single diocesan process produced a universal decree**. Instead, the ecclesial response has come through **individual local ordinaries**, whose judgments bind within their own jurisdictions and carry real weight as the assessments of the Church's lawful pastors.

## 2. The Ecclesial Record

### 2.1 Archdiocese of Brisbane, Australia (2013) — "Patently fraudulent"

Bishop John Coleridge examined the messages and rejected them in the strongest terms. His statement was circulated by the Diocese of Portland:

> I have examined some of these messages and found them to be **patently fraudulent and corrosive of true Christian faith** as the Catholic Church teaches it. The alleged recipient of the messages **operates anonymously and refuses to identify and present herself to local Church authority for a theological examination** of the content of her messages.
>
> The messages contain certain theological and historical errors **some of which fall within the category of bogus millenarianism**, and they are more likely to **provoke fear rather than the peace of the Spirit**. They **play on elements of the Catholic faith only to undermine it at its core**. Moreover, they are **outrageous in their claims against Pope Francis**. Therefore, the messages of Maria Divine Mercy are **not to be taken seriously, discussed, or distributed** within the Archdiocese of Brisbane.

### 2.2 Diocese of Portland, Maine, USA (27 August 2013) — Prohibition

Bishop Richard J. Malone, then Apostolic Administrator of Portland and Bishop of Buffalo, issued a formal prohibition to the clergy:

> Since some of the "messages" promote **negative attitudes towards the teachings of the Second Vatican Council and of the Papacy**, I have made further inquiry as to the "authenticity" of these alleged revelations. … Given the fact that the Church has not yet pronounced on the authenticity of these "revelations", and given the fact that **more than one bishop has judged them to undermine the Catholic faith at its core**, I hereby **prohibit the dissemination or promotion** of Maria Divine Mercy materials in the Diocese of Portland. … **This ban will be in effect, until such time as the "revelations" are authenticated by the Church.**

No such authentication has ever been given.

### 2.3 Archdiocese of Dublin, Ireland (16 April 2014) — "No ecclesiastical approval"

Because the alleged recipient "may live in the Archdiocese of Dublin," Archbishop Diarmuid Martin issued the following public statement:

> Requests for clarification have been coming to the Archdiocese of Dublin concerning the authenticity of alleged visions and messages received by a person who calls herself "Maria Divine Mercy" and who may live in the Archdiocese of Dublin. **Archbishop Diarmuid Martin wishes to state that these messages and alleged visions have no ecclesiastical approval and many of the texts are in contradiction with Catholic theology.** These messages **should not be promoted or made use of within Catholic Church associations**.

This is the single most authoritative published statement about the corpus from the territory where the recipient is believed to reside, and it does two things: it records the absence of approval, and it identifies **doctrinal contradiction** as present in many of the texts.

### 2.4 Catholic Bishops' Conference of the Philippines (2019) — Conference-wide warning

In 2019 the CBCP and several of its dioceses moved against groups promoting the messages, in response to complaints raised by faithful devoted to the authentic Divine Mercy devotion.

- **CBCP**, letter of 29 June 2019 signed by Archbishop Romulo Valles of Davao, CBCP President, warned the faithful to be "very careful" with the "Maria Divine Mercy" group, stating:

> They bring **confusion** and propagate **erroneous, false and distorted teachings** to our Catholic faithful.

- **Diocese of San Carlos**, circular of 15 April 2019 by Bishop Gerardo Alminaza:

> The message of Maria Divine Mercy is **not to be taken seriously, discussed or distributed** within the Diocese of San Carlos. … And **adherence to such teachings would lead on to confusion and eventually being led astray** to what our Magisterium upholds.

- **Diocese of Baguio**, circular of 20 September 2019 by Bishop Victor Bendico, withdrew an earlier endorsement of a lay group after discovering its ties to "Maria Divine Mercy," finding the group's teachings "**erroneous, false and distorted**" and "contrary to official Church doctrines."

## 3. The Doctrinal Problems

The bishops' statements identify several distinct problems. These are not matters of taste or emphasis but of **doctrine**.

### 3.1 Millenarianism (the central error)

The corpus teaches that after the Second Coming there will be a **literal 1,000-year earthly reign of Christ** before the end of the world, and reads "Peter the Roman" as the historical St. Peter ruling first from heaven and then on earth. This is **millenarianism** (also called chiliasm): the expectation that the messianic kingdom will be realized _within history_, as a terrestrial reign before the Last Judgment.

The Church has condemned this reading:

> The Antichrist's deception already begins to take shape in the world every time the claim is made to realize within history that messianic hope which can only be realized beyond history through the eschatological judgement. **The Church has rejected even modified forms of this falsification of the kingdom to come under the name of millenarianism.** (CCC 676; cf. CCC 675–677)

This is why Bishop Coleridge described the messages as containing "bogus millenarianism." Because the corpus builds its entire program on a literal earthly millennium, the error is not incidental — it is structural. The Church's authentic eschatology is set out in [eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md](../eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md).

### 3.2 Claims against the papacy and the risk of schism

The corpus asserts that Pope Benedict XVI was the "last true Pope," ousted by a Vatican plot, and that his successor is a "false prophet" presiding over "the shell of the Catholic Church on earth."

Two problems follow:

1. **It contradicts the Pope's own testimony.** Benedict XVI stated that he renounced the ministry "with full freedom" and for reasons of age and strength. To claim otherwise is to accuse him of falsehood.
2. **It invites schism.** A Catholic who accepts the claim that a legitimately elected Pope is a false prophet and that the visible Church is a "shell" has, in effect, withdrawn submission from the Supreme Pontiff. Canon 751 defines schism as "the refusal of submission to the Supreme Pontiff or of communion with the members of the Church subject to him," and Canon 1364 §1 attaches _latae sententiae_ excommunication to schism. This is a far more serious matter than reading an unapproved book: **it is a canonical crime that severs one from the sacraments until repentance and reconciliation.**

### 3.3 Negative attitude toward the Second Vatican Council

Bishop Malone's prohibition cited the messages' "negative attitudes towards the teachings of the Second Vatican Council." An alleged revelation that sets the faithful against an ecumenical council's teaching fails the criterion of doctrinal orthodoxy.

### 3.4 Anonymity and refusal of ecclesiastical examination

Bishop Coleridge noted that the recipient "operates anonymously and refuses to identify and present herself to local Church authority for a theological examination." Authentic Catholic visionaries have ordinarily submitted to the local bishop; the absence of such submission removes the means by which the Church could ever authenticate the messages. (It also means the Church could not investigate the person even had it wished to.)

### 3.5 Confusion with authentic devotions

The corpus borrows the vocabulary and imagery of legitimate Catholic devotions — Divine Mercy, the Eucharist, the "Seal of God," consecration to Mary — and reassembles them into its own devotional system. The CBCP warning arose precisely because **devotees of the authentic Divine Mercy devotion** complained that the movement was sowing confusion among the faithful. The Church's own devotions are documented in [liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md](../liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md) and [liturgy/divine-mercy-feast-and-novena.md](../liturgy/divine-mercy-feast-and-novena.md).

### 3.6 Unfulfilled predictions

As set out in [the-book-of-truth.md](the-book-of-truth.md#7-unfulfilled-predictions), the corpus attached specific, checkable predictions to itself — the coerced removal of Benedict XVI; a global "Warning" tied to his departure; a Great Tribulation ending around 2016; the immediate installation of an antipope and a "New World Religion." These did not come to pass as described. Scripture gives the test: "when a prophet speaks in the name of the LORD, if the word does not come to pass … that is a word which the LORD has not spoken" (Deuteronomy 18:21–22).

### 3.7 The claim to be an angelic "seventh messenger"

The recipient claims to be "the 7th Messenger, the 7th Angel sent to reveal to the world the contents of the Seals." Setting aside the absence of any such figure in Scripture, the claim conflates a human being with an angel — a category error in Catholic angelology (see [angelology/angelic-hierarchy-choirs.md](../angelology/angelic-hierarchy-choirs.md)) and a hallmark of the genre of self-authorizing end-times prophecy.

## 4. What the Church Has _Not_ Said

Clarity cuts both ways. It should be stated precisely what has and has not happened:

- **There is no universal decree** of the Holy See (the CDF/DDF) specifically naming _The Book of Truth_ and declaring it non-supernatural. What exists is the convergent action of **local ordinaries** and one **bishops' conference**.
- **It has not been declared an approved revelation** anywhere, and no bishop has attested to its supernatural character.
- **The judgments that do exist bind within their territories** and are, at minimum, the considered pastoral assessment of the Church's lawful pastors — which the faithful are bound to respect.
- **Silence is not approval.** The mere fact that the messages have not been the subject of a Roman decree does not license their use; it simply reflects the diffuse, anonymous, internet-driven nature of the corpus.

## 5. Practical Consequences for a Catholic

- **Do not promote or circulate** the materials, and do not use them in any Church association, group, or parish setting (per the Archdiocese of Dublin).
- **In the dioceses listed above, dissemination is forbidden**; respect that prohibition.
- **Do not treat the messages as prophecy**, and do not allow them to shape your expectations of the end times. The Church's doctrine on the Last Things is in [eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md](../eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md).
- **Guard against the spiritual harm of fear.** Bishop Coleridge warned that the messages "provoke fear rather than the peace of the Spirit." The peace of Christ, the sacraments, and the Church's authentic eschatological hope are the remedy.
- **If the messages have led you toward rejecting the Pope or the Church's teaching**, speak with a priest. Absolution from schism, should it have been incurred, is available through the Sacrament of Penance; see [sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md](../sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md).
