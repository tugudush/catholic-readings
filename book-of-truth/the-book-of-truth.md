# The Book of Truth — An Overview of the Corpus

> ⚠️ **Not approved by the Church.** This document describes the _content and claims_ of the corpus promoted as _The Book of Truth_ for the purpose of study. It reports those claims as claims. It does **not** propose them for belief. For the Church's assessment, see [church-assessment-and-warnings.md](church-assessment-and-warnings.md); for how to evaluate such claims, see [catholic-discernment-guide.md](catholic-discernment-guide.md).

---

## 1. What the Corpus Claims to Be

_The Book of Truth_ is a multi-volume collection of alleged private revelations, presented as messages dictated by God the Father, Jesus Christ, the Holy Spirit, and the Virgin Mary to an anonymous woman who writes under the pseudonym **"Maria Divine Mercy."** Their stated purpose is to prepare the world for the Second Coming of Christ.

Three features define how the corpus presents itself:

1. **It claims to be an "unsealing" of Revelation.** The recipient claims to have been told by Jesus that she is the **"seventh messenger"** — the seventh angel sent to reveal the contents of the seals of the Book of Revelation (cf. Revelation 5–8) — and that she is "the end time prophet." The messages were to be published, it is claimed, as each seal is opened.
2. **It claims urgency and worldwide scope.** The texts address "My dearly beloved daughter" and "My children," warn of imminent tribulation, and direct readers to form prayer groups and recite specific prayers.
3. **It claims authority over the reader's response.** Messages are said to instruct that the content be recorded and disseminated "without alteration."

> **Catholic note:** The claim to be an angelic "seventh messenger" and "end time prophet" is one of the most serious difficulties with the corpus. Authentic Catholic visionaries have ordinarily been willing to submit their identity and their writings to the local bishop for examination; the Catechism and the Church's norms give the diocesan bishop the competence to judge alleged revelations in his territory (see [catholic-discernment-guide.md](catholic-discernment-guide.md)).

## 2. The Alleged Locutionist

The recipient has never publicly identified herself. She describes herself on the former official website as "a Roman Catholic married mother of a young family living in Europe," and says she remains anonymous at the command of Jesus "to protect her family and to avoid any distraction from the messages." In a promotional video interview she describes herself as a former businesswoman and speaks with an accent identified as Irish.

Press and Church sources have generally reported the woman to be Irish, and some have named her as **Mary Carberry**, a married mother living in Ireland. The Archdiocese of Dublin referred in its 2014 statement to "a person who calls herself 'Maria Divine Mercy' and who may live in the Archdiocese of Dublin." **The Church itself has never confirmed the person's identity**, and this repository treats the name as a widely reported but officially unconfirmed attribution.

## 4. The Corpus: Volumes, Messages, and Prayers

The printed collection is commonly marketed as a **five-volume set** under the title _The Book of Truth_, though volume boundaries and edition details vary between publishers and print runs, so exact figures should be treated as the promoters' own.

| Component         | Reported detail                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Messages          | Approximately 1,250 to 1,330, received 8 November 2010 – 4 March 2015                                                                                  |
| Volumes           | Commonly five printed volumes, organized chronologically by year and month (some editions publish messages and prayers together; others separate them) |
| "Crusade Prayers" | Roughly 170 short prayers attributed to Jesus and Mary, arranged for use in "Crusade Prayer" groups                                                    |
| Litanies          | A small number (about six) of litanies included with the prayers                                                                                       |
| Special editions  | e.g., a shorter compilation of messages said to be directed to priests and sacred servants; prayer cards and audio recordings in multiple languages    |

Promoters describe the messages as having been received in daily dictation sessions and transcribed before posting. The corpus was also circulated as free PDFs and through social-media groups (notably the Facebook page "Jesus to Mankind") and video interviews.

## 5. Principal Claims and Themes

The following are the recurring claims of the corpus, reported as claims. Where the corpus is quoted, the wording is as quoted in the _National Catholic Register_ analysis of the published messages.

### 5.1 The "Warning" (Illumination of Conscience)

Central to the corpus is a predicted global event called **"the Warning"** — an Illumination of Conscience in which God makes His presence known and reveals to each person his or her own sins. The messages allegedly state that "billions of people will convert during The Warning," and that this event would occur "after My Holy Vicar [Benedict XVI] has left Rome."

> **Catholic note:** The _idea_ of a coming "illumination of conscience" is not unique to this corpus; it appears in various alleged revelations and in popular Catholic literature (see [eschatology/alar-end-times-interview.md](../eschatology/alar-end-times-interview.md)). But the specific attached date claims did not come to pass as stated, and the claim derives no authority from the Church. For a full treatment of the Warning in this corpus and across other private revelations, see [the-warning.md](the-warning.md).

### 5.2 Pope Benedict XVI and the Papacy

The messages describe Benedict XVI as "the last Pope on earth … chosen by Me to lead My Church during the last days," and predicted that he would be "ousted from the Holy See in Rome" through a plot launched "within the corridors of the Vatican," and later that men "will now try to kill him … [and] will say that he was guilty of a crime of which he is totally innocent."

After Benedict's free resignation in February 2013 (which the corpus later claimed to have "predicted"), the messages asserted that the next elected pope would be a **"false prophet"** who would "take over the Seat in Rome," announce a plan to merge world religions, "condone sin," and lead "the shell of the Catholic Church on earth."

> **Catholic note — a grave matter.** Pope Benedict XVI himself declared that he renounced the ministry "with full freedom" and for reasons of age and strength. To claim he was coerced is to contradict the Pope's own testimony. More seriously, to treat a legitimately elected Roman Pontiff as a "false prophet" and the Church's visible hierarchy as "the shell of the Catholic Church" is to set the reader on the road to **schism**, which the Code of Canon Law defines as "the refusal of submission to the Supreme Pontiff or of communion with the members of the Church subject to him" (CIC 751), a delict carrying _latae sententiae_ excommunication (CIC 1364 §1).

### 5.3 The Antichrist and the "New World Religion"

The corpus predicts the appearance of the Antichrist as a "peace negotiator," an ally of the false prophet, under whom a "New World Religion" and a satanic one-world government and currency would be established, and a "Great Tribulation" period would unfold. One message allegedly placed the final three-and-a-half years of the Tribulation as beginning in December 2012 (which would have ended it around May 2016).

### 5.4 A Literal 1,000-Year Earthly Reign (Millenarianism)

The messages teach a **literal** thousand-year earthly reign of Christ following the Second Coming: "Know that the 1,000 years referred to in the Book of Revelation means just that," and the Second Coming "will mean the END TIMES when Satan and his followers … will be BANISHED FROM EARTH FOR 1,000 YEARS," distinct from the end of the world.

They further claim that **"Peter the Roman"** refers not to a future pope but to the historical St. Peter, who "will rule My Church from the Heavens … until … the Second Coming, when he will begin to rule on earth."

> **Catholic note — the central doctrinal error.** This is a form of **millenarianism** (chiliasm): the expectation of a worldly, earthly messianic kingdom before the final judgment. The Church has explicitly rejected it. The Catechism teaches that the Church "has rejected even modified forms of this falsification of the kingdom to come under the name of millenarianism" (CCC 676; cf. CCC 675–677). This error alone is sufficient to disqualify the corpus as authentic Catholic private revelation. The Church's actual eschatology — the Second Coming, the resurrection, the Last Judgment, and the new heavens and new earth, without any interim earthly millennium — is set out in [eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md](../eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md).

### 5.5 The "Seal of the Living God" and Devotional Program

The corpus prescribes a devotional program: the "Crusade Prayers," the "Seal of the Living God" (prayer cards printed in many languages), prayer groups, and particular devotions centered on the Eucharist and Divine Mercy. These practices borrow the _language_ of approved Catholic devotions while redirecting allegiance toward the corpus and its claims.

## 6. Voice and Style

The messages are written in the first person in an authoritative, urgent, and repetitive style, addressing the reader as "My dearly beloved daughter" or "My children." They blend scriptural allusions (especially to Daniel, Revelation, and the Gospels) with contemporary references, and intersperse Latin phrases and prayer formulas. The style is deliberate and emotionally direct, aiming at immediacy and personal appeal. Critics have noted that the content mixes material from other visionaries with **premillennial** ideas of Protestant origin.

---

## 7. Unfulfilled Predictions

A decisive test of alleged prophecy is whether its predictions come to pass (cf. Deuteronomy 18:21–22). The corpus attached specific, checkable claims to itself, and several did not occur:

- **The "ousting" of Benedict XVI.** The messages predicted the Pope would be forced out by a Vatican plot. He in fact freely resigned for reasons of age and health, and said so explicitly.
- **The Warning after Benedict "leaves Rome."** The predicted global Illumination of Conscience, tied to Benedict's departure, did not occur as described.
- **The Great Tribulation's conclusion.** A tribulation period finishing around 2016 did not materialize.
- **The "false prophet" and the New World Religion.** The predicted immediate installation of an antipope who would merge world religions did not occur.

By contrast, the corpus's supporters retroactively interpret events (such as the 2013 resignation) as "fulfilled prophecy" — a pattern common to failed prophetic movements and itself a warning sign in the discernment of spirits.
