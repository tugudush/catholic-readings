## Overview: A Catholic Lay Fraternal Order

The **Knights of Columbus** is a Catholic fraternal benefit society of laymen — a "mutual aid" association united around faith, charity, and service — founded in the United States in the late nineteenth century and now present throughout the world with **more than two million members** in over sixteen thousand local councils. It is the largest Catholic fraternal service organization in existence.

The order describes its mission in four words — **Charity, Unity, Fraternity, Patriotism** — and its motto is **"In service to one, in service to all."** Its members are practicing Catholic men who commit themselves to the service of the Church, their families, and their neighbors, and who support one another and the Church's mission through mutual aid and organized works of charity.

The Knights of Columbus is a **lay** organization: it is governed by laymen (the Supreme Knight and the elected officers of the Supreme Council), while remaining under the pastoral authority of the Church, served by chaplains at every level and bound to fidelity to the Magisterium and the Successor of Peter. Its origin lies in a twofold pastoral insight of its founder: to strengthen Catholic families through **mutual financial assistance** in times of illness or death, and to keep Catholic men from joining fraternal societies whose secrecy and indifferentism were incompatible with the Catholic faith. From this modest beginning has grown an order whose charitable activity, defense of life, and support of the Holy See have been repeatedly commended by the Popes.

## Historical Origins

### The Context: Catholic Immigrant Life in Nineteenth-Century America

The Knights of Columbus arose in a specific historical setting: the Catholic immigrant communities of the United States in the decades after the great waves of Irish, German, and other European immigration. Catholics in nineteenth-century America faced:

- **Economic vulnerability**: working-class families, often headed by a single breadwinner, could be plunged into destitution by the illness, disability, or death of the father, leaving widows and orphans without support.
- **Social exclusion**: Catholics were barred from many of the fraternal and benefit societies of the era, which often required secret oaths, excluded Catholics, or were indifferent to religion.
- **The lure of secret societies**: many Catholic men, seeking insurance protection and fraternal companionship, were drawn to organizations whose secrecy, indifferentism, and oath-bound character the Church had long condemned (see [enemies-of-the-church/freemasons/freemasons.md](../enemies-of-the-church/freemasons/freemasons.md)).
- **Anti-Catholic prejudice**: Catholics faced systematic discrimination in employment, politics, and public life, and were often caricatured as disloyal to the Republic.

The response of the Church to these circumstances was the encouragement of distinctly **Catholic** associations — bodies in which Catholic men could find fraternal support, insurance protection, and a school of virtue without compromising their faith.

### Blessed Michael J. McGivney (1852–1890)

The founder of the Knights of Columbus was **Michael Joseph McGivney**, born on **August 12, 1852**, in **Waterbury, Connecticut**, to Irish immigrant parents, the eldest of thirteen children. According to the biographical record of the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, he was "a leading figure in the Church in the United States in the 19th century."

- **Priestly formation**: He entered the seminary and, upon the death of his father, interrupted his studies to support his family before returning to complete his formation.
- **Ordination**: He was ordained a priest on **December 22, 1877**, and was assigned to serve the large Irish-American Catholic community, first as an assistant (curate) at St. Mary's Church in **New Haven, Connecticut**, and later as pastor in **Thomaston, Connecticut**, not far from his hometown. The Church's record describes him as a man of profound faith and a "Good Samaritan."
- **Pastoral zeal**: His pastoral care of immigrant families — the sick, the bereaved, and the poor — impressed upon him the urgent need for a Catholic association of mutual aid.
- **Death**: He died in **1890**, at the age of thirty-eight, of illness contracted in the exercise of his ministry.

### The Founding of the Order (1881–1882)

Working together with a group of lay parishioners of St. Mary's Parish in New Haven, Father McGivney conceived of a **Catholic fraternal benefit society**: an association that would unite Catholic men in faith and friendship, and provide for the families of members — especially widows and orphans — through a system of mutual insurance and charity.

The first organizational meetings were held in **1881**, and the order was formally **incorporated under the laws of the State of Connecticut on March 29, 1882**. The name **"Knights of Columbus"** was chosen in honor of **Christopher Columbus**, the Catholic navigator whose voyages stood as a symbol of Catholic heritage in the New World and of the compatibility of Catholic faith with American citizenship — a deliberate assertion of Catholic identity against the anti-Catholic prejudice of the age.

The founding purposes, as recorded in the beatification documentation of the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, were:

- To **reinforce the faith** of Catholic men and their families.
- To provide **financial assistance** to Catholic families who had lost their provider — especially the **poor, the widows, and the orphans**.
- To keep Catholic men within the life of the Church and away from condemned secret societies.
- To promote **fraternal union and charity** among Catholics.

From its first council in New Haven, the order grew rapidly: within a few years it spread across New England and the United States, then into Canada, and eventually throughout the world. Blessed Michael McGivney's beatification record notes that the organization he founded, "present throughout the world with over one and a half million members," is committed to "evangelization, charity, the promotion of racial integration and the defense of religious freedom."

## The Four Cardinal Principles

The order's entire program is organized around four principles, each of which is enshrined in its ritual and its apostolate:

| Principle      | Meaning                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Charity**    | Love of God and neighbor expressed in works of mercy — the first and greatest principle, the soul of the order's service.                                     |
| **Unity**      | Union of heart and mind among members and with the Church, her bishops, and especially the Successor of Peter.                                                |
| **Fraternity** | The bond of brotherhood among members, who support one another spiritually and materially as sons of one Father.                                              |
| **Patriotism** | Love of one's country, expressed by loyal citizenship and by the service of the common good — a principle that grounds the order's distinctive Fourth Degree. |

These principles are not merely slogans; they structure the order's degrees, its ritual, its charitable programs, and its witness in public life. In Catholic terms, the four principles are a lay appropriation of the twofold great commandment — love of God and love of neighbor (Matthew 22:37–40) — lived out in the concrete circumstances of family, parish, and nation.

## Organizational Structure

### Governing Bodies

The Knights of Columbus is governed by a hierarchical structure of elected lay officers, with clergy serving as chaplains:

- **Local Councils**: The basic unit of the order, attached to parishes or communities, each with its own officers, chaplain, and program of activities. Councils are numbered, the original council in New Haven being Council No. 1.
- **Districts and Assemblies**: Councils are grouped into districts under a District Deputy; the Fourth Degree is organized separately into **Assemblies**.
- **State and Provincial Councils**: The order is organized by state councils (in the United States) and provincial councils (in Canada and other nations), each under a State/Provincial Deputy.
- **The Supreme Council**: The order's highest governing body, meeting annually, with its permanent headquarters in **New Haven, Connecticut**, at the order's international headquarters. The Supreme Council elects the **Supreme Knight**, the order's chief executive officer, and the other supreme officers.

### Membership

Membership is open to **practicing Catholic men** aged eighteen and above. Members are expected to:

- Practice the Catholic faith and live in communion with the Church.
- Support their parish and their bishop.
- Participate in the works of charity and fraternity of the order.
- Avail themselves of the order's spiritual program, which encourages daily prayer, the reception of the sacraments, and devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary.

The order also sponsors formation for youth through the **Columbian Squires** (founded 1925), the order's youth leadership program for boys, and supports circles and associations for women in many jurisdictions.

## The Degrees of the Order

Like many fraternal bodies, the Knights of Columbus admits members through a series of **degrees** — ceremonial instructions in the order's principles. The four degrees are:

| Degree            | Name       | Theme                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **First Degree**  | Charity    | The duty of charity toward God and neighbor; the candidate is received into the order.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Second Degree** | Unity      | The unity of members with one another and with the Church; fraternal concord.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Third Degree**  | Fraternity | The bond of brotherhood and the mutual obligations of members to one another and to their families.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Fourth Degree** | Patriotism | The patriotic and chivalric degree, established in 1900, devoted to love of country and the service of the common good. Members of the Fourth Degree constitute the order's **Color Corps**, distinguished by their plumed chapeaux and ceremonial regalia at civic and religious events. |

The degrees are **ceremonial instructions in Catholic moral and civic virtue**, not religious mysteries. They are entirely compatible with the Catholic faith: the order's ceremonies contain nothing contrary to doctrine or morals, involve **no oaths contrary to faith or loyalty to the Church**, and are conducted in an explicitly Catholic and even Marian devotional atmosphere. This stands in deliberate contrast to the oath-bound, religion-indifferent secret societies condemned by the Church (see [Section 12](#the-knights-and-catholic-identity-distinction-from-secret-societies)).

## The Fraternal Benefit System: The Insurance Program

From its foundation, the Knights of Columbus has been a **fraternal benefit society** — that is, an association whose members insure one another. This was the founder's central pastoral insight: that Catholic families should be able to rely on **Catholic** mutual aid rather than on the secular or secret fraternal societies of the age.

- **Original purpose**: To provide a death benefit to the families of deceased members — so that a widow and her children would not be left destitute when the breadwinner died — and to support members in times of sickness and disability.
- **Modern scale**: Today the Knights of Columbus operates one of the largest life insurance programs in the world, with hundreds of billions of dollars of insurance in force, licensed to do business across the United States, Canada, and other jurisdictions. Its financial strength has made it one of the largest and most stable fraternal insurers.
- **Catholic character**: The insurance program remains explicitly Catholic in its governance and ethos — its proceeds support the order's charitable works, and its fraternal structure keeps the program "in service to one, in service to all" rather than in service to profit alone.

The fraternal benefit system is the material foundation of the order's charitable activity: the same "mutual aid" by which members provide for one another's families also funds the order's works of charity for the wider community.

## Charitable Works and Programs

The Knights of Columbus has organized its charitable activity under the program heading **"Faith in Action,"** coordinating the following works among many others:

- **Support for the Church and vocations**: From its founding, the order has supported seminarians and priestly vocations — one of Blessed Michael McGivney's original intentions — as well as Catholic education, parish life, and evangelization.
- **Care for the poor and the hungry**: Programs such as **Coats for Kids** (providing winter coats to children in need), **Food for Families** (food drives), and the **Global Wheelchair Mission** (distributing wheelchairs to the disabled worldwide).
- **Support for the disabled and marginalized**: Long-standing partnership with **Special Olympics**, and the order's historic commitment to "the promotion of racial integration," noted in the beatification record of its founder.
- **Defense of life**: Major investments in the **pro-life cause**, including the funding of **ultrasound machines for pregnancy care centers**, support for crisis pregnancy care, and public advocacy for the dignity of human life "from conception until death," as Pope John Paul II put it.
- **Defense of religious freedom and the faith**: The order's **"Keep Christ in Christmas"** campaign, its support for persecuted Christians worldwide, and its assistance to refugees and displaced Christians — including major relief efforts for Christians of the Middle East and, in recent years, for the people of Ukraine.
- **Support of the Holy See**: Through the **Vicarius Christi Fund**, the order assists the Pope "in a practical way in the fulfillment of his duties" (see [Section 9](#papal-recognition-and-magisterial-endorsement)). In 1998 Pope John Paul II thanked the order for its "generous payment of the mortgage of the Holy See's Permanent Observer Mission to the United Nations Organization."
- **Service to the military**: During the First and Second World Wars, the order operated hundreds of **"Knights of Columbus huts"** — recreation and catechetical centers serving Catholic servicemen — and has long supported **Catholic military chaplains**. This tradition of service to the armed forces continues to the present.
- **Evangelization and catechesis**: The order promotes the New Evangelization through its publications (including the monthly magazine _Columbia_), its **museum** in New Haven, and its support of catechetical and apologetic initiatives.

The scale of this charitable activity is extraordinary for a lay body: the order regularly reports charitable donations and volunteer hours in the hundreds of millions of dollars and tens of millions of hours each year, distributed through thousands of local councils to parishes and communities worldwide.

## The Apostolate of the Laity: Theological Foundations

The Knights of Columbus is best understood within the Church's teaching on the **apostolate of the laity** — the participation of the lay faithful in the saving mission of the Church.

- The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the laity, "by their very vocation, seek the kingdom of God by engaging in temporal affairs and by ordering them according to the plan of God" (CCC 898; cf. _Lumen Gentium_, 31).
- The Second Vatican Council's decree on the apostolate of the laity, [_Apostolicam Actuositatem_](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651118_apostolicam-actuositatem_en.html), teaches that the lay apostolate is a participation in the salvific mission of the Church, exercised "in the Church, in the family, and in the world" (cf. [CCC 897–913](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/897)).
- Pope John Paul II described the Knights of Columbus precisely in these terms — as an example of **lay participation in the Church's mission**, rooted in faith and charity, and praised their efforts by which, "through upright personal conduct and through public action," the Knights help "transform society from within."

The order is thus a concrete embodiment of the lay vocation: Catholic men, in communion with their bishops and the Pope, sanctifying the temporal order — family, work, and citizenship — through organized charity and public witness. Its activities are lay activities in the strict sense: carried out by laymen in the world, ordered to the building up of the Church and the transformation of society according to the Gospel.

## Papal Recognition and Magisterial Endorsement

The Knights of Columbus has enjoyed the approbation and blessing of successive Popes from the beginning of its history to the present day.

### Pope Leo XIII (1878–1903)

The order received the blessing of **Pope Leo XIII**, the great Pope of Catholic social teaching, in the years after its founding. The Knights of Columbus has thus enjoyed papal favor from the late nineteenth century onward.

### Pope St. John Paul II (1978–2005)

Pope St. John Paul II repeatedly welcomed the order's leadership to Rome and addressed its members with praise and apostolic blessing:

- **October 17, 1988**: Addressing the Knights in the Hall of the Consistory, the Pope praised the order as an example of **lay participation in the Church's mission**, commended their support of the Catholic faith and their charitable works, and praised the "spirit of love for, and fidelity to, the Catholic Church" that inspires their activity. He concluded by imparting his **Apostolic Blessing**.
- **March 20, 1993**: To the members on pilgrimage in Rome, John Paul II said:

> "From its foundation, your Order has been committed to the material and spiritual well-being of its members and to the promotion of the Church's mission in communion of mind and heart with the Successor of Peter. I assure you of my gratitude for the many ways in which these noble ideals have been realized in the abundant good works of your Order, particularly in your concern for the needs of your brothers and sisters throughout the world and in your defence of the sacredness of God's gift of life."

- **October 18, 1993** and **November 6, 1995**: The Pope received the order's officers and executive board in audience.
- **October 15, 1998**: To the order's Board of Directors, the Pope praised "the witness of Christian faith, fraternal solidarity and firm commitment to the Church's apostolate which has always been the hallmark of your Order," and thanked the Knights for the **Vicarius Christi Fund** and for paying "the mortgage of the Holy See's Permanent Observer Mission to the United Nations Organization." He encouraged the order to continue "strenuous efforts for the pro-life cause."
- **October 9, 2003**: To the order's Board of Directors, John Paul II again expressed gratitude for the order's support of the Church's mission, especially in solidarity with the Successor of Peter, and imparted his Apostolic Blessing.

### Pope Benedict XVI (2005–2013)

In a homily delivered in **New York on April 19, 2008**, Pope Benedict XVI cited **Father Michael McGivney** as "an exemplary American priest" whose "vision and zeal led to the establishment of the Knights of Columbus," explicitly connecting the founder's vision to the growth and legacy of the order in the American Church.

### Pope Francis (2013– )

- **Beatification of the founder**: It was under Pope Francis that the Church declared **Venerable Michael McGivney** **Blessed** on **October 31, 2020**, formally recognizing the order's founder among the Church's beatified (see [Section 10](#blessed-michael-j-mcgivney-founder-and-patron)).
- **Continued encouragement**: Pope Francis has received the order's leadership on multiple occasions and has encouraged the Knights in their works of charity, defense of life, and support of persecuted Christians.

### A Note on Authority

The papal commendations of the Knights of Columbus are exercises of the Church's **pastoral teaching and encouragement** addressed to a particular lay association — they are not definitions of doctrine, and they bind no one to join the order. They do, however, constitute a sustained magisterial approbation of the order's existence, character, and works, and they articulate the principles (fidelity to the Church, charity, defense of the family and of life, solidarity with the Pope) that the Church expects of Catholic lay associations.

## Blessed Michael J. McGivney: Founder and Patron

### The Cause of Beatification

The cause of Father Michael McGivney was opened by the **Archdiocese of Hartford** in 1996. The diocesan investigation was closed in 2000, and on **March 15, 2008**, **Pope Benedict XVI** recognized his heroic virtues and declared him **Venerable**.

### The Miracle

For the beatification, the Church examined an alleged miraculous healing attributed to Father McGivney's intercession: the case of a newborn whose mother had been diagnosed in early pregnancy (January 2015) with a grave fetal condition — **non-immune fetal hydrops with multiple effusions, in a subject with trisomy 21 (Down syndrome)** — carrying a very high probability of intrauterine death. The parents, joined by family and friends, prayed for the intercession of the Venerable Servant of God; an ultrasound the following month showed the disappearance of the hydrops, and the child was born alive on **May 15, 2015**, at 31 weeks gestation, in Tennessee, United States.

### Beatification

After the medical and theological consultations of the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, and with the approval of the cardinals and bishops of the Dicastery, **Pope Francis** authorized the promulgation of the decree on the miracle, and **Blessed Michael J. McGivney** was **beatified on October 31, 2020** — the first beatification celebrated on United States soil. The liturgical record of the Church established his annual memorial to be observed on **August 13**.

### The Founder's Legacy

Blessed Michael J. McGivney is remembered as a model **diocesan priest** and the father of the lay apostolate of the Knights of Columbus. The Church's record describes the order he founded as committed to "evangelization, charity, the promotion of racial integration and the defense of religious freedom." He is a patron of priests, of Catholic families, and of the lay apostolate, and his feast offers the Church an occasion to give thanks for the flowering of Catholic lay associations in the modern age.

The Knights of Columbus has numbered among its members many notable Catholic men in public life, including:

The order's roll of members reflects its character as a broad association of the Catholic laity — from workingmen to presidents — united in faith and service.

## The Knights and Catholic Identity: Distinction from Secret Societies

The Knights of Columbus must be clearly distinguished from the **secret societies** that the Catholic Church has consistently condemned. The contrast is instructive (see [enemies-of-the-church/freemasons/freemasons.md](../enemies-of-the-church/freemasons/freemasons.md)):

| Catholic Fraternal Order (Knights of Columbus)                                                                                                        | Secret Society (e.g., Freemasonry)                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Openly and explicitly **Catholic** in identity, teaching, and prayer.                                                                                 | Religion-indifferent or deistic in principle, requiring no specifically Christian belief.                                                      |
| Fully **subordinate to the Church**: under the pastoral authority of bishops, served by chaplains, bound to fidelity to the Pope and the Magisterium. | Independent of and indifferent to Church authority, and repeatedly condemned by the Church (Clement XII, _In Eminenti_, 1738, to the present). |
| Degrees that are **moral and catechetical instruction**, free of oaths contrary to faith or loyalty.                                                  | Oath-bound secrecy with rituals that mimic religion and inculcate indifferentism.                                                              |
| Seeks to **keep Catholic men in the Church** and strengthen their faith.                                                                              | Historically drew men **away from** Catholic faith and practice into a religion of indifferentism.                                             |
| Its "secret" is the ordinary confidentiality of a private association; its works are public and its principles are openly taught.                     | Secrecy is of the essence, with concealed rites and obligations.                                                                               |

The Knights of Columbus was, in fact, founded precisely as a **Catholic alternative** to such societies — offering Catholic men the fraternal companionship and insurance protection they sought, without the religious indifferentism and secret oaths the Church condemned. The order's explicit Catholic identity, its chaplains, its Marian devotion, and its unreserved loyalty to the Holy See mark it as a thoroughly Catholic body.

## The Knights of Columbus in the Philippines

Given the order's worldwide reach, it is worth noting its presence in the **Philippines**, a nation whose Catholic life this repository documents extensively. The **first council of the Knights of Columbus established outside North America** was **Manila Council No. 1000**, chartered in the Philippines in **1905**. From this beginning, the order grew throughout the archipelago, and the Knights of Columbus has long been a significant presence in Filipino Catholic life — supporting parishes, vocations, and charitable works, and counting among its members many Filipino Catholic lay leaders.

## Summary

The Knights of Columbus is the world's largest Catholic fraternal service organization: a lay association founded in 1882 by **Blessed Michael J. McGivney** to strengthen Catholic families through mutual aid and to keep Catholic men within the life of the Church. Organized around the four principles of **Charity, Unity, Fraternity, and Patriotism**, it provides fraternal insurance to its members and coordinates vast works of charity — support of the Church and vocations, care of the poor, defense of life and religious freedom, assistance to persecuted Christians, and practical solidarity with the Holy See through the Vicarius Christi Fund.

The order embodies the Church's teaching on the **apostolate of the laity** (CCC 897–913; _Apostolicam Actuositatem_): laymen, in communion with their bishops and the Pope, sanctifying the temporal order through organized charity and public witness. It has received the sustained approbation of the Popes — from the blessing of Leo XIII, through the repeated addresses of Pope St. John Paul II and the praise of Pope Benedict XVI, to Pope Francis, who in 2020 beatified the order's founder.

The Knights of Columbus stands as a model of what Catholic lay associations can be: openly Catholic, wholly loyal to the Church, free of the indifferentism of condemned secret societies, and devoted "in service to one, in service to all."
