# Chief Cornerstone Reformed Baptist Church (Antipolo City)

> **Study note.** This document records a **local congregation of a separated ecclesial community** — a Reformed ("Particular") Baptist church in Antipolo City, Philippines — within the repository's Catholic comparative-study collection. Following the pattern of this directory, it **reports** what the congregation's own public sources state, **compares** its confessional position with Catholic doctrine, and **situates** each difference as a matter of dogma, ecclesiastical discipline, or theological opinion. It is written **for study, not endorsement**, and it is not intended to disparage the congregation or its members — who, having received Trinitarian baptism, are separated brethren truly incorporated into Christ ([_Unitatis Redintegratio_ 22](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_unitatis-redintegratio_en.html); CCC 818, 838–819).

## 1. Identity and Self-Description

The congregation presents itself on its public Facebook page under the description **"A local church holding to the 1689 Second London Baptist Confession"** and the category **"Baptist Church."** Its YouTube channel is likewise described as the "Official youtube channel of Chief Cornerstone Reformed Baptist Church."

The name is drawn from Scripture's designation of Christ as the cornerstone — "the chief cornerstone" (Ephesians 2:20; cf. 1 Peter 2:6–7). The self-designation **"Reformed Baptist"** combines two distinct claims, which the repository treats separately:

- **"Reformed"** denotes a commitment to the Calvinistic doctrines of grace and to confessional subscription — the tradition documented in [protestantism/reformed-churches.md](reformed-churches.md).
- **"Baptist"** denotes believer's baptism by immersion and congregational church government — the tradition documented in [protestantism/baptists.md](baptists.md).

The two terms describe different things rather than one thing, as explained in [protestantism/calvinism-and-baptists.md](calvinism-and-baptists.md): Calvinism is a **soteriology**; Baptist is an **ecclesiology**. A "Reformed Baptist" church sits in the overlap — a Particular Baptist congregation.

## 3. Confessional Identity — The 1689 Second London Baptist Confession

The congregation's defining public claim is subscription to the **Second London Baptist Confession of Faith**, commonly called "the 1689" (from its adoption by over a hundred Particular Baptist churches in London that year; it was first drafted in 1677). It is a Calvinistic Baptist confession — largely a Baptist revision of the Westminster Confession of Faith and the Savoy Declaration — in thirty-two chapters.

Its full text, its descent from Westminster and Savoy, and a chapter-by-chapter **Catholic assessment** are documented in the repository's companion study, [protestantism/1689-london-baptist-confession.md](1689-london-baptist-confession.md). That study shows how the confession retains much Nicene and Chalcedonian orthodoxy — the Trinity, the two natures of Christ, the bodily resurrection, the authority of Scripture — while setting aside, on the Baptist side, the sacramental system, the ordained hierarchy in apostolic succession, and the Petrine office.

Two points deserve emphasis when reading this congregation in particular:

- **A high view of Scripture.** The confession's opening chapter treats Scripture as the inspired and sufficient rule of faith and practice. This is a real point of contact with Catholic reverence for the Word of God — but _sola scriptura_ is the standing point of division from the Catholic rule of faith (Scripture and Sacred Tradition, with the Church's teaching office; CCC 74–83, 95).
- **The confession's polemic against the papacy.** The 1689 confession's chapter on the Church (26.4) identifies the Roman Pontiff with the "antichrist" — a class of claim the Catholic Church rejects, holding instead the divine institution of the Petrine ministry ([Vatican I, _Pastor Aeternus_](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/i-vatican-council/documents/vat-i_const_18700718_pastor-aeternus_en.html); CCC 880–887).

Other recurring features reported by the congregation:

- **A weekday Bible reading and study** "open for all," held on **Friday evenings** (announced for 7:45 p.m.), e.g. a session on **Matthew 12** taught by **Bro. Micko Magallanes**.
- **Sunday, not Saturday, as the weekly day of worship**, numbered "Lord's Day" by year. This is a point of **substantial agreement** between the congregation and Catholic practice: the apostolic observance of the first day of the week as the Lord's Day is common Christian ground (Acts 20:7; Revelation 1:10; [_Dies Domini_](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/1998/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_05071998_dies-domini.html) 1–3, 19).
- **Bible-class and preaching themes** with an apologetic and pastoral tone — for example a class titled "Hidden in Plain Sight: The Subtle Rise of Pseudo-Christian Cults Today" (2025), and expository sermons such as the Matthew 27 passage above.

## 6. Catholic Assessment

Judged by the Church's own standard (_Unitatis Redintegratio_ 3–4; _Dominus Iesus_ 17), the congregation is an **ecclesial community** rather than a particular Church in the full theological sense, because the communities born of the Reformation lack some or all of the elements — above all the ordained priesthood in apostolic succession and the Eucharistic sacrifice — that constitute a particular Church. What the congregation retains and what it lacks may be graded as follows.

| Question                                           | Position of the Reformed Baptist tradition it subscribes to                          | Catholic doctrine                                                                                                                                             | Weight of the difference |
| :------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :----------------------- |
| **Rule of faith**                                  | Scripture alone is the final authority; the confession is an aid, not an authority   | Scripture and Sacred Tradition form one deposit of faith, entrusted to the Church's teaching office (CCC 74–83, 95; Trent, Session IV)                        | **Dogma**                |
| **Canon of Scripture**                             | The 66 books (the deuterocanonical books excluded)                                   | The 73 books, defined at Trent (Session IV); see [scripture/bible-history-canon.md](../scripture/bible-history-canon.md)                                      | **Dogma**                |
| **Justification**                                  | Forensic and by faith alone, in Christ's imputed righteousness                       | Grace infused, faith working through love; Trent, Session VI; CCC 1987–2029                                                                                   | **Dogma**                |
| **Baptism**                                        | An ordinance for professing believers only, by immersion                             | A sacrament, the gateway to life in the Spirit; infant Baptism retained (Trent, Session VII, canon 13; CCC 1251–1252)                                         | **Dogma**                |
| **The Lord's Supper**                              | A memorial and spiritual feeding received by faith; not transubstantiation (2LCF 30) | The true, real, and substantial Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity of Christ; the propitiatory sacrifice of the Mass (Trent, Sessions XIII, XXII; CCC 1373–1381) | **Dogma**                |
| **Ministry and orders**                            | Congregational elders; no apostolic succession in the Catholic sense                 | The threefold ordained ministry in an unbroken apostolic succession (CCC 1554–1571; [sacraments/holy-orders.md](../sacraments/holy-orders.md))                | **Dogma**                |
| **The Papacy**                                     | Rejected; the 1689 confession identifies the Pope as "antichrist" (2LCF 26.4)        | The Petrine ministry is of divine institution (Vatican I, _Pastor Aeternus_; CCC 880–887)                                                                     | **Dogma**                |
| **Purgatory and prayer for the dead**              | Rejected; the soul passes immediately to its final state (2LCF 31)                   | Purgatory defined (Lyons II; Florence; Trent, Session XXV; CCC 1030–1032)                                                                                     | **Dogma**                |
| **Invocation of the saints**                       | Not warranted by Scripture                                                           | The intercession of the saints is part of the communion of the one Body (CCC 956, 2683)                                                                       | **Dogma**                |
| **The Lord's Day**                                 | Sunday worship                                                                       | Sunday worship — the dies Domini, the day of the Resurrection                                                                                                 | **Agreement**            |
| **The Trinity, the Incarnation, the Resurrection** | Affirmed in the confession's Nicene and Chalcedonian content                         | The same faith of the creeds                                                                                                                                  | **Agreement**            |

Two things should be said plainly on either side of this table.

First, the points of **agreement are real and substantial**: the triune God, the Incarnation of the eternal Son, His bodily resurrection, the authority and inspiration of Sacred Scripture, the moral law, the resurrection of the body, and the observance of the Lord's Day. The congregation's own teaching on doctrine and on "pseudo-Christian cults" presumes a concern for fidelity that the Catholic reader can respect.

Second, the points of **difference are not peripheral**: they are precisely the matters on which the Church has defined her faith — the rule of faith, justification, the sacramental economy, and the constitution of the Church. This is why the Church can call such communities true ecclesial communities and her members separated brethren, while still teaching that the fullness of the means of salvation subsists in the Catholic Church (_Unitatis Redintegratio_ 3; _Dominus Iesus_ 16–17).

## 7. A Caution on Names

The initials **"CCRBC"** and the string **"ccrbc1689"** are used by **two unrelated congregations**, and they should not be conflated in citation:

- **Chief Cornerstone Reformed Baptist Church**, Antipolo City, Philippines — the subject of this document; its home is its Facebook page and YouTube channel, both at the handle `ccrbc1689`.
- **Christ Covenant Reformed Baptist Church**, Arlington, Texas, United States — a distinct church (a 2023 merger of Faith Community RBC and Sovereign Joy RBC) whose website is `ccrbc1689.com`.

The two share the "1689" marker because both subscribe to the Second London Baptist Confession. **The website `ccrbc1689.com` belongs to the Texas congregation, not to the Antipolo congregation**; its "About," covenant, constitution, and service-times pages should therefore not be cited for the Antipolo church.
