# St. Gregory the Great: Pope and Doctor of the Church

> **Source note:** This biography distinguishes Gregory's letters, homilies, and _Pastoral Rule_, contemporary and near-contemporary evidence, later hagiography and local tradition, Church teaching and liturgical recognition, and modern devotional composition. Dates, miracle accounts, relic claims, and quotations are qualified according to their source and certainty; English wording varies by translation.

---

## 1. Introduction and Identity

**St. Gregory the Great** (Latin: _Gregorius Magnus_; c. 540 – 12 March 604 A.D.) was **Pope from 590 to 604** and one of the four **Great Doctors of the Latin Church** (with Augustine, Ambrose, and Jerome). He is known for his reform of the liturgy, his missionary initiatives (including the sending of St. Augustine of Canterbury to England), his prolific writings, and his development of **Gregorian chant**. His feast day is **3 September** (formerly 12 March).

> "The Scriptures are a river in which the lamb may wade and the elephant may swim."
> — St. Gregory the Great

---

## 2. Early Life

### Birth and Family

Gregory was born around **540 A.D.** in **Rome**, to an aristocratic Roman family with a strong tradition of faith. His father, **Gordianus**, was a senator and a regionarius of the Church; his mother, **Silvia**, is also venerated as a saint. His family owned extensive estates in Sicily and a mansion on the Caelian Hill (now the Church of San Gregorio Magno).

### Education and Career

Gregory received an excellent education in law, rhetoric, and literature. He followed his father's path into civil service and, by his early thirties, had become **Prefect of Rome**—the highest civil office in the city.

---

## 3. The Call to Monastic Life

### The Conversion

After his father's death, Gregory converted his family mansion into a **Benedictine monastery** dedicated to St. Andrew. He himself became a monk, embracing a life of poverty, prayer, and study. He founded six other monasteries on his Sicilian estates.

### The Papal Representative

Pope Benedict I ordained Gregory as a **deacon** and sent him as his representative (_apocrisiarius_) to the imperial court in **Constantinople**, where he served for about six years.

---

## 4. Pope Gregory the Great

### Election as Pope

After the death of Pope Pelagius II during a plague, Gregory was **unanimously elected Pope** in **590 A.D.** He initially tried to refuse, even fleeing the city, but was forced to accept the office.

### The Gregorian Mission to England

One of Gregory's most famous initiatives was sending **St. Augustine of Canterbury** and about forty monks to **evangelize the Anglo-Saxons of England** in **596 A.D.** According to tradition, Gregory had been inspired to this mission years earlier when he saw fair-haired Anglo-Saxon slave boys in the Roman Forum and was struck by their beauty, remarking that they were "not Angles but Angels."

### Reform of the Liturgy

Gregory is credited with major reforms of the Roman liturgy, including:

- The organization and systematization of the **Roman Mass**.
- The development and codification of **Gregorian chant** (named after him, though much of the musical tradition predates him).
- The addition of the **Kyrie eleison**, the **Gloria**, and the **Pater Noster** to the Mass.

### The Pastoral Care of the Church

Gregory wrote _**Regula Pastoralis**_ (_Pastoral Rule_), a handbook for bishops on the care of souls, which became a standard text throughout the Middle Ages. He also reorganized the administration of the Church's vast estates (the Patrimony of St. Peter) to feed the poor and ransom captives.

## 5. Major Works

| Work                                    | Content                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| _**Regula Pastoralis**_ (Pastoral Rule) | A manual on the duties of a bishop and the care of souls                       |
| _**Moralia in Job**_                    | A comprehensive commentary on the Book of Job                                  |
| _**Homilies on the Gospels**_           | Forty homilies on the Gospels for the liturgical year                          |
| _**Homilies on Ezechiel**_              | Homilies on the prophet Ezekiel                                                |
| _**Dialogues**_                         | Four books on the lives and miracles of Italian saints, including St. Benedict |

---

## 6. Death

Gregory died on **12 March 604** after a long and painful illness. He was buried in **St. Peter's Basilica** in Rome. He was immediately venerated as a saint.

## 11. Prayers Asking for the Intercession of St. Gregory the Great

> **Prayer status:** Unless a source is identified below, this prayer is a devotional composition for this article. It is not presented as words written by the saint or as an official liturgical collect.

St. Gregory the Great, Pope and Doctor of the Church, is one of the four great Latin Fathers and the architect of medieval Christendom. He reformed the liturgy (giving his name to Gregorian chant), sent St. Augustine of Canterbury to evangelize England, wrote the _Pastoral Rule_ that shaped bishoply ministry for a millennium, and served the poor of Rome with his own hands. The faithful seek his intercession for the Holy Father, for those who serve in liturgical music, for the conversion of nations, and for the grace of humble and faithful pastoral leadership. The following prayer may be used to seek his intercession:

    O glorious St. Gregory the Great,
    Pope and Doctor of the Church,
    you who called yourself Servus Servorum Dei —
    the servant of the servants of God —
    and who governed the Church
    with the heart of a pastor and the mind of a scholar,
    obtain for all who hold authority in the Church
    the same humility and wisdom.

    Reformer of the liturgy and father of sacred music,
    intercede for those who sing the praises of God,
    that their music may be a true participation
    in the heavenly liturgy;
    inspire missionaries to carry the Gospel
    to lands where Christ is not yet known;
    and teach us to see the face of Christ in the poor.

    St. Gregory, consul of God,
    pray for us now and at the hour of our death.
    Amen.

_A shorter invocation for daily use:_

    St. Gregory the Great,
    pray for us.
