# The Examination of Conscience

A complete guide to the prayerful self-examination by which the faithful prepare for the **Sacrament of Penance and Reconciliation** (Confession). This document presents the theology of conscience, the classical method of examination, the distinction of mortal and venial sin, and a full set of examination questions structured by the **Ten Commandments**, the **Precepts of the Church**, the **seven capital sins**, the **works of mercy**, and the **duties of one's state in life** — together with the Eastern Catholic practices of the evening examination and the Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian.

For the step-by-step ritual of the sacrament itself, see [order-of-confession.md](order-of-confession.md). For the doctrinal and theological foundations of Penance, see [penance-reconciliation.md](penance-reconciliation.md). For a personal confession script, see [private-confessions/confession.md](../private-confessions/confession.md).

> **A note on levels of doctrinal weight.** This guide spans several levels of authority, which the reader should keep distinct:
>
> - **Divine law (binding):** the obligation to confess every mortal sin of which one is conscious, by kind and number, after a diligent examination of conscience (Council of Trent, Session XIV, c. 5; [CCC 1456](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1456)).
> - **Ecclesiastical prescription:** the Catechism directs that the faithful prepare for the sacrament "with an examination of conscience" ([CCC 1454](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1454)); the concrete form and frequency of that preparation are pastoral discipline, not dogma.
> - **Devotional and pastoral aids:** the question sets below are tools handed on by the tradition of confession manuals, the Spiritual Exercises of St. Ignatius of Loyola, and the Eastern practice of _nepsis_ (watchfulness). They are helps to honesty, not binding formulas; the Holy Spirit may lead a penitent to examine differently.

## I. What the Examination of Conscience Is

### 1. Definition

The examination of conscience (Latin: _examen conscientiae_) is a **prayerful review of one's thoughts, words, deeds, and omissions in the light of God's law**, undertaken in order to recognize sin, to repent of it, and to confess it. The Catechism of the Catholic Church prescribes it as the immediate preparation for the sacrament:

> "Before celebrating the Sacrament of Penance, an individual should prepare himself with an examination of conscience, which involves a prayerful self-reflection on one's words and deeds in the light of the Gospel and involves a certain moral discernment about one's conformity to the will of God." ([CCC 1454](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1454))

The examination is therefore not an exercise in morbid introspection or self-accusation. It is a movement of the soul **into the light of God** — the same light in which the Psalmist prays and in which every sin, once confessed, is forgiven: _"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness"_ (1 John 1:9).

### 2. The Inner Light: Conscience

The examination of conscience presupposes the dignity of conscience itself. "Deep within his conscience man discovers a law which he has not laid upon himself but which he must obey... His conscience is man's most secret core, and his sanctuary. There he is alone with God, whose voice echoes in his depths" ([CCC 1776](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1776), quoting the Second Vatican Council, _Gaudium et Spes_ 16).

Conscience is not infallible; it can be "ignorant or make erroneous judgments" ([CCC 1793](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1793)). Precisely for this reason the Church directs the faithful to the formation of conscience — through Sacred Scripture, the teaching of the Magisterium, the gifts of the Holy Spirit, and the advice of others ([CCC 1783–1785](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1783)). A well-formed conscience is the necessary instrument of a truthful examination: the penitent examines his life **against God's objective law** (summarized in the Decalogue), not against his own shifting standards or the opinion of the age.

### 3. Biblical Foundations

The summons to examine oneself runs through the whole of Sacred Scripture (Catholic canon of 73 books; quotations below follow the RSV–Catholic Edition wording, with the Vulgate/Psalter numbering noted where it differs — see [scripture/bible-history-canon.md](../scripture/bible-history-canon.md)):

- **Lamentations 3:40** — _"Let us test and examine our ways, and return to the LORD!"_
- **Psalm 139:23–24** (Vulgate, Psalm 138) — the classic prayer of self-examination: _"Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts! And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting!"_
- **1 Corinthians 11:28** — _"Let a man examine himself, and so eat of the bread and drink of the cup"_ — self-examination as the disposition for Holy Communion.
- **2 Corinthians 13:5** — _"Examine yourselves, to see whether you are holding to your faith. Test yourselves."_
- **1 John 1:8–9** — _"If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves... If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness."_

### 4. Historical Development

The examination of conscience developed through distinct stages in the tradition:

| Era                                | Witness                                                                    | Contribution                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Old Testament**                  | Lamentations 3:40; Psalm 139                                               | Prayerful self-testing "in the light" of God, not of self                                                                                                             |
| **New Testament**                  | 1 Corinthians 11:28; 2 Corinthians 13:5; 1 John 1:9                        | Self-examination before the Eucharist; confession of sin as condition of cleansing                                                                                    |
| **Apostolic era**                  | The _Didache_ and the _Epistle of Barnabas_ — "two ways" of life and death | Catechetical review of conduct commandment by commandment (see [church-history/apostolic-fathers.md](../church-history/apostolic-fathers.md))                         |
| **Desert monasticism (4th c.)**    | Evagrius Ponticus; St. John Cassian, _Conferences_ and _Institutes_        | _Nepsis_ (watchfulness) and the classification of the eight evil thoughts                                                                                             |
| **St. Gregory the Great (6th c.)** | _Moralia in Job_                                                           | Consolidation of the seven **capital vices** "which engender other sins" ([CCC 1866](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1866)) |
| **St. Ignatius of Loyola (1548)**  | _Spiritual Exercises_, First Week                                          | The daily five-point examen; the particular examen (see [Section III](#iii-the-method-the-five-movements-of-the-examination))                                         |
| **St. Francis de Sales (1609)**    | _Introduction to the Devout Life_, Part II, ch. XI                         | The morning and evening examination adapted for the laity in the world                                                                                                |
| **Roman Catechism (1566)**         | Catechism of the Council of Trent                                          | Examination of penitents by the Ten Commandments as the standard method of confession manuals                                                                         |
| **Eastern tradition (continual)**  | _Philokalia_ (watchfulness); _Christ Our Pascha_ (2002)                    | The evening examination "in the light of God's commandments" (see [Section X](#x-latin-and-eastern-catholic-practice))                                                |

The Catechism of Trent, prepared for confessors after the Council of Trent, fixed the **examination by the Ten Commandments** as the standard structure of confession preparation in the Latin Church — the structure retained by nearly every printed examination of conscience since.

## II. Before You Begin: Dispositions, Place, and Prayer

The examination is itself a prayer, not a bookkeeping exercise. Before reviewing your life:

1. **Choose a quiet time and place.** Traditionally the evening (before sleep) or a period of silence in a church before going to confession. Ten to twenty minutes is sufficient for a regular confession; a first confession after many years may require an hour or more.
2. **Begin with gratitude, not accusation.** Recall God's blessings first. An examination made without gratitude collapses into discouragement; one made with gratitude becomes an encounter with mercy.
3. **Invoke the Holy Spirit**, who alone can search the depths of the heart (1 Corinthians 2:10–11), and ask for the light to see yourself as God sees you.
4. **Resolve to be honest.** The purpose is truth, not flattery of self and not despair. St. Francis de Sales counsels that the examination be made with simplicity and gentleness of heart, since self-knowledge is ordered to humility and confidence in God's mercy, never to servile fear (_Introduction to the Devout Life_, Part II, ch. XI).

**A suggested prayer before the examination:**

    Come, Holy Spirit, Spirit of Truth,
    search my heart and enlighten my conscience.
    Give me light to see my sins as You see them,
    honesty to conceal nothing,
    sorrow to repent of all of them,
    and trust in the mercy of Christ,
    who came not to call the righteous but sinners.
    Mary, Mother of Mercy, and my guardian angel,
    be with me in this hour of self-knowledge. Amen.

**The Church's own prayer of examination (Psalm 139:23–24):**

    Search me, O God, and know my heart!
    Try me and know my thoughts!
    And see if there be any wicked way in me,
    and lead me in the way everlasting!

_(Douay-Rheims: "Prove me, O God, and know my heart: examine me, and know my paths. And see if there be in me the way of iniquity: and lead me into the eternal way." — Vulgate Psalm 138.)_

## III. The Method: The Five Movements of the Examination

St. Ignatius of Loyola fixed the classical shape of the daily examination in the _Spiritual Exercises_ ([First Week](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/7912717c-f041-48e0-bf8a-51b5f72220c1/ref/First%20Week)). The same five movements structure the deeper examination made before confession:

| #   | Movement                 | Action                                                                                                                                             |
| --- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Thanksgiving**         | Thank God for the benefits received — the day given, grace received, sacraments received, providences noticed.                                     |
| 2   | **Petition for light**   | Ask the Holy Spirit for the grace to know your sins and to cast them out; without grace, self-examination produces either blindness or despair.    |
| 3   | **Review**               | Look over the period since your last good confession — hour by hour, or by the structures below: thoughts, then words, then deeds, then omissions. |
| 4   | **Contrition**           | Ask pardon for what you have found; stir sorrow for having offended God (see [Section XI](#xi-from-examination-to-contrition)).                    |
| 5   | **Purpose of amendment** | With God's grace, resolve to amend; identify the **near occasion of sin** to avoid.                                                                |

**The daily and the pre-confession examination are distinct but related:**

- The **daily examination** (morning resolve and evening review, per St. Francis de Sales) covers one day and takes five to ten minutes.
- The **particular examen** (St. Ignatius) tracks one specific fault or virtue — examined after dinner and again after supper — and is a powerful instrument against a single dominant vice.
- The **examination before confession** covers the whole period since the last good confession and is the subject of this guide.

**Practical rules for the pre-confession examination:**

- **Begin from your last good confession.** Everything after that moment is on the table — including the confession of venial sins and the failures in the penance previously assigned.
- **Examine by structure, not by mood.** Work through the Ten Commandments (Section V), the Precepts of the Church (Section VI), the capital sins (Section VII), the works of mercy (Section VIII), and the duties of your state in life (Section IX). Structure prevents both omission and obsession.
- **Write down what you find** — many penitents do, and the Church's pastoral practice permits bringing a written list into the confessional. **Destroy the list afterward.**
- **Note the kind and, for mortal sins, the number** (or approximate frequency) of each grave sin. Trent requires confession of mortal sins "by kind and as far as possible by number" (Session XIV, c. 5).
- **Include omissions.** Sins of omission — failed duties, neglected prayer, withheld mercy — are real sins (James 4:17).
- **Do not examine other people.** The examination is of your own heart; judging others is itself matter for the examination (see the Eighth Commandment).
- **Do not rush contrition.** An examination that ends in a merely factual list has missed its purpose; it must end in sorrow and resolve (Section XI).

## IV. Distinguishing Mortal from Venial Sin

The examination must distinguish what **must** be confessed from what **may** be confessed. This is not legalism but the truth of grace: mortal sin destroys charity in the heart; venial sin wounds but does not destroy it ([CCC 1855](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1855)).

### 1. Mortal Sin

A sin is **mortal** when three conditions are present together ([CCC 1857](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1857)):

1. **Grave matter** — specified by the Ten Commandments ([CCC 1858](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1858));
2. **Full knowledge** of the sinful character of the act ([CCC 1859](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1859));
3. **Deliberate consent** — a choice sufficiently deliberate to be a personal decision ([CCC 1859](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1859)).

Grave matter is "specified by the Ten Commandments," and its gravity varies: "murder is graver than theft," and the identity of the person wronged matters ([CCC 1858](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1858)).

### 2. Venial Sin

Venial sin is sin "in a less serious matter, or... which is committed without full knowledge or deliberate consent" ([CCC 1862](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1862)). It "does not set us in direct opposition to the will and friendship of God," but it weakens charity, merits temporal punishment, and disposes to graver sin ([CCC 1863](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1863)).

### 3. What Must Be Confessed

- **Every mortal sin** of which the penitent is conscious after a diligent examination must be recounted in confession, "even if they are most secret," by kind and number ([Council of Trent, Session XIV, c. 5](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/); [CCC 1456](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1456)). Deliberately withholding a known mortal sin makes the confession invalid and sacrilegious.
- **A mortal sin forgotten after diligent examination is nonetheless forgiven** by sacramental absolution; if it is later recalled, it must be confessed at the next confession (Code of Canon Law, c. 988 §2; [CCC 1456](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1456)).
- **Venial sins are not required** to be confessed, but their confession is "strongly recommended by the Church" — it "helps us to confess our sins with humility and grace" and forms the conscience ([CCC 1458](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1458)).

### 4. Two Diseases of Conscience: Scrupulosity and Laxity

_Pastoral guidance (level of spiritual counsel, not dogma):_ conscience can err by excess or by defect ([CCC 1790](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1790)).

- **The scrupulous** conscience inflates uncertainty into guilt, confesses endlessly, and cannot rest in absolution. The remedy: confess what one is **morally certain** is sinful; do not confess mere temptations or feelings (temptation is not sin; consent is); receive absolution in trust; and obey the confessor's judgment, who acts in place of Christ.
- **The lax** conscience shrinks grave matter to convenience ("everyone does it," "it's only mortal if..."). The remedy: an honest reading of the Commandments below, taken at full strength; frequent confession; and openness to the Magisterium rather than the culture in forming judgment.

| Question                                             | Mortal sin                                                                                                                                | Venial sin                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Was the matter grave** (against a Commandment)?    | Yes                                                                                                                                       | No — or grave matter without full knowledge or full consent                                                                               |
| **Did I know** what I was doing was seriously wrong? | Yes                                                                                                                                       | Partly, or not                                                                                                                            |
| **Did I consent** freely and deliberately?           | Yes                                                                                                                                       | Imperfectly, under fear, habit, or passion                                                                                                |
| **Effect**                                           | Destroys charity; deprives the soul of grace ([CCC 1861](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1861)) | Weakens charity; impedes the soul's progress ([CCC 1863](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1863)) |
| **Confession**                                       | Obligatory, by kind and number, before Communion                                                                                          | Recommended and salutary, not obligatory ([CCC 1458](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1458))     |

## V. The Examination by the Ten Commandments

The Decalogue is the standard structure of the examination because it is the structure of grave matter itself: "Grave matter is specified by the Ten Commandments" ([CCC 1858](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1858)). Yet the Decalogue must be read through the **twofold command of charity** to which Christ reduced the whole Law: _"You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind... You shall love your neighbor as yourself"_ (Matthew 22:37–39). The first three commandments govern the love of God; the last seven, the love of neighbor. And the Decalogue is "one whole": to transgress one commandment is to offend the Lawgiver (James 2:10–11; [CCC 2069](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2069)).

Beneath each commandment: the doctrinal anchor, then examination questions. Pray each set slowly; pause where a question strikes home.

### The First Commandment — "I am the LORD your God: you shall not have strange gods before me." (Exodus 20:2–3; CCC 2084–2141)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Commands faith, hope, and love of God above all; forbids idolatry, superstition, divination, irreligion, and unbelief ([CCC 2084–2141](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2084)).

**Questions:**

- Do I pray daily — or has prayer died of neglect? Have I deliberately omitted morning or evening prayer for long periods?
- Have I placed a creature above God — money, career, reputation, pleasure, another person, myself?
- Have I despaired of God's mercy (assuming my sin is beyond forgiveness) — or presumed upon it (sinning because "God will forgive")? Both are sins against hope ([CCC 2091–2092](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2091)).
- Have I doubted articles of the faith, or negligently neglected to learn what the Church teaches?
- Have I practiced superstition — attributing power to charms, omens, or rituals apart from God ([CCC 2111](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2111))?
- Have I consulted divination or magic — horoscopes, astrology, tarot, palm reading, mediums, ouija boards, crystals, or any form of the occult — "thereby contradicting the honor, respect, and loving fear that we owe to God alone" ([CCC 2115–2117](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2115))?
- Have I joined, promoted, or lent support to sects, occult societies, or movements opposed to the Church? (See [enemies-of-the-church/README.md](../enemies-of-the-church/README.md).)
- Have I committed sacrilege by treating sacred persons, places, or things (the Eucharist above all) with contempt ([CCC 2120](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2120))?
- Have I been ashamed of my faith before others — concealing it, mocking it, or failing to acknowledge God when He was denied?
- Have I received the sacraments without proper dispositions, or broken vows made to God ([CCC 2102–2103](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2102))?

### The Second Commandment — "You shall not take the name of the LORD your God in vain." (Exodus 20:7; CCC 2142–2167)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Commands reverence for God's name and for all that is holy; forbids blasphemy, false oaths, and perjury ([CCC 2142–2167](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2142)).

**Questions:**

- Have I blasphemed — spoken words of contempt or hatred against God, Jesus Christ, the Blessed Virgin Mary, or the saints ([CCC 2148](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2148))?
- Have I used God's name carelessly, as an expletive, in cursing, or in jokes that profane it?
- Have I sworn falsely, made a promise I did not intend to keep in God's name, or committed perjury ([CCC 2150–2151](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2150))?
- Have I used the name of God to justify my own anger, prejudice, or plans?
- Have I treated the saints' names, holy images, or the Church's rituals with casual contempt?

### The Third Commandment — "Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy." (Exodus 20:8; CCC 2168–2195)

**Doctrinal anchor:** The Eucharistic precept — Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation, and the sanctification of the Lord's Day ([CCC 2168–2195](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2168)).

**Questions:**

- Have I deliberately missed Mass on a Sunday or holy day of obligation without a serious reason (illness, care of the sick, impossibility)? This is grave matter ([CCC 2181](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2181)).
- Have I arrived at Mass habitually and unrepentantly late, left early, or attended in body but withheld my mind deliberately throughout?
- Have I done unnecessary servile work on Sunday, or conducted business so as to prevent others from keeping the day ([CCC 2185](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2185))?
- Do I keep Sunday as a day of rest, joy, family, and charity — or merely as an empty weekend?
- Have I failed to assist elderly or dependent relatives to attend Mass and the sacraments when it was in my power?

### The Fourth Commandment — "Honor your father and your mother." (Exodus 20:12; CCC 2196–2257)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Governs the family and every human community — children and parents, spouses, citizens and civil authority, employers and employees ([CCC 2196–2257](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2196)).

**Questions:**

- As a child: have I been disobedient, disrespectful, or hateful toward my parents? Have I neglected them in their age, illness, or need ([CCC 2218](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2218))?
- As a parent: have I neglected the religious and moral education of my children ([CCC 2221–2229](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2221))? Have I failed in affection, discipline, protection, or good example? Have I harmed my children by anger, violence, or neglect?
- As a spouse: have I failed in fidelity, love, patience, or the duties of my vocation?
- Have I nurtured resentment, refused forgiveness, or cut off family members without cause?
- As a citizen: have I evaded taxes, disobeyed just laws, refused civic duty, or failed to vote with a formed conscience when I could ([CCC 2238–2241](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2238))?
- As an employer or manager: have I dealt unjustly or harshly with those under me? As an employee: have I given honest work for my wage?
- Have I disobeyed lawful authority out of pride rather than conscience?

### The Fifth Commandment — "You shall not kill." (Exodus 20:13; CCC 2258–2330)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Human life is sacred from conception to natural death; direct and intentional killing of the innocent is gravely contrary to human dignity and the Creator's law ([CCC 2258–2330](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2258)).

**Questions:**

- Have I procured an abortion, paid for one, encouraged one, or cooperated materially in one — by providing money, transportation, information, or consent? (See [abortion-excommunication.md](abortion-excommunication.md) and [private-confessions/abortion.md](../private-confessions/abortion.md).)
- Have I procured, advised, or consented to euthanasia or assisted suicide ([CCC 2276–2279](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2276))?
- Have I attempted suicide or seriously desired my own death, treating my life as my own possession rather than God's gift ([CCC 2280–2283](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2280))?
- Have I given **scandal** — leading others (especially children) into sin by word, example, or permission ([CCC 2284–2287](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2284))?
- Have I nursed anger, hatred, or the desire for revenge against anyone ([CCC 2302–2303](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2302))?
- Have I struck, threatened, abused, or endangered anyone — including reckless driving, violence in sport or temper, or cruelty to the weak?
- Have I abused alcohol, drugs, food, or medicine to the grave harm of my health or the danger of others — intemperance against my own life ([CCC 2290–2291](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2290))?
- Have I procured or consented to unjust sterilization or mutilation ([CCC 2297](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2297))?
- Have I harbored contempt for persons because of race, nationality, disability, or class ([CCC 1935–1937](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1935))?
- Have I neglected my own health gravely, or engaged in self-harm?

### The Sixth Commandment — "You shall not commit adultery." (Exodus 20:14; CCC 2331–2400)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Sexuality is ordered to the conjugal love of husband and wife, open to life; the gravely disordered uses of sexuality are enumerated by the Church with clarity and mercy ([CCC 2331–2400](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2331)).

**Questions:**

- Have I had sexual relations outside marriage — fornication, adultery, or a "free union" or cohabitation ([CCC 2353](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2353); [2380–2390](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2380))?
- Have I masturbated ([CCC 2352](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2352))?
- Have I viewed, produced, or paid for pornography ([CCC 2354](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2354))? Have I frequented prostitutes ([CCC 2355](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2355))?
- Have I used contraception in a deliberate act intended to prevent procreation ([CCC 2370](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2370))?
- Have I procured or participated in in vitro fertilization or other reproductive technologies that separate procreation from the conjugal act ([CCC 2376–2377](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2376))?
- Have I engaged in homosexual acts ([CCC 2357](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2357)) — remembering the Church's insistence that such persons "must be accepted with respect, compassion, and sensitivity" ([CCC 2358](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2358))?
- Have I spoken or dressed immodestly, or solicited others to lust ([CCC 2521–2524](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2521))?
- Have I divorced civilly and attempted remarriage, or entered a union with a divorced person whose sacramental marriage persists ([CCC 2382–2386](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2382))? (See [convalidation.md](convalidation.md) and [communion-civil-marriage.md](communion-civil-marriage.md).)
- Have I flirted with, pursued, or emotionally courted someone not my spouse?

### The Seventh Commandment — "You shall not steal." (Exodus 20:15; CCC 2401–2463)

**Doctrinal anchor:** The universal destination of goods and the right of private property; justice in exchange, wages, and restitution ([CCC 2401–2463](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2401)).

**Questions:**

- Have I taken, kept, or knowingly bought what was not mine — from shops, employers, schools, family, or the state ([CCC 2408](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2408))?
- Have I kept lost property without trying to return it, borrowed without returning, or damaged others' goods ([CCC 2409](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2409))?
- Have I cheated — on exams, contracts, taxes, games, measurements, or declarations ([CCC 2409](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2409))?
- Have I withheld wages due, payment of debts, or support owed to family ([CCC 2434](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2434))?
- Have I worked dishonestly — idleness on the job, theft of time, plagiarism?
- Have I gambled in a way that deprived my family of necessities or enslaved me ([CCC 2413](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2413))?
- Have I wasted goods extravagantly while neglecting the poor, or gravely abused the environment against its God-given purpose ([CCC 2415–2418](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2415))?
- **Do I owe restitution?** Injustice committed must be repaired — return of stolen goods or compensation ([CCC 2459–2460](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2459)). Ask the priest how and when; restitution can be arranged after absolution, but the resolve to make it belongs to a good confession.

### The Eighth Commandment — "You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor." (Exodus 20:16; CCC 2464–2513)

**Doctrinal anchor:** The truth bears witness to God; every lie, detraction, calumny, and rash judgment wounds the neighbor's honor and the communion of persons ([CCC 2464–2513](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2464)).

**Questions:**

- Have I lied — and how gravely? The gravity of a lie is measured against the truth it deforms and the harm it does ([CCC 2484](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2484)).
- Have I committed **detraction** — revealing another's faults without objectively valid reason — or **calumny** — harming another by false statements? Both are distinct sins against reputation ([CCC 2477](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2477)).
- Have I made **rash judgments** — assuming evil of another without sufficient foundation ([CCC 2478](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2478))?
- Have I gossiped, spread rumors, forwarded unverified claims, or posted uncharitable or false content online — modern forms of false witness governed by the Church's teaching on social communications ([CCC 2493–2499](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2493))?
- Have I boasted, flattered to my own advantage, or mocked and ridiculed others ([CCC 2481](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2481))?
- Have I betrayed professional or legitimate secrets, or broken promises of confidence ([CCC 2492](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2492))?
- **Do I owe repair of a reputation?** One who has damaged another's name must repair the harm ([CCC 2487](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2487)).

### The Ninth Commandment — "You shall not covet your neighbor's wife." (Exodus 20:17; CCC 2514–2533)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Purity of heart — the battle against concupiscence in thought and desire, the interior counterpart of the Sixth Commandment ([CCC 2514–2533](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2514)).

**Questions:**

- Have I deliberately entertained lustful thoughts or desires about someone ([CCC 2528](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2528))? (Temptation that is resisted is not sin; what is consented to and delighted in is.)
- Have I sought out occasions of impurity — immodest media, websites, conversations, glances — knowing I would fall?
- Have I cultivated fantasy relationships or emotional attachments that feed lust?
- Do I pray for purity of heart, which "beats with unsatisfied love" and will know fulfillment in the Beatific Vision ([CCC 2519–2520](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2519))?

### The Tenth Commandment — "You shall not covet your neighbor's goods." (Exodus 20:17; CCC 2534–2557)

**Doctrinal anchor:** Detachment from riches and the conquest of envy and avarice in the desires of the heart — the interior counterpart of the Seventh Commandment ([CCC 2534–2557](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2534)).

**Questions:**

- Have I envied another's goods, success, talents, appearance, or relationships, and rejoiced at their loss ([CCC 2538–2540](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2538))?
- Has avarice — the disordered passion for riches and their power — ruled my choices ([CCC 2536](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2536))?
- Have I schemed to acquire what belongs to another, or longed for another's goods so as to wish them deprived of them?
- Am I discontented and ungrateful for what God has given me?
- Do I trust in wealth rather than providence — "Blessed are the poor in spirit" ([CCC 2546](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2546))?

## VI. The Precepts of the Church (CCC 2041–2043)

Alongside the Decalogue, the examination must include the **positive laws** by which the Church ensures the minimum of prayer, sacramental life, and penance:

1. **"You shall attend Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation"** — Have I fulfilled this precept (cross-reference the Third Commandment)?
2. **"You shall confess your sins at least once a year"** — Have I confessed my grave sins within the past year? ([CCC 2042](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2042))
3. **"You shall humbly receive your Creator in Holy Communion at least during the Easter season"** — Have I received the Eucharist at least at Easter, and always in a state of grace?
4. **"You shall keep holy the holy days of obligation"** ([CCC 2043](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2043))
5. **"You shall observe the prescribed days of fasting and abstinence"** — Have I observed them? (See [liturgy/fasting-abstinence.md](../liturgy/fasting-abstinence.md).)

The precepts "situated in a context of moral and Christian life bound up with the liturgical life" guarantee the very minimum of the spirit of prayer, sacramental life, and moral effort ([CCC 2041](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2041)).

## VII. The Seven Capital Sins (CCC 1866)

"Vices can be classified according to the virtues they oppose, or also be linked to the capital sins which Christian experience has distinguished, following St. John Cassian and St. Gregory the Great. They are called 'capital' because they engender other sins, other vices" ([CCC 1866](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1866)). Examining by the capital sins uncovers the **root patterns** beneath particular sins:

| Capital sin        | Opposing virtue | Examination questions                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------ | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pride**          | Humility        | Do I prefer myself to others? Am I offended by correction? Do I attribute my gifts to myself? Do I refuse to kneel, confess, obey?                                                   |
| **Avarice**        | Generosity      | Is my security in money and possessions? Do I give alms? Does the fear of lacking rule me?                                                                                           |
| **Envy**           | Charity/zeal    | Do I grieve at another's good? Do I secretly rejoice at another's fall? Do I compare constantly?                                                                                     |
| **Wrath**          | Meekness        | Does anger govern me — in speech, driving, work, family? Do I hold grudges? Do I punish others with silence or contempt?                                                             |
| **Lust**           | Chastity        | Do I feed the desire for impurity through eyes, imagination, media? Do I dress, speak, or behave to excite desire in myself or others?                                               |
| **Gluttony**       | Temperance      | Do I eat and drink without moderation? Do I use food or alcohol to escape feeling? ([CCC 2290–2291](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2290)) |
| **Sloth (acedia)** | Diligence/zeal  | Am I spiritually lazy — neglecting prayer, Mass, formation? Do I escape duty, sorrow, or God in distraction and busyness?                                                            |

Sloth deserves particular attention in the modern age: _acedia_ is not mere laziness but a sorrow at the good of God Himself — a weariness with spiritual things that scatters the soul in entertainment, work, or noise. It is often the soil in which every other capital sin grows.

## VIII. Sins of Omission: The Works of Mercy (CCC 2447)

The examination must ask not only "What have I done?" but "What have I failed to do?" — for _"whoever knows what is right to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin"_ (James 4:17). The works of mercy are "charitable actions by which we come to the aid of our neighbor in his spiritual and bodily necessities" ([CCC 2447](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2447)); Christ makes them the criterion of the Last Judgment (Matthew 25:31–46).

**Corporal works of mercy — have I:**

- Fed the hungry and given drink to the thirsty — or passed by?
- Clothed the naked and sheltered the homeless — within my means?
- Visited the sick and the imprisoned — the lonely, the elderly, the grieving?
- Bury the dead — accompanied funerals, prayed for the dead ([CCC 2447](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2447))?
- Given alms from my substance — or withheld while spending on myself?

**Spiritual works of mercy — have I:**

- Instructed the ignorant and counseled the doubtful — when entrusted to me?
- Admonished sinners with charity — or stayed silent to be liked?
- Borne wrongs patiently and forgiven offenses willingly — or nursed wounds?
- Comforted the afflicted and prayed for the living and the dead?

The gravity of omissions varies with capacity and obligation; the question is not to multiply guilt but to awaken the love that "is shown in deeds" (1 John 3:18).

## IX. The Duties of One's State in Life

Every Christian receives a **grace of state** for his vocation, and the examination must weigh one's fidelity to it — this is the ancient "duties of one's state in life" section of every traditional confession manual.

**For the laity — spouses:** fidelity of heart and body; patience and courtesy in daily life; prayer together; openness to life; forgiveness after quarrels.

**For the laity — parents:** teaching the faith by word and example; regular confession and Communion for the household; guarding children from occasions of sin (media, company, corruption); forming their conscience; praying for them daily.

**For the laity — workers and professionals:** honest labor; justice toward colleagues; integrity where no one watches; the sanctification of ordinary work.

**For children and students:** obedience and respect; diligence in study; purity; honesty; the practice of the faith amid peer pressure.

**For priests and religious:** fidelity of prayer (the Divine Office); chastity according to one's state; faithful ministry (confessions, Masses, preaching); simplicity of life; obedience to superiors; care of souls entrusted to them.

**For all:** the **particular examen** (Section III) is the Church's most practical instrument against the one fault that dominates one's state — chosen with a director or confessor, resolved upon in the morning, and examined twice daily (St. Ignatius, _Spiritual Exercises_, First Week).

## X. Latin and Eastern Catholic Practice

The doctrine of the examination of conscience is one; its practice carries the color of each tradition. For the liturgical comparison of the sacrament itself, see [penance-reconciliation.md, §9](penance-reconciliation.md#9-latin-rite-vs-eastern-catholic-tradition).

### 1. The Latin Tradition

- The **confession manuals** formed on the Roman Catechism (1566) fixed the examination by the Ten Commandments, Precepts, capital sins, and duties of state — the structure followed in Section V above.
- The **Ignatian examen** gave the Latin Church its daily five-movement examination (Section III).
- The _Rite of Penance_ (1973) prescribes the preparation of the penitent before the sacrament, of which the examination of conscience is the first act (see [order-of-confession.md](order-of-confession.md), Section I).
- Latin piety adds the **seven penitential psalms** (Psalms 6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143 — Vulgate numbering 6, 31, 37, 50, 101, 129, 142) as a biblical accompaniment to the examination, above all **Psalm 51** (_Miserere_).

### 2. The Eastern Catholic Tradition

The Eastern Churches — Byzantine, Ukrainian, Maronite, Chaldean, and the rest — know the same examination under the discipline of **watchfulness** (Greek: _nēpsis_), the continual guarding of the heart taught in the monastic literature of the East, from which both the eight evil thoughts of Evagrius and the West's capital vices descend.

The Catechism of the Ukrainian Catholic Church, _Christ – Our Pascha_, prescribes the evening examination for all the faithful:

> "In the evening before retiring to sleep, we perform a prayer practice called the examination of conscience. It consists in reviewing the day that has passed in the light of God's commandments. The purpose is to see in the day that has passed the signs of God's care and providence as well the expressions of our sinfulness and weakness. To contemplate the events of the day through God's eyes is an occasion to see them in truth — as they really are." ([Christ – Our Pascha, §696](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/5c0fb401-3c71-494e-bfce-ac8641dafd3b/ref/696))

Distinctive Eastern practices bound to the preparation for Holy Repentance (_Hagia Metanoia_):

- The **Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian** — the great Lenten prayer of the Byzantine tradition, prayed with prostrations (full _metanoias_); it is itself an examination in miniature, asking not for a list of deeds but for the spirits that produce them:

        O Lord and Master of my life,
        take from me the spirit of sloth, despondency, lust for power, and idle talk.
        But give rather the spirit of chastity, humility, patience, and love to Thy servant.
        Yea, O Lord and King, grant me to see my own transgressions
        and not to judge my brother,
        for blessed art Thou unto ages of ages. Amen.

- The **penitential psalms and the pre-communion prayers** — Byzantine evening prayers include Psalm 50 (Psalm 51 in Hebrew numbering, _Have mercy on me, O God_) and the office of preparation before Holy Communion, which contains a confession of daily sins.
- The **Prayer of St. Basil the Great** and the liturgical preparation proper to the rite of Holy Repentance (see the comparison table in [penance-reconciliation.md, §9.2](penance-reconciliation.md#9-latin-rite-vs-eastern-catholic-tradition)).

> **Shared substance.** East and West agree: the examination is made in the light of God's commandments, it is prayer before it is audit, and it ends not in self-knowledge alone but in confession, contrition, and mercy.

## XI. From Examination to Contrition

The examination exists for the sake of what follows it. Having reviewed your life:

1. **Stir up contrition** — sorrow of the soul and detestation for the sin committed, together with a resolution not to sin again. **Imperfect contrition** (attrition), born of the ugliness of sin or fear of punishment, suffices for the sacrament; **perfect contrition**, born of the love of God above all things, is to be sought ([CCC 1451–1453](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1451); see [penance-reconciliation.md, §2.1](penance-reconciliation.md#2-the-matter-and-form-of-the-sacrament)).
2. **Form the purpose of amendment** — a firm resolve to sin no more and to avoid the near occasions of sin. Without it, contrition is doubtful and the confession formal.
3. **Prepare the list** — the mortal sins by kind and number; the venial sins you wish to confess; the questions you wish to ask.
4. **Pray the Act of Contrition** — the traditional and modern formulas are given in [liturgy/foundational-prayers.md](../liturgy/foundational-prayers.md#7-the-act-of-contrition) and in [order-of-confession.md](order-of-confession.md), Step 7.

**A suggested prayer after the examination:**

    O my God, I thank You for the light of this examination.
    What I have found in my soul, I now bring to the tribunal of Your mercy.
    Give me true sorrow for my sins
    and a firm purpose of amendment,
    that nothing I have discovered here may remain unconfessed,
    and nothing confessed may remain unforgiven.
    Mary, Mother of Mercy, and Saint Michael the Archangel, pray for me. Amen.

You are now ready for the confessional. The complete ritual — from "Bless me, Father" to the completion of your penance — is in [order-of-confession.md](order-of-confession.md).

## XII. Frequently Asked Questions

**How long should the examination take?**
For a monthly or weekly confession of venial sins, ten to fifteen minutes. For a first confession after many years, an unhurried hour, possibly spread over several days. The annual confession of grave sins (Precept 2) deserves an equally thorough examination.

**Must I confess venial sins?**
No — but their confession is strongly recommended ([CCC 1458](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1458)). Devout penitents typically confess a few representative venial sins, not an exhaustive catalogue.

**What if I forget a mortal sin during the examination?**
If the examination was diligent, the forgotten mortal sin is forgiven by absolution; confess it at your next confession when it is recalled (Code of Canon Law, c. 988 §2; [CCC 1456](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1456)).

**What if I deliberately hide a mortal sin?**
The confession is invalid and sacrilegious, and absolution does not remit the hidden sin. The sin must be brought to a subsequent confession. Honesty with God, who already knows the sin, is always the lighter path.

**What is a "general confession"?**
A review of one's **whole life** (or a long past period) rather than since the last confession — customary for converts, for those returning after many years, and for those making the Spiritual Exercises. It is ordinarily made with the guidance of a priest or spiritual director; see the counsel for returning Catholics in [confessing-heresy-apostasy.md](confessing-heresy-apostasy.md).

**May I bring a written list into the confessional?**
Yes — this is common and permitted pastoral practice. Destroy the list afterward; the seal of confession protects what the priest hears (Code of Canon Law, c. 983), and your list deserves the same discretion.

**How do I examine if I am scrupulous?**
Confess what you are morally certain of; do not re-confess past sins already absolved; resist the compulsion to confess feelings and temptations as deeds; trust absolution completely; and follow your confessor's instructions exactly, which bind your conscience. See Section IV.4.

**How do I examine if I tend toward laxity?**
Take each Commandment at full strength as the Church defines it, not as the culture interprets it; ask the Holy Spirit daily for the light you did not know you needed; and confess at a fixed rhythm (monthly is a sound custom) so that honesty has a schedule.
