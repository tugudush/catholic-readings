# Fr. Chad Ripperger on the Last Things: An Interview on Eschatology, Angels, and Divine Mercy

> **Source**: Interview with Fr. Chad Ripperger, S.T.D., Ph.D., [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rXEd5JvROz8) (~14:50). Fr. Ripperger is a Catholic priest, exorcist, and theologian of the Society of the Most Sorrowful Mother (the Servants of the Holy Family). He holds a doctorate in theology from the Pontifical University of the Holy Cross (Rome) and a Ph.D. in philosophy from the University of St. Thomas (Houston). He is the author of _Dominion_, _Introduction to the Science of Mental Health_, and numerous works on spiritual warfare and Thomistic psychology. This document synthesizes the theological content of the interview in a structured catechetical format, with supporting citations to Sacred Scripture, St. Thomas Aquinas, and Magisterial sources.

---

## 1. The Final Battle and the Antichrist

Fr. Ripperger describes the eschatological sequence preceding the Second Coming of Christ in stages grounded in the Church's traditional reading of Sacred Scripture (cf. [2 Thessalonians 2:3–10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=3); [Revelation 13](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rev&g=13); [Revelation 20:7–10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rev&g=20&p=7)):

1. **Moral Implosion of Humanity**: The proximate condition for the Antichrist's rise is a catastrophic collapse of human morals — exacerbated by wars, the ruins of wars, and widespread social disorder — to such a degree that humanity is left grasping for a solution.

2. **The Antichrist as False Solution**: Into this vacuum of moral and social collapse, the Antichrist presents himself as _the_ solution to humanity's problems. He gains significant control over the human population through deception and power. However, Fr. Ripperger notes that there remains a portion of humanity over whom the Antichrist does _not_ gain control.

3. **Enoch and Elijah, the Two Witnesses**: At a certain stage after the Antichrist's appearance, the two witnesses — identified by the Church's tradition with the patriarch **Enoch** and the prophet **Elijah**, neither of whom died (cf. [Genesis 5:24](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=gen&g=5&p=24); [2 Kings 2:11](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=4ki&g=2&p=11)) — return to preach and bear witness against the Antichrist. The Antichrist puts them to death (cf. [Revelation 11:7–10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rev&g=11&p=7)).

4. **St. Michael the Archangel Enters the Battle**: After the martyrdom of Enoch and Elijah, St. Michael the Archangel appears and engages the Antichrist in battle. However — and this is a theological nuance Fr. Ripperger emphasizes — St. Michael does **not** put the Antichrist to death. The final victory belongs to Christ alone.

5. **Christ Himself Delivers the Final Blow**: The Antichrist is definitively destroyed by **Jesus Christ at His Coming** (the Parousia). As St. Paul teaches: "whom the Lord Jesus shall kill with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming" ([2 Thessalonians 2:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=8)). St. Michael's role is to announce or prepare, but the eschatological victory is Christ's alone.

> **Theological Note**: This sequence is consistent with the _Catechism of the Catholic Church_ on the Church's ultimate trial: "The Church will enter the glory of the kingdom only through this final Passover, when she will follow her Lord in his death and Resurrection. The kingdom will be fulfilled, then, not by a historic triumph of the Church through a progressive ascendancy, but only by God's victory over the final unleashing of evil" ([CCC 677](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/677)).

## 2. The Levels of Heaven and Hell

### 2.1 Hell: An Inverted Hierarchy of Depravity

Fr. Ripperger explains that hell is structured according to an **inverted hierarchy**: the lower one descends in hell, the _more_ depraved and evil one was — but that very depravity confers a kind of _dominion_ over others in hell. This is why, he notes, Satanists pursue greater and greater evils: they seek to "raise" their place in hell (which is, in reality, a lowering). The damned are ranked not by honor but by the degree of their rebellion against God.

This understanding flows from the traditional teaching that the punishment of the damned is proportionate to the gravity of their sins (cf. [CCC 1035](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1035); St. Thomas Aquinas, _Summa Theologiae_ I-II, q. 87, a. 4 on the proportion of punishment to fault).

### 2.2 Heaven: Graded by Sanctifying Grace

Heaven, by contrast, is structured according to **sanctifying grace** — the indwelling of God in the soul. Fr. Ripperger draws on St. Paul, who teaches that the indwelling of God is a qualitative transformation of the soul (cf. [1 Corinthians 3:16](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1co&g=3&p=16); [1 Corinthians 6:19](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1co&g=6&p=19); [Galatians 2:20](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=gal&g=2&p=20)). The degree to which God indwells a soul at the moment of death determines the soul's _capacity_ for beatitude. The holier one is, the more the _lumen gloriae_ (the light of glory) elevates the intellect, and the greater one's capacity to see and enjoy God.

This is the traditional Catholic doctrine of the **inequality of heavenly glory**, taught by the Council of Florence (1439): "the souls of the just… see the divine essence… in proportion to the diversity of merits, one more perfectly than another" (_Denzinger_ 1305; cf. [CCC 1024](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1024)). St. Thomas Aquinas treats this extensively in _Summa Theologiae_ I, q. 12, a. 6.

#### The Nine Choirs of Angels and the Mansions of Heaven

Fr. Ripperger indicates that the gradations of heavenly glory correspond in some way to the **nine choirs of angels** (see [angelology/angelic-hierarchy-choirs.md](../angelology/angelic-hierarchy-choirs.md)). The blessed are said to occupy "different parts" or "mansions" in heaven (cf. [John 14:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=14&p=2)), which is not a spatial division but a difference in the _mode and degree_ of enjoying God. Each of the blessed finds particular joy in the virtues they cultivated on earth and the good actions they performed — but all possess the one essential beatitude: the **Beatific Vision**.

#### No Envy in the Graded Glory

A crucial point Fr. Ripperger makes is that there is **no envy** among the blessed despite the inequality of glory. The person who enjoys a lesser degree of beatitude sees the higher glory of another and _rejoices in it_ — for he sees how good God is to have so exalted another soul, and his own charity is perfected to the point that he delights in his neighbor's good as his own (cf. St. Thomas Aquinas, _Summa Theologiae_ I, q. 108, a. 8; suppl., q. 93, a. 1).

All the blessed enjoy **perfect happiness** — each is filled to his own capacity, as a thimble and a barrel may both be full of water, though one contains more than the other. There is no deprivation in the lesser capacity, for it is filled completely by God.

## 3. The Beatific Vision: Seeing God Face to Face

Fr. Ripperger provides a detailed theological explanation of the **Beatific Vision** (the direct, intuitive vision of God enjoyed by the blessed in heaven), drawing on St. Paul's promise: "We see now through a glass in a dark manner; but then face to face. Now I know in part; but then I shall know even as I am known" ([1 Corinthians 13:12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1co&g=13&p=12)).

### 3.1 The Mechanism: The Light of Glory (_Lumen Gloriae_)

Since the human intellect is a finite, created faculty, it cannot by its own natural power see the infinite, uncreated essence of God. For the Beatific Vision to be possible, God must **elevate** the created intellect through a supernatural gift called the **light of glory** (_lumen gloriae_). Fr. Ripperger explains this Thomistic doctrine as follows:

- God **strengthens** the created mind by infusing the _lumen gloriae_, which "peels back" the veil of created reality so that the soul can perceive spiritual realities as they truly are.
- God then **presses His very nature upon the mind**, so that the soul sees God _literally face to face_ — without any medium or intermediary. This is not an image, concept, or representation of God; it is God Himself directly present to the intellect.

This teaching is defined dogma: Pope Benedict XII in the constitution _Benedictus Deus_ (1336) defined that the souls of the just "see the divine essence with an intuitive and even face-to-face vision, without the mediation of any creature" (_Denzinger_ 1000; cf. [CCC 1023](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1023)).

### 3.2 What the Blessed See: God and All Things in God

Once the soul sees God face to face, it sees _everything that God causes_: all the glory of the created order, all of salvation history, the entire tapestry of divine providence. Fr. Ripperger notes that the blessed cannot comprehend the totality of God's works _all at once_, but they can contemplate them successively from different "angles" — and this will occupy the blessed for all eternity.

The holier the soul, the more _lumen gloriae_ it receives, and therefore the more it understands God, the more it perceives His glory and goodness, and the **happier** it is. Happiness in heaven is thus proportionate to holiness on earth.

### 3.3 Christ's Unique Possession of the Beatific Vision on Earth

A key Christological point Fr. Ripperger makes is that **Christ possessed the Beatific Vision from the moment of His conception**. This is the constant teaching of the Church's theological tradition and is one of the reasons we know He was not a "wayfarer" (_viator_) — He was not on the way to beatitude; He already possessed it in His human nature from the first instant of the Incarnation. This explains the Gospel accounts where Christ "knew their thoughts" ([Matthew 9:4](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=9&p=4); [Luke 6:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=6&p=8)): His human mind saw in His divine nature what God was causing in the hearts of men.

This doctrine is treated by St. Thomas Aquinas in _Summa Theologiae_ III, q. 9–12, and is reaffirmed in [CCC 473](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/473).

## 4. Interaction with the Deceased: The Communion of Saints in Practice

### 4.1 Do the Blessed Interact with Us?

Fr. Ripperger affirms that there is **real interaction** between the blessed in heaven and the faithful on earth, consistent with the dogma of the **Communion of Saints**. He offers personal testimony: on two occasions when he could not solve a mechanical problem, he asked his deceased father (Daniel) for help — and both times received instantaneous clarity that resolved the issue.

He further recounts the testimony of a fellow exorcist who, during exorcism sessions, would call upon his holy deceased mother to be present, and the possessed person could **see** the mother's spirit. This accords with the Church's understanding that the blessed in heaven intercede for us and can be sent by God to assist the faithful (cf. [CCC 956](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/956)–[957](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/957)).

### 4.2 Limits and Cautions

Fr. Ripperger adds important qualifications:

- The communication is generally **one-way**: we can communicate with the blessed (through prayer), and they can _do_ certain things for us (illumination, assistance, intercession), but **authentic apparitions** of the blessed to the living are "very, very rare." He states he has never personally witnessed an authentic case, only read of them.

- The blessed in heaven are **not omniscient** — they know what God wills them to know, and their knowledge of earthly affairs is mediated through the Beatific Vision (they see in God what He chooses to reveal to them).

- This is **not necromancy** or conjuring the dead (which is gravely forbidden; cf. [Deuteronomy 18:10–12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=deu&g=18&p=10); [CCC 2116](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2116)). It is the legitimate Catholic practice of _invoking_ the intercession of the saints, who are alive in Christ.

### 4.3 Will We See Our Lineage in Heaven?

When asked whether we will see our direct lineage when we die, Fr. Ripperger responds that we will see **everyone's lineage** — both our ancestors and our descendants — because in the Beatific Vision, we see all things in God, including the entire history of the human family descended from Adam and Eve.

## 5. Did Christ Have a Guardian Angel?

Fr. Ripperger addresses a classic theological question: **Did Christ, in His human nature, have a guardian angel?**

### 5.1 The Thomistic Answer: No

St. Thomas Aquinas treats this question in _Summa Theologiae_ III, q. 33, a. 3, and the answer has been the common teaching of theologians and saints since. The reasoning:

1. **Guardian angels are given to wayfarers** (_viatores_) — souls who are on the journey toward beatitude, in need of protection to reach their final end (cf. [CCC 336](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/336)).
2. **Christ was not a wayfarer**: From the moment of His conception, His human soul possessed the Beatific Vision. He was _already at the destination_ — His human nature was already in perfect union with God. Therefore, He did not require a guardian angel's protection to reach heaven.
3. The entire tradition of the Church, including the Fathers, spoke of Christ's earthly life as the state of a _viator_ only in a qualified sense — He truly suffered, hungered, and was tempted, but He simultaneously enjoyed the beatific knowledge of God in the summit of His soul.

### 5.2 All Angels Served Christ

That Christ did not have a _single_ guardian angel does not mean angels were absent from His life. On the contrary, **all the angels of heaven served Christ**:

- Angels ministered to Him after His fasting in the desert ([Matthew 4:11](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=4&p=11); [Mark 1:13](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mrk&g=1&p=13)).
- An angel strengthened Him in His agony in Gethsemane ([Luke 22:43](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=22&p=43)).
- When the Jews attempted to seize Him prematurely, angels could "block" them so that Christ passed through their midst unharmed ([Luke 4:30](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=4&p=30); [John 8:59](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=8&p=59)).
- Christ Himself told Nathanael (identified by some with Bartholomew) and the Apostles: "Amen, amen I say to you, you shall see the heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man" ([John 1:51](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=1&p=51)) — an allusion to Jacob's ladder ([Genesis 28:12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=gen&g=28&p=12)), signifying that Christ is the true bridge between heaven and earth, and all angelic ministry is ordered to Him.

As Fr. Ripperger summarizes: "He didn't have a guardian angel, but they all served him."

## 6. Divine Mercy: Definition and Theology

The final question of the interview is the most fundamental: **What is Divine Mercy?** Fr. Ripperger provides a precise Thomistic definition.

### 6.1 Definition: Loosening the Bonds of Justice

Fr. Ripperger defines mercy as: **"Loosening the bonds of justice for the good of the individual."**

- **Justice** would require that a person suffer a **proportionate evil** in response to the evil they committed — the principle of _lex talionis_ (the law of retaliation: "an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth"; cf. [Exodus 21:24](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=exo&g=21&p=24); [Leviticus 24:20](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=lev&g=24&p=20)). The punishment must fit the crime.
- **Mercy** intervenes when the full application of justice would not be _good for the person_ — that is, when suffering the full punishment of sin would brutalize rather than heal.

God therefore "loosens the bonds of justice" — He does not apply the full punishment due to sin — because it is better for the sinner's spiritual well-being. This is, as St. Augustine teaches, the **greatest manifestation of God's goodness**: that He does _not_ punish as justice alone would demand (cf. St. Augustine, _Enarrationes in Psalmos_, on Psalm 144).

### 6.2 Two Reasons for Mercy

Fr. Ripperger identifies **two reasons** God grants mercy, drawn from both the Old and New Testaments:

1. **Mercy for the Stupid** (_misericordia propter miseriam_): When a person sins out of weakness, ignorance, or foolishness — not out of cold, calculating malice — God "cuts them some slack." He does not always shield them from the consequences of their stupidity (sometimes He lets them "suffer their stupidities so they clue in"), but He does not exact the full measure of punishment.

2. **Mercy for the Contrite** (_misericordia propter contritionem_): When a person is **sorry** for their sin — when they sorrow over the evil they have done and turn back to God — this sorrow is itself an _ordering_ of the soul. At the moment of sin, the sinner was focused on the disordered good. In contrition, the sinner focuses rightly on the evil of the sin and the offense against God. When God sees that **right order** restored in the soul, He responds with mercy: He loosens the bonds of justice precisely _because_ the soul has been set right again.

This is why the Psalmist can proclaim: "His mercy endureth forever" ([Psalm 135](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=135)). Even in heaven, the blessed — all of whom (except the faithful angels) are recipients of mercy — live under God's merciful gaze for all eternity.

### 6.3 Mercy and Justice: Not Opposed

Fr. Ripperger emphasizes that God's mercy is not opposed to His justice — in a sense, God's mercy _is_ His justice, because God has bound Himself by His own word:

- If you meet the conditions (contrition, confession, satisfaction), He **will** loosen the bonds of justice — this is His merciful justice.
- If you do _not_ meet the conditions, He will _not_ — and this too is just, for God is true to His word.

The correlation is exact: God's mercy is promised to the contrite, and God's fidelity to His promise is an act of divine justice.

### 6.4 Mercy from the Perspective of Eternity

A final profound point: since God is **outside of time** and sees the totality of time in one eternal _now_, He sees simultaneously the sin you committed in the past _and_ your present contrition. For God, for all eternity, He beholds your offense — yet He also beholds your sorrow, your turning back to Him, and your placing yourself under His mercy. This is why the Cross of Christ has an **infinite** and **eternal** effect: Christ's sacrifice extends across all time, covering the sins of every age.

As the Psalmist says, "His mercy endureth forever" ([Psalm 135](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=135)). **The only ones who never enjoyed God's mercy are the faithful angels** — because they never committed any evil to be forgiven. The rest of us — all of redeemed humanity — live under His mercy for all eternity.

## 8. About Fr. Chad Ripperger

**Fr. Chad Alec Ripperger, S.T.D., Ph.D.** (b. 1964) is an American Catholic priest, exorcist, philosopher, and theologian. He is a founding member of the **Society of the Most Sorrowful Mother** (the Servants of the Holy Family), a society of apostolic life dedicated to the Traditional Latin Mass. He holds:

- A **doctorate in sacred theology** (S.T.D.) from the Pontifical University of the Holy Cross, Rome
- A **doctorate in philosophy** (Ph.D.) from the University of St. Thomas, Houston
- A master's degree in philosophy from the Center for Thomistic Studies

Fr. Ripperger has served as a professor of theology at several seminaries, including St. Gregory the Great Seminary (Lincoln, Nebraska) and Our Lady of Guadalupe Seminary (Denton, Nebraska). He is widely known for his work in **spiritual warfare** and **exorcism**, having served as an exorcist for the Archdiocese of Denver and the Diocese of Tulsa, and for his extensive writing and speaking on Thomistic psychology, moral theology, and the spiritual life.

His published works include _Dominion_, _Introduction to the Science of Mental Health_, _The Spiritual Theology of the Priesthood_, _Deliverance Prayers for the Laity_, and numerous articles and lectures on Thomistic philosophy, spiritual warfare, and Catholic moral teaching.
