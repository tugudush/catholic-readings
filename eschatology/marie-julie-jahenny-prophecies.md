# The Prophecies of Marie-Julie Jahenny (1850–1941)

> **Doctrinal status: unapproved private revelation.** The Church has made **no formal declaration** that the alleged revelations of Marie-Julie Jahenny are of supernatural origin. They have not been condemned either. Belief in them is **not required** for salvation and they **cannot add to or correct** the public Revelation of Jesus Christ ([CCC 66–67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)). The Palmarian "canonization" of 1978 is an act of a **schismatic** community and is not a Catholic credential. This study presents her claims for examination, not for belief.

Marie-Julie Jahenny — the "Breton Stigmatist" of La Fraudais — is one of the most frequently cited sources in traditional Catholic end-times literature. Her name appears in treatments of the [Three Days of Darkness](three-days-of-darkness.md), the [Warning](the-warning.md), the [signs in the heavens](signs-in-the-heavens-and-the-cross.md), and the [Purple Scapular](../sacramentals/other-approved-scapulars.md). Much of what circulates under her name, however, is a **composite** of nineteenth-century ecstasy transcripts, mid-twentieth-century compilation, later English translation, and devotional commentary. This document separates, as far as the published sources allow, what she is reported to have said, how those texts reached us, and what a Catholic may and may not conclude from them.

## 2. Who She Was

Marie-Julie Jahenny was born into a poor and pious peasant family, the eldest of five children of Charles Jahenny and Marie Boya. Her mother consecrated her to the Blessed Virgin at birth, and she was baptized the following day, Ash Wednesday. When she was three or four the family moved to a cottage at La Fraudais, a hamlet northeast of Blain, where she would live for the rest of her life. She received only about six months of schooling and, at sixteen, spent six months in domestic service before her frail constitution forced her home. In young adulthood she entered the Third Order of St. Francis and was known in the parish for her modesty, her love of prayer, and her devotion to the Blessed Sacrament.

Her life changed in 1873. On 6 January, during the parish Mass, she was seized with a severe illness; by mid-February the doctor had given up hope and she received Extreme Unction. On 22 February she reported a vision of the Blessed Virgin, clothed in white and standing beside a great white cross, who told her she would be healed on 2 May at three o'clock and asked whether she would accept a share in the sufferings of Christ for the conversion of sinners. Marie-Julie consented. On 15 March the request was repeated and she consented again. On 21 March 1873, before a number of witnesses, the Five Wounds appeared on her hands, feet, and side; the stigmata bled and, from that day, she is said to have relived the Passion every Friday. Further Passion marks followed in stages: the crown of thorns in early October 1873, the wound of the shoulder on 25 November 1873, the scourging marks on 6 December 1873, the marks of the cords on the wrists and a wound over the heart in January 1874, and an espousal ring in blood on the ring finger in February 1874. A cross and flower with the words _O Crux Ave_ are said to have appeared on her chest on 7 December 1874, accompanied by an extraordinary perfume.

She also reported long periods of complete abstinence from food and drink — ninety-four days from 12 April 1874, and then, from 28 December 1875, five years, one month, and twenty-two days — sustained, in the accounts, by the Eucharist alone. Witnesses described miraculous Communions in which a host appeared on her tongue, and a bleeding picture of the Crucifixion given to her by her spiritual director, Father David, in January 1877.

From June 1880, for about four years, she is said to have been deprived of speech, hearing, sight, and the use of her limbs, while still able to hear a priest speaking Latin and to speak during her ecstasies. Her biography records that during this period she could make the Way of the Cross only on Fridays, when the paralysis lifted for a time. These phenomena were investigated by Doctor Imbert-Gourbeyre of the Faculty of Medicine at Clermont-Ferrand, who concluded that the wounds were not natural or self-inflicted and wrote to the Bishop of Nantes that "there was no fraud in La Fraudais."

She died at La Fraudais on 4 March 1941 and was buried in the cemetery at Blain. Her cottage has since become a place of pilgrimage, and the Association of the Friends of Marie-Julie Jahenny, founded in 1959 by the Marquis de La Franquerie, preserves her texts and promotes her memory.

## 4. The Mystical Phenomena and the Ecclesial Record

### 4.1 The Passion phenomena

The reported phenomena are of a kind found in the lives of many recognized mystics: the stigmata, the Friday reliving of the Passion, the crown of thorns, the shoulder wound, the scourging, total fasting, miraculous Communion, and the discernment of blessed and unblessed objects. Doctor Imbert-Gourbeyre's observations — the wounds' failure to infect, the regular Friday remission of the paralysis, the absence of food and drink, the ability to distinguish a consecrated from an unconsecrated host — were presented as evidence against fraud, hysteria, and natural explanation. The details are recorded in the devotional literature and in La Franquerie's biography; they have not been subjected to a modern canonical process.

Two cautions follow. First, extraordinary phenomena are **not** the measure of holiness, and their authenticity is not the same question as the truth of every message. Second, the Church's caution in the 2024 norms applies with full force: phenomena can be genuine, mixed, or explained naturally, and the faithful are not to treat them as more certain than the Gospel.

### 4.2 The investigation under Bishop Fournier

Bishop Félix Fournier of Nantes is reported to have opened an inquiry in the early 1870s and to have entrusted the medical investigation to Doctor Imbert-Gourbeyre. In a letter of 6 June 1875 the bishop wrote that the reports showed "more and more the action of God on this soul," that "she is sincere: what she manifests is supernatural," and that he saw "nothing but good, edifying and in conformity with the principles of spirituality." This letter is the basis of the frequent claim that "her bishop approved her revelations."

The claim needs precision. The letter was a **private letter to a doctor**, not a formal decree; it concerned the stigmata and ecstasies he had investigated, not the whole later corpus of prophecies; and it was not upheld by his successors. It is a serious and favorable testimony, but it is not a universal ecclesiastical approbation, and it does not make her predictions part of the faith.

### 4.3 Persecution and the lifting of the ban

After Bishop Fournier's death in 1877, the climate changed. The chapter of curates is said to have ordered that Marie-Julie be deprived of the sacraments, and the new bishop, Le Coq, upheld the decision. The deprivation lasted about eleven years. In 1888, according to her promoters, Pope Leo XIII ordered an inquiry and the ban was lifted. The episode is important for two reasons. It shows that the local Church was **divided** about her, and it shows that the Holy See's intervention was a **disciplinary** act — restoring her access to the sacraments — not a doctrinal approval of her revelations.

Marie-Julie's own conduct during the persecution is one of the most edifying elements in the record: she is described as never murmuring against the bishop or the Church, but remaining obedient to the successive bishops of Nantes. That obedience is a standing rebuke to those who later invoke her name to justify contempt for the hierarchy.

### 4.4 What the Church has and has not said

- **Has said:** nothing formally. There is no decree declaring her revelations of supernatural origin, and no decree condemning them.
- **Has not said:** that the Three Days of Darkness, the Two Days of Darkness, the Great Monarch, the Angelic Pontiff, or any particular date or remedy is part of the Catholic faith.
- **Has not opened a cause:** as of the sources consulted, no beatification cause has been officially opened. Claims that a cause is "underway" should be verified with the Diocese of Nantes, not accepted from devotional websites.
- **Has not canonized her:** the 1978 Palmarian act is null. The Palmarian Catholic Church is a schismatic community founded at El Palmar de Troya; its "popes" and "canonizations" have no standing in the Catholic Church.

## 5. How the Texts Reached Us

The published corpus is not a single book written by Marie-Julie. It is a collection of transcripts of her ecstasies, taken down by scribes while she spoke, together with later compilations, translations, and commentary. The biographical literature names Monsieur Charbonnier, a former notary of Nantes, and later Monsieur and Madame Cluzeau as scribes; visitors were reportedly forbidden to take notes, so that the authorized transcripts might be preserved intact. In 1939, according to the account, the documents were entrusted to André Le Sage, Marquis de La Franquerie, to keep them from the German occupation. La Franquerie published _The Breton Stigmatist_ in 1977; E. A. Bucchianeri's _We Are Warned_ (2011) is a large English compilation based on La Franquerie and on the French texts published by the Association.

This history imposes limits on any reconstruction:

- **Selection.** Only a portion of the transcripts has been published, and the published texts are not a critical edition.
- **Translation.** The English texts are translations of nineteenth- and early twentieth-century French, often of a visionary and rhetorical character. Translation errors have occurred and have been corrected by the compilers — for example, a passage about the number saved at Paris was first rendered "less than twelve" and later corrected to eighty-eight, and a remedy ("ground ivy") was accidentally omitted from one English edition.
- **Date uncertainty.** Some texts are undated, some dates are confused in the transcriptions, and the material is **cyclical rather than chronological**: the same themes recur, and the reader cannot simply line up the messages as a timeline.
- **Commentary.** The published editions include the compilers' interpretations, which are sometimes presented with more confidence than the texts warrant. The most consequential example is the interpretation of the Great Monarch material (see §6.4).

The quotations in this study are therefore given as **the published texts attribute them**, with dates where the sources give dates. They are reported, not endorsed.

## 6. The Prophetic Message

Marie-Julie's reported messages are dominated by a single pastoral motive, stated repeatedly: "I want My people warned." The warnings are not given for curiosity or date-setting but for repentance, prayer, and preparation. The following sections present the main themes as the texts give them, with the doctrinal evaluation reserved for §8.

### 6.1 The chastisement of France

The most consistent theme is a coming chastisement of France, presented as the consequence of the Revolution, the regicide of Louis XVI and Marie-Antoinette, the rejection of the Catholic monarchy, the spread of Freemasonry, and a general apostasy. The texts speak of:

- **Paris as the "Center"** of the evil and the starting point of the punishment, called in one text a "cursed Sodom."
- **A civil war** and a time of massacre, with churches closed, the clergy persecuted, the Sacré-Cœur at Montmartre turned into a "boardroom," and the shrine of Our Lady of Victories protected.
- **The desecration of the liturgy**, the parodies of the sacraments, and a "new religion" preached with the support of many bishops.
- **The loss of children's souls** through the corruption of innocence, and the sins of impurity, blasphemy, and abortion.
- **Brittany as a place of refuge**, with the faithful urged to leave Paris and, if possible, to go to Brittany.

A representative text, dated 19 August 1881 and attributed to Our Lady, laments that "it is blasphemy that brings hell on earth," that "the clergy is weak," and that "the French clergy is more responsible because it is more educated." Another, dated 12 November 1924, describes the "room of hell" — the Chamber of Deputies — where "all is delivered up, everything is sold."

These are **private claims and political judgments**, not Church teaching. The Church does condemn the principles of Freemasonry ([Leo XIII, _Humanum Genus_, 1884](../enemies-of-the-church/freemasons.md)), and she has always taught that nations are accountable to God. But the identification of a particular political order with Satan, and the detailed prediction of a particular civil war, are not doctrines of the faith. Catholics are free to weigh them prudently and to reject the political program that some later readers have built upon them.

### 6.2 Warnings in nature and plagues

Before the great chastisement, the texts describe a series of **lesser warnings**: torrential rains, strange lightning and thunder, lightning without thunder, spots or bands in the sun, a red and black rainbow, a "red rain" that coagulates on the ground for seven weeks, earthquakes lasting many days, crop failures, and epidemics. One especially prominent warning is a "burning disease" that attacks the heart, the mind, and the tongue, leaving the flesh raw as after a deep burn; the remedy given is an infusion of the leaves of white hawthorn, picked and prepared in a specified way.

The texts also speak of a time when "the wheat is not the third node of growth," of fruit that blackens, of potatoes that rot, and of animals afflicted. The remedies include blessed St. Benedict medals planted in the fields in the form of a cross, blessed candles, holy water, and the medals and scapulars discussed below.

These details have a long afterlife in devotional literature. They are **not** Church teaching, and the medical recommendations in particular must not be treated as a substitute for ordinary medical care. The Church does not ask anyone to rely on a private revelation for the treatment of disease.

### 6.3 The Two Days and the Three Days of Darkness

The Two Days and the Three Days are distinct in the texts. The **Two Days** are a warning: a darkness of two days and two nights, with a purple and red sky, thunder, and a foul rain, intended to convert the wicked before the greater punishment. The **Three Days** are the great chastisement itself.

The most frequently quoted passage, dated 4 January 1884 and attributed to Our Lord, describes the Three Days in these terms:

> "There will be three days of physical darkness. For three days less one night, there will be a continual night. The blessed wax candles will be the only ones that give light in this terrible darkness: only one will suffice for three days, but in the homes of the wicked, they will not give any light. During these three days and two nights, the demons will appear under the most hideous forms. You will hear in the air the most horrible blasphemies. The lightning will enter your homes, but will not extinguish the candles; neither wind, nor the storm can put them out. Red clouds like blood will ride across the sky. The crash of thunder will shake the earth. Sinister lightning will cut across the dense clouds, in a season when they never occur. The earth will be shaken down to the foundations. The sea will rise thundering waves that will spread across the continent. Blood will flow in such abundance that the earth will become a vast cemetery. The corpses of the wicked and the righteous ones will litter the ground. The famine will be great. Everything will be in turmoil and three-quarters of men will perish."

Other details recur: the Three Days will be "a Thursday, Friday, and a Saturday — days of the Most Holy Sacrament, of the Cross, and of Our Lady"; no window or door may be opened; "everything will shake except the piece of furniture on which the blessed candle is burning"; the family should gather around a crucifix and an image of Our Lady; and the candles must be of **pure wax**, since "all those that are not of this paste will not help."

The texts do not give a date. They place the Three Days after the civil war and the desecration of the Church, and before the Great Renewal. The details are **private claims**, not Catholic doctrine. The Church has never defined a three-day darkness, and the closest biblical material — the darkness at the Crucifixion ([Matthew 27:45](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=27&p=45)), the cosmic signs before the Parousia ([Matthew 24:29](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=24&p=29), [Joel 2:10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joel&g=2&p=10), [Isaiah 13:10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=is&g=13&p=10)), and the prophetic "day of the Lord" — does not require it. See the full treatment in [The Three Days of Darkness](three-days-of-darkness.md).

### 6.4 The Great Monarch and the Angelic Pontiff

The second great theme is the promised restoration of France and the Church through a **Great Monarch** and a holy Pope. The monarch is repeatedly named **Henry V**, the "miracle child," the exiled king. The historical referent is Henri Charles Ferdinand Marie Dieudonné d'Artois, Duke of Bordeaux and Count of Chambord (1820–1883), the last direct male heir of the senior Bourbon line, who was born seven months after his father's assassination, was king in law for a week in 1830, spent most of his life in exile, refused the tricolour and the constitutional compromise, and died childless at Frohsdorf in 1883.

The texts describe the king as:

- **chosen from his birth** and "reserved for the great epochs";
- **exiled** and **hidden**, a "King of exile," a "hidden Saviour," a "mortal Saviour";
- the bearer of the **white flag** and the **fleur-de-lis**;
- a ruler who will **return with armies**, be aided by St. Michael, restore the monarchy, consecrate France to the Sacred Heart, and **rescue the Pope**;
- a king whose reign will be followed by a **Great Renewal** of faith and peace.

A representative text, dated 22 March 1881, is addressed to the exiled king:

> "All My veritable prophetic words, O King of exile, call you and acclaim you. Without deferring too much, I will return to your subjects and your people he who bears on his head the same flower that you love. This flower is the lily, O King, miracle child."

Another, dated 5 August 1879, says: "Do not look for another Saviour. Do not rely on men! My Divine Heart alone will save the kingdom." And on 14 February 1882: "From Heaven, you will see the triumph of the Church hovering over the forehead of My true servant Henry of the Cross."

The **Angelic Pontiff** is a holy pope who reigns with the Great Monarch and restores the Church. The texts also contain a darker strand: a **martyr pope** who is "discarded and thrust aside," bishops who revolt against him, a "new religion" established with their support, and fragmentary passages about a vacancy of the Holy See and "two successive antipopes." A text dated 19 March 1878, attributed to St. Joseph, says:

> "The Holy Father will suffer torments that are beyond his powers. He will be discarded and thrust aside, pushed and shoved, like the sea when it braces."

**Two later interpretations must be distinguished from the texts themselves.**

1. **The hidden-bloodline theory.** The Marquis de La Franquerie inferred that the Great Monarch would be a descendant of Louis XVI and Marie-Antoinette preserved in a hidden line. This is an inference, not a statement of the texts; Marie-Julie is reported to have spoken only of the "King and Queen Martyrs," without naming a hidden descent.
2. **The resurrection theory.** E. A. Bucchianeri argues that, since Henry V died in 1883 and the texts continue to speak of his return, the king must be **raised from the dead**. This too is an inference. It is not a formal claim of the alleged revelations, and it raises serious theological and historical questions. Catholics are not obliged to accept either theory, and neither may be preached as faith.

The Great Monarch tradition itself — found in varying forms in Ven. Bartholomew Holzhauser, Bl. Anne Catherine Emmerich, and others — is a **theological opinion or pious belief**, not a defined doctrine. The Church has explicitly rejected **millenarianism**, the expectation of a literal earthly reign of Christ before the final judgment ([CCC 676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/676)), and the 1979 CDF document _Some Aspects of Christian Eschatology_ warns against a "political" reading of the end times ([DH 3839](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/3839)).

### 6.5 The Great Renewal and La Fraudais

After the chastisement, the texts promise a **Great Renewal**: France will be "resurrected," the monarchy restored, the Church purified and enlarged, many conversions will follow, and the Reign of the Sacred Heart will be established. The peace is said to last "25 or 30 years" in one text and "25 to 35 years" in another. The renewal is not the end of history: after it, the Antichrist will come, the final persecution will unfold, and Christ will return in glory.

La Fraudais itself is given a large place in the promises. Our Lord is said to have told Marie-Julie that after her death great graces would be granted there, that a great sanctuary of the Holy Cross would be built, that a miraculous spring would rise, that angels would help the builders, and that the Great Monarch would come to plant his standard there in thanksgiving. These are **private promises about a private place**; they are not part of the faith, and they do not oblige anyone to make a pilgrimage or to support a particular building project.

### 6.6 The Antichrist and the final trial

The corpus is less a systematic Antichrist chronology than a series of warnings about apostasy, persecution, and the final combat. It places the Antichrist **after** the Great Renewal, not before it: the renewal is a time of peace and faith, and the final trial comes at the end. This distinguishes her sequence from the _Book of Truth_ corpus's sequence, in which the Warning, the chastisement, and the millenarian reign are arranged differently. Catholics should remember that the **only** sure eschatological outline is the one given in Scripture and the Catechism: the final trial, the Antichrist, the conversion of Israel, the Second Coming, the resurrection, the Last Judgment, and the new heavens and new earth ([CCC 668, 675–677, 1038–1050](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)). No private revelation may be used to fill in the day or the hour ([Mark 13:32](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mk&g=13&p=32)).

Two further points belong here. First, the **Friday death** prediction: Our Lord is said to have promised on 1 December 1876 that Marie-Julie would die on a Friday, but she died on Tuesday, 4 March 1941. Her promoters have proposed that she died a "hidden" death in the coffin, or that the promise was fulfilled in some unrecorded way. These are speculative attempts to rescue the prediction; the honest conclusion is that the discrepancy is unresolved. Second, the **cyclical and conditional** character of the warnings: many of the texts present the chastisement as conditional on repentance, as at Nineveh ([Jonah 3](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=jonah&g=3&p=1)), and the Church has always taught that God desires the salvation of all ([1 Timothy 2:4](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1tm&g=2&p=4); [2 Peter 3:9](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2pt&g=3&p=9)). A prediction of judgment is a call to conversion, not a fatalistic timetable.

### 7.1 The Purple Scapular

The Purple Scapular is the best known of these devotions. In the reported vision of 23 August 1878, Our Lady is said to have shown Marie-Julie a large violet scapular, a little larger than the palm of a hand, with three nails and three drops of blood, a chalice surrounded by a crown of thorns, and, on the other side, the Virgin holding the dead Christ, with an angel wiping His feet, a ladder, and the reed of the Passion. The straps were to be woollen, with three knots on the left shoulder and two on the right. The promises attached include protection of the family and home from fire, protection from thunder, sudden death, and accidents, and protection during chastisements; the scapular was to be blessed by a priest.

Two things must be said clearly. First, the **object** is a sacramental in the broad sense: a devotional object may be blessed by a priest, and the Church permits the faithful to use blessed objects with faith and piety ([CCC 1667–1679](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)). Second, the **promises** are private claims. The Purple Scapular is **not** a Church-approved scapular in the sense that the Brown Scapular of Our Lady of Mount Carmel is; it has no approved confraternity, no liturgical rite of enrollment, and no ecclesiastical guarantee of the promises. It must never be treated as a charm, and its possession does not replace faith, grace, the sacraments, or ordinary prudence ([CCC 2111, 2117](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)). See [Other Approved Scapulars](../sacramentals/other-approved-scapulars.md) and [The Brown Scapular](../sacramentals/brown-scapular.md).

### 7.2 Two prayers from the corpus

The following short prayers are reproduced as the devotional literature gives them, in the line-by-line form proper to prayer texts. They are private prayers, not liturgical texts.

**The short _Crux Ave_**

> O Crux, ave, spes unica!
> Et Verbum caro factum est.
> O Jesus, Vanquisher of death, save us!

**The longer _Crux Ave_**

> I salute You, I adore You, I embrace You,
> O Adorable Cross of my Saviour!
> Protect us, guard us, save us!
> Jesus loved You so much;
> following His example, I love You.
> Your Holy Image calms my fears;
> I feel only peace and confidence.

**The five-minute thanksgiving after Communion**

> Thank you, my Beloved,
> You live in me and I live in You.

At the fifth minute, the text says, ask for the conversion of a thousand sinners.

### 7.3 A necessary caution about "remedies"

The corpus contains detailed instructions about hawthorn leaves, St. John's wort, violets, blessed medals, candles, and even a small piece of paper with a prayer written on it, to be swallowed. Some of these are harmless devotional practices; others are not. The Church does not require belief in any of them, and several raise serious concerns:

- **Medical care comes first.** Hawthorn and St. John's wort interact with medications; a private revelation is not a prescription. Anyone who is ill should consult a doctor.
- **Do not treat sacramentals as charms.** A blessed object used with faith is a sacramental; a blessed object used as a guarantee against harm, independent of faith and the sacraments, is superstition ([CCC 2111, 2117](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)).
- **Do not swallow paper or other objects** on the strength of a private revelation. This is not a Catholic practice, and it may be harmful.
- **Do not let fear dominate.** "God hath not given us the spirit of fear: but of power, and of love, and of sobriety" ([2 Timothy 1:7](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2tm&g=1&p=7)).

## 8. Catholic Evaluation

### 8.1 What a Catholic may affirm

A Catholic may affirm that God is free to grant private revelations, that he has done so in the lives of the saints, and that the Church has approved some of them as worthy of belief. A Catholic may also recognize in Marie-Julie's reported life the marks of a sincere soul devoted to the Passion, the Eucharist, and the Church, and may be edified by her obedience under persecution and her love of the Cross. Her moral exhortations — penance, reparation, frequent Confession and Communion, the Rosary, the Way of the Cross, love of the Blessed Sacrament, and prayer for the conversion of sinners — are entirely consonant with the Gospel and the Church's teaching.

A Catholic may also affirm the **general truths** her warnings presuppose: that God judges nations as well as individuals; that temporal chastisements are possible; that the Church will undergo a final trial; that Christ will return in glory; and that the world will pass away ([CCC 668, 675–677, 1038–1050](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)).

### 8.2 What is not defined and cannot be required

No Catholic is obliged to believe any of the following:

- the **Two Days** or **Three Days of Darkness** as a future historical event, or any of their physical details;
- that **only pure wax candles** will burn, or that blessed objects will function as described;
- that **Henry V** will return, whether from exile, from a hidden bloodline, or from the dead;
- that an **Angelic Pontiff** will reign with a Great Monarch;
- that **France** will be chastised in a particular way, or that **Brittany** will be a refuge;
- that the **Great Renewal** will last a specified number of years;
- that any particular **pope** is the "martyr pope" or that any particular **bishops** are the ones described;
- that a **"new Mass"** was foretold in the texts, or that the reformed Roman liturgy is invalid or displeasing to God;
- that the **Purple Scapular**, the **Cross of Pardon**, the **medal of Bonne Garde**, or the **lilies** carry the promises attached to them;
- that the **remedies** are medically effective or spiritually necessary.

### 8.3 Problems and risks

**1. The ambiguity of "approved by her bishop."** The 1875 letter of Bishop Fournier is a favorable private testimony, not a formal approbation of the whole corpus. The subsequent persecution and the 1888 lifting of the ban are disciplinary facts, not a doctrinal endorsement. Catholics should not repeat the claim that "the Church approved her revelations" without qualification.

**2. The Palmarian "canonization."** The 1978 act of the Palmarian sect is sometimes presented as proof of her sanctity. It is the opposite: it associates her name with a schismatic community whose orders and canonizations have no standing in the Catholic Church. It is a caution, not a credential.

**3. Political messianism.** Her language about democracy, monarchy, and France is the language of a nineteenth-century French royalist, not the language of the Magisterium. The Church teaches that the concrete form of government is a matter of prudential judgment and that "the diversity of political regimes is morally acceptable, provided they serve the legitimate good of the communities that adopt them" ([CCC 1901](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1901); [Vatican II, _Gaudium et Spes_ 74–76](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033)). A Catholic may prefer a monarchy; a Catholic may not treat a private revelation as proof that democracy is diabolical or that a particular political program is the will of God.

**4. The Great Monarch theories.** The hidden-bloodline theory and the resurrection theory are later inferences. Neither is a formal claim of the texts, and neither is Church teaching. The resurrection theory in particular raises questions about the uniqueness of Christ's resurrection and the manner of the general resurrection ([1 Corinthians 15:20–23](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1co&g=15&p=20)). It should not be preached as faith, and it should not be used to construct genealogical or political schemes.

**5. Failed or unfalsifiable predictions.** The Friday death prediction was not fulfilled as stated. Some predictions are vague, some are retrospective, some depend on a "hidden" or "conditional" reading. A private revelation is not proved by a list of coincidences, and a failed prediction is a warning against overconfidence.

**6. The "new Mass" readings.** Some readers interpret the texts of 21 July 1881 and 27 November 1901/1902 — about a "book of the second celebration," a "Mass that contains words odious in My sight," and "new celebrations" — as predictions of the post-Vatican II Roman Missal. This is a **contested private interpretation**, not a Church judgment. The Church has authority over the liturgy ([Vatican II, _Sacrosanctum Concilium_ 22](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033); [CIC 838](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib4-cann834-839_en.html)). A private revelation cannot be used to declare the Church's lawful liturgy invalid, to justify attending illicit celebrations, or to separate oneself from the Pope. Marie-Julie's own obedience to her bishops is the decisive counterexample.

**7. Sedevacantism and the "two antipopes."** The fragmentary passages about a vacant See and "two successive antipopes" are undated, incomplete, and surrounded by editorial reconstruction. They cannot be used to deny the legitimacy of the popes since Vatican II. The Church's indefectibility and the unbroken succession of Peter are matters of faith ([Vatican I, _Pastor Aeternus_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033); [CCC 882, 869](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)). The promoter of the principal English compilation himself argues against the sedevacantist use of these fragments. See [Traditionalist Societies: SSPX and FSSP](../church-history/traditionalist-societies-sspx-fssp.md).

**8. Children and the "loss of souls."** Some texts speak of children being lost before the age of reason through the corruption of their innocence. This rhetoric must not be read as denying the Church's teaching that God's mercy embraces infants who die without Baptism ([CCC 1261](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1261); International Theological Commission, _The Hope of Salvation for Infants Who Die Without Being Baptised_, 2007). The Church hopes for their salvation; she does not consign them to hell.

**9. Muslims and Jews.** Some passages have been used to foster hostility toward Muslims or Jews. The Church rejects such use. She teaches respect for Muslims ([Vatican II, _Nostra Aetate_ 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033)) and for the Jewish people, whose covenant with God has never been revoked ([Nostra Aetate 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033); [Romans 11:28–29](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rom&g=11&p=28)). A private revelation may not be used to justify antisemitism, anti-Muslim bigotry, or collective guilt.

**10. Superstition and fear.** The apparatus of candles, medals, scapulars, prayers, and remedies can easily become a system of guarantees. The Church warns against superstition and against treating sacramentals as magic ([CCC 2111, 2117](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f)). The proper fruit of a warning is repentance, not anxiety.

## 9. Pastoral Guidance

**Receive with gratitude, not credulity.** Private revelation is a gift to the Church when it is authentic, and it is always subordinate to the Gospel. St. Paul's rule is the rule here: "Test everything; hold fast to what is good; abstain from every form of evil" ([1 Thessalonians 5:21–22](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1th&g=5&p=21)).

**Stay close to the sacraments.** Confession, the Eucharist, and the Anointing of the Sick are the ordinary means of grace. No scapular, candle, medal, or private prayer can replace them. If the texts move you to love the Eucharist and the Passion more, that is their best fruit.

**Pray and do penance.** The warnings are a call to conversion, not a timetable. Pray the Rosary, make the Way of the Cross, offer reparation, perform works of mercy, and intercede for sinners.

**Do not set dates.** "Of that day or hour no one knows, neither the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father" ([Mark 13:32](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mk&g=13&p=32)). Any use of Marie-Julie's texts to fix a year or a day is contrary to the Gospel.

**Do not embrace fear.** "God hath not given us the spirit of fear: but of power, and of love, and of sobriety" ([2 Timothy 1:7](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2tm&g=1&p=7)). A Christian prepares for judgment by holiness, not by panic.

**Remain in communion with the Church.** Marie-Julie obeyed her bishops even when they deprived her of the sacraments. Anyone who uses her name to justify schism, sedevacantism, contempt for the Pope, or attendance at illicit sacraments acts against her example and against the faith.

**Be prudent, not superstitious.** It is reasonable to keep a blessed candle, a crucifix, and holy water in the home; it is not reasonable to treat them as automatic protection. Provide for your family's ordinary needs; do not turn the Gospel into a survivalist program.

**Consult a confessor or spiritual director.** If these texts disturb you, or if you are tempted to make them the center of your spiritual life, speak to a priest. The Church's discernment is a gift, and the ordinary path of holiness is enough.

## 10. Common Questions

**Was Marie-Julie Jahenny a canonized saint?**
No. She has not been beatified or canonized by the Catholic Church. The 1978 "canonization" by the Palmarian sect is schismatic and has no Catholic standing.

**Did the Church approve her revelations?**
No formal approbation of the whole corpus exists. Bishop Fournier of Nantes wrote favorably about the stigmata and ecstasies in 1875, but his letter was private and was not upheld by his successors. The Holy See lifted the ban on the sacraments in 1888; that was a disciplinary act, not a declaration that every message is supernatural.

**Must I believe in the Three Days of Darkness?**
No. It is a private claim. You may believe it with prudence, or set it aside, without sin. The Church has never defined it.

**Is the Purple Scapular a Church-approved scapular?**
It is not approved in the sense that the Brown Scapular is. A priest may bless the object as a devotional, but the Church has not approved the alleged promises. It should never be treated as a charm.

**Will Henry V return from the dead?**
That is a theory proposed by later promoters, not a formal claim of the texts and not Church teaching. You are not obliged to accept it, and it should not be preached as faith.

**Did she predict the Second Vatican Council or the "New Mass"?**
Some readers interpret certain texts that way. It is a contested private interpretation, not a Church judgment. The Church's lawful liturgy is binding, and private revelation cannot be used to declare it invalid or to justify schism.

**What about the "two antipopes" and the vacant See?**
The passages are fragmentary, undated, and reconstructed. They cannot be used to deny the legitimacy of the popes since Vatican II. The Church's indefectibility and the succession of Peter are matters of faith.

**Is it wrong to read her prophecies?**
No. It is good to read them with discernment, as one reads any private revelation: gratefully, critically, and in subordination to Scripture and the Magisterium.

**What is the best fruit of her message?**
Repentance, love of the Eucharist and the Passion, reparation, prayer for sinners, and fidelity to the Church. If reading her makes you holier, more humble, and more obedient, that is a good sign; if it makes you fearful, proud, or contemptuous of the Church, that is a warning sign.
