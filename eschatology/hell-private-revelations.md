# Hell in Private Revelation: Levels, the Damned, and Catholic Discernment

> **Pastoral Note**: This document treats the descriptions of hell found in Catholic **private revelations** — the visions of hell reported by approved seers, canonized mystics, and other visionary writers — with the **doctrinal distinctions** required by the Church. The reality and eternity of hell are **dogma** ([CCC 1033–1037](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1033)). The **internal structure of hell**, any **"levels"** within it, and above all any **identification of specific persons** as damned are **not** part of the deposit of faith. They belong to private revelation, theological opinion, and in some cases pure literature, and must be received with the prudence the Church requires of all private claims. The purpose of every authentic vision of hell is **conversion and hope**, never curiosity, sensationalism, or the condemnation of one's neighbor.

---

## 1. The Dogmatic Foundation: What the Church Definitively Teaches

The Church defines that hell is **real and eternal** — the definitive state of those who die in unrepentant mortal sin, freely and definitively separated from God. The principal punishment is **eternal separation from God** (_poena damni_); Scripture also speaks of a punishment of the senses (_poena sensus_), imaged as "fire" ([Matthew 25:41](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=25&p=41); [Mark 9:43–48](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mrk&g=9&p=43)).

- **Council of Lyon II (1274)**: defined that the souls of those who die in mortal sin **"descend immediately into hell"**.
- **Council of Florence (1439)** and **Trent (Session VI)**: reaffirmed that the impenitent are delivered to eternal death.
- **Fourth Lateran Council (1215)**: all the dead will rise and "receive according to their works, whether good or evil" ([DH 801–802](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/801)).
- **CCC 1033**: hell is "**the state of definitive self-exclusion from communion with God and the blessed**."
- **CCC 1035, 1058**: the Church "**prays that no one may be lost**" and "**does not know** who is in hell; she does not despair of any person's salvation" — a principle that governs everything below.

What the Church does **not** define:

- The **internal geography** of hell — whether there are "levels," how many, or their arrangement.
- The **proportion and mode** of punishment in detail (Trent deliberately left this to theological investigation).
- **Whether any specific human being** is in hell (see [Section 6](#6-the-question-of-specific-persons-in-hell)).

For the full magisterial dossier, see [eschatology/doctrinal-foundations.md](doctrinal-foundations.md).

## 2. The "Levels of Hell" in Tradition, Theology, and Literature

Popular fascination with "levels of hell" draws on **four distinct sources of unequal authority**, and the Catholic must not confuse them:

### 2.1 The Doctrine of Proportionate Punishment (Theological — Well-Grounded)

The Church and her theologians teach that **punishment is proportioned to guilt**: the more grave and numerous one's sins, the more intense one's suffering. This is the theological kernel beneath every "levels" schema.

> "By the merit of his good works, or by the demerit of his sins, each man is rewarded or punished **in proportion to the degree** of his merit or demerit, in soul and body." — St. Thomas Aquinas, _Summa Theologiae_, I-II, q. 87, a. 4

St. Thomas likewise held that the punishment of the damned varies "according to the diversity of sins" (cf. _ST_, Supplement, q. 69, a. 7; [CCC 1035](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1035)). This doctrine of **gradation** — not a precise "nine levels" — is the only element that rests on solid theological ground.

### 2.2 The "Inverted Hierarchy" (Theological Opinion)

Some contemporary theologians and exorcists, including Fr. Chad Ripperger, S.T.D., describe hell as an **inverted hierarchy of depravity**: the deeper one descends, the more depraved and rebellious the soul, and the more "dominion" the damned exercise over others — a rank measured by rebellion against God, not by honor. This is a **theological synthesis**, not dogma. For the full treatment, see [eschatology/ripperger-interview-last-things.md](ripperger-interview-last-things.md).

### 2.3 Dante's _Inferno_: Poetry, NOT Revelation

The most familiar "nine circles of hell" come from **Dante Alighieri's** _Divine Comedy_ (c. 1308–1321) — one of the greatest poems of Western literature, but a **literary and allegorical work**, **not** a private revelation, and **not** Catholic doctrine. Dante himself frames the poem as a fiction of vision. No theologian treats its nine circles (Limbo, Lust, Gluttony, Greed, Anger, Heresy, Violence, Fraud, Treachery) as revealed truth. Catholics may admire Dante's art and moral seriousness, but must not cite _Inferno_ as a source of eschatological fact.

### 2.4 Apocryphal and Apocalyptic "Tours of Hell" (Non-Canonical)

Long before Dante, apocryphal literature — the **Apocalypse of Peter** (2nd century) and the **Apocalypse of Paul** (_Visio Pauli_, 4th century) — contained elaborate "tours of hell," assigning specific punishments to specific sins (blasphemers hung by the tongue, the proud in pits, etc.). These works were **never received into the canon of Scripture**; the Church's canon (see [scripture/bible-history-canon.md](../scripture/bible-history-canon.md)) excludes them. They are valuable only as evidence of early Christian imagination about divine justice, not as revelation.

### 2.5 Medieval Vision Literature (Literary-Pious, Not Binding)

The Middle Ages produced a genre of "vision literature" — the **Vision of Tundale** (_Visio Tnugdali_, 1149), the **Vision of the Monk of Eynsham**, the **Tractatus de Purgatorio Sancti Patricii** ("St. Patrick's Purgatory") — in which a penitent or dying man tours the next world, often naming well-known contemporaries among the damned. These texts had genuine pastoral and literary influence, but they are **pious fiction of uncertain provenance**, not private revelation in the technical sense, and the Church has given them no authority.

> **Summary**: Of the "levels" schemas in circulation, only the **doctrine of proportionate punishment** (2.1) rests on theology; the "inverted hierarchy" (2.2) is an opinion; the **nine circles** (2.3), the **apocryphal tours** (2.4), and the **medieval visions** (2.5) are literature, not revelation.

## 3. Approved and Recognized Private Revelations of Hell

The Church has approved (declared "worthy of belief," or recognized the sanctity of the seer) several revelations in which hell was shown. These are the most authoritative descriptions available to the faithful — though even here, only the **general content** is approved, not every detail.

### 3.1 Our Lady of Fatima — The Vision of the Sea of Fire (13 July 1917) — Approved

During the third apparition at the Cova da Iria, the Blessed Virgin showed the three shepherd children — Lúcia dos Santos, Francisco, and Jacinta Marto — a terrifying vision of hell, later written by Sister Lúcia in her **Third Memoir** (31 August 1941) and published with theological commentary by the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in **_The Message of Fatima_** (2000):

> "Our Lady showed us a great sea of fire which seemed to be under the earth. Plunged in this fire were demons and souls in human form, like transparent burning embers, all blackened or burnished bronze, floating about in the conflagration, now raised into the air by the flames that issued from within them together with great clouds of smoke, now falling back on every side like sparks in a huge fire, without weight or equilibrium, and amid shrieks and groans of pain and despair, which horrified us and made us tremble with fear. The demons could be distinguished by their terrifying and repellent likeness to frightful and unknown animals, all black and burnished. This vision lasted but a moment." — Sister Lúcia, Third Memoir, in _The Message of Fatima_ (CDF, 2000)

**Canonical weight**: The apparitions of Fatima were declared "worthy of belief" by the Bishop of Leiria (13 October 1930) and repeatedly confirmed by the Holy See; the seers Francisco and Jacinta were canonized in 2017, and Sister Lúcia has been declared Venerable. The vision of hell is therefore among the **most strongly attested private descriptions of hell** in the modern Church. Notably, the vision showed **souls in general** — no individual was identified. Its purpose, as the CDF commentary stresses, is the **call to conversion and prayer for sinners**.

### 3.2 Saint Maria Faustina Kowalska — The Seven Tortures of Hell (_Diary_, ¶741) — Recognized Devotion

St. Faustina, the Apostle of Divine Mercy (canonized 2000; her writings approved after investigation), recorded that on a given day she was "led by an Angel to the chasms of hell" and shown its tortures. She enumerated **seven tortures**:

1. The **loss of God** — the soul is deprived of God forever.
2. **Perpetual remorse of conscience** — the unending knowledge of one's own sin.
3. The certainty that **one's condition will never change** — the eternity of the state.
4. The **fire that penetrates the soul without destroying it** — a purely spiritual fire "lit by God's anger."
5. **Continual darkness and a terrible suffocating heat**, in which the damned and the demons see one another and all their own evil.
6. The **constant presence of Satan** — the companionship of the demons.
7. **Horrible despair, hatred of God, vile words, curses, and blasphemies**.

She concluded with a striking observation:

> "But I noticed one thing: that **most of the souls there are those who disbelieved that there is a hell**." — St. Faustina, _Diary_, ¶741

**Canonical weight**: The devotion revealed to St. Faustina is approved and promoted by the Church; her sanctity is canonized truth. The **description of hell's tortures** is a recognized private revelation, worthy of pious belief, but **not** binding dogma. For the full Divine Mercy context, see [miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md](../miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md).

### 3.3 Saint Teresa of Ávila — "The Place Prepared for Me" (_Life_, ch. 32)

St. Teresa of Ávila (Doctor of the Church, canonized 1622) records in her autobiography a vivid experience of the place reserved for her sins — one of the most psychologically precise descriptions of hell in mystical literature:

> "It pleased the Lord that I should see in an instant the place which my sins had merited... I felt a fire in the soul which I cannot possibly describe... The soul seemed to be torn and devoured... I learned more of the malice of sin from this experience than from all other means together." — St. Teresa of Ávila, _The Life_, ch. 32 (faithful synthesis of the chapter's account)

She describes a narrow, dark, suffocating "hollow" — no light, no exit, an eternal strangulation of the soul — and reports that the experience left her permanently changed, giving her a "most lively fear" and a deep sense of God's mercy in having spared her. Her description contains **no "levels" and no named persons**; it is an interior revelation of the state of the damned. For her full biography, see [saints/st-teresa-avila.md](../saints/st-teresa-avila.md).

### 3.4 Saint John Bosco — The Dream of Hell (3 May 1868)

St. John Bosco (canonized 1934), the great educator of Turin, recounted to his boys a famous **dream of hell** (in the _Biographical Memoirs of St. John Bosco_, vol. IX). He described a vast plain and a **deep, terrible pit of fire**, with souls tormented by demons **in groups arranged according to their sins**: the impure, the blasphemers, thieves, the violent, the envious, and other categories each suffering its own punishment. He was deeply moved, and used the dream to plead with his boys for their perseverance in grace and devotion to the Blessed Virgin.

**Canonical weight**: Bosco's dreams — including this one — were a recognized instrument of his pastoral ministry to the young, and his sanctity is canonized truth. The **dream's content**, however, is a private communication used for moral exhortation; it is not a revelation of the internal architecture of hell, and it identified **no specific persons** in the form the Church has received it.

## 4. Other Mystics and Visionary Accounts (Varying Authority)

The following figures are frequently cited in popular literature on visions of hell. Their **sanctity or beatification** is recognized, but the specific content of their reported visions has **varying, and in some cases disputed, authority**:

| Mystic | Status | Reported vision of hell | Authority of the report |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blessed Anne Catherine Emmerich** (1774–1824) | Beatified 2004 | Reported visions of hell with distinct regions of punishment | **Disputed**: recorded by the poet Clemens Brentano, whose editorial embellishment is acknowledged by scholars; not approved as revelation; never used by the Church to name the damned |
| **St. Bridget of Sweden** (1303–1373) | Canonized, Co-Patroness of Europe | _Revelations_ contain descriptions of the afterlife and of particular judgment | Approved devotion to her revelations generally; details of hell are edifying but non-binding |
| **St. Frances of Rome** (1384–1440) | Canonized 1608 | Vision of the torments of hell during ecstasy | Traditional account; edifying, non-binding |
| **St. Gemma Galgani** (1878–1903) | Canonized 1940 | Reports of being shown hell and the pains of the damned | Traditional account; edifying, non-binding |
| **St. John Vianney** (1786–1859) | Canonized, Patron of Parish Priests | Preached vividly on hell; reportedly given a vision of a soul in hell | Traditional account; moral exhortation, non-binding |
| **Mechtild of Magdeburg** (c. 1210–c. 1285) | Venerated mystic of Helfta | _The Flowing Light of the Godhead_ contains "realistic descriptions of the hereafter" | As the _Catholic Encyclopedia_ cautions, it is "difficult to ascertain how far her narrations are faithful reproductions of her visions, and how far they are additions made by her own poetic fancy" |

**Governing principle**: Beatification or canonization certifies **heroic virtue and holiness**, not the factual accuracy of every reported vision. The Church has never approved the specific eschatological details of any of these accounts as binding, and none of them may be used to make assertions about specific individuals.

## 5. What the Visions Report About the Damned: Categories and "Levels"

The private revelations that describe hell consistently agree on a set of **core themes** — and consistently decline to name individuals:

### 5.1 The Damned Are Punished According to Their Sins

Both the theological tradition (St. Thomas, _ST_ I-II, q. 87) and the visions (Bosco's groups of sinners, the apocryphal tours) present the damned as punished **according to the kinds of sins** they committed: impurity, blasphemy, theft, violence, pride, envy, and so on. This corresponds to the revealed principle that the servant who knew his master's will and did not prepare will be "beaten with many stripes" ([Luke 12:47–48](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=12&p=47)). This is the only "classification" of the damned that rests on both revelation and theology.

### 5.2 Unbelief in Hell Itself Is the Predominant Sign

St. Faustina's observation — that **most of the souls in hell are those who disbelieved in hell** — is echoed in Catholic preaching and in other mystics (St. John Vianney famously preached the same). It is a sobering pastoral point: the root of final impenitence is frequently **unbelief and spiritual presumption**, not merely weakness.

### 5.3 "Many Souls Go to Hell Because They Have No One to Pray for Them"

At the Beatification of Francisco and Jacinta (13 May 2000), **Pope John Paul II** quoted the words Our Lady is reported to have spoken at Fatima:

> "Pray, pray much and make sacrifices for sinners; **many souls go to hell because they have no one to pray and make sacrifices for them**." — Pope John Paul II, Homily at Fatima, 13 May 2000

This is the **pastoral heart** of the Fatima vision of hell: the vision exists to move the faithful to **prayer, penance, and reparation for sinners** — not to feed speculation about who is damned.

## 6. The Question of Specific Persons in Hell

This is the point at which the Catholic must be most careful, because it is the point at which popular literature, gossip, and uncharitable speculation most easily depart from the faith.

### 6.1 The Church Has Never Declared Any Human Being to Be in Hell

The Church has **canonized** saints (declaring, infallibly, that they are in heaven) — but she has **never** made a corresponding negative declaration about any specific human being's damnation. No pope, council, or dicastery has ever taught that a particular person is in hell. Pope John Paul II stated this with full authority in his catechesis on hell:

> "Damnation remains a real possibility, but **it is not granted to us, without special divine revelation, to know which human beings are effectively involved in it**. The thought of hell — and even less the improper use of biblical images — must not create anxiety or despair, but is a necessary and healthy reminder of freedom within the proclamation that the risen Jesus has conquered Satan." — Pope John Paul II, General Audience, 28 July 1999

### 6.2 Private Revelations Cannot Serve to Name the Damned

Even where a private revelation **appears** to identify a specific person as damned, the Church's principles apply without exception:

- Private revelation **never adds to or corrects** the public Revelation of Jesus Christ ([CCC 67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67)); it can never be used to settle the eternal state of any soul.
- Ecclesiastical approval of a revelation means only that it "**contains nothing contrary to faith or morals**" — not that every detail, and certainly not any identification of a person, is historically certain.
- The Church "**despairs of no one**" ([CCC 1058](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1058)) and prays for all the dead; the funeral rites of the Church express hope for every soul, without exception.
- The 2024 _Norms_ of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith explicitly warn against treating private claims as more certain than the Gospel itself — a warning directly relevant to claims about who is damned.

### 6.3 Alleged Identifications in Visionary Literature — How to Understand Them

Popular books and websites sometimes circulate claims that a mystic "saw" a famous person in hell (e.g., in the disputed Brentano records of **Blessed Anne Catherine Emmerich**, or in the medieval vision literature that named kings, bishops, and even popes). The Catholic must understand these claims correctly:

- They are **private claims of the most fragile kind** — unverifiable, often from records of disputed reliability, and never confirmed by any Church authority.
- They are frequently **contradictory** (different mystics "seeing" the same figure in different states), which reveals their unreliable character.
- The Church has **consistently declined** to treat any such claim as a ground for asserting anyone's damnation.
- The _Catholic Encyclopedia_ and every serious theological authority caution against treating visionary reports of named individuals as factual.
- Where a well-attested mystic (e.g., St. Teresa of Ávila, St. Faustina) describes hell, she describes the **state of the damned in general** — not identifiable persons.

Accordingly, this document deliberately **does not repeat** the names alleged in such literature. Doing so would (a) exceed the Church's authority, (b) violate the charity owed to the dead, and (c) turn a call to conversion into gossip. The faithful are bound to **hope for all** and to **judge no one** — a duty that extends even to the most notorious sinners of history.

### 6.4 The One Certainty: The Demons Are Damned

The only beings whose damnation is revealed with certainty are **Satan and the rebel angels**, who "have already said no" to God (cf. [Fourth Lateran Council, DS 800–801](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/800)). For human beings, the Church's proclamation is one of **hope**: "God does not predestine anyone to hell" ([CCC 1037](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1037)), and "the Church prays that no one may be lost" ([CCC 1058](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1058)).

## 7. The Eastern Catholic Perspective

Catholic eschatology — including the mystery of hell — is **not the property of the Latin Rite alone**. The Eastern Catholic _sui iuris_ Churches preserve their own rich tradition (see [church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md)):

### 7.1 The Harrowing of Hell (_Anastasis_)

The Eastern tradition gives a central place to **Christ's descent into hell** — the Harrowing of Hades — proclaimed in the Byzantine Paschal liturgy and depicted in the icon of the _Anastasis_: the risen Christ, robed in light, trampling the gates of death and raising Adam and Eve from the tomb. Hell is understood first and foremost as **the place that Christ has already invaded and emptied of its tyranny** — a truth that frames every Eastern consideration of damnation in the light of victory and hope.

### 7.2 Hell as the Experience of Divine Love Rejected

The Eastern Fathers — especially **St. Isaac of Nineveh**, **St. Maximus the Confessor**, and **St. John Damascene** (_Exposition of the Orthodox Faith_, Bk. IV) — develop the understanding that the "fire" of hell is not a distinct created substance so much as the **uncreated fire of God's own love**, experienced as torment by those who have rejected it. The same divine presence that is bliss to the blessed is burning to the damned, because the damned have made themselves incapable of receiving love. This relational, non-spatial emphasis tempers every Western image of "levels" or "geography."

### 7.3 Hope for All — Without Universalism

Some Eastern Fathers (notably **St. Gregory of Nyssa**) entertained a hope for the eventual salvation of all — a **theological opinion that has never been defined and is not Catholic dogma**, and which the Church has consistently refused to assert (Origen's universalism was condemned). The Eastern Catholic Churches hold the **same dogma** as the Latin Church: hell is real and eternal, and God condemns no one whom free will has not definitively rejected. Eastern spirituality, however, characteristically **weeps more than it maps**: it dwells less on describing hell than on urging repentance, and it never names the damned.

## 8. How the Faithful Should Receive These Revelations

The Church's own guidance — from [CCC 66–67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/66), the CDF's 1979 letter on eschatology, and the 2024 Dicastery _Norms_ — yields clear pastoral principles:

1. **Hold the dogma firmly**: hell is real, eternal, and entered by final impenitence in mortal sin ([CCC 1033–1037](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1033)). This is a truth of faith, not a scare tactic.
2. **Receive approved visions for what they are**: Fatima, St. Faustina, St. Teresa, and St. John Bosco's accounts are spiritually valuable, edifying, and in the first case formally approved — but they are **private revelations**, worthy of pious belief, never binding, and subordinate to the Gospel ([CCC 67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67)).
3. **Do not treat "levels" as fact**: the internal architecture of hell is not revealed; schemas from Dante, the apocrypha, or medieval visions are literature or opinion.
4. **Never name the damned**: the Church has not done so; private revelations cannot do so; charity and hope forbid the faithful from doing so. "Judge not, that you may not be judged" ([Matthew 7:1](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=7&p=1)).
5. **Let the vision become prayer**: the authentic response to every vision of hell is the response Our Lady of Fatima asked for — **prayer, penance, and sacrifice for sinners**, that "many souls" may not be lost for want of someone to pray for them.
