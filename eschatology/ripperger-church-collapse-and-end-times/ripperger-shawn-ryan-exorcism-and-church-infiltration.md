## Source and Scope

This document analyses a **12-minute-51-second Facebook Reel** ([facebook.com/reel/1140087231885822](https://www.facebook.com/reel/1140087231885822)) consisting of a segment from an interview between **Shawn Ryan** (host of _The Shawn Ryan Show_) and **Fr. Chad Ripperger, S.T.D., Ph.D.**

In the clip, Fr. Ripperger makes four connected claims:

1. During exorcisms, demons disclose — without being asked — the crimes of high-ranking churchmen, including specific names, and other exorcists have received the same disclosures.
2. Some of that leadership is merely morally compromised; some of it, in his judgement, is involved in something "very dark," including men who entered the priesthood already committed to the other side.
3. There is a causal chain in which the moral and spiritual collapse of the Church's own members, greased by Masonic, communist, and satanist infiltration of seminaries and religious institutes in the 1930s–1950s, removes graces that would otherwise restrain civil society, producing a corresponding collapse in civil life with a **ten-to-twenty-year lag**.
4. The teaching authority of a bishop remains real even if the bishop himself is compromised, because authority attaches to the office and not to the man's moral state; but such a bishop will never grant the faculties needed to act on it. Ripperger declines to name anyone, says only four or five non-exorcists know whom he has in mind, and states that the situation is "beyond human correction" — his own duty being to "defend my own foxhole."

The clip is **not** a doctrinal exposition, a Church document, or verified reporting. It is exorcist testimony plus a historical thesis. Several elements converge with defined Catholic teaching; several are unverifiable claims about named-by-implication individuals; one historical citation is misdated. All three categories are separated below.

## The Four Claims in Detail

### 1. Demons as informants

The clip's opening claim is that the exorcist need not interrogate: the demon is "compelled sometimes by Our Lord" to speak, and frequently manifests during the Mass that begins a session precisely because God requires a disclosure. Ripperger says the content has concentrated on the hierarchy rather than on civil officials, has included enough detail to be identifying, and has been corroborated in fragments by other exorcists.

Three things are worth separating. First, the **phenomenon** — demons speaking during a session — is not in dispute as a description of what exorcists report; the classical and modern literature is full of it. Second, the **interpretation** (that the disclosure is divinely compelled for the exorcist's benefit) is a theological reading of an experience, not a rubric. Third, the **reliability** of the speaker is precisely the question: the Church has never treated a demon as a trustworthy witness, and the 1985 CDF letter to ordinaries explicitly asked bishops to stop unauthorised gatherings in which demons are questioned and their identity sought. The full text of that letter is at [magisterium.com/docs/f59d0c4b](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/f59d0c4b-c37e-4442-a657-adc98242d22e/ref/page1).

### 2. Some priests entered "already on the other team"

The strongest of his claims, and the least evidenced: not merely that compromised men were tolerated after ordination, but that at least some were deliberate infiltrators who sought the priesthood as a source of power to be used against the Church. The distinction matters pastorally. That Judas was among the Twelve is dogma-adjacent Scripture ([John 6:70–71](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=6&p=70)); that a particular living prelate is a conscious Satanist is a claim that requires a canonically and evidentially serious procedure, which this clip does not supply.

Ripperger is careful at one point and careless at another: he refuses to name the Roman exorcist who corroborates him, expressly so that the man is not badgered with "who are these people?" — and yet he tells a global audience that the rot reaches "close to the very, very top." The refusal to name is prudent; the placement of the accusation without a name is still an accusation, and hearers should notice that it cannot be tested, only repeated.

### 3. The "ten-to-twenty-year lag" thesis

The argument runs: grace flows into the world through the Church as the Mystical Body of Christ; therefore, when the Church's own members fail in a particular area of moral and spiritual life, the corresponding grace is withheld; therefore the world's corresponding failure follows a generation later. On this account the sexual revolution of the 1960s tracks the clergy's failures in the 1950s, and the later normality of homosexuality tracks a second wave.

As a piece of historical reasoning this is a **single-factor theory of a multi-causal event** and would need to be argued against the standard historiography of the sexual revolution (contraception, pharmacology, war demobilisation, media, economics, jurisprudence). As theology it depends on a stronger version of "all grace comes through the Church" than the Church herself defines — see the assessment below.

### 4. Authority attaches to the office

Ripperger is at his most classically Catholic here, and the answer is correct in substance. The power to bind and loose, to consecrate, and to command in exorcism is conferred by ordination and jurisdiction, not by the minister's sanctity — the whole Donatist controversy was settled on exactly this point, and the principle is why the Church can say that the sacraments act _ex opere operato_ ([CCC 1128](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1128)). A bishop's authorisation, and a demon's submission to it, do not become void because the bishop is a bad man. The corollary he adds — that a compromised bishop simply will not grant the faculties, so the theoretical authority is never operative — is a sharp observation about how institutional corruption actually works.

## Catholic Doctrinal Assessment

### 1. What the clip gets right

- **Exorcism is a priestly ministry requiring the express permission of the local Ordinary.** Canon 1172 §1 forbids anyone to perform exorcisms over the possessed without "special and express permission" of the local Ordinary, and §2 restricts the faculty to priests distinguished by piety, knowledge, prudence and integrity of life ([CIC 1172](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/927224e3-8c2d-44ed-a9fb-dc736030081d/ref/1172)). The Catechism states the same and insists that possible mental illness be excluded first ([CCC 1673](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1673)).
- **Authority is attached to office, not to personal merit.** This is the settled doctrine against the Donatists, and the reason the validity of the sacraments does not depend on the minister's state of grace ([CCC 1128](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1128)). Ripperger's answer to Ryan's question is therefore theologically correct.
- **Satan is real, personal, and active.** The Church teaches the existence of the devil and of the fallen angels, their malice, and their limited power under God's permission ([CCC 391–395](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/391); [CCC 409](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/409)); St. Thomas treats the assault of the demons at length (_Summa Theologiae_ I, q. 114). The spiritual combat is not a metaphor.
- **The Church is the universal sacrament of salvation, and all salvation comes from Christ through her.** Vatican II calls the Church "in Christ like a sacrament" of union with God and of the unity of the human race ([Lumen Gentium 1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/5e832a8b-f1b9-4740-9bf4-8209f9910f2f/ref/1)) and the "universal sacrament of salvation" ([Lumen Gentium 48](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/5e832a8b-f1b9-4740-9bf4-8209f9910f2f/ref/48)); the Catechism re-formulates "outside the Church there is no salvation" to mean that "all salvation comes from Christ the Head through the Church which is his Body" ([CCC 846](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/846)).
- **Serious corruption in the clergy is a real and documented wound.** Ripperger is not inventing the abuse crisis; he is interpreting it. The Church's own juridical response includes the 2001 norms on _delicta graviora_, the 2019 motu proprio [Vos estis lux mundi](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/papa-francesco-motu-proprio_20190507_vos-estis-lux-mundi.html) with its 2023 revision, and the 2005 CDF instruction on seminary admission discussed below.
- **Moral decay in a society's elites precedes its general decay.** This is a theme of Scripture ([Isaiah 1:23](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=isa&g=1&p=23); [Hosea 4:9](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ose&g=4&p=9); [Romans 1:24–32](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rom&g=1&p=24)) and of the Church's social doctrine. What is not doctrine is the specific quantitative lag.

### 3. Notes of historical and canonical precision

1. **"Chapter one" of the rite.** Fr. Ripperger refers to introductory rubrics. Whether the 1998 _De Exorcismis et Supplicationibus Quibusdam_ contains a rubric expecting disclosure of crimes cannot be confirmed from the extract he describes, and should not be asserted as a rubric on his word alone. The safe statement is narrower: authorised exorcists do report such disclosures, and the Church forbids unauthorised questioning of demons ([CDF 1985](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/f59d0c4b-c37e-4442-a657-adc98242d22e/ref/page1)).
2. **Fr. Gabriele Amorth.** Fr. Amorth (1925–2016) was appointed exorcist of the Diocese of Rome in 1986 and served for roughly three decades, not the "four years" the transcript attributes to Ripperger; he founded the International Association of Exorcists in 1993. His published complaints that demons named high-ranking churchmen are on the record in his books, so Ripperger's appeal to him is at least checkable at that level.
3. **The "1960" document.** The instruction Ripperger means is **_Religiosorum institutio_**, issued by the **Sacred Congregation for Religious in 1961** (not 1960), on the careful selection and training of candidates for the states of perfection and Sacred Orders ([full text](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b9ec2c9f-7330-41e6-a9ef-1962641af8ee/ref/page1)). It is a genuine precursor in the line that continues through the 1961 text's concern with "perverse inclinations," the 1990 directives on formation in religious institutes, and the CDF's 2005 [Instruction on the Admission of Candidates with Homosexual Tendencies to the Seminary and Holy Orders](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/e2d22cce-f739-49e8-bb65-f8e5ff1c7c04/ref/page1), which excludes men who practise homosexuality or have deep-seated homosexual tendencies while treating transitory tendencies separately; the 2016 [Ratio Fundamentalis](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/12c635ea-5060-47cf-88d0-2024e27d6c1d/ref/VIII) carries the discipline forward. The existence of these documents supports the narrower claim that the Church herself judged the mid-century formation system to be failing. It does not establish the wider infiltration narrative.
4. **"All grace comes into the world by means of the Catholic Church."** The defined core is that salvation comes from Christ through the Church ([CCC 846–848](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/847)); the phrase as Ripperger states it — a universal conduit of grace whose failure mechanically withholds grace from the world — is a theological opinion, and he himself concedes the obvious qualification that Protestants receive grace. The conclusion he draws from it (a measurable civic lag) is not a doctrine of the Church and should not be taught as one.
5. **Freemasonry.** The Church's condemnation of Freemasonry is doctrinal and juridical, not a conspiracy theory: from Clement XII's _In Eminenti_ (1738) through the 1983 CDF declaration and its 2023 reaffirmation (see [enemies-of-the-church/freemasons.md](../../enemies-of-the-church/freemasons.md)). That condemnation is one thing; the claim that Masons as such engineered the abuse crisis is another, and rests on evidence not presented in this clip.

### 4. Discernment cautions

- Sacred Scripture describes the devil as "a liar, and the father of lies" ([John 8:44](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=8&p=44)) who disguises himself as an angel of light ([2 Corinthians 11:14](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2co&g=11&p=14)) and prowls like a roaring lion ([1 Peter 5:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1pe&g=5&p=8)). Truth told by a demon is truth told for a destructive purpose — usually to sow suspicion, to induce despair, or to make the hearer feel initiated into a secret. Delivering it to a podcast audience is precisely the risk.
- The Church's own caution in this area is explicit: the 1985 CDF letter asks bishops to prevent even non-possessed gatherings in which demons are directly questioned and their identity sought ([CDF 1985](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/f59d0c4b-c37e-4442-a657-adc98242d22e/ref/page1)). Whatever one makes of disclosures inside a duly authorised session, their export into public discourse is not covered by any faculty.
- Anonymous accusation of the "close to the top" kind is the classic structure of a conspiracy narrative: unfalsifiable, endlessly repeatable, and immunising the teller against contradiction ("they don't want this known"). The remedy is not disbelief of the teller but a demand for proportion: pray for the Church, report concrete crimes to the proper authorities, and refuse to circulate names that no one has substantiated.
- Paul VI's warning in his homily of 29 June 1972 — that the "smoke of Satan" has entered the temple of God — is the magisterial register of the same anxiety, and it is a call to penance rather than to a hunt.

### 5. Pastoral bottom line

Fr. Ripperger's practical conclusion is, in its own way, sober: he does not claim a mandate to expose anyone, he does not name anyone, he says he keeps doing the work he was given, and he says the situation is being prayed over. That is more restrained than the clip's framing suggests and is the part most worth keeping. What a Catholic should take from the twelve minutes is:

1. **The reality of the enemy and the reality of the office.** The sacraments and the ministry of the Church do not depend on the sanctity of her ministers — which is both a consolation and, as the abuse crisis showed, a warning that holiness cannot be assumed from a collar.
2. **No private revelation or exorcist testimony outranks the sacraments.** Confession, the Eucharist, prayer, fasting, the Rosary, and the intercession of St. Michael are the ordinary means; a viral clip is not.
3. **Reputation is a good to be protected** ([CCC 2477–2479](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2477)). Believe the possibility of corruption in the abstract; refuse to convict the unnameable in the concrete.
4. **Real crimes belong to real forums.** Allegations of abuse or of occult criminality in the clergy go to the diocesan safeguarding office, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, and civil police — not to a comment section ([Vos estis lux mundi](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/papa-francesco-motu-proprio_20190507_vos-estis-lux-mundi.html)).
5. **Hope is a theological virtue, not an optimism about institutions.** "Beyond human correction" is not a Catholic conclusion about the Church, because the Church's reform is not a human project ([CCC 853](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/853)); and the gates of hell will not prevail against her ([Matthew 16:18](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=16&p=18)).
