## Why this sits under Eschatology

The clip is not primarily an exorcism manual. Its argument is a claim about the **shape of the present crisis and what it portends**: that corruption in the Church's own authority structure withholds the graces that restrain civil society, that the resulting collapse arrives roughly a generation later, and that the rot reaches "close to the very top." That is an eschatological claim — a reading of the times as a prelude to judgment — and it belongs with the repository's other studies of the crisis, prophecy, and the last things rather than with the angelology surveys.

## What the report does and does not claim

- It reports Fr. Ripperger's testimony and his historical thesis as **attributed source material**, with approximate timestamps.
- It separates four grades: defined Catholic doctrine (the ministry of exorcism, authority attaching to office, the Church as universal sacrament of salvation); exorcist testimony that cannot be verified; a historical thesis (Masonic and communist infiltration of mid-century seminaries); and theological opinion (the "ten-to-twenty-year lag").
- It names **no one**. Fr. Ripperger names no one, and the report does not guess. Anonymous accusation of living persons is a matter of canon 220 and CCC 2477–2479, not of edification.
- It corrects two factual slips in the clip: the "1960 _Institutio Religiosorum_" is the 1961 **_Religiosorum institutio_** of the Sacred Congregation for Religious, and Fr. Gabriele Amorth served as Rome's exorcist for roughly three decades, not four years.
