Chris Alar, MIC, at the National Shrine of The Divine Mercy in Stockbridge, Massachusetts on 25 July 2020, and published the same day on the Divine Mercy YouTube channel. Fr. ## Overview

Fr. Alar sets out to answer three questions: **what the Church actually teaches about the end of the world, what she does not teach, and how a Catholic should prepare.** He frames the talk as an exercise in truth versus heresy, and he is explicit from the outset that a great deal of what circulates on the subject in American popular religion is not Catholic at all:

> "Do we believe in the Antichrist? Yes. The Rapture? No. The Tribulation? Sort of. We're going to sort all of that out." (≈ 2:49)

The structure is catechetical rather than speculative. He begins from [CCC 670](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/670) to establish that Christians already live in "the last hour," then sets the Catholic reading against the dispensationalist scheme popularized by the _Left Behind_ novels, then walks through five scriptural signs, then turns to the private revelations on the illumination of conscience and the three days of darkness, then to the place of the Jewish people and the rebuilding of the Temple, and finally to a long exegetical refutation of the claim that the Catholic Church is the "Whore of Babylon."

Two features of the talk deserve notice at the outset. First, Fr. Alar **names his sources** — he credits the Catholic writer Taylor Marshall for the summary of the "last signs" and the writer he calls Robert Haddad for the framework of the second-coming "elements," and he explicitly declines to endorse Marshall's wider theological positions ("I'm not here to embrace his theology," ≈ 6:01). Second, he **repeatedly labels his own material**: at the point where he moves from Scripture to private revelation he says plainly, "This is not church teaching" (≈ 25:59), and again of the mystics, "this is not dogmatic revelation or doctrinal church teaching" (≈ 32:57). That discipline, unevenly as it is observed, is the reason the talk is usable as catechesis at all.

## 1. Why the end times matter: "we are already at the last hour"

Fr. Alar's first move is to remove the subject from the realm of speculation and place it inside the Church's ordinary doctrine of Revelation. His argument runs: public Revelation ended with the apostolic age, therefore the whole period since the Ascension is "the end times," and therefore the question is not _whether_ we live in them but _how_ we live in them.

He concedes the objection immediately — "Jesus said nobody knows the day or the hour. True." — and answers it by distinguishing the _certainty of the age_ from the _uncertainty of the date_: "we don't know how long those end times will be... it could end tonight... it could be five hundred years" (≈ 3:26–3:47). This is precisely the shape of the Church's own teaching. [_Dei Verbum_ 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/4) closes the matter of new public revelation, and [CCC 66](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/66) adds that Revelation is complete yet "not made completely explicit" — which is why doctrine develops without new revelation being added.

He then reads [CCC 670](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/670) at the lectern:

> "Since the Ascension God's plan has entered into its fulfilment. We are already at 'the last hour'... 'Already the final age of the world is with us, and the renewal of the world is irrevocably under way; it is even now anticipated in a certain real way, for the Church on earth is endowed already with a sanctity that is real but imperfect.'"

Two observations. The paragraph is a citation of _Lumen Gentium_ 48 §3, and the speaker handles it correctly: he draws from it the ecclesial conclusion — "Christ's Kingdom has already manifested itself here on earth in the Church" (≈ 4:29) — rather than a chronological one. This matters, because the same move controls his later rejection of millenarianism (§8 below): if the Kingdom is already present in the Church, then the Second Coming cannot be the _establishment_ of Christ's reign but only its _manifestation_.

A precision worth adding: the shorthand "public Revelation ended with the death of the last Apostle" is standard theological usage for the close of the apostolic age, but the Council and the Catechism state the doctrine in terms of the finality of Christ rather than of a date marked by an apostle's death. The talk uses the shorthand; the substance is unaffected.

## 2. The contrast with dispensationalist "Left Behind" eschatology

Fr. Alar then sets the Catholic position over against the scheme most Americans absorb by osmosis. He describes the popular version, taking the _Left Behind_ novels as its vehicle, with care to state it fairly:

> "Seven years before the end of the world... the good will be raptured... those remaining on earth will be all non-believers and Catholics. So we are labelled in that category." (≈ 6:50–7:22)

The scheme he outlines is the classical dispensationalist sequence: a secret removal of believers; a seven-year tribulation divided at its mid-point by the appearance of the Antichrist; the conversion of those "left behind" under a new leadership; and a final public return of Christ to inaugurate a literal thousand-year earthly reign. His criticism is historical rather than merely polemical — "this doctrine was invented in the eighteen hundreds... it doesn't conform to scripture or the church fathers" (≈ 8:23), a claim that is accurate as to the system's pedigree: the pre-tribulation rapture as a defined expectation emerges with John Nelson Darby in the 1830s, is disseminated through the Scofield Reference Bible (1909), and reaches mass culture through Hal Lindsey and the _Left Behind_ series.

He is also careful about tone, twice disclaiming contempt for Protestants, and noting that many Protestants do not hold the doctrine at all: "I know so many very good Protestants; they've said, Father, I don't believe that" (≈ 1:05:08). For the Catholic doctrinal grounds on which the scheme is rejected, see §7.

## 3. The five signs of the Second Coming

The heart of the talk is a five-point schema of signs that must precede the _parousia_ (παρουσία), which he glosses correctly as "the divine manifestation of Jesus" (≈ 10:54). He insists that the schema is drawn from Scripture and from St. Thomas Aquinas rather than from any private locution — "I'm not giving you the teaching of some lady that called me on Wednesday... I'm giving you what scripture says" (≈ 10:27).

A necessary qualification: the Church has not defined a closed, numbered list of signs. What she has done is teach, in [CCC 673–677](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/675), the substance of the signs — the Church's final trial, the deception of the Antichrist, the completion of the Gentile mission and the entry of "all Israel," and the final unleashing of evil. The five-fold arrangement is a catechetical synthesis of that scriptural material, useful and legitimate, but its numbering carries no doctrinal weight.

### Sign one — the Gospel preached to all the nations

Ground: [Matthew 24:14](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=24&p=14) — "this gospel of the kingdom will be preached throughout the whole world, as a testimony to all nations; and then the end will come."

Fr. Alar ties the sign to the Church's missionary nature: "there must be a penetration of the Church into all nations" (≈ 11:53). He then adds an excursus, dated to the circumstances of 2020, arguing that global livestreaming has brought the completion of the sign nearer than at any previous point — reaching "from South America to Antarctica... as far East as The Philippines... Arabic countries... Africa" (≈ 12:27–13:02). This is a homiletic application rather than a theological claim, and it is worth noting as such: the sign's fulfillment is a matter of the Church's universal proclamation, not of any particular communications technology, and the Lord himself declined to draw a timetable from it.

The passage also carries a subsidiary point about the "sovereignty of nations," grounded in words he attributes to Our Lord's direction to St. Faustina to pray for one's own country. That is a devotional application of a private revelation to a political question; it carries the authority of neither.

### Sign two — the great apostasy

Ground: [Matthew 24:4–5, 11](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=24&p=4) ("many will come in my name... and lead many astray") and [2 Thessalonians 2:3](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=3) ("unless the rebellion comes first"). He defines apostasy as "falling away of the faith, rejection of God" (≈ 13:28) and reads the present period as its evident beginning.

His most useful observation here is that the agents of deception need not be outside the Church: "those who could lead us astray could even be religious... bishops, religious, sisters, priests... even Catholics, politicians, family even" (≈ 14:00). This is a sober reading of Matthew 24 and it anticipates the Catechism's own phrase, "a religious deception offering men an apparent solution to their problems at the price of apostasy from the truth" ([CCC 675](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/675)).

### Sign three — the conversion of "all Israel"

Ground: [Romans 11:25–26](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ro&g=11&p=25) — "a hardening has come upon part of Israel, until the full number of the Gentiles come in, and so all Israel will be saved."

This is the sign the talk treats with the most doctrinal confidence, and rightly so, because it is Catechism teaching: [CCC 674](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/674) states that "the glorious Messiah's coming is suspended at every moment of history until his recognition by 'all Israel'." Fr. Alar also invokes St. Thomas Aquinas and St. Peter's Pentecost sermon ([Acts 3:19–21](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ac&g=3&p=19)), both of which the Catechism cites in the same paragraph.

Two remarks of his deserve recording. First, he quotes Cardinal Ratzinger to the effect that the end "cannot occur" until the Gentiles' number is complete and Israel has entered — an accurate paraphrase of the exegetical point, though the speaker offers no citation, and it should be attributed to Ratzinger as a private theological judgment rather than a magisterial act. Second, he draws a pastoral conclusion that is the most quietly excellent moment in the talk: since Our Lady "belongs to Israel... she was a Jew," it is fitting to ask her help for the conversion of Israel (≈ 16:23–16:42). This is theologically sound — the Church's prayer for the Jewish people has an explicitly Marian register in the tradition — and it is a good deal more defensible than most popular treatments of the same subject.

### Sign four — the revelation of the Antichrist

Ground: [2 Thessalonians 2:3–12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/675), the Johannine epistles, and [CCC 675](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/675).

Fr. Alar lists the elements in order: the Antichrist will be a **man**, "fully human and not the devil incarnate"; he will be **possessed** by Satan; he will work **deceptive miracles** that "will look like true miracles"; he will head the apostasy; he will be "persuasive and powerful"; he will demand worship under penalty of death; and he will be destroyed by Christ "by the breath of his mouth" ([2 Thessalonians 2:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=8)).

The theological reasoning he gives for the first point is sound and worth preserving: "the devil doesn't have the power to become incarnated. Only God has the power to become incarnate" (≈ 18:39). This is right, and it is not a minor point: the Incarnation is the assumption of a human nature into the unity of a divine Person, an act proper to God alone ([CCC 456–460](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/456)). Diabolic possession is a different thing altogether — an alien influence on a person who remains himself. The distinction protects the uniqueness of the hypostatic union.

Three of his additions require grading:

1. **"He will appear halfway through that seven-year Tribulation... three and a half years before the end."** The forty-two months of [Revelation 11:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=11&p=2) and [13:5](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=13&p=5), and the "midst of the week" of [Daniel 9:27](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=dn&g=9&p=27), are real scriptural data, and a three-and-a-half-year tribulation is a widely held reading. But the Church has defined no seven-year chronology and no dateable mid-point. The talk states the point with more confidence than the sources bear ("Most theologians and Church Fathers... we agree here with some Protestants"), and elsewhere in the same talk he concedes of the seven-year frame only that "the Tribulation? Sort of." Treat this as a defensible theological opinion, not as doctrine.
2. **"Many say only those who have been confirmed will be able to withstand it."** He grounds this in "many saints" and in the grace of Confirmation. The underlying sacramental theology is real — Confirmation confers a character and the strength of the Holy Spirit for witness and combat ([CCC 1302–1305](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1302)) — and the pastoral exhortation to be confirmed is entirely right. The specific claim that the unconfirmed will be unable to endure is a claim from private revelation, not doctrine, and should be presented as such.
3. **"He will be worshipped in the temple."** This is [2 Thessalonians 2:4](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=4), not 2 Corinthians; see the corrections table. Whether the temple in question is a rebuilt Jerusalem sanctuary or the Church herself is a live exegetical question the talk does not raise.

He reads [CCC 675](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/675) in full, and it is the strongest passage in the talk precisely because it is the Church's own text:

> "Before Christ's second coming the Church must pass through a final trial that will shake the faith of many believers... The supreme religious deception is that of the Antichrist, a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh."

### Sign five — the great tribulation

Ground: [Matthew 24–25](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=24&p=9), which he calls with the exegetical tradition "the little Apocalypse." His description is conventional and correct: "natural disasters and man made problems"; "delivered up to tribulation and hatred by all nations" ([Matthew 24:9](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=24&p=9)); famines, wars, persecutions, earthquakes; and the final **conflagration**, which he notes will not annihilate creation but "change its form and appearance" (≈ 22:53) — a point that sits exactly on [CCC 677](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/677), which speaks of "the final cosmic upheaval of this passing world," and on [2 Peter 3:12–13](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2pe&g=3&p=12).

His pastoral framing of the sign is the best passage in the talk:

> "The Apocalypse does not mean death and destruction, but literally unveiling. This is what we will see in the unveiling at the end: Christ in the fullness of His glory." (≈ 24:45)

He then names the moral difference between the present and the recent past: earlier generations "could just skate through as a Catholic," whereas now the disputed questions — abortion, pornography, the redefinition of marriage — "are becoming ballot issues," so that "we have to take a stand one side or the other" (≈ 23:23–23:53). Whatever one makes of the sociology, the theological point is right: tribulation is the occasion of a decision, and the Church's prayer is not for the removal of the trial but for endurance within it ([Luke 18:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=lk&g=18&p=8); [CCC 675](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/675)).

## 4. Private revelation: the illumination of conscience and the three days of darkness

At 0:25:36 the talk crosses a boundary that it marks clearly. What follows is not Scripture or Catechism but alleged private revelation, and Fr. Alar says so twice.

He opens with a personal judgment, labelled as such: "this is not church teaching. I believe the world is so far gone that it will take a direct intervention from God to turn it around" (≈ 25:59).

### The illumination of conscience

The claim: at some point before the end, each soul will be shown itself as God sees it — a "mini judgment" within the soul, in which time seems to stand still and conscience is flooded with its own state. Fr. Alar grounds the plausibility of the claim in St. Faustina's observation that even the smallest sins greatly offend God, and he offers a personal anecdote of waking at 3 a.m. with an unexpected self-knowledge, which he reads as "a mini way" of the same grace (≈ 27:58). He insists — correctly, and this is the right theological instinct — that such an event would be **mercy rather than punishment**: "that way we can make corrections."

This is the motif treated at length in [the-warning.md](the-warning.md), where it is traced through St. Faustina (_Diary_ 36), Bl. Anna Maria Taigi, Bl. Elisabetta Canori Mora, Garabandal, Medjugorje, Fátima, La Salette, Akita, and Fr. Gobbi's Marian Movement of Priests. Two points from that study bear directly on this talk. First, the illumination of conscience has **no defined dogmatic content**; it is not an article of faith and no Catholic is bound to expect it. Second, the motif has become the central claim of the unapproved _Book of Truth_ corpus, which several bishops have prohibited — a fact the talk does not mention and which hearers should know. See [book-of-truth/church-assessment-and-warnings.md](../book-of-truth/church-assessment-and-warnings.md).

### The three days of darkness

The claim: a period of three days and nights of total darkness, during which no artificial light will burn and only blessed candles will give light; the doors must be kept shut, since "he who out of curiosity opens his windows... will fall dead on the spot"; the air will carry pestilence; demons will be loosed and will imitate the voices of loved ones at the door.

Fr. Alar handles this material with more care than most popular treatments:

- He gives the **biblical precedent** correctly: the ninth plague of Egypt, "darkness over the land of Egypt for three days" ([Exodus 10:21–23](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ex&g=10&p=21)), which he notes is genuine Scripture, not invention.
- He tells his hearers to **get the candles blessed by a priest** — the right instinct, since it is the blessing that makes them sacramentals at all ([CCC 1671–1673](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1671)).
- He is **honest about the disputed question** of beeswax: "some say yes, others say no... I've not been able to find in any of the writings of the saints... where it says they absolutely have to be beeswax. Personally, I got mine as beeswax anyway. To be safe, what does it hurt?" (≈ 28:52–29:11). That is exactly the right calibration: an unresolved question in a private revelation, resolved prudentially at no theological cost.
- He **labels the claim** as non-dogmatic: "this is not dogmatic revelation or doctrinal church teaching" (≈ 32:57).
- He reads the pandemic lockdown of 2020 as a providential "preparation" for such an event — an interpretation offered as his own reading, not as revelation.

The comprehensive treatment of this devotion, including the 1915 Holy Office decree on La Salette, the witnesses, the question of superstition, and the 2024 Dicastery norms, is in [three-days-of-darkness.md](three-days-of-darkness.md).

His treatment of the door and the voices is the point at which the talk is most likely to be misused, and he handles it pastorally: the demons will come, "even voices that sound like your loved ones... and that's going to be tempting. But we have to pray for God's discernment, the gift of the Holy Spirit" (≈ 33:46–34:00). The remedy he proposes is a charism, not a technique — which is the correct ordering.

### Medjugorje

The talk recommends Medjugorje and the Mary TV broadcasts as a source for Our Lady's messages, and reports the seers' ten secrets as involving "prophecies of global chastisement," with three warnings followed by "a great sign centered at the apparition site, but visible all over the world," which he expects to be "a giant Red Cross of light in the sky" (≈ 34:09–34:51).

Two corrections of fact and of framing are required for a reader in 2026:

1. **Canonical status.** The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith issued its Note on Medjugorje, _"The Queen of Peace,"_ on 19 September 2024, granting a **_nihil obstat_**. That judgment is positive about the spiritual fruits and authorizes pastoral care and devotion, but it makes **no declaration of the supernatural character** of the alleged apparitions, and it directs that the messages be called "alleged messages." No Catholic is obliged to believe them. The 2024 norms distinguish sharply between a _nihil obstat_ and a declaration of supernatural origin — a distinction the talk's confident phrasing does not observe. See [miracles/marian-apparitions/ongoing-investigations/medjugorje.md](../miracles/marian-apparitions/ongoing-investigations/medjugorje.md).
2. **The "giant Red Cross"** is the speaker's own expectation, not a message he quotes. It should be reported as his inference.

### The "post-2000" sequence

Fr. Alar then describes a shift he perceives in Catholic end-times expectation since the year 2000. The older sequence, which he associates with St. Vincent Ferrer ("the Doctor of the Apocalypse") and other medieval teachers, ran: a great ruler and a great Pope, then the Antichrist, then the end. The newer sequence, which he associates with the book _The Warning_ — which he candidly says he has not read and cannot comment on — runs:

> **the Warning** (the illumination of conscience, a "mini judgment" within each soul, during which time seems to stand still) → **the great miracle** (the greatest ever worked, converting Russia and the world, leaving a permanent sign that can be photographed but not touched) → **the chastisement** (worse than a fire, its severity conditioned on human repentance) → **the three days of darkness** → **the era of peace**, which he identifies with the triumph of the Immaculate Heart.

This is a coherent devotional schema and he presents it as such. It is also entirely private-revelation and theological-opinion territory. Several elements need flagging: the claim that the chastisement's severity is **conditional** is theologically sound and consistent with Fátima ("if men do not cease offending God") and with the conditional character of all prophetic warnings ([Jonah 3](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=jon&g=3&p=10)); the claim of a permanent, photographable sign is a specific prediction of private revelation and should be held as one; and the identification of the "era of peace" with a defined historical period must be held together with [CCC 676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/676), which forbids expecting "within history" the messianic hope that can only be realized beyond history. The Church permits the hope of a final Marian triumph without permitting a millenarian timetable. See [the-warning.md](the-warning.md) and [signs-in-the-heavens-and-the-cross.md](signs-in-the-heavens-and-the-cross.md).

## 5. The place of the Jewish people

This is the longest and most delicate stretch of the talk, and it should be read with [Nostra Aetate 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033) open beside it. The speaker is aware of the sensitivity ("I apologize to any Jewish people watching," ≈ 43:40) and he states the theological premises correctly. But the material is of a kind that has historically been misused, and the pastoral cautions in the final section of this document are not optional.

### Israel, the Temple, and the Antichrist

Fr. Alar's exegetical argument runs in four steps:

1. **The re-establishment of Israel in 1948** is read as a fulfillment of biblical prophecy (≈ 39:07). This is a common reading in both Catholic and Protestant eschatology, though the Church has made no definitive judgment on the prophetic significance of 1948, and Catholic exegesis has generally been more reticent than dispensationalist exegesis about reading modern political events as direct fulfillments.
2. **The Temple site** is occupied by the Dome of the Rock; the traditional site of Abraham's sacrifice of Isaac. He reports, on the authority of a conversation with a priest he names as Fr. Seraphim, that plans for a rebuilt Temple exist and await a final authorization; that the red heifer required for the first sacrifice has been identified; and that a priestly line descended from Aaron (identifiable by the surname Cohen) has been located.
3. **The Temple will be rebuilt**, he argues, because Scripture requires it: the Antichrist "will be worshipped in the temple" ([2 Thessalonians 2:4](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=4)). He notes that Christ himself is the true Temple ([John 2:19–21](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=jn&g=2&p=19)) and that the Church has possessed that Temple for two thousand years, yet he still expects a literal rebuilding.
4. **The Antichrist will be received as the Jewish Messiah** — "some say he will look like Jesus, but not be of the truth" (≈ 40:50).

Steps 1 and 4 are theological opinion, and step 4 in particular requires great care. The talk itself, later, concedes the point of authority: "some Church Fathers say the Antichrist will be Jewish. It's not a tenet of the faith... this part is not scriptural. This is more tradition" (≈ 1:01:55). That is the correct grading, and it is to the speaker's credit. But it is worth stating plainly, because the claim has a long and poisonous history in Christian preaching: the Church has explicitly repudiated the notion that the Jewish people as such are accursed or rejected by God ([Nostra Aetate 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033); [CCC 597](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/597), which warns that "the Jews should not be presented as rejected or accursed by God"). A speculative opinion about the Antichrist's ethnicity must never be preached as though it licensed suspicion of Jews, and it is notable that the same Bible that the talk invokes for Israel's restoration also insists that "all Israel will be saved" ([Romans 11:26](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ro&g=11&p=26)) — the sign of hope, not of reproach.

### Why the nations rage against Israel

The talk's most theologically interesting construction is its answer to the question of why so many nations have attempted the destruction of the Jews — Assyrians, Babylonians, Canaanites, Muslims, Turks, Russians, Nazis. His answer: Satan wants to destroy them in order to prove God a liar, since God promised Abraham that his descendants would be as the sands of the seashore and the stars of the sky ([Genesis 22:17](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=gn&g=22&p=17)) (≈ 41:35–42:41).

This is a genuine theological insight and it is not his invention: it is essentially the argument of the serpent's strategy throughout Scripture, that God's word can be falsified by the destruction of the people to whom it was sworn, and it echoes the reasoning of [Exodus 32:12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ex&g=32&p=12) and [Deuteronomy 9:28](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=dt&g=9&p=28), where Moses pleads with God precisely on the ground that the Egyptians would say he could not bring the people in. Read this way, the survival of Israel is a witness to God's fidelity rather than a provocation. That is a far better use of the material than the more common apocalyptic reading.

### Nuremberg, Purim, and the ten sons of Haman

Fr. Alar reports that Hitler had ten henchmen dedicated to the destruction of the Jews; that the last of them, before being hanged, shouted a word nobody understood; and that a rabbi present explained that it was the name of Purim, the feast commemorating the deliverance narrated in the Book of Esther (≈ 42:41–44:42). In Esther, Haman has ten sons and plots the destruction of the Jews, and the plot is foiled.

The historical core here is real but the account is garbled, and the corrections table sets out the details. In brief: at Nuremberg twelve defendants were sentenced to death, ten were actually hanged on 16 October 1946 (Göring having taken poison the night before, and Bormann having been tried in absentia), and it was **Julius Streicher** — not "the last henchman" — whose reported final words included a shout of "Purimfest 1946!" alongside curses on the Bolsheviks and a final "Heil Hitler." Streicher was the movement's chief antisemitic propagandist, which makes the report both credible in origin and grimly ironic in substance.

The number ten and the Purim invocation are documented. What is **not** documented is a providential correspondence: Purim in 1946 fell in March, the executions took place in October, and the parallel with the ten sons of Haman is a devotional reading of a reported utterance, not a fulfillment of prophecy. It should be presented as a striking historical coincidence with theological resonance — nothing more.

## 6. "Elements of the second coming": confusion, fear, and the Whore of Babylon

The final major section, which he credits to the writer he calls Robert Haddad, is a sustained exegetical response to the claim — ubiquitous in fundamentalist polemic — that the Catholic Church is the Whore of Babylon and the Pope the Antichrist. He says he has "researched this top to bottom," and he offers Haddad's framework as the most coherent he has found (≈ 49:06).

### Confusion and fear among the nations

Ground: [Luke 21:25–26](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=lk&g=21&p=25) — "signs in sun and moon and stars, and on the earth distress of nations in perplexity... men fainting with fear and with foreboding of what is coming on the world."

He applies the text to the present: economic crisis, debt, civil unrest, violence, and — his emphasis — **confusion**, which he illustrates principally from the contested questions of marriage and gender identity. The passage on this point is the talk's weakest: it moves from a scriptural text to a cultural complaint with no theological argument between them, and it includes a jarring aside ("I'm a guy. If you can't tell, that's just who I am. That's who God made me," ≈ 47:26). A Catholic treatment of the confusion the Lord foretold would want to add that confusion is also a judgment visited on a culture that has rejected a coherent anthropology, and that the response to it is not contempt but the recovery of [CCC 369–373](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/369) and the pastoral patience the same Catechism requires ([CCC 2358](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2358)).

### The beast with seven heads

He reads [Revelation 17:9–11](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=17&p=9) closely:

> "This calls for a mind with wisdom: the seven heads are seven hills on which the woman is seated; they are also seven kings, five of whom have fallen, one is, the other has not yet come, and when he comes he must remain only a little while. As for the beast that was and is not, it is an eighth but it belongs to the seven, and it goes to perdition."

His identification: **the beast is first-century pagan Rome** — the city on seven hills, ruled by a succession of Caesars who persecuted Christians for refusing to worship them, one of whom was **Nero**, whose name and title in Hebrew letters yields 666, and under whom Peter and Paul were martyred. He notes that the text itself demands wisdom to interpret, and that "it requires wisdom" is the reason a symbolic count should not be treated as a cipher for whatever ruler one happens to dislike.

This identification has the strongest traditional support of any reading: the seven hills are Rome's own signature, "Babylon" is a transparent first-century code ([1 Peter 5:13](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1pe&g=5&p=13)), and the Nero redivivus legend is attested in the period. It is legitimate Catholic exegesis, though the Church has defined no identification of the beast.

### The harlot and the beast

His second identification: **the harlot is apostate Jerusalem** of the first century, and her "riding" the beast signifies the alliance between Rome and the anti-Christian Jewish leadership that together procured Christ's death — an alliance he illustrates from [Luke 23:12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=lk&g=23&p=12), where Pilate and Herod "became friends" that day (≈ 53:44–55:12).

The exegetical warrant he offers is real: the harlot imagery of Revelation 17 draws heavily on the Old Testament's condemnation of Jerusalem as an unfaithful spouse ([Isaiah 1:21](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=is&g=1&p=21); [Jeremiah 2–3](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=jr&g=2&p=1); [Ezekiel 16](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ez&g=16&p=1) and [23](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ez&g=23&p=1)), and the city that "crucified the Lord" is the natural referent of a harlot who is condemned for the blood of prophets and saints.

He then draws the conclusion that is the point of the whole section: since [Revelation 18:24](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=18&p=24) says that in Babylon "was found the blood of prophets and of saints," and since the Catholic Church has not put the prophets and saints to death, the harlot cannot be the Catholic Church. He also notes [Revelation 11:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=11&p=8), where the city is called "Sodom and Egypt" — a designation applied to Jerusalem.

The argument is effective against the polemical use of the text. Two qualifications should be carried along with it. First, the harlot's identification is a contested exegetical question: many Catholic interpreters read Babylon as Rome throughout, with the harlot as the pagan imperial order (or as a symbol of worldly power generally), and the "apostate Jerusalem" reading, while defensible and well represented, is a reading rather than the Church's teaching. Second, the argument "the Church has never persecuted the prophets and the saints" is true of the Church as such — holy, without spot or wrinkle ([Ephesians 5:27](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=ep&g=5&p=27)) — but not of every one of her members, and the distinction (which the talk does make elsewhere, at ≈ 1:12:10: "the church is human and divine and in her human nature can fail") should be kept visible when the argument is deployed, so that it does not become a claim of institutional sinlessness in matters of fact.

He then completes the reading with [Revelation 17:16–17](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=17&p=16): the ten horns and the beast "will hate the harlot... make her desolate and naked, devour her flesh and burn her with fire." Historically, he argues, this is the Roman turn against Jerusalem in AD 66–70, the destruction of the city, and the flight of the Christians (≈ 56:51–59:19). The AD 70 referent for much of Revelation is a mainstream reading, and the detail that the Church fled to Pella is preserved in the early tradition.

### The one still to come

The section closes with the elements the talk has been saving: the beast and the harlot may be **past** figures, but the **Antichrist is still to come**. Grounds: [2 Thessalonians 2:3–4](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=3) (he will oppose and exalt himself, proclaiming himself God); [1 John 2:22](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1jn&g=2&p=22) (whoever denies that Jesus is the Christ); [2 John 7](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2jn&g=1&p=7) (the deceiver who denies Jesus coming in the flesh); [2 Thessalonians 2:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=8) (the Lord will destroy him "by the breath of his mouth"). He observes, well, that the God who breathed life into man will destroy his adversary by that same breath.

He also states [CCC 677](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/677) as though it said the Church "will be persecuted to the point of extinction." That is a paraphrase, and a strong one: the Catechism's actual claim is that the Church "will enter the glory of the kingdom only through this final Passover," and that the kingdom will be fulfilled "not by a historic triumph of the Church through a progressive ascendancy, but only by God's victory over the final unleashing of evil." The substance — that the Church's victory is paschal, not political — is exactly right and is worth stating in the Catechism's own words.

The section ends with a pastoral pivot that rescues the whole: "the end of the world for thousands will end today with natural death. So focus on the time you have left on this earth... What matters is did you love God above all else and the neighbor as yourself. If you do that, you have nothing to fear" (≈ 1:02:07–1:02:27).

## 7. The refutation of the secret rapture

Fr. Alar's argument against the pre-tribulation rapture is a single, clean logical point, and it is the strongest doctrinal work in the talk.

**The premise (as he states it):** the dispensationalist scheme requires Christ to come twice after his first coming — once secretly, to remove believers before the tribulation and (in some versions) to inaugurate the millennium, and once publicly, in glory, at the end. He works through the difficulty in real time, correcting himself twice about the order of the two comings, and lands on the telling formulation: "that would be two second comings of Christ, one secretly, one public. But the Bible tells us there's only one second coming" (≈ 1:04:04–1:04:43).

**The grounds:**

- [1 Thessalonians 4:15–17](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1th&g=4&p=15), the proof-text of the rapture, says that the dead in Christ rise first and that "we who are alive... shall be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air." Fr. Alar does not deny the text; he denies the placement. The meeting in the air belongs to the one public coming.
- [Revelation 1:7](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=1&p=7): "Behold, he is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see him." The coming is not secret.
- [Matthew 24:30](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mt&g=24&p=30): the sign of the Son of Man, the mourning of all the tribes of the earth, the Son of Man coming on the clouds with power and great glory.
- [2 Thessalonians 2:8](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2th&g=2&p=8): Christ's coming destroys the Antichrist — therefore he must come **after** the Antichrist, not before him.

**The conclusion** he draws is the correct one and he states it precisely: "There will be a rapture of sorts — we don't use the word — but not seven years before the end of the world" (≈ 1:07:38). Christians living at the Lord's return will be gathered with those who have died in Christ to be with him forever, and this gathering happens **at** the one public and glorious coming, not before it, and it precedes the general judgment and the end of the world.

This is continuous with the Church's faith as professed in the Creed — "he will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead" — and with [CCC 675–677](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/675). The one thing to add is the point the Catechism makes and the talk does not: the return is **unpredictable** ("like a thief in the night," [1 Thessalonians 5:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1th&g=5&p=2)), and the sequence of signs does not license a calendar. The talk is generally careful here; the "seven years" and "three and a half years" it retains from the dispensationalist frame are themselves part of the scheme it is refuting, and they sit oddly with the refusal to date the end.

## 8. Amillennialism: the thousand years are the age of the Church

The talk's most precise doctrinal statement comes near the end, and it is correct.

> "Millennialism... says that Jesus will establish a throne in this world and reign for a thousand years. But this isn't true." (≈ 1:09:39–1:10:09)

He grounds the rejection in [1 Corinthians 15:24](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1co&g=15&p=24) (Christ hands over the kingdom to the Father after destroying every rule and authority and power), in [CCC 668–669](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/668), and in [CCC 676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/676), which rejects even "modified forms" of millenarianism under the name condemned by the Holy Office ([DS 3839](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/3839)).

His positive reading: the "thousand years" of [Revelation 20](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=20&p=1) is the era of the Church between the first and second comings; "a thousand" in the Hebrew idiom signifies a long time, not a literal millennium; and Revelation is not arranged in chronological order, so its visions are not a timetable. He then offers a suggestive historical illustration — that the period of the Church's true authority from Constantine to Leo XIII was "just over a thousand years," during which Satan was bound, and he was then loosed again (≈ 1:12:56). He is careful to mark the illustration as such, and it should be so marked: it is a homiletic application, not exegesis, and it does not have the standing of the doctrine it illustrates.

The theological spine of the passage is worth isolating, because it is the answer to the whole talk's central question. If Christ reigns **now**, in the Church, then his coming is not the inauguration of his reign but its unveiling; and therefore every scheme that postpones the Kingdom to a future earthly millennium has mislocated it. This is why the Church is amillennial, and it is why the talk's own emphasis on [CCC 670](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/670) at the beginning and on [CCC 676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/676) at the end is a single argument.

The one place where he wobbles is the description of the Church: "the church is human and divine and in her human nature can fail. In her divine nature, she's perfect. Gates of hell will not prevail against her" (≈ 1:12:10). The instinct is right, and it echoes the "real but imperfect sanctity" of [CCC 670](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/670) and _Lumen Gentium_ 48. But the formulation is loose: the Church is not "divine by nature" — she is a creature, the Body of Christ, holy with a holiness received. A more exact statement is that her members can and do sin, while her indefectibility in faith and her possession of the means of grace are guaranteed by her Head ([CCC 869](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/869)).

## 9. Satan loosed: the Leo XIII vision and the "hundred years"

The closing argument is that Scripture foretells a release of Satan after his binding ([Revelation 20:7](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rv&g=20&p=7)), and that we may be living in that release.

Fr. Alar recounts the well-known story that Pope Leo XIII, in 1884, overheard a conversation between Christ and Satan in which Satan claimed he could destroy the Church if given more time and more power, and was granted "about one hundred years"; and that the Pope thereafter composed the Prayer to St. Michael and ordered the Leonine prayers after Mass (≈ 1:13:35–1:14:10). He then weighs two possible starting points for the century — 1884 itself, or 1917, the year of Fátima, the First World War, the Bolshevik seizure of Russia, and Margaret Sanger's first birth-control clinic — and declines to decide, offering 1917 as the more plausible.

This material needs four clarifications:

1. **The vision is not documented history.** It rests on later testimony about Leo XIII's experience; the precise content (the dialogue, the "hundred years") does not appear in a contemporary source. The Prayer to St. Michael and the Leonine prayers are, by contrast, entirely real and remain in the Church's treasury (see [saints/st-michael-archangel.md](../saints/st-michael-archangel.md)). The story should be told as a pious tradition, not as an archival fact.
2. **No hundred-year period can be dated.** Even if the tradition were secure, a century measured from 1884 or 1917 has long since elapsed, which is a further reason to treat the "hundred years" as a devotional figure rather than a countdown. The talk does not draw this conclusion, and it should.
3. **The 1917 convergence is real and worth noting as a historical observation**: the First World War, the Russian Revolution, and the beginning of organized contraception do fall in that year, and Fátima falls in it too. But the Church does not read history by convergence, and the same year could be assembled from almost any decade with equal ingenuity.
4. **The Pius XII quotation** — that mankind in the 1950s was more sinful than at the time of the Flood (≈ 1:14:49) — is widely repeated in this literature but is not securely documented; it should be cited, if at all, with that caveat.

He ends where the talk should end: "the answer, God will never be outdone, was Fatima" (≈ 1:15:30). Whatever one makes of the chronology, that is the right proportion — the remedy precedes and exceeds the chastisement, and the last word in Catholic eschatology is not the darkness but the triumph of grace.

## 10. Conclusion and the pastoral answer

Fr. Alar closes with an explicit refusal of the gloomy reading a hearer might take away:

> "Father, you gave us all this gloom and doom. No, it's not gloom and doom... if you are living in a state of grace, following Christ through his church... following the sacraments, which are his guaranteed way of grace, you should be excited." (≈ 1:16:18–1:16:48)

The practical content of the answer, gathered from across the talk, is:

- **Live in a state of grace.** This is the recurring refrain and the one the talk returns to when the speculation has run its course.
- **Go to Confession** — the ordinary means of restoration, and the answer to the "illumination of conscience" that most of the middle section describes.
- **Be confirmed**, if one has not been. Whatever the standing of the claim that only the confirmed will endure, the sacramental exhortation is sound and the grace is real.
- **Pray the Rosary and practice penance**, as Our Lady asked at Fátima — the conditional warnings of Fátima being the frame within which all of this private revelation is properly read.
- **Trust Divine Mercy**: the talk's answer to the darkness is that Christ comes first as King of Mercy before he comes as just Judge (St. Faustina, _Diary_ 83), and that "pray, hope and do not worry" (his rendering of Padre Pio's counsel) is the correct disposition.
- **Do not be afraid.** He quotes the Lord's own rebuke of fear and turns the whole subject toward hope: the end of the world, for each person, is the moment of entering the ultimate home.

He then announces Part 2 — the Four Last Things, death, judgment, heaven, and hell — and, at 1:18:13, a livestream with Jonathan Roumie of _The Chosen_ on 30 July. The video's final five minutes (1:19:29–1:24:14) are a promotional feature for the Association of Marian Helpers, including St. Faustina's 1936 vision of a future association of laypeople devoted to Divine Mercy, six reasons for membership, and contact details. That segment is institutional advertising rather than catechesis and is noted here only for completeness.

## What the talk does not cover

For a balanced reading, note what Part 1 leaves out and what Part 2 was to supply:

- **The Four Last Things.** Death, judgment, heaven, and hell are named as the subject of Part 2 and are not treated here. For the doctrine, see [doctrinal-foundations.md](doctrinal-foundations.md); for purgatory, [purgatory.md](purgatory.md); for hell, [hell-private-revelations.md](hell-private-revelations.md).
- **The particular judgment and the intermediate state.** The talk is almost entirely about the _general_ eschatology of the end of the world, and says nothing about what happens to the individual soul at death — which is, for every person, the actual end of the world.
- **The resurrection of the body**, which the Creed professes and which is the content of Christian hope ([CCC 988–1019](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/988)). It is not mentioned.
- **The Eastern Catholic perspective.** The talk is entirely Latin in its sources and idiom. The Byzantine tradition's eschatology — the _Parousia_ as the manifestation of the transfigured cosmos, the emphasis on _theosis_, the Dormition and the dread judgment — is absent. See [church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md).
- **The 2024 Dicastery norms** on the discernment of alleged supernatural phenomena, which postdate the talk by four years and which materially change how the Medjugorje material should be presented.
- **The unapproved status of the _Book of Truth_**, which is the principal contemporary vehicle of the "Warning" and "three days of darkness" motifs in popular circulation, and which several bishops have judged contrary to the faith. See [book-of-truth/README.md](../book-of-truth/README.md).

## Pastoral cautions

1. **Do not date the end, and do not let the signs become a calendar.** The talk is generally careful, but its retention of the "seven years" and the "three and a half years" from the very scheme it refutes invites date-setting. The Church forbids it: the day comes "like a thief in the night," and even modified millenarianism "cannot be safely taught" ([DS 3839](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/3839); [CCC 676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/676)).
2. **Do not treat private revelation as doctrine, and do not let it eclipse the Gospel.** St. Faustina, Bl. Anna Maria Taigi, and Bl. Elisabetta Canori Mora are trustworthy witnesses, and the Church has approved devotion to them; but [CCC 67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67) is explicit that private revelation does not add to or surpass the deposit of faith, and belief in it is not required. The speaker says this himself, and a reader should hold him to it.
3. **Refuse antisemitism absolutely.** The material on the Jewish people in §5, whatever its intent, is the kind that has been used to justify contempt and violence. The Church has closed that door: the Jews are not "rejected or accursed by God" ([Nostra Aetate 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033); [CCC 597](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/597)), and the Abrahamic promise the talk invokes is a promise of mercy, not a warrant for suspicion. Any use of prophecy to sow hostility toward Jews is a betrayal of the Gospel.
4. **Do not let the darkness displace the mercy.** The sequence of the talk is significant: it ends with Divine Mercy, not with the three days of darkness. The Church's own order is the same — the warnings are conditional, the chastisement is avoidable by repentance, and the last word belongs to grace.
5. **Beware of superstition in the sacramentals.** Blessed candles are sacramentals, and their power is the power of the Church's prayer ([CCC 1667–1673](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1667)). They are not talismans, and the "closed door" of the private revelations is not a mechanism. The talk's own instinct — get them blessed, and do not worry about the wax — is the right one.
6. **Remember that the end comes first for each of us.** The talk says it well and it bears repeating: "the end of the world for thousands will end today with natural death." The doctrine of the Last Things is a doctrine of vigilance, and vigilance means confession, charity, and perseverance — not the decoding of prophecy.
