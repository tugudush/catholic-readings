# Plenary Indulgences for the Holy Souls in November

## A Source Verification of the EWTN Vatican Guide for All Saints' to All Souls' (2026)

Each year at the turn of November, Catholic media circulate a short guide to the **plenary indulgences available for the souls in Purgatory from 1 to 8 November**. The version examined here is the **EWTN Vatican** post circulated on 31 October 2026, whose text is summarised in § 1 below. It is a serviceable and substantially accurate explainer — unusually so for social media — but it is also **incomplete in four respects that matter pastorally**, and one of its sentences is loose enough to mislead.

This document verifies the post clause by clause against the governing law, quotes the norm it is summarising, and supplies what it leaves out.

**Authority note.** EWTN is a private Catholic media apostolate, not a dicastery of the Roman Curia. Its explainers are **catechetical aids**, not acts of the Magisterium, and carry no more weight than the accuracy of their citations. The binding norms are those of the **_Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_** (4th edition, 1999) — the _Normae_ and _Concessiones_ — together with the _Code of Canon Law_ (cc. 992–997) and the Catechism ([CCC 1471–1479](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1471)). The doctrinal foundation of Purgatory, temporal punishment, and suffrage is set out in [purgatory.md](purgatory.md).

---

## 1. What the post claims

The post makes the following assertions, in its own order:

1. During the first eight days of November the Church offers "a special opportunity" to obtain **plenary indulgences** for the souls in Purgatory.
2. The **indulgenced acts** are: a devout visit to a **cemetery** with silent prayer for the deceased, on **1–8 November**; or, on **2 November (All Souls' Day)**, a visit to a **church or oratory** with the **Our Father** and the **Apostles' Creed**.
3. The **usual conditions** are: sacramental **Confession**, **Holy Communion**, **prayer for the intentions of the Holy Father** ("an Our Father and a Hail Mary are recommended"), the **intention** of gaining the indulgence, and **complete detachment from all sin, including venial sin**.
4. **One** sacramental Confession "can suffice for several plenary indulgences", but **Communion and the papal prayer must be repeated for each**.
5. Confession, Communion, and the papal prayer "may be completed several days before or after the indulgenced act", though "Communion and prayer for the Pope's intentions should take place on the same day".
6. If all the conditions are not fulfilled, "the indulgence will be **partial** rather than plenary".
7. It quotes [CCC 1471](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1471) on the remission of temporal punishment, and adds that "indulgences can be applied to the living or to the faithful departed".

Every one of these statements is defensible against the current norms. The problems lie in what is **not said**.

## 2. The governing grant: _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_, Concessio 29

The post is summarising a single entry of the _Enchiridion_ — **Concessiones 29, "Pro fidelibus defunctis"**. Its full text should be seen, because two of its provisions never reach the popular guides: the **transfer** of the All Souls' church visit, and the **partial** grants of § 2.

> **§ 1.** Plenaria indulgentia, animabus in Purgatorio detentis tantummodo applicabilis, conceditur christifideli qui 1° singulis diebus, a primo usque ad octavum novembris, coemeterium devote visitaverit et, vel mente tantum, pro defunctis exoraverit; 2° die Commemorationis omnium fidelium defunctorum (vel, de consensu Ordinarii, die Dominico antecedenti aut subsequenti aut die sollemnitatis Omnium Sanctorum) ecclesiam aut oratorium pie visitaverit ibique recitaverit Pater et Credo.
>
> **§ 2.** Partialis indulgentia, animabus in Purgatorio detentis tantummodo applicabilis, conceditur christifideli qui, 1° coemeterium devote visitaverit et, vel mente tantum, pro defunctis exoraverit; 2° Laudes vel Vesperas Officii defunctorum, vel invocationem _Requiem aeternam_ devote recitaverit.

In English:

> **§ 1.** A plenary indulgence, **applicable only to the souls detained in Purgatory**, is granted to the Christian faithful who (1) **on each day from the first to the eighth of November** devoutly visit a cemetery and pray for the dead, **even if only mentally**; or (2) **on the day of the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed** — or, **with the consent of the Ordinary**, on the preceding or following Sunday or on the solemnity of All Saints — piously visit a church or oratory and there recite the **Our Father and the Creed**.
>
> **§ 2.** A partial indulgence, **applicable only to the souls detained in Purgatory**, is granted to the Christian faithful who (1) devoutly visit a cemetery and pray for the dead, even if only mentally; (2) devoutly recite Lauds or Vespers of the Office of the Dead, or the invocation _Requiem aeternam_.

The grants therefore number five, not two:

| Grant       | Work                                                            | When                                                                            | May be applied to |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Plenary** | Devout cemetery visit with prayer for the dead                  | Each day, 1–8 November                                                          | The dead only     |
| **Plenary** | Church or oratory visit, with Our Father and Creed              | 2 November (or, with the Ordinary's consent, All Saints or the adjacent Sunday) | The dead only     |
| **Partial** | Devout cemetery visit with prayer for the dead                  | Any day of the year                                                             | The dead only     |
| **Partial** | Devout recitation of Lauds or Vespers of the Office of the Dead | Any day                                                                         | The dead only     |
| **Partial** | Devout invocation _Requiem aeternam dona eis, Domine_           | Any day                                                                         | The dead only     |

**Historical note.** The eight-day window is the survivor of the former **octave of All Saints** (1–8 November), suppressed as an octave in the calendar reforms of 1955 and 1969. The Church kept the period for this grant because the whole octave had long been given to suffrage for the dead. Older devotional literature also reports local _toties quoties_ privileges on All Souls' Day; the universal grant is the one in Concessio 29, and the universal grant is what the faithful should follow.

## 4. What the post leaves out

### 4.1 These two plenary grants cannot be applied to oneself

This is the single most consequential omission. The post's closing line — "Indulgences can be applied to the living or to the faithful departed" — is true of indulgences in general ([Normae 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Normae); [can. 994](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib-iv-cann_992-997_sacramentals_en.html)), but **Concessio 29 disposes otherwise, and says so twice**: both of its plenary grants are for the faithful who act "animabus in Purgatorio detentis **tantummodo** applicabilis" — applicable **only** to the souls detained in Purgatory. A Catholic who keeps the cemetery octave in order to shorten **his own** purification has simply misunderstood the grant. For oneself, the ordinary plenary works of the _Enchiridion_ apply — Eucharistic adoration for half an hour, the Stations of the Cross, the Rosary in a church or family, half an hour's Scripture reading, and the rest (see the table in [purgatory.md § 6.4](purgatory.md#6-reducing-temporal-punishment-while-still-living)).

### 4.2 Only one plenary indulgence can be gained each day

The post never mentions [**Normae 6**](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Normae): "Indulgentia plenaria **semel tantum in die** acquiri potest." This matters acutely on **2 November**, when two plenary grants overlap: the cemetery visit (1–8 November) and the church or oratory visit. A person who gains the church-visit plenary on All Souls' Day cannot gain a **second** plenary the same day by going to the cemetery; the second indulgenced work is commonly understood to yield a **partial** indulgence only. In practice, therefore, one chooses which plenary to gain on 2 November and takes the other work as a partial — or spreads the two across the octave, since the cemetery visit is available every day from the first to the eighth.

### 4.3 Purgatory is not measured in earthly time

"The remission of all temporal punishment" is a true statement about what the Church asks God to grant; it is **not** a promise that a soul departs Purgatory on a given calendar day. The days-and-years arithmetic of pre-1967 indulgences was abolished by Pope St. Paul VI in _Indulgentiarum Doctrina_ (1967), precisely so that the faithful would stop reckoning indulgences as time-served. Purgatory is a state of purification, not a prison term measured by clocks; the Church's teaching on this is set out in [purgatory.md § 7](purgatory.md#7-time-in-purgatory--a-necessary-clarification).

### 4.4 The Mass remains the pre-eminent suffrage

Indulgences are a subordinate instrument. The Council of Trent defined that "the souls there detained are helped by the suffrages of the faithful, **but principally by the acceptable sacrifice of the altar**" (Session XXV, [DH 1820](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1820); cf. [CCC 1032](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1032), [1371](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1371)). A November guide that mentions indulgences twenty times and the Mass not at all inverts the Church's own order of values. Have a Mass offered for the dead — the practice and its canonical framework are in [Mass Intentions](../liturgy/mass-intentions.md) — and let the octave's indulgences be added to it.

### 4.5 The partial grants that remain all year

Concessio 29 § 2 is not confined to November. A devout cemetery visit with prayer for the dead carries a **partial** indulgence on **any** day; so does the devout recitation of **Lauds or Vespers of the Office of the Dead**, and so does the invocation:

    Requiem aeternam dona eis, Domine,
    et lux perpetua luceat eis.
    Requiescant in pace. Amen.

This is the formula the Church herself attaches the indulgence to — not the whole of Psalm 130 as such. On that frequent confusion, and on the older "partial indulgence" note printed beside the _De Profundis_, see [the Novena for the Poor Souls § 6.2](../liturgy/novena-poor-souls-in-purgatory.md#62-the-de-profundis-and-its-partial-indulgence).

## 5. The conditions, precisely

The _Normae_ state the requirements far more compactly than the catechetical tradition that has grown around them. The operative texts:

- **Normae 7.** To gain a plenary indulgence there is required the **performance of the indulgenced work** and the fulfilment of **three conditions**: sacramental confession, Eucharistic communion, and prayer for the intentions of the Supreme Pontiff. There is required in addition the **exclusion of all affection toward any sin whatsoever, even venial**. If this full disposition is lacking, or the conditions are not met (apart from the provision for the "impeded", Normae 11), **the indulgence will be only partial**.
- **Normae 8.** The three conditions may be fulfilled **several days before or after** the performance of the prescribed work; it is fitting, however, that Communion and the prayer for the Pope's intention be performed **on the day the work is done**. (The widely repeated pastoral figure of "about twenty days" is a custom of confessors, not the wording of the 1999 norms.)
- **Normae 9.** **One** sacramental confession can serve for **several** plenary indulgences; but one Eucharistic communion and one prayer for the Pope's intention gain **only one** plenary indulgence.
- **Normae 10.** The condition of praying for the Pope's intention is **fully satisfied** by reciting **one Our Father and one Hail Mary**; each of the faithful remains free to say any other prayer according to his own devotion toward the Roman Pontiff.
- **Normae 11.** For those genuinely "impeded" — living where access to confession or Communion is impossible or very difficult — the local Ordinary may permit a plenary indulgence without actual confession and Communion, provided the person is contrite of heart and intends to approach the sacraments as soon as possible.

Two pastoral corollaries follow. First, the **state of grace** is presupposed: Eucharistic Communion is itself impossible for one in grave sin, so the octave is a standing invitation to Confession — the Sacrament treated in [penance-reconciliation.md](../sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md) and, practically, [order-of-confession.md](../sacraments/order-of-confession.md). Second, **detachment, not sinlessness, is the interior condition**. The scrupulous should take comfort: the norm excludes the **affection** to sin, and the honest soul who detests his venial faults while committing them is not thereby excluded from the grant.

## 6. Why an indulgence can help the dead at all

The post assumes the answer; the doctrine deserves stating.

Indulgences rest on the **communion of saints**. Christ's expiation is infinite; the satisfactions of the Blessed Virgin and the saints are superabundant; and the Church, as "minister of redemption", dispenses this **treasury of satisfactions** by the power of the keys ([CCC 1474–1477](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1474); Paul VI, _Indulgentiarum Doctrina_ 5; [can. 992](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib-iv-cann_992-997_sacramentals_en.html)). Because the faithful departed "are still members of the same communion of saints", the living may help them by obtaining indulgences for them, "so that the temporal punishments due for their sins may be remitted" ([CCC 1479](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1479); cf. [CCC 958](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/958)).

But the Church draws a careful distinction that the phrase "applied to the dead" can obscure:

| Aspect              | Indulgence for the living                                                                                                                                                                                              | Indulgence for the dead                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mode of application | By the Church's **jurisdiction** — an act of authority over her subjects                                                                                                                                               | **Per modum suffragii** — by way of **suffrage**, entreaty                                                                                  |
| Certainty of effect | The Church's authority binds                                                                                                                                                                                           | Commended to God's mercy; not a jurisdictional claim                                                                                        |
| Normative text      | [Normae 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Normae); [can. 994](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib-iv-cann_992-997_sacramentals_en.html) | Same texts; Trent's "suffrages of the faithful" ([DH 1820](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1820)) |

The Church's jurisdiction does not reach beyond death, and so she does not **command** the release of a soul; she **entreats**, offering the treasure of Christ's satisfactions on the soul's behalf. The doctrine is certain ([CCC 1032](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1032); [2 Maccabees 12:46](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=2ma&g=12&p=46)); the measure of its effect in any particular case rests with God. This is why the Church's language about the dead is always "may they rest in peace", never "they are now released".

## 8. Keeping the octave well

In **2026**, 31 October falls on a Saturday, so the octave runs:

| Date         | Day        | What the Church grants that day                                                                                                                        |
| ------------ | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 November   | **Sunday** | All Saints. Plenary for a cemetery visit with prayer for the dead; partial for the Office of the Dead or _Requiem aeternam_                            |
| 2 November   | Monday     | All Souls. **Either** the church-visit plenary (Our Father and Creed) **or** the cemetery plenary — not both, since only one plenary is gained per day |
| 3–8 November | Tue–Sun    | A cemetery plenary each day, one per day                                                                                                               |

A practical order for the week:

1. **Go to Confession** on or before the first day of the octave — one confession covers the whole week.
2. **Attend Mass and receive Communion** on each day you intend to gain a plenary indulgence, praying there one Our Father and one Hail Mary for the Holy Father's intentions. This is the simplest way to satisfy both day-specific conditions at once.
3. **Visit a cemetery** on each of the eight days if possible — a family grave, a parish plot, or any cemetery. At the graveside, pray for the dead. A spoken formula is not required; the _Requiem aeternam_ is the traditional one, and prayer "even only mentally" suffices.
4. **On 2 November**, choose deliberately: the church or oratory visit with the Creed, or the cemetery.
5. **Have Mass offered** for the souls you are praying for. The indulgence is an addition to the Church's principal suffrage, not a substitute for it.
6. **Remember the dead beyond the octave.** The partial grants of Concessio 29 § 2 remain all year, and the commemoration of the dead is not a season but a habit of the communion of saints — see [prayers-for-the-dead.md](../liturgy/prayers-for-the-dead.md) and the [Novena for the Poor Souls](../liturgy/novena-poor-souls-in-purgatory.md).

One caution belongs with any discussion of indulgences: the Church's grants are **gifts to be received**, not mechanisms to be worked, and nothing in Concessio 29 obliges anyone under pain of sin. The _Directory on Popular Piety_ warns explicitly that visits to cemeteries should be seen as "deriving from the bonds existing between the living and the dead and **not from any form of obligation, non-fulfilment of which involves a superstitious fear**" ([Directory on Popular Piety 260](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/260)).

## 9. The Eastern Catholic perspective

Purgatory, temporal punishment, and the efficacy of suffrage for the dead are **doctrine binding on all twenty-four Churches**; the **indulgence discipline**, however, is a Western canonical institution. The Eastern Catholic Churches in communion with Rome do not administer indulgences, and their faithful are not bound by Concessio 29 — but they are not without the reality the indulgence signifies. The Divine Liturgy offered for the departed, the **Panikhida**, the **third-, ninth-, and fortieth-day** memorials, the **Soul Saturdays** (_Zadušnice_, _Hramoty_) and the offering of **koliva** are the Eastern Catholic form of the same communion of saints, and Latin-rite Catholics may share in them without scruple. The comparative treatment is in [purgatory.md § 9](purgatory.md#9-the-eastern-catholic-perspective) and, for the theology of the Eastern Churches generally, [eastern-catholic-churches.md](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md).
