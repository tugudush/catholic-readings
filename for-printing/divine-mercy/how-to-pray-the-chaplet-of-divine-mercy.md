# How to Pray the Chaplet of Divine Mercy

A printable companion for praying the Chaplet of Divine Mercy, with the order of the beads, the full text of every prayer, the promises our Lord attached to the devotion, and the hour at which He asked that it be prayed above all.

For the fuller study of this devotion — its origin in the revelations to St. Maria Faustina Kowalska, its doctrinal status, its comparison with the Holy Rosary, and the Eastern Catholic dimension — see [liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md](../../liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md). For the Hour of Great Mercy (3:00 p.m.) devotion of which this Chaplet is the heart, see [liturgy/hour-of-great-mercy.md](../../liturgy/hour-of-great-mercy.md). For Divine Mercy Sunday and the Divine Mercy Novena, see [liturgy/divine-mercy-feast-and-novena.md](../../liturgy/divine-mercy-feast-and-novena.md).

---

## Before You Begin

- **What you need:** a set of rosary beads — a standard five-decade Rosary. Any prayer beads may be used, provided they allow one large bead followed by ten small beads, five times over.
- **How long it takes:** roughly ten to twelve minutes when prayed at a steady pace.
- **Where to pray:** anywhere — at home, in a pew, in a chapel, beside a bed, on a journey. The Chaplet may also be prayed before the Blessed Sacrament.
- **When to pray:** at any hour and for any intention — for oneself, for a sick person, for a dying person, for sinners, or for the whole world.
- **An image of Divine Mercy**, signed _"Jesus, I trust in You,"_ may be placed where you pray; a candle is optional.

---

## The Beads at a Glance

The Chaplet is prayed on the ordinary beads of a five-decade Rosary, in a repeating pattern of one large bead followed by ten small beads, five times over.

| Part of the Rosary                     | What is prayed there              |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Crucifix                               | The Sign of the Cross             |
| First large bead                       | The Our Father                    |
| Next small bead                        | The Hail Mary                     |
| Before the first decade                | The Apostles' Creed               |
| Large bead of each of the five decades | The Eternal Father prayer         |
| Ten small beads of each decade         | The decade prayer, ten times      |
| After the fifth decade                 | The Closing Doxology, three times |

---

## The Order of Prayer, Step by Step

1. **Begin at the crucifix.** Make the **Sign of the Cross**.
2. **(Optional)** Recite the **Optional Opening Prayers** below — St. Faustina's Prayer for Sinners, the prayer _"You expired, Jesus,"_ and the invocation of the Blood and Water said three times.
3. **On the first large bead**, pray the **Our Father**.
4. **On the next small bead**, pray the **Hail Mary**.
5. **Before the first decade**, pray the **Apostles' Creed**.
6. **On the large bead of the first decade**, pray the **Eternal Father** prayer.
7. **On each of the ten small beads**, pray the **decade prayer** once.
8. **On the large bead of the second decade**, repeat the **Eternal Father** prayer, then the decade prayer ten times more. Repeat this pattern for the third, fourth and fifth decades.
9. **After the fifth decade**, recite the **Closing Doxology** three times.
10. **(Optional)** Recite the **Optional Closing Prayers**, then make the Sign of the Cross.

## The Prayers of the Chaplet

### The Sign of the Cross

    In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

### Optional Opening Prayers

**St. Faustina's Prayer for Sinners** (Diary, § 72)

    O Jesus, eternal Truth, our Life,
    I call upon You and I beg Your mercy for poor sinners.
    O sweetest Heart of my Lord, full of pity and unfathomable mercy,
    I plead with You for poor sinners.
    O Most Sacred Heart, Fount of Mercy
    from which gush forth rays of inconceivable graces upon the entire human race,
    I beg of You light for poor sinners.
    O Jesus, be mindful of Your own bitter Passion
    and do not permit the loss of souls
    redeemed at so dear a price of Your most precious Blood.
    O Jesus, when I consider the great price of Your Blood,
    I rejoice at its immensity,
    for one drop alone would have been enough for the salvation of all sinners.
    Although sin is an abyss of wickedness and ingratitude,
    the price paid for us can never be equalled.
    Therefore, let every soul trust in the Passion of the Lord,
    and place its hope in His mercy.
    God will not deny His mercy to anyone.
    Heaven and earth may change,
    but God's mercy will never be exhausted.
    Oh, what immense joy burns in my heart
    when I contemplate Your incomprehensible goodness, O Jesus!
    I desire to bring all sinners to Your feet
    that they may glorify Your mercy throughout endless ages.
    (Diary, § 72)

**Opening Prayer**

    You expired, Jesus, but the source of life gushed forth for souls,
    and the ocean of mercy opened up for the whole world.
    O Fount of Life, unfathomable Divine Mercy,
    envelop the whole world and empty Yourself out upon us.

**The Blood and the Water** _(to be repeated three times)_

    O Blood and Water, which gushed forth from the Heart of Jesus
    as a fount of mercy for us, I trust in You!

### The Our Father

    Our Father, who art in heaven,
    hallowed be Thy name;
    Thy kingdom come;
    Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven.
    Give us this day our daily bread;
    and forgive us our trespasses
    as we forgive those who trespass against us;
    and lead us not into temptation,
    but deliver us from evil. Amen.

### The Hail Mary

    Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee;
    blessed art thou among women,
    and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus.
    Holy Mary, Mother of God,
    pray for us sinners,
    now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

### The Apostles' Creed

    I believe in God, the Father almighty,
    Creator of heaven and earth,
    and in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord,
    who was conceived by the Holy Spirit,
    born of the Virgin Mary,
    suffered under Pontius Pilate,
    was crucified, died and was buried;
    He descended into hell;
    on the third day He rose again from the dead;
    He ascended into heaven,
    and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty;
    from there He will come to judge the living and the dead.
    I believe in the Holy Spirit,
    the holy catholic Church,
    the communion of saints,
    the forgiveness of sins,
    the resurrection of the body,
    and life everlasting. Amen.

### The Eternal Father Prayer _(on each large bead)_

    Eternal Father,
    I offer You the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity
    of Your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ,
    in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.

### The Decade Prayer _(on each of the ten small beads)_

    For the sake of His sorrowful Passion,
    have mercy on us and on the whole world.

### The Closing Doxology _(after the fifth decade)_

    Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One,
    have mercy on us and on the whole world.

_(This doxology is recited three times.)_

### Optional Closing Prayers

**Prayer of Entrustment**

    Eternal God, in whom mercy is endless
    and the treasury of compassion inexhaustible,
    look kindly upon us and increase Your mercy in us,
    that in difficult moments we might not despair nor become despondent,
    but with great confidence submit ourselves to Your holy will,
    which is Love and Mercy itself. Amen.

**Alternative Closing Prayer** (Diary, § 1570)

    O Greatly Merciful God, Infinite Goodness,
    today all mankind calls out from the abyss of its misery to Your mercy —
    to Your compassion, O God;
    and it is with its mighty voice of misery that it cries out.
    Gracious God, do not reject the prayer of this earth's exiles!
    O Lord, Goodness beyond our understanding,
    Who are acquainted with our misery through and through,
    and know that by our own power we cannot ascend to You,
    we implore You: anticipate us with Your grace
    and keep on increasing Your mercy in us,
    that we may faithfully do Your holy will
    all through our life and at death's hour.
    Let the omnipotence of Your mercy shield us
    from the darts of our salvation's enemies,
    that we may with confidence, as Your children,
    await Your final coming — that day known to You alone.
    And we expect to obtain everything promised us by Jesus
    in spite of all our wretchedness.
    For Jesus is our Hope:
    through His merciful Heart, as through an open gate,
    we pass through to Heaven.
    (Diary, § 1570)

## When to Pray the Chaplet

- **At any hour.** The Chaplet may be prayed at any time of the day or night, for any intention.
- **At the Hour of Mercy (3:00 p.m.).** Our Lord asked St. Faustina that the Chaplet be prayed especially at the hour of His death on the Cross, promising: _"In this hour you can obtain everything for yourself and for others for the asking; it was the hour of grace for the whole world — mercy triumphed over justice."_ (Diary, § 1572)
- **For the dying.** Our Lord promised that when the Chaplet is recited at the bedside of a dying person, His mercy envelops the soul and His anger is placated (Diary, § 1565, § 687).
- **On Divine Mercy Sunday** (the Second Sunday of Easter), the solemnity on which our Lord asked that the whole world be reminded of His mercy, and on which the Church has attached a plenary indulgence to the observance of the day.

---

## The Promises of Our Lord

> _"Say unceasingly the chaplet that I have taught you. Whoever will recite it will receive great mercy at the hour of death."_ (Diary, § 687)

> _"The souls that say this chaplet will be embraced by My mercy during their lifetime and especially at the hour of death."_ (Diary, § 754)

> _"As often as you hear the clock strike the third hour, immerse yourself completely in My mercy, adoring and glorifying it; invoke its omnipotence for the whole world, and particularly for poor sinners; for at that moment mercy was opened wide for every soul. In this hour you can obtain everything for yourself and for others for the asking; it was the hour of grace for the whole world — mercy triumphed over justice."_ (Diary, § 1572)

The graces of the devotion are bound to **trust** in God's goodness and mercy, to **active love of neighbor** in the works of mercy, and to the **state of sanctifying grace** — the reception of the Sacraments of Confession and Holy Communion, especially worthily on Divine Mercy Sunday.

## Notes for the One Praying

- **The Chaplet is a form of reparation.** Its heart is the offering of the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of Jesus Christ to God the Father, in atonement for the sins of the whole world. The words are few; the offering is the whole work of the Passion.
- **Repetition is the point.** Ten times the same aspiration on the small beads is not tedium but trust, and each repetition is a separate petition for mercy. Pray it slowly enough to mean it.
- **The whole world is in every petition.** Both the Eternal Father prayer and the decade prayer name _"the whole world."_ Do not narrow them to a private need alone.
- **The beads may be held and turned as at the Rosary**, or simply laid on a table or held in the folded hands; the devotion does not depend on a particular way of counting.
- **The body may pray too.** A modest posture — kneeling, sitting, standing — may be kept, and a slight bow made on the large beads. None of this is prescribed.
- **The Chaplet may be prayed in community**, one voice leading and the assembly responding, and is customarily joined to the Rosary in many parishes.
- **The Hours of the Passion.** Our Lord asked that the Chaplet be accompanied by meditation on His Passion — the Stations of the Cross, adoration before the Blessed Sacrament, or a brief meditation on the wounds and the Blood — especially at 3:00 p.m.
- **An Eastern Catholic note.** The Chaplet is a Latin-Rite devotion, though it is widely loved in the Eastern Catholic Churches. Eastern Catholics may make the Sign of the Cross with the Eastern formula and add an Eastern-style introductory prayer or the Trisagion.
- **No particular formula is required.** The Chaplet is a devotion, not a sacrament; the Church commends it and grants indulgences attached to the Divine Mercy observance, but the praying of it is governed by custom and pastoral guidance rather than by divine law.
