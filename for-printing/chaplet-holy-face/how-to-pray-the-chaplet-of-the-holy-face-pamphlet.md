# How to Pray the Chaplet of the Holy Face of Jesus — The Beads, the Five Senses, and the Prayers

The Chaplet of the Holy Face of Jesus — called in its classic printed source the _Little Chaplet of the Holy Face_ — is a Catholic devotional prayer of **reparation** offered in honour of the five senses of Our Lord Jesus Christ and for the triumph of His holy Church. It belongs to the devotion that arose at the Carmel of Tours, France, in the 1840s through the interior communications reported by Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre (Perrine Éluère, 1816–1848), and it is prayed on a small string of thirty-nine beads with a medal of the Holy Face. It is **popular piety**, not liturgy: it adds nothing to the one sacrifice of Calvary and never substitutes for the Mass.

## The Beads

The chaplet is not a Rosary and should not be confused with one. Its parts are:

- **A cross**, on which the opening versicle and the first _Gloria_ are said.
- **Six large beads**, one before each group of six and one before the final group of three.
- **Thirty-three small beads**, in honour of the thirty-three years of the Lord's mortal life: thirty recall His hidden life, arranged in five groups of six for the five senses of His Holy Face; three recall His public life and honour the wounds of His adorable Face.
- **A medal of the Holy Face**, on which the closing prayer is said.

The chaplet is often made of knotted brown cord by Carmelite communities. Any string of beads with the right count may be used; no enrolment or ceremony is required, and the Church attaches no power to the material object apart from the faith of the one who prays.

## Step by Step

1. **On the cross.** Make the Sign of the Cross; pray the opening versicle and the _Gloria_.
2. **On the first large bead**, in honour of the sense of touch, say _"My Jesus, mercy!"_ and the _Gloria_.
3. **On the next six small beads**, say six times: _"Arise, O Lord, and let Thy enemies be scattered, and let them that hate Thee flee from before Thy Face."_
4. **Repeat** steps 2 and 3 for hearing, sight, smell, and taste.
5. **On the sixth large bead**, in honour of Our Lord's three years of public life and the wounds of His Holy Face, say _"My Jesus, mercy!"_ and the _Gloria_.
6. **On the last three small beads**, say the "Arise, O Lord" verse three times.
7. **On the medal**, conclude with the closing prayer and make the Sign of the Cross.

The _Gloria_ is said seven times in all — once on the cross, and once after each of the six large beads — in honour of the seven words of Jesus upon the Cross and the seven sorrows of the Immaculate Virgin. Modern Carmelite usage names the sense aloud before the _"My Jesus, mercy!"_ ("In honour of the sense of touch of the Holy Face"); the 1887 _Manual_ leaves the intention implicit. Both forms are in use.

## The Prayers

### The Sign of the Cross

    In the name of the Father, and of the Son,
    and of the Holy Ghost. Amen.

### The Opening Versicle (on the cross)

    O God, come to my assistance;
    O Lord, make haste to help me.
    Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost.
    As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be,
    world without end. Amen.

The versicle is the same with which the Church opens the Liturgy of the Hours each day.

### On Each Large Bead

    My Jesus, mercy!

### On Each Small Bead

    Arise, O Lord,
    and let Thy enemies be scattered,
    and let them that hate Thee flee from before Thy Face.

This is Psalm 67:2 almost verbatim — "Let God arise, and let his enemies be scattered: and let them that hate him flee from before his face" — the verse the tradition recorded by the Carmelites holds that Saint Athanasius prized above every other for putting the demons to flight. It is said thirty-three times, once for each year of the Lord's life.

### On the Medal: The Closing Prayer

    Behold, O God our protector:
    and look on the face of thy Christ.

The prayer gathers the whole chaplet into one petition: it asks the Father to look upon the Face of His Son — the Face the chaplet has been honouring — and, for the sake of that Face, to look mercifully upon us.

### The Archconfraternity's Daily Invocation

    Lord, show us Thy Face, and we shall be saved.

Members of the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face were enrolled with a simple daily obligation: one _Our Father_, one _Hail Mary_, one _Glory Be_, and this invocation of Psalm 79:4 — an obligation the _Manual_ expressly says does not bind under pain of sin.

## The Five Senses of the Holy Face

The five groups of six beads invite a sustained meditation on what the Passion cost the Redeemer in each of His senses. Isaiah is the Church's own commentary on the Holy Face: "there is no beauty in him, nor comeliness… a man of sorrows" (Isaiah 53:2–3).

**1. Touch.** The hands laid upon the Face of Jesus: the blows, the buffeting, the crown of thorns pressed down. "Then did they spit in his face, and buffeted him: and others struck his face with the palms of their hands" (Matthew 26:67). _Reparation:_ for every blow of the hand and for the hardness of heart that strikes Christ again in His members.

**2. Hearing.** What the Sacred Ears heard: the blasphemies of the passers-by, the mockery of the priests and elders. "And they that passed by, blasphemed him, wagging their heads" (Matthew 27:39). _Reparation:_ for blasphemy, the sin the devotion was given to repair, and for every word that wounds the Name of God.

**3. Sight.** What the Eyes of Jesus saw: the spitting, the blindfolding, the mob, the sorrow of His Mother. "And some began to spit on him, and to cover his face, and to buffet him" (Mark 14:65). _Reparation:_ for every immodest look, and for the indifference with which men pass before the Face of Christ in the Blessed Sacrament.

**4. Smell.** What the Sacred Face endured on the hill of execution: the spittle, the dust, the blood. "My strength is dried up like a potsherd" (Psalm 21:15). _Reparation:_ for sensuality and every indulgence of the senses contrary to the law of God.

**5. Taste.** What the Mouth of Jesus tasted: the gall, the vinegar. "And they gave him wine to drink mingled with gall. And when he had tasted, he would not drink" (Matthew 27:34). _Reparation:_ for gluttony and for every refusal to receive the chalice of the Lord's will.

**The three final beads** recall the three years of the public life and honour all the wounds of the adorable Face: the crown of thorns, the spitting and buffeting, and the veil offered by the woman of Jerusalem on which, according to the Church's cherished tradition, the image of His Face was left imprinted — the devotion's link with Saint Veronica and the Sixth Station of the Cross.

## When to Pray the Chaplet

- **At any time and in any place**, alone or in common; no permission, enrolment, or ceremony is required, and the recommendation to recite it daily is counsel, not obligation.
- **Before the Blessed Sacrament**, where the Face the chaplet honours is present in the Most Holy Sacrament.
- **After hearing blasphemy**, as a habitual act of reparation — the purpose for which the devotion was given.
- **On Sundays and holy days**, in reparation for their profanation; and especially on **Shrove Tuesday**, the Feast of the Holy Face, or on **Tuesdays**, following the practice of Blessed Maria Pierina De Micheli.
- **For the dying and for sinners**, joined to the Rosary, the Chaplet of Divine Mercy, or an act of spiritual communion.

## The Promises Recorded in the Manual

The _Manual of the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face_ (Tours, 1887) collects nine promises "in favour of all those who honour His holy Face" — two from the medieval revelations of Saint Gertrude and Saint Mechtilde, the rest from the communications to Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre:

1. Those who honour the Holy Face "shall shine with a brightness surpassing that of many others in eternal life" (Saint Gertrude).
2. "Not one of them shall be separated from me" (Saint Mechtilde).
3. Our Lord "will imprint his divine likeness on the souls of those who honour his most holy countenance."
4. "By my Holy Face you shall work miracles."
5. "By my Holy Face you will obtain the conversion of many sinners… If you knew how pleasing the sight of my Face is to my Father!"
6. "As in a kingdom you can procure all you wish for with a coin marked with the prince's effigy, so in the kingdom of Heaven you will obtain all you desire with the precious coin of my holy Humanity, which is my adorable countenance."
7. "All those who honour my Holy Face in a spirit of reparation will by so doing perform the office of the pious Veronica."
8. "According to the care you take in making reparation to my Face disfigured by blasphemies, so will I take care of yours which has been disfigured by sin… I will render it as beautiful as it was on leaving the Baptismal font."
9. Those who defend His cause "by words, by prayers, or in writings" He will defend before His Father, and at their death He will restore their souls to their primitive beauty.

These are **private revelation**: they may be believed as pious testimony, but they are not articles of faith, and no one is bound to believe them. Their substance is consonant with revealed truth — the justified are conformed to the image of the Son (Romans 8:29) — but they are promises made to devotion as the Church understands devotion: faith, humility, charity, and perseverance, never the mere recitation of words.

## An Eastern Catholic Note

The Holy Face devotion is Latin in its historical form, but the mystery it honours is the common inheritance of the whole Church. The Eastern tradition's _acheiropoieton_ — the image of Christ "not made by hands," associated with Edessa — is the Byzantine counterpart of the Veil of Veronica, and the First Sunday of Great Lent commemorates the restoration of the holy images in 843. The Jesus Prayer — "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner" — is the East's own unceasing prayer of the Holy Name, closely parallel in spirit to the chaplet's repeated aspirations. An Eastern Catholic may pray this chaplet privately without any difficulty; it is a devotion, not a liturgical rite.

## Doctrinal Status at a Glance

- **The prohibition of blasphemy and of profaning the Lord's Day** — divine law, the Second and Third Commandments (Exodus 20:7–8; CCC 2142–2188).
- **Reparation in union with Christ** — Catholic doctrine: the members of His Body can offer satisfaction for sin in union with Him, not adding to the Cross but participating in its application (Colossians 1:24; CCC 961, 1475).
- **Christ's true and bodily Passion** — dogma: His humanity became "the free and perfect instrument of his divine love" (CCC 609).
- **The chaplet itself, its beads and prayers** — popular piety: approved, optional, no obligation.
- **The interior locutions and the promises** — private revelation (CCC 66–67), not required for belief.
- **The Archconfraternity, the 1885 brief of Leo XIII, and the 1958 Feast** — ecclesiastical institution and liturgical discipline, mutable.

## Prayers to the Holy Face

### The Prayer of Saint Thérèse of Lisieux

Saint Thérèse, "of the Child Jesus and of the Holy Face," took up the offering of the Face to the Father that lies at the heart of the whole devotion:

    Eternal Father, since Thou hast given me for my inheritance
    the adorable Face of Thy Divine Son,
    I offer that face to Thee and I beg Thee,
    in exchange for this coin of infinite value,
    to forget the ingratitude of souls dedicated to Thee
    and to pardon all poor sinners.

### Prayer to Reproduce the Image of God in the Soul

    I salute You, I adore You and I love You,
    adorable Face of my beloved Jesus,
    as the noble stamp of the Divinity!
    Completely surrendering my soul to You,
    I most humbly beg You to stamp this seal upon us all,
    so that the image of God may once more be reproduced
    by its imprint in our souls. Amen.

## The Optional Concluding Prayers

Contemporary Holy Face devotional material often adds the following prayers after the medal. They are later additions, not part of the 1887 _Manual_, and may be omitted; the second block gathers the devotion's last instinct into the words of Christ Himself.

    Eternal Father, I offer Thee the Cross of our Lord Jesus Christ
    and all the other instruments of His Holy Passion,
    that Thou mayest put division in the camp of Thine enemies;
    for as Thy beloved Son hath said,
    a kingdom divided against itself shall fall.

    Lord, I do not desire the death of the sinner,
    but I want him to be converted and to live.
    Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.

## A Note on These Texts

- **Attribution.** The 1887 _Manual_ attributes the chaplet to Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre in a single line — "The little chaplet comes to us from Sister Saint-Pierre" — but gives no date and no separate locution instituting it. It should be described as a devotion derived from her communications and attributed to her by the Archconfraternity, not as a prayer whose origin is documented to a particular day. The fuller account of the devotion, the Golden Arrow, Léon Dupont, and the papal approvals is found in the companion study documents of the repository.
- **Indulgences.** The _Manual_ prints a "100 days" indulgence for "My Jesus, mercy," a grant of the pre-1967 discipline. Pope Saint Paul VI's _Indulgentiarum doctrina_ (Norm 4) abolished the designation of partial indulgences in days and years, and the current _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_ attaches no concession to the Holy Face chaplet as such. The chaplet should be prayed for its own sake — for the glory of God, the conversion of sinners, and the triumph of the Church — and not for a quantified remission.
- **Wording.** The 1887 _Manual_ renders the closing prayer "God, our Protector, look on us, and cast Thy eyes upon the Face of Thy Christ"; the form above follows the Psalm text directly. Modern editions prefix an Apostles' Creed or an act of contrition and add concluding prayers offering the instruments of the Passion; these are pious additions that do not alter the substance of the chaplet. The meditations on the five senses are offered as an aid, not as part of the chaplet's received form.
