# The Order of Confirmation

This companion follows the **Latin Rite** when Confirmation is celebrated separately from Baptism, ordinarily during Mass. It highlights the candidate's responses and actions. Follow the directions and the liturgical text given by your parish.

## Before the Celebration

Confirmation strengthens the grace of Baptism and seals the Christian with the gift of the Holy Spirit. The confirmed are called to witness to Christ in word and deed. The sacrament leaves a permanent spiritual character and is received only once.

Prepare through prayer and, if conscious of grave sin, receive the Sacrament of Penance before Confirmation. Speak with your priest or catechist if you have questions about preparing or receiving Holy Communion.

Confirm the time and place with your parish. Know where you and your sponsor should sit and how you will approach the minister. If a name is to be announced at the anointing, check the parish's instructions in advance.

## 1. Listening and Presentation

At Mass, listen to the readings and the Gospel. The candidates are presented to the bishop, and he gives the homily. A priest with the faculty to confirm may administer the sacrament instead of a bishop.

After the homily, follow the directions to stand for the renewal of baptismal promises. Listen to the entire question before responding with the other candidates.

## 2. Renewal of Baptismal Promises

The bishop asks the candidates to renounce Satan and his works and seductions. In the English form, answer:

    I do.

He then asks you to profess faith in God the Father, in Jesus Christ the Son, in the Holy Spirit, and in the Catholic Church and the other articles of faith. Answer **“I do”** after each question.

The bishop concludes by proclaiming the faith of the Church. With the assembly, answer **“Amen.”** These promises recall the faith professed at your Baptism: you reject evil and profess your faith in the Triune God. Listen for the bishop's words, and respond together with the other candidates. Remain in place until the parish ministers direct you to approach.

## 3. Prayer and Laying On of Hands

The bishop invites the assembly to pray, then extends his hands over the candidates and asks God to send the Holy Spirit with His gifts. Listen prayerfully; you do not need to say the prayer with him.

At the end, answer **“Amen.”** The Church prays for the gifts of wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, piety, and fear of the Lord.

## 4. Anointing with Chrism

When directed, approach the minister with your sponsor. The sponsor places a right hand on your shoulder. The sponsor presents your name, or you may be asked to say it yourself.

The bishop anoints your forehead with sacred chrism, tracing the sign of the Cross. He says your name and the sacramental words:

    Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit.

You answer:

    Amen.

The bishop then offers you the greeting of peace. When he says **“Peace be with you,”** answer:

    And with your spirit.

Return to your place as directed. The prayer of the faithful follows. When Confirmation is celebrated within Mass, the Liturgy of the Eucharist follows in the usual way.

## Cebuano / Bisaya Cues

The following are **local examples**, not a verified official Cebuano Confirmation text. The minister's words below are short cues, not complete questions or the complete sacramental formula. Ask your parish or catechist for the actual wording used at your celebration.

**Renunciation:** the minister may begin **“Isalikway ba ninyo …?”** One Confirmation Mass slideshow gives **“Isalikway”** as a response.

**Profession of faith:** a local service program has questions beginning **“Nagatuo ba kamo …?”** The slideshow gives **“Nagatoo”** as a response.

**Anointing:** a local program begins the minister's words **“Dawata kining timaan …”** Answer **“Amen.”**

**Greeting of peace:** one local slideshow gives **“Maanaa usab kanimo”** after “Amen,” but the precise Confirmation greeting and response have not been independently verified. Confirm them with your parish.

Another local Easter Vigil example uses **“Nagsalikway ako”** and **“Nagatoo ako.”** It is a different celebration and does not establish what will be said at your Confirmation.

## After Confirmation

Thank God for the gift of the Holy Spirit. Continue to grow in the life begun at Baptism through prayer, the sacraments, learning the faith, and charity toward others. Confirmation strengthens you for the Church's mission; it is not the end of Christian formation.

**Remember the responses.** In English, say **“I do”** to the questions renewing your baptismal promises. Say **“Amen”** after the prayer over the candidates and after your anointing. Reply **“And with your spirit”** when the bishop greets you with peace.

**If you feel nervous.** You are not expected to perform a speech. Listen, wait for the minister's words, and answer simply. Your sponsor and the parish ministers are there to help you. If the language or response differs from this guide, follow the celebration as directed.

**The seven gifts.** The Church prays that the Holy Spirit will grant you wisdom and understanding, counsel and fortitude, knowledge and piety, and fear of the Lord. These gifts help Christians live under the guidance of the Holy Spirit.

## A Short Personal Prayer

The following prayer is for personal preparation; it is not a prayer prescribed by the rite.

    Holy Spirit, guide me in faith,
    strengthen me to follow Jesus,
    and help me serve the Church with love.
    Amen.

## A Note on These Texts

The English cues follow the Latin Rite order; editions and local instructions may differ. The Cebuano cues are drawn from local examples and are not presented as an approved ritual translation. Eastern Catholic Chrismation has its own liturgical forms and practices.
