# How to Pray the Chaplet of the Most Precious Blood — The Beads, the Seven Sheddings, and the Prayers

The Chaplet of the Most Precious Blood is a devotional prayer of the Latin Church. It meditates on the seven moments in which the Blood of Christ was shed, and it offers that Blood to the Eternal Father in atonement, in supplication, and in thanksgiving. It is **popular piety**, not liturgy: it adds nothing to the one sacrifice of Calvary and never substitutes for the Mass. The Church names it in the _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (§ 178), which lists it first among the devotions by which the veneration of the Blood of Christ passed from the Liturgy into the prayer of the faithful.

## The Beads

The chaplet is counted in **thirty-three Our Fathers**, in honour of the thirty-three years of the Lord's earthly life, and they are distributed in seven groups: six groups of five for the first six sheddings, and one group of three for the seventh.

A dedicated chaplet of thirty-three beads may be used, and a standard five-decade Rosary serves equally well, counting the Our Fathers across its beads. No beads are required at all: the prayers may be counted on the fingers. The Church imposes no obligation of matter or form.

The number thirty-three honours the traditional reckoning of the Lord's earthly life; the Gospels say only that He was "about thirty years of age" when He began His ministry (Luke 3:23).

## Step by Step

1. **At the crucifix.** Make the Sign of the Cross.
2. **Optional opening.** Recite the Apostles' Creed.
3. **Before the first group.** Pray the opening versicle — _O God, come to my assistance; Lord, make haste to help me_ — and then the Glory Be.
4. **The first six groups.** Announce the Mystery and its petition, pray five Our Fathers, then the Glory Be and the versicle and response. Repeat for each of the first six Mysteries.
5. **The seventh group.** Announce the Seventh Mystery, pray three Our Fathers, then the Glory Be and the versicle and response.
6. **Conclusion.** Pray the closing offering and make the Sign of the Cross.
7. **Optional.** Pray the Litany of the Most Precious Blood, or the _Anima Christi_.

Some editions place the versicle _O God, come to my assistance_ before the Our Fathers of every Mystery rather than only at the beginning. Both arrangements are in use, and neither alters the substance of the devotion.

## The Seven Sheddings

The _Directory_ describes the seven as the "effusions of the Blood of Christ" implicitly or explicitly mentioned in the Gospels. The number seven is a **devotional schema**, not a datum of revelation: the _Directory_ itself lists six in its summary, and the common chaplet reaches seven by distinguishing the Crucifixion from the piercing of the Side. Each Mystery is announced with its petition, which is the grace asked for while its Our Fathers are prayed.

**1. The Circumcision** (Luke 2:21). _Petition: chastity of soul and body._ On the eighth day the Child is circumcised and receives the name Jesus, "for he will save his people from their sins." The first shedding of the Redeemer's Blood therefore takes place in obedience — in submission to the Law, before He can speak or choose.

**2. The Agony in the Garden** (Luke 22:44). _Petition: the spirit of prayer._ "Being in an agony he prayed more earnestly; and his sweat became like great drops of blood falling down upon the ground." The Blood is shed here not under the whip but in prayer, in the struggle of the human will before the cup: "not my will, but yours, be done."

**3. The Scourging** (John 19:1; Isaiah 53:5). _Petition: the grace of mortification._ Pilate takes Jesus and scourges Him, and the prophecy is fulfilled: "with his stripes we are healed." The Blood shed here is the price of our healing, and the petition is the disciplined putting to death of sin, not a hatred of the body.

**4. The Crowning with Thorns** (Matthew 27:29; John 19:2). _Petition: contempt of worldly honours._ The soldiers crown Him with thorns and salute Him in mockery. The thorns recall the curse pronounced on the ground after the Fall; the New Adam now wears the curse of the old creation on His brow, and the Blood runs down the face that had shone on Tabor.

**5. The Carrying of the Cross** (Luke 23:26–31; John 19:17). _Petition: patience._ He goes out bearing His own cross, and Christian devotion has always seen the road to Calvary as a bloodstained road. Simon is pressed into service, the women of Jerusalem weep, and the petition is the grace to carry the cross without complaint and to help others carry theirs.

**6. The Crucifixion** (Luke 23:33–46; John 19:18, 30). _Petition: contrition for our sins._ At the place called the Skull they crucify Him; the sky darkens, the veil of the Temple is torn, and He cries out, "It is finished." Here the offering is complete and the new covenant is sealed in His Blood. The petition is the sorrow that looks on the Crucified and recognizes, with the centurion, "Truly this was the Son of God."

**7. The Piercing of the Side** (John 19:34). _Petition: the grace of perseverance._ One of the soldiers pierces His side, and at once blood and water come out — the birth of the sacraments from the open heart of Christ. The last shedding is the most fruitful, and the last petition is fidelity to the end.

## The Prayers

### The Sign of the Cross

    In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

### The Apostles' Creed (optional)

    I believe in God, the Father almighty,
    Creator of heaven and earth,
    and in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord,
    who was conceived by the Holy Spirit,
    born of the Virgin Mary,
    suffered under Pontius Pilate,
    was crucified, died and was buried;
    He descended into hell;
    on the third day He rose again from the dead;
    He ascended into heaven,
    and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty;
    from there He will come to judge the living and the dead.
    I believe in the Holy Spirit,
    the holy catholic Church,
    the communion of saints,
    the forgiveness of sins,
    the resurrection of the body,
    and life everlasting. Amen.

### Opening Versicle

    V. O God, come to my assistance.
    R. Lord, make haste to help me.
    V. Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.
    R. As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be,
       world without end. Amen.

### The Our Father

    Our Father, who art in heaven,
    hallowed be Thy name;
    Thy kingdom come;
    Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven.
    Give us this day our daily bread;
    and forgive us our trespasses
    as we forgive those who trespass against us;
    and lead us not into temptation,
    but deliver us from evil. Amen.

The Lord's Prayer is the model of all prayer; the Catechism calls it "truly the summary of the whole gospel" (CCC 2761, quoting Tertullian).

### The Glory Be

    Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.
    As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be,
    world without end. Amen.

### Versicle and Response

    V. We beseech You, Lord, help Your servants.
    R. Whom You have redeemed with Your Precious Blood.

### The Closing Offering

    Eternal Father,
    I offer You the Precious Blood of Jesus Christ
    in atonement for my sins,
    in supplication for the holy souls in Purgatory,
    and for the needs of Holy Church. Amen.

A common variant reads:

    Eternal Father,
    I offer You the Most Precious Blood of Jesus Christ
    in satisfaction for my sins,
    for the needs of Holy Church,
    and for the relief of the souls in Purgatory. Amen.

### Aspirations

Any of these may be used at the close of the chaplet or during the day:

    Precious Blood of Jesus, save us.
    Blood of Christ, price of our salvation, save us.
    Blood of Christ, without which there is no forgiveness, save us.

### The Anima Christi

    Soul of Christ, sanctify me.
    Body of Christ, save me.
    Blood of Christ, inebriate me.
    Water from the side of Christ, wash me.
    Passion of Christ, strengthen me.
    O good Jesus, hear me.
    Within Thy wounds hide me.
    Suffer me not to be separated from Thee.
    From the malignant enemy defend me.
    In the hour of my death call me,
    and bid me come to Thee,
    that with Thy saints I may praise Thee
    for ever and ever. Amen.

## The Litany of the Most Precious Blood

The _Directory_ names the Litany of the Blood of Christ alongside the chaplet, noting that it "clearly traces the line of salvation history through a series of biblical references and passages," and that in its present form it was approved by Pope St. John XXIII on 24 February 1960. It may be prayed after the chaplet or on its own.

    Lord, have mercy on us.
    Christ, have mercy on us.
    Lord, have mercy on us.
    Christ, hear us.
    Christ, graciously hear us.

    God, the Father of heaven, have mercy on us.
    God, the Son, Redeemer of the world, have mercy on us.
    God, the Holy Spirit, have mercy on us.
    Holy Trinity, one God, have mercy on us.

    Blood of Christ, only-begotten Son of the eternal Father, save us.
    Blood of Christ, Incarnate Word of God, save us.
    Blood of Christ, of the New and Eternal Testament, save us.
    Blood of Christ, falling upon the earth in the Agony, save us.
    Blood of Christ, shed profusely in the Scourging, save us.
    Blood of Christ, flowing forth in the Crowning with Thorns, save us.
    Blood of Christ, poured out on the Cross, save us.
    Blood of Christ, price of our salvation, save us.
    Blood of Christ, without which there is no forgiveness, save us.
    Blood of Christ, Eucharistic drink and refreshment of souls, save us.
    Blood of Christ, stream of mercy, save us.
    Blood of Christ, victor over demons, save us.
    Blood of Christ, courage of Martyrs, save us.
    Blood of Christ, strength of Confessors, save us.
    Blood of Christ, bringing forth Virgins, save us.
    Blood of Christ, help of those in peril, save us.
    Blood of Christ, relief of the burdened, save us.
    Blood of Christ, solace in sorrow, save us.
    Blood of Christ, hope of the penitent, save us.
    Blood of Christ, consolation of the dying, save us.
    Blood of Christ, peace and tenderness of hearts, save us.
    Blood of Christ, pledge of eternal life, save us.
    Blood of Christ, freeing souls from Purgatory, save us.
    Blood of Christ, most worthy of all glory and honor, save us.

    Lamb of God, who takest away the sins of the world,
       spare us, O Lord.
    Lamb of God, who takest away the sins of the world,
       graciously hear us, O Lord.
    Lamb of God, who takest away the sins of the world,
       have mercy on us.

    V. Thou hast redeemed us, O Lord, in Thy Blood.
    R. And hast made us, for our God, a kingdom.

    Let us pray.
    Almighty and eternal God,
    Thou hast appointed Thine only-begotten Son
    the Redeemer of the world,
    and willed to be appeased by His Blood.
    Grant, we beseech Thee,
    that we may worthily adore this price of our salvation
    and through its power be safeguarded from the evils of this life,
    so that we may rejoice in its fruits forever in heaven.
    Through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

Modern-language renderings substitute "you" and "your" for "Thou" and "Thy," and "who takes away" for "who takest away"; the invocations and the collect are the same.

## When to Pray the Chaplet

- **In July**, the Month of the Most Precious Blood, and especially on 1 July, where the feast is still observed.
- **On Fridays**, in honour of the Passion, and particularly on the First Fridays of the month.
- **In Holy Week and the Paschal Triduum**, when the Church most explicitly celebrates the Blood of the New Covenant.
- **For the souls in Purgatory**, for the needs of the Church, for the conversion of sinners, and for the dying.
- **In thanksgiving** after Holy Communion, when the Blood received sacramentally is offered back in adoration.

## An Eastern Catholic Note

An Eastern Catholic may pray this chaplet as a private devotion, using the Eastern form of the Sign of the Cross and, if desired, the Trisagion — "Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us" — as an opening. The East's own devotional forms of the same mystery are the Akathist to the Passion of Christ, the Paraklesis, and the Moleben; the chaplet is a Latin form prayed with an Eastern heart. None of this touches the doctrine: the Blood of Christ is adored in every rite, and the difference between East and West here is one of expression, not of faith.

## Doctrinal Status at a Glance

- **The Blood of Christ was shed for the remission of sins and for our redemption** — dogma (Matthew 26:28; 1 Peter 1:18–19; Council of Trent, Session VI, ch. 7; CCC 613, 1366).
- **The Blood of Christ is truly present in the Eucharist** — dogma (Council of Trent, Session XIII, canons 1–4; CCC 1374–1376).
- **The Mass is the same sacrifice as Calvary, offered in an unbloody manner** — dogma (Council of Trent, Session XXII; CCC 1366–1367).
- **The Blood of Christ is worthy of adoration** — Catholic doctrine: it is adored because it is His, united to the Person of the Word (Trent, Session XIII, ch. 3).
- **The seven sheddings, the thirty-three Our Fathers, and the chaplet itself** — popular piety, named in the _Directory_ § 178: no obligation, and no private revelation behind it.

## A Note on These Texts

- **Origin.** The chaplet is a devotional composition of the early nineteenth century, associated with Msgr. Francesco Albertini and the Archconfraternity of the Most Precious Blood in Rome (1808–1809). It is not a private revelation; the Church commends it through her ordinary pastoral practice and through the _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (§ 178).
- **Indulgences.** The current _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_ attaches no named concession to the chaplet as such. It grants a partial indulgence for the devout recitation of approved litanies, expressly including the Litany of the Most Precious Blood (Concessio 22, § 2). Claims that a fixed number of souls is released from Purgatory by a given number of recitations are not the Church's doctrine.
- **Wording.** English renderings of the prayers vary; the traditional "Thou" is retained in the Litany, and the modern form is equally lawful. The seven meditations above are the compiler's, drawn from the Gospel texts and the Church's doctrine, and are offered as an aid, not as part of the chaplet's substance.
