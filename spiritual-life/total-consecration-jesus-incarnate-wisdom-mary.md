# Total Consecration to Jesus Incarnate Wisdom through Mary

> 📝 **Scope note**: This document presents the devotion of **Total Consecration to Jesus Incarnate Wisdom through the hands of the Blessed Virgin Mary** — the "perfect devotion" composed by **St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort** (1673–1716) in his treatise _True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin_ and _The Secret of Mary_. The act of consecration is an **approved ecclesiastical devotion** grounded in revealed doctrine (the divine maternity and Mary's maternal mediation), recommended but never imposed by the Church. It is not a dogma, not a sacrament, and not an obligation binding any member of the faithful. See [Section 2](#2-doctrinal-classification-levels-of-authority) for the full classification and [Section 14](#14-doctrinal-cautions) for the Church's cautions.

---

## 1. What the Devotion Is

The **Total Consecration to Jesus Incarnate Wisdom through Mary** is a formal act of self-surrender in which the Christian, by a free and deliberate decision, gives himself entirely to **Jesus Christ, the Incarnate Wisdom of God**, by the hands of His Mother — renewing the promises of Baptism, renouncing Satan and his pomps, and delivering to Mary his body, soul, goods, and even the value of his good works, so that she may present them perfectly to her Son.

Its purpose is entirely **Christological**. St. Louis de Montfort insists that "Jesus, our Saviour, true God and true man, must be the ultimate end of all other devotions" (_True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin_, §61), and that:

> "Our entire perfection consists in being conformed, united and consecrated to Jesus Christ. Hence the most perfect of all devotions is undoubtedly that which conforms, unites and consecrates us most perfectly to Jesus Christ." (_True Devotion_, §120)

Mary is not the terminus of the consecration but its **surest and shortest way**: "The more a soul is consecrated to her, the more will it be consecrated to Jesus Christ" (quoted by Pope St. John Paul II, [Letter for the 50th Anniversary of the Canonization of St. Louis-Marie Grignion de Montfort](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/9dcc13ae-25e3-4496-a883-a1c4a7488ecf/ref/4), 7 June 1997). The devotion is therefore sometimes called:

- **Total Consecration to Jesus through Mary**
- **True Devotion** (from the title of Montfort's treatise)
- **Holy Slavery** or the **slavery of love** (_esclavage d'amour_), from the language of the act itself

The **Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy** (9 April 2002), §204, names Montfort explicitly as the master of this spirituality, recalling him as one "who proposed to Christians consecration to Christ through the hands of Mary as an effective means of living faithfully the commitments of Baptism."

A Catholic may live in heroic holiness without ever practicing this devotion; its promotion by the Magisterium is a commendation, not a precept.

## 3. The Author: St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort (1673–1716)

**St. Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort** — priest, itinerant missionary, and founder — was born in the Breton village of Montfort on **31 January 1673**. Ordained a priest at Paris on 5 June 1700 after his studies with the Sulpicians, he spent sixteen years preaching parish missions across western France — Poitou, Brittany, Anjou, and Normandy — with such fire that he was frequently reassigned from diocese to diocese. In 1706 he walked to Rome, where **Pope Clement XI** confirmed him as an **Apostolic Missionary**. He died at **Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre** in the Vendée on **28 April 1716**, at the age of forty-three, worn out by apostolic labor.

| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1673      | Born at Montfort, Brittany, on 31 January                                                                         |
| 1693–1700 | Studies at Saint-Sulpice, Paris; ordained priest 5 June 1700                                                      |
| 1703      | Foundress-collaborator phase at Poitiers: co-founds the **Daughters of Wisdom** with Blessed Marie Louise Trichet |

| 1715–1716 | Founds the **Company of Mary** (Montfort Missionaries, S.M.M.)                                                    |
| 1716      | Dies at Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre on 28 April                                                                       |
| 1842      | The manuscript of _True Devotion_ is discovered; first printed 1843                                               |
| 1888      | Beatified by **Pope Leo XIII** (22 January)                                                                       |
| 1947      | Canonized by **Pope Pius XII** (20 July)                                                                          |

His foundation of the **Daughters of Wisdom** (for the education of poor girls and care of the sick) and the **Company of Mary** (missionary priests) continues today, together with the **Brothers of Saint Gabriel** who took their rule from him. He died possessed of nothing, having lived the poverty he preached; his epitaph could well be his own aspiration: **"God Alone"** (_Dieu seul_).

His canonization by Pius XII on 20 July 1947 (Acta Apostolicae Sedis 39 [1947]) was an implicit endorsement of the spirituality the Church now formally commends in [DPPL §204](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/c3db4362-44df-43c0-ba26-eb53d17bb438/ref/204).

## 4. Sources of the Devotion

### 4.1 _True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin_ (c. 1712)

The classical text of the devotion. Montfort wrote it around 1712 and deposited it in a chest at the mother house of the Daughters of Wisdom at Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre, where it lay **hidden for 126 years** — surviving the French Revolution — until the Montfort Father **Jean-Baptiste Petit** discovered it in 1842. First published in 1843, it became one of the most influential Marian books in the history of the Church. Its central claim is that there exists a devotion "to Our Lady" which is not merely pious but **perfect**, because it conforms, unites, and consecrates the soul to Christ most completely (§§120 ff.), and that this devotion is expressed formally in the act of consecration given at §§225–233.

### 4.2 _The Secret of Mary_

A shorter companion treatise presenting the same consecration in condensed form (the act itself at §§66–67) and the "particular practices" for living it (§§70–73). It distinguishes the three forms of devotion to Mary and explains the interior dispositions of the consecrated soul.

### 4.3 _The Love of Eternal Wisdom_

The Christological fountainhead of the whole devotion. Here Montfort meditates on the Eternal Wisdom of the Father (Proverbs 8; Sirach 24; Wisdom 7) who, "being in the form of God... emptied himself, taking the form of a slave" (Philippians 2:6–7), and became incarnate **in the virginal womb of Mary**. The consecration's address to "Jesus, the Incarnate Wisdom" and its opening adoration of Wisdom "in the bosom and splendours of Thy Father during eternity, and in the virginal bosom of Mary, Thy most worthy Mother, in the time of Thine incarnation" flow directly from this book.

### 4.4 Other Writings

The _Letter to the Friends of the Cross_, _The Secret of the Rosary_, _Prayer for Missionaries_, and Montfort's 164 hymns complete the corpus. See the doctrinal context in [christology/README.md](../christology/README.md) and the liturgical setting of Marian devotion in [liturgy/rosary/history.md](../liturgy/rosary/history.md).

## 5. Theological Foundations

### 5.1 To Jesus Alone, through Mary

The famous Montfortan maxim — **"to Jesus through Mary"** (_ad Iesum per Mariam_) — rests on the Gospel scene in which the Eternal Word, becoming man, made Himself subject to Mary (Luke 2:51). Montfort reasons that the **God of all holiness was pleased to subject Himself to a creature for thirty years**, hiding His infinite glory in her womb and home; the disciple who wishes to reach Jesus by the shortest path imitates this divine choice. The devotion is therefore fundamentally **an imitation of the Incarnation itself**: as the Son came to us through Mary, so we go to the Son through Mary.

This reasoning is devotional and theological rather than definitional. The Church grounds it more soberly in **Mary's maternal mediation**: at Cana and at the Cross, Mary's maternal cooperation directs every grace she mediates toward her Son — "Do whatever he tells you" (John 2:5).

### 5.2 The Incarnate Wisdom

The consecration is addressed to Christ under His Sapiential title: **Jesus, the Incarnate Wisdom** — "Christ [the] power of God and [the] wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24), in whom "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3). Montfort's Christology is unabashedly prostrate before the mystery of the _kenosis_: Wisdom annihilated Himself, "taking the form of a slave" (Philippians 2:7), and freely submitted to His Mother. The act of consecration deliberately adopts the same **two-sided movement**: adoration of the humiliated Wisdom, and identification with Him as His "slave of love."

### 5.3 Mary's Maternal Mediation, Strictly Subordinated to Christ

The Second Vatican Council supplies the doctrinal frame within which the devotion is read:

- **_Lumen Gentium_ 60**: Mary's maternal role "in no way obscures or diminishes" the unique mediation of Christ, but rather "shows forth its power." Her salutary influence flows from the superabundance of Christ's merits, rests upon His mediation, and depends entirely upon it.
- **_Lumen Gentium_ 62**: Mary is rightly called **Advocate, Helper, Benefactress, and Mediatrix** — yet this neither takes away from nor adds anything to the dignity and efficacy of Christ the one Mediator (cf. 1 Timothy 2:5).
- **[CCC 970](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/970)**: Mary's "motherhood in the order of grace continues uninterruptedly... until the eternal fulfilment of all the elect" — a maternal office entirely ordered to the one mediation of her Son.
- **[CCC 971](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/971)**: devotion to Mary is "intrinsic to Christian worship"; but "this special devotion... differs essentially from the cult of adoration given only to the Incarnate Word," and it "substantially conforms to and leads to the worship of God" — "all those Marian titles... are meant to lead believers more deeply into the worship of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit."

Pope St. John Paul II's encyclical **_Redemptoris Mater_** (25 March 1987), §§45–47, describes the exact spiritual structure of the consecration: at the Cross, "the Redeemer entrusts his mother to the disciple, and at the same time he gives her to him as his mother" (John 19:26–27). The disciple's filial entrustment "not only has its beginning in Christ but can also be said to be definitively directed towards him"; for Mary "continues to say to each individual the words which she spoke at Cana in Galilee: 'Do whatever he tells you.'" The more the consecrated soul lives under Mary's maternal charity, "the nearer Mary leads them to the 'unsearchable riches of Christ'" (Ephesians 3:8).

### 5.4 The Consecration as a Perfect Renewal of Baptism

The [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/c3db4362-44df-43c0-ba26-eb53d17bb438/ref/204), §204, gives the canonically precise reading of the act: it is "a conscious recognition of the unique place occupied by Mary of Nazareth in the mystery of Christ and the Church; of the exemplary and universal value of her Gospel witness; of confidence in her intercession and in the efficacy of her patronage; and of the manifold maternal role she exercises, as the true mother in the order of grace, on behalf of all and each of her children."

The Directory further notes the precise ecclesial framing: the act "must be expressed correctly, in what might be called a liturgical framework: **to the Father, through Christ, in the Holy Spirit**, imploring the glorious intercession of Mary, to whom one entrusts oneself totally, **in order to observe faithfully the commitments of Baptism** and to live in a filial attitude toward her." This is exactly what Montfort's formula does — the consecrated soul declares: "I renew and ratify today in thy hands the vows of my Baptism; I renounce forever Satan, his pomps and works."

The act is thus a **re-actualization of Baptism**, the baptismal character being the true foundation of every Christian consecration. It shares in and draws its force from the sacraments of Baptism and Confirmation; it does not confer a new sacramental grace.

### 5.5 Holy Slavery: The Loving Servitude of Jesus in Mary

Montfort distinguishes three forms of servitude toward God and Mary (_The Secret of Mary_, §§40–42; _True Devotion_, §§71–72):

1. **Servile servitude** — service rendered from fear of punishment (imperfect);
2. **Mercenary servitude** — service rendered for reward (imperfect);
3. **Filial and loving servitude** — the service of children and slaves of love, rendered "because God deserves to be served" — the only form worthy of the consecration.

The striking term **"slave of Jesus in Mary"** (not "of Mary," but "of Jesus _in_ Mary") deliberately echoes **Philippians 2:7**: Christ Himself "took the form of a slave." The consecrated soul wants to belong to Jesus as He subjected Himself to Mary. Montfort describes the act as having the gravity of a vow — a total, irrevocable gift — though canonically it remains a **private devotional commitment** and does not bind with the canonical force of a religious vow.

The **outward sign** of this "holy slavery" was traditionally a small **iron chain** worn about the neck, wrist, or waist — a practice Montfort recommends (optional, never obligatory) as a permanent reminder of belonging to Jesus and Mary (cf. _The Secret of Mary_, §73).

### 5.6 Why "By the Hands of Mary"?

The consecration gives Mary not the worship of latria but the **entire disposition of one's merits**. Concretely, the consecrated soul gives Mary:

- his **body** with its senses and members;
- his **soul** with its faculties and powers;
- his **material goods** and possessions;
- his **spiritual goods** — merits, virtues, satisfactions — both present and future;

and asks her to **dispose of all of it** for the greater glory of God, as a true mother disposes the goods of the household. The rationale is Montfort's principle of mediate perfection: actions performed in union with Mary's merits and intentions are purified of self-love, imperfection, and inordinateness, and reach God "in the vessel of gold" rather than "in a cracked vessel" (_True Devotion_, §172 ff., drawing on the figure of Rebecca and Jacob, Genesis 27). This reasoning belongs to the order of **pious theology** — illuminating but not defined — and must always be read under _Lumen Gentium_ 60, which forbids any reading that would make Mary an autonomous channel beside Christ.

## 6. True and False Devotion: Montfort's Discernment

Before proposing the consecration, Montfort examines "the types of false devotion" and the marks of the true. His criteria remain a serviceable **examination of conscience** for any Marian piety (_True Devotion_, §§90–110):

**Four characteristics of true devotion to Mary:**

1. **Interior** — from the mind and the heart, not merely the lips;
2. **Tender** — confident and filial, like a child toward a loving mother;
3. **Holy** — leading away from sin and to imitation of Mary's virtues;
4. **Constant and disinterested** — strengthened by resistance, sustained in dryness, seeking God alone rather than consolations.

**Montfort's four classes of souls** (§§115–117) — from the _critics_ who scorn every Marian practice, through the _scrupulous_ who fear excess, to the _external_ devotees and _presumptuous_ devotees, up to the **faithful and persevering souls** who belong wholly to Mary — remain a mirror for the discernment of spirits in Marian devotion (cf. [gifts-holy-spirit/discernment-of-spirits.md](../gifts-holy-spirit/discernment-of-spirits.md)).

## 7. The 33-Day Preparation

Montfort prescribes a **33-day spiritual preparation**, divided so that the soul is progressively emptied of the spirit of the world and filled with the knowledge of self, of Mary, and of Jesus. The consecration is made on the 34th day.

| Stage    | Duration | Focus                                                                                   | Principal Prayers                                                                                                                                               |
| -------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emptying | 12 days  | Renunciation of the spirit of the world; penance and mortification                      | _Veni Creator Spiritus_, _Ave Maris Stella_, _Magnificat_, _Gloria Patri_                                                                                       |
| Week 1   | 7 days   | **Knowledge of Self** — humility, sin, the mercy of God                                 | Litany of the Holy Spirit, Litany of the Blessed Virgin Mary, _Ave Maris Stella_, _Magnificat_                                                                  |
| Week 2   | 7 days   | **Knowledge of Mary** — her privileges, virtues, and maternal office                    | Litany of the Holy Spirit, Litany of Loreto, _Ave Maris Stella_, Montfort's prayer to Mary, five decades of the [Holy Rosary](../liturgy/rosary/how-to-pray.md) |
| Week 3   | 7 days   | **Knowledge of Jesus Incarnate Wisdom** — His incarnation, mission, and union with Mary | Litany of the Holy Spirit, _Ave Maris Stella_, Litany of the Holy Name of Jesus, Montfort's prayer to Jesus, _O Jesus Living in Mary_                           |

**Choosing the day of consecration.** The consecration is traditionally made on a **feast of Our Lady**, the date of which fixes the day on which the 33-day preparation begins. Commonly chosen feasts (observed in both calendars) include:

| Begin the 33 Days | Consecration Day (Marian Feast)                       |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 9 January         | Our Lady of Lourdes — 11 February                     |
| 20/21 February    | The Annunciation — 25 March                           |
| 13 June           | Our Lady of Mount Carmel — 16 July                    |
| 13 July           | The Assumption — 15 August                            |
| 6 August          | Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary — 8 September     |
| 13 August         | Our Lady of Sorrows — 15 September                    |
| 19 October        | Presentation of the Blessed Virgin Mary — 21 November |
| 5 November        | The Immaculate Conception — 8 December                |
| 9 November        | Our Lady of Guadalupe — 12 December                   |

Montfortan tradition has long favored **the Annunciation (25 March)**, since this feast commemorates the mystery at the heart of the devotion: the Incarnation itself, when the Eternal Wisdom took flesh in Mary's womb and made Himself subject to her (see [liturgy/liturgical-calendar.md](../liturgy/liturgical-calendar.md)).

## 8. The Day of Consecration

Montfort's directions for the 34th day (_True Devotion_, §§225–227; _The Secret of Mary_, §§64–65) are ascetical and sacramental:

1. **Penance** — the day is observed in a spirit of mortification: fasting, almsgiving, or an offering made for another.
2. **Confession** — the soul approaches the Sacrament of Penance (see [sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md](../sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md)), either that day or within the preceding eight.
3. **Holy Communion** — Mass is offered and Holy Communion received with the express intention of giving oneself to Jesus as a slave of love by the hands of Mary. Montfort supplements this with a Marian method of assistance before, during, and after Communion (the Supplement to _True Devotion_).
4. **The Act of Consecration** — kneeling before an image of the Blessed Virgin (before the tabernacle where possible), the soul recites the act of consecration slowly and deliberately, then **signs it**, keeping a dated copy as a memorial of the day.
5. **Oblation** — some give a token offering (alms, candle, flowers) to honor the Mother of God.

The act should be free and personal: the Directory requires that it "must result not from a passing emotion, but from a personal, free decision, matured within an accurate understanding of the dynamism of grace" (DPPL §204).

## 9. The Act of Consecration

### 9.1 The Formula of St. Louis de Montfort

The following is the traditional English text of the act (in the classic translation of Fr. Frederick William Faber, 1863), as given in _True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin_ §§225–233 and _The Secret of Mary_ §§66–67. Where the formula reads "I, N. ____," the consecrating person inserts **his or her own name**; it is the soul itself that consecrates, not a delegate. The formula is addressed first to **Jesus, the Incarnate Wisdom**, then — in its central act — to **Mary**, that she may present the gift to her Son.

    O Eternal and Incarnate Wisdom, O sweetest and most adorable Jesus,
    true God and true man,
    only Son of the Eternal Father and of Mary, always virgin,
    I adore Thee profoundly in the bosom and splendours of Thy Father during eternity;
    and I adore Thee also in the virginal bosom of Mary, Thy most worthy Mother,
    in the time of Thine incarnation.

    I give Thee thanks for that Thou hast annihilated Thyself,
    taking the form of a slave in order to rescue me
    from the cruel slavery of the devil.
    I praise and glorify Thee for that Thou hast been pleased
    to submit Thyself to Mary, Thy holy Mother, in all things,
    in order to make me Thy faithful slave through her.

    But, alas, ungrateful and faithless as I have been,
    I have not kept the promises which I made so solemnly to Thee in my Baptism;
    I have not fulfilled my obligations;
    I do not deserve to be called Thy child, nor yet Thy slave;
    and as there is nothing in me which does not merit Thine anger and Thy repulse,
    I dare not come by myself before Thy most holy and august Majesty.
    It is on this account that I have recourse to the intercession of Thy most holy Mother,
    whom Thou hast given me for a mediatrix with Thee.
    It is through her that I hope to obtain of Thee contrition,
    the pardon of my sins,
    and the acquisition and preservation of wisdom.

    Hail, then, O immaculate Mary,
    living tabernacle of the Divinity,
    where the Eternal Wisdom willed to be hidden and to be adored by angels and by men!
    Hail, O Queen of Heaven and earth,
    to whose empire everything is subject which is under God.
    Hail, O sure refuge of sinners, whose mercy fails no one.
    Hear the desires which I have of the Divine Wisdom,
    and for that end receive the vows and offerings which in my lowliness I present to thee.

    I, N. ____, a faithless sinner,
    renew and ratify today in thy hands the vows of my Baptism;
    I renounce forever Satan, his pomps and works;
    and I give myself entirely to Jesus Christ, the Incarnate Wisdom,
    to carry my cross after Him all the days of my life,
    and to be more faithful to Him than I have ever been before.

    In the presence of all the heavenly court
    I choose thee this day for my Mother and Mistress.
    I deliver and consecrate to thee, as thy slave,
    my body and soul,
    my goods, both interior and exterior,
    and even the value of all my good actions, past, present, and future;
    leaving to thee the entire and full right of disposing of me,
    and all that belongs to me, without exception,
    according to thy good pleasure,
    for the greater glory of God, in time and in eternity.

    Receive, O benignant Virgin, this little offering of my slavery,
    in honour of, and in union with,
    that subjection which the Eternal Wisdom deigned to have to thy maternity;
    in homage to the power which both of you have over this poor sinner;
    and in thanksgiving for the privileges with which the Holy Trinity has favoured thee.
    I declare that I wish henceforth, as thy true slave,
    to seek thy honour and to obey thee in all things.

    O admirable Mother,
    present me to thy dear Son as His eternal slave,
    so that as He has redeemed me by thee, by thee He may receive me!
    O Mother of mercy, grant me the grace to obtain the true Wisdom of God;
    and for that end receive me among those whom thou lovest and teachest,
    whom thou leadest, nourishest and protectest as thy children and thy slaves.

    O faithful Virgin,
    make me in all things so perfect a disciple, imitator and slave
    of the Incarnate Wisdom, Jesus Christ thy Son,
    that I may attain, by thine intercession and by thine example,
    to the fullness of His age on earth and of His glory in heaven. Amen.

### 9.2 The Closing Aspiration

The treatise seals the act with the Latin formula from which Pope St. John Paul II took the motto of his pontificate:

    Totus tuus ego sum et omnia mea tua sunt,
    o dulcis Iesu, per Mariam, tuam sanctam Matrem.

    I am all yours, and all that I have is yours,
    O dear Jesus, through Mary, your holy Mother.

(_True Devotion_, §233)

### 9.3 Short Form for Monthly Renewal

For the ongoing renewal of the consecration, the following abbreviated form is traditional:

    I am all thine, and all that I have is thine,
    O dear Jesus, through Mary, thy holy Mother.

and, as a daily aspiration of the consecrated soul:

    I am all thine, Immaculate One, with all that I have,
    in time and in eternity.

## 10. Living the Consecration

Consecration is not a single ceremony but a **permanent spiritual program**. Montfort prescribes its "particular practices" (_The Secret of Mary_, §§70–73; _True Devotion_, §§230–233):

- **Daily renewal** — each morning, entrust the day to Jesus through Mary with the daily aspiration above.
- **The Little Crown of the Blessed Virgin** — Montfort's chaplet of three Our Fathers, Hail Marys, and Glory bes in honor of Mary's excellence, power, and goodness, followed by twelve Hail Marys honoring the twelve stars of her crown (Revelation 12:1).
- **The Holy Rosary** — the daily recitation of the Rosary, "the greatest of all Marian devotions" in Montfort's judgment (see [liturgy/rosary/how-to-pray.md](../liturgy/rosary/how-to-pray.md)); or, for those able, the **Little Office of the Blessed Virgin Mary**.
- **The Magnificat** — frequent recitation of Our Lady's canticle (Luke 1:46–55), Mary's own thanksgiving.
- **Imitation of Mary's virtues** — above all her lively faith, profound humility, universal mortification, and total obedience to God's will.
- **Special devotion to the Mystery of the Incarnation** — frequent meditation on the Word made flesh, especially through the Joyful Mysteries and the feasts of the Incarnation (Annunciation, Nativity).
- **Marian manner of Communion** — uniting oneself with Mary before, during, and after Holy Communion, so that Jesus may come "by the same way He came the first time."
- **The chain** — optional wearing of a small iron chain as an outward sign of the slavery of love.
- **Contempt for the spirit of the world** — persevering renunciation of the maxims of the world, renewed in temptation and dryness.
- **Annual renewal** — the consecration is renewed each year on its anniversary, ideally by repeating the 33-day preparation.
- **Confraternity** — enrollment, where available, in the **Confraternity of Mary, Queen of All Hearts**, the Montfortan association for those living the consecration.

## 11. Magisterial and Papal Reception

### 11.1 The Church's Formal Commendation

The [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/c3db4362-44df-43c0-ba26-eb53d17bb438/ref/204), §204, formally records the practice:

> "With regard to the pious practice of 'consecration to Mary,' expressions of appreciation by the Roman Pontiffs are not uncommon, and the formulas publicly recited by them are well known. A well-known teacher of the spirituality underlying this practice is Saint Louis Mary Grignion de Montfort, who proposed to Christians consecration to Christ through the hands of Mary as an effective means of living faithfully the commitments of Baptism."

The same section adds the pastoral cautions reproduced in [Section 14](#14-doctrinal-cautions).

### 11.2 Pope St. John Paul II: Totus Tuus

No pontiff embodied the devotion more fully than **Pope St. John Paul II**, whose papal motto — **Totus Tuus** ("Totally yours") — was drawn from the closing formula of Montfort's consecration (§9.2 above). In his [Letter for the 50th Anniversary of the Canonization of St. Louis-Marie Grignion de Montfort](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/9dcc13ae-25e3-4496-a883-a1c4a7488ecf/ref/4) (7 June 1997), he wrote that "the person of Christ dominates the thought of Grignion de Montfort," and he renewed the entrustment in the Montfortan form:

    Totus Tuus ego sum et omnia mea Tua sunt.
    Accipio te in mea omnia.
    Praebe mihi cor Tuum, Maria.

    I am all yours, and all that I have is yours.
    I receive you in all that is mine.
    Give me your heart, Mary.

In his Address to the Eighth International Mariological Colloquium ([13 October 2000](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/3b329302-e09b-4e0e-9f7d-42c763c49baa/ref/2)), the Pope gave his personal testimony:

> "When I was working as a clandestine seminarian at the Solvay factory in Kraków, my spiritual director advised me to meditate on the _True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin_. Many times and with great spiritual profit I read and reread this precious little ascetical book with the blue, soda-stained cover." He added: "I then realized that I could not exclude the Mother of the Lord from my life without disregarding the will of God-the-Trinity."

His Marian program as pope — the motto, the entrustments of the world to the Immaculate Heart (1982, 1984), the encyclical _Redemptoris Mater_ (1987) — carries the Montfortan imprint (see [saints/st-john-paul-ii.md](../saints/st-john-paul-ii.md)).

### 11.3 The Conciliar Frame

Everything the devotion asserts about Mary is governed by the Second Vatican Council's charter for Marian devotion, **_Lumen Gentium_** 66–67: the Church "honestly and prudently" encourages Marian devotion; yet theologians and preachers must "refrain as much from all false exaggeration as from too summary an attitude of mind" toward the singular dignity of the Mother of God — and study her under the Scripture and the doctrine of the Fathers, "looking always to Christ, the unique Mediator," who is "the source of all truth, sanctity and piety."

## 12. The Eastern Catholic Perspective

The consecration as formulated by St. Louis de Montfort is a **Latin devotional development**, shaped by seventeenth-century French spirituality and the school of Sulpician asceticism. The twenty-three **Eastern Catholic Churches** _sui iuris_ do not possess an institutionalized "Montfort method," and nothing in the Latin formula should be presented to them as their own liturgical heritage (see [church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md)).

The **theology the devotion expresses, however, is fully shared**. The Christian East entrusts itself to the Mother of God through its own ancient and liturgically integrated channels:

- **The Akathist Hymn** (_Akathistos_, "not seated") — the great Byzantine hymn of praise to the Theotokos, sung especially on the Saturdays of the Great Fast and containing the whole mariology of the East in poetic salutations to her maternal protection.
- **The Paraklesis (Supplicatory Canon)** — the service of intercession to the Theotokos prayed in times of need, in which the faithful entrust body, soul, and all their circumstances to her protection.
- **Entrustment of persons and homes** — the Byzantine and Maronite traditions entrust children to the Theotokos at their churching, dedicate households under her protection, and commend the dying to her maternal intercession (cf. [liturgy/prayers-for-the-dead.md](../liturgy/prayers-for-the-dead.md)).
- **Daily Theotokia** — the constant refrain of Byzantine prayer, "Most Holy Theotokos, save us," and the attribution of every Christian's growth to her maternal care, express in liturgical form what Montfort expresses in devotional form.

In this sense, the Montfortan act of consecration and the Eastern practice of **commendatio** (_entrustment_) are parallel expressions of one and the same dogmatic reality — Mary's maternal mediation in the order of grace (cf. [Section 5.3](#53-marys-maternal-mediation-strictly-subordinated-to-christ)). An Eastern Catholic who adopts the Latin consecration does so as a private devotion; the Church's norm remains that popular piety should draw "with sensitivity from the liturgical tradition of one's own Church" (cf. DPPL §204 in light of the principle of liturgical integrity).

## 13. Modern Adaptations and Related Devotions

The Montfortan consecration has generated an extensive family of contemporary adaptations, each preserving the 33-day or comparable preparatory structure:

- **_33 Days to Morning Glory_** (Fr. Michael Gaitley, MIC, 2011) — the most widely used modern preparation, pairing Montfort with St. Maximilian Kolbe, Bl. Teresa of Calcutta, and St. John Paul II. Its consecration text is a contemporary rendering of the formula in [Section 9](#9-the-act-of-consecration).
- **Consecration to St. Joseph** (Fr. Donald Calloway, MIC, 2020) — applies the Montfortan program to St. Joseph as spiritual father; see [liturgy/st-joseph-prayer-for-purity.md](../liturgy/st-joseph-prayer-for-purity.md) for the related Josephite devotion.
- **The Brown Scapular** — the scapular of Our Lady of Mount Carmel is a standing, external sign of Marian consecration, "a sacred sign that signifies and disposes the wearer to live a life of Marian consecration" ([sacramentals/brown-scapular.md](../sacramentals/brown-scapular.md)); many consecrated souls wear the chain or the scapular as its abiding memorial.
- **The Five First Saturdays** — the reparatory devotion to the Immaculate Heart of Mary requested at Fatima (see [liturgy/first-saturdays-devotion.md](../liturgy/first-saturdays-devotion.md)), doctrinally classified as approved private revelation ([CCC 67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67)), complements the consecration with its program of reparation.
- **The Apostles of the Latter Times** — Montfort's prophetic pages (_True Devotion_ §§48, 114) on great saints "of the last days" who will be signed with Mary's seal have fed contemporary end-times devotional literature; their proper doctrinal weight is that of private theological opinion (cf. [Section 2](#2-doctrinal-classification-levels-of-authority), and, for the careful treatment of such themes, [eschatology/README.md](../eschatology/README.md)).

## 14. Doctrinal Cautions

The Church attaches explicit conditions to the practice, which this document reproduces for the integrity of the devotion ([DPPL §204](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/c3db4362-44df-43c0-ba26-eb53d17bb438/ref/204)):

1. **Terminology**: the term "consecration" is used with breadth and imprecision in popular practice. Since consecration proper is the offering of oneself to God whose end is God, whose characteristics are totality and perpetuity, whose guarantee is the intervention of the Church, and whose foundation is the sacraments of Baptism and Confirmation, the Directory suggests that terms such as **"entrustment"** or **"self-offering"** may sometimes express the act more rigorously. The act to Mary is "only analogous to consecration to God."
2. **Freedom**: the act must proceed "not from a passing emotion, but from a personal, free decision, matured within an accurate understanding of the dynamism of grace."
3. **Trinitarian framing**: it must be expressed "to the Father, through Christ, in the Holy Spirit, imploring the glorious intercession of Mary" — never as a terminus in Mary herself.
4. **Eucharistic distinction**: it "must be carried out outside the celebration of the Eucharistic Sacrifice, since it is an act of devotion not assimilable to the Liturgy." Entrustment to Mary is "substantially distinct from other forms of liturgical consecration."
5. **No exaggerated claims**: Montfort's strongest devotional formulas — that God gives "all graces through Mary," that she is "the sure way" — are pious theological expressions, not defined doctrine. They must be interpreted within _Lumen Gentium_ 60–62, which teaches that Mary's mediation neither adds to nor subtracts from Christ's unique mediation, and within the Council's warning against "false exaggeration" (LG 67). The ancient maxim _de Maria numquam satis_ ("of Mary, never enough") describes the inexhaustibility of her praise, not a license for doctrinal speculation beyond revelation.
6. **No binding force**: the act is a private commitment of piety. It is not a vow in the canonical sense, creates no impediment, imposes no religious obligation, and a person who neglects it after making it sins (if at all) against piety, not against justice.
