# Novena for the Poor Souls in Purgatory (24 October – 1 November)

_A nine-day devotion of suffrage for the faithful departed, closing on the eve of All Souls' Day._

The **Novena for the Poor Souls in Purgatory** is a nine-day exercise of popular piety in which the faithful ask God to complete the purification of the elect and to bring them into the joy of His presence. It is timed to end on the vigil of the **Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed** (All Souls' Day, 2 November), when the whole Church prays most explicitly for the dead.

This page documents the form of the novena published by **The Catholic Crusade** (thecatholiccrusade.com), announced on its Facebook page on 21 October 2026 for the cycle that begins on **Saturday, 24 October 2026** and concludes on **Sunday, 1 November 2026**. The novena may be prayed at any time of year; the October–November cycle is the customary one.

| Item                      | Detail                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dates (2026 cycle)**    | Saturday, 24 October – Sunday, 1 November (nine days)                        |
| **Daily structure**       | Meditation → the day's prayer → the Closing Prayers                          |
| **Intention**             | Every soul in Purgatory, known and unknown                                   |
| **Doctrinal standing**    | Popular piety (CCC 1674–1676); not a liturgical rite, and not obligatory     |
| **Indulgence**            | None attached to the novena as such — see [§6](#6-indulgences-and-suffrages) |
| **Publisher's materials** | Prayer text and daily prayer videos at thecatholiccrusade.com                |

> **Doctrinal classification.** The **duty and privilege of praying for the dead**, and the **efficacy of those prayers for the souls in Purgatory**, are dogma of the Catholic faith. The **novena** as a nine-day devotional form, its meditations, and its imagery are popular piety. Nothing in this devotion adds to the deposit of faith, and no Catholic is bound to pray it ([CCC 66–67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67); [CCC 1674–1676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1674)).

## 1. The Devotion and Its Place in Catholic Doctrine

The novena rests on three truths that the Church holds with different degrees of authority.

### 1.1 Purgatory is a dogma of the faith

The existence of a final purification for those who die in God's grace but are not yet perfectly purified is **defined doctrine**: the Second Council of Lyon (1274), the Council of Florence (_Laetentur Caeli_, [DH 1304](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1304)), and the Council of Trent, Session XXV ([DH 1820](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1820)). The Catechism states the doctrine in [CCC 1030–1032](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1030):

> "All who die in God's grace and friendship, but still imperfectly purified, are indeed assured of their eternal salvation; but after death they undergo purification, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven." ([CCC 1030](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1030))

What purgatory **is** — the nature of its pain, its "place," and its duration — Trent deliberately left undefined and forbade the preaching of uncertain and speculative questions as though they were settled doctrine. The full treatment, including the reduction of temporal punishment, is in [Purgatory: The Final Purification of the Elect](../eschatology/purgatory.md).

### 1.2 The living can help the dead

The Church's suffrages for the departed — above all the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass — are **defined** as beneficial to the souls in Purgatory (Florence, [DH 1304](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1304); Trent, Session XXV, [DH 1820](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1820)). This is the theological engine of the whole novena. The publisher's summary of the devotion states the point exactly: "Those souls cannot help themselves, but we can help them."

- The souls in Purgatory are **confirmed in grace** and assured of heaven; they can no longer sin, and they can no longer **merit** for themselves (Trent, Session VI, canon 30, [DH 1580](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1580)).
- The living, by contrast, are still in the "time of merit." Prayer, almsgiving, fasting, indulgences, and above all the Mass can be offered **on their behalf** by way of suffrage ([CCC 1032](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1032); [CCC 1471–1479](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1471)).

### 1.3 The communion of saints spans death

Prayer for the dead is an exercise of the **communion of saints** — the one Church of heaven, purgatory, and earth ([CCC 954–959](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/956)). The Catechism puts the bond in a single sentence: "Our prayer for them is capable not only of helping them, but also of making their intercession for us effective" ([CCC 958](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/para-958)). That sentence grounds the closing invocation of this novena, which asks the Holy Souls to pray for us in return (see [§7](#7-blessed-souls-pray-for-us-asking-the-holy-souls-to-intercede)).

### 1.4 Why nine days

The novena form is not of divine institution. Its model is the **nine days of prayer** that the Apostles and the Blessed Virgin spent in the Upper Room between the Ascension and Pentecost ([Acts 1:14](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=act&g=1&p=14)) — the pattern the _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (§ 155) presents as the archetype of every novena. The general theology and discipline of the novena — its kinds, its relation to the Mass, and the caution against merging devotion into the liturgy — is treated in [Novenas and Novena Masses](novenas.md).

## 2. How to Pray This Novena

The publisher's order for each of the nine days is simple:

1. **Begin with the Sign of the Cross.**
2. **Read the day's meditation** slowly, as a subject for reflection rather than information to be got through.
3. **Pray the day's prayer**, which turns the meditation into petition for oneself and for the Holy Souls.
4. **Pray the Closing Prayers** ([§5](#5-the-closing-prayers)) — one Our Father, one Hail Mary, the versicle, the Prayer to Our Suffering Savior, the invocation of the Holy Souls, and the _De Profundis_.
5. **Name your intention.** The publisher invites the one praying to mention the favour he hopes to obtain through the intercession of the Holy Souls; the intention may also be simply "for all the faithful departed, known and unknown."

**Practical notes.**

- **Have Mass offered.** The novena is a devotion; the **Holy Mass** is the preeminent suffrage for the dead. Where possible, have a Mass offered for the deceased, or attend Mass during the novena and unite your intention to it ([Mass Intentions](mass-intentions.md)).
- **Add the customary works of suffrage** where you can: a cemetery visit with prayer (indulgenced in November — [§6](#6-indulgences-and-suffrages)), almsgiving, the Rosary, or the Office of the Dead. See [Prayers for the Dying and the Dead](prayers-for-the-dead.md).
- **Pray it with others.** A novena kept in a household or a prayer group is the ordinary way this devotion has been handed on.
- **It may be prayed at any time.** The October–November cycle is customary, not required; a mourner may begin it on the day of a death or at any point afterward.

## 3. The 2026 Calendar and the Nine Meditations

Each day of this novena takes one **sorrow of the Holy Souls** as its subject. The nine meditations progress from the memory of sin to the souls' desire for the vision of God.

| Day | Date (2026)    | Meditation subject                                                                |
| --- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Sat 24 October | Their sins in life are the cause of their present torments                        |
| 2   | Sun 25 October | The time lost in life, when merits for heaven could have been gained              |
| 3   | Mon 26 October | The hideous vision of their guilt                                                 |
| 4   | Tue 27 October | Having, by their sins, displeased God Whom they so ardently love                  |
| 5   | Wed 28 October | Their ignorance of the hour of their deliverance                                  |
| 6   | Thu 29 October | Their ingratitude for the Passion of Christ and the Holy Sacrament of the Altar   |
| 7   | Fri 30 October | Their ardent desire for the Beatific Vision                                       |
| 8   | Sat 31 October | The graces they received, and the sins by which they compelled God to purify them |
| 9   | Sun 1 November | Separation from their Divine Spouse and deprivation of His Beatific Vision        |

_Sunday, 1 November 2026 is also the Solemnity of All Saints; Monday, 2 November is All Souls' Day._

## 4. The Nine Days

### Day 1 — Saturday, 24 October

**Meditation.** Manifold are the sufferings which these Blessed Souls must endure, but the greatest of all is the reflection that their sins in life are the cause of their present torments.

**Prayer.**

    O Jesus, my Savior, I have so often deserved to be cast into Hell;
    how great would be my suffering if I were now cast away
    and obliged to think that I, myself, had caused my damnation!
    I thank Thee for the patience which Thou hast endured me.
    My God, I love Thee above all things
    and I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee
    because Thou art infinite goodness.
    I would rather die than offend Thee again.
    Grant me the grace of perseverance;
    have pity on me, and at the same time on those Blessed Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 2 — Sunday, 25 October

**Meditation.** The second pain which causes these Holy Souls much suffering is the time lost in life when they might have gained merits for Heaven; and the thought that they were unable to repair this loss, because the time of life and merit is passed.

**Prayer.**

    Woe to me, unhappy being, so many years have I already spent on earth
    and have earned naught but Hell!
    I give Thee thanks, O Lord, for granting me time even now to atone for my sins.
    My good God, I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee.
    Send me Thy assistance that I may apply the time yet remaining to me
    for Thy love and service;
    have compassion on me, and, at the same time, on the Holy Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 3 — Monday, 26 October

**Meditation.** Another great pain of the Holy Souls is caused by the hideous vision of their guilt, for which they now suffer. In this life the hideousness of sin is not seen as in the life to come; and this is one of the greatest sufferings in Purgatory.

**Prayer.**

    O my God! because Thou art infinite goodness,
    I love Thee above all things,
    and repent with my whole heart of my offenses against Thee.
    Grant me the grace of holy perseverance.
    Have compassion on me, and, at the same time, on the Holy Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 4 — Tuesday, 27 October

**Meditation.** The pain that afflicts these Holy Souls most, the spouses of Jesus, is the thought of having, during life, by their sins, displeased God, Whom they so ardently love. Some penitents have felt so much pain and sorrow in thinking of their offenses, that they died of grief. The Souls in Purgatory understand far better than we do the claim that God has to our love; they love Him with all their strength. Hence, at the thought of having offended Him during life, they experience pain that surpasses all other pain.

**Prayer.**

    O my God! because Thou art infinite goodness,
    I am sorry with my whole heart for having offended Thee.
    I would rather die than ever offend Thee again.
    Give me holy perseverance;
    have pity on me, and have pity on those Holy Souls
    that burn in the cleansing fire and who love Thee with all their hearts.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 5 — Wednesday, 28 October

**Meditation.** Another great suffering of these Holy Souls is their ignorance of the hour of their deliverance from Purgatory. Though they are certain of being released one day, the uncertainty of the hour of when their Purgatorial term will end gives them great pain.

**Prayer.**

    Woe to me, miserable being, if Thou, O Lord, shall cast me into eternal Hell;
    for from that dungeon of eternal pain there is no deliverance.
    I love Thee above all things, O infinite God,
    and I am sincerely sorry for having ever offended Thee.
    Grant me the grace of holy perseverance.
    Have compassion on me, and, at the same time on the Holy Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 6 — Thursday, 29 October

**Meditation.** The Holy Souls are, indeed, comforted by the recollection of the Passion of Jesus Christ, and the Holy Sacrament of the Altar, since they know they are saved by the Passion of Jesus Christ, and have received, and still receive, so much consolation from Holy Masses and Holy Communions. Nevertheless, they are greatly pained by the recollection of their ingratitude for these two great gifts of the love of Jesus Christ.

**Prayer.**

    O my Divine Redeemer, Thou didst die for me on the Cross
    and hast so often united Thyself with me in Holy Communion,
    and I have repaid Thee only with ingratitude.
    Now, however, I love Thee above all things, O supreme God;
    and I am more grieved at my offenses against Thee than at any other evil.
    I would rather die than offend Thee again.
    Grant me the grace of holy perseverance.
    Have compassion on me, and, at the same time, on the Holy Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 7 — Friday, 30 October

**Meditation.** A further great sorrow of these Holy Souls consists in their ardent desire for the Beatific Vision. Slowly and painfully the moments of their Purgatorial imprisonment pass by; for they love God deeply, and desire to be delivered from their sad state in order to praise Him forever.

**Prayer.**

    O God, Father of Mercy, satisfy this, their ardent desire!
    Send them Thy holy Angel to announce to them
    that Thou, their Father, art now reconciled with them
    through the suffering and death of Jesus,
    and that the moment of their deliverance has arrived.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 8 — Saturday, 31 October

**Meditation.** Another bitter sorrow of these Souls is caused by their reflection that God had bestowed upon them so many graces not granted to others, and that they compelled Him, by their sins, to condemn them to these sufferings. They had deserved Hell, but were pardoned and saved only by the mercy of God.

**Prayer.**

    O my God! I also am one of these ungrateful beings,
    having received so many graces, and yet despised Thy love;
    and deserve to be cast by Thee into Hell.
    But Thy infinite goodness has spared me until now.
    Therefore, I now love Thee above all things,
    and I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee.
    Grant me the grace of holy perseverance.
    Have compassion on me, and, at the same time, on the Holy Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.

### Day 9 — Sunday, 1 November (All Saints)

**Meditation.** Great are all the sufferings of the Holy Souls in Purgatory: the fire, the grief, the darkness; the uncertainty of the time of their deliverance from prison. But the greatest of all these sorrows is this: that these Holy Souls are separated from their Divine Spouse, and deprived of His Beatific Vision.

**Prayer.**

    O my God! how was it possible that I,
    for so many years, have borne tranquilly the separation from Thee and Thy holy grace!
    Henceforth, I shall love Thee above all things.
    I am deeply sorry for having offended Thee.
    Grant me the grace of holy perseverance,
    and do not permit that I should ever again fall into sin.
    Have compassion on the Holy Souls suffering in Purgatory.
    I pray Thee, moderate these sufferings; shorten the time of their misery;
    call them soon unto Thee in Heaven,
    that they may behold Thee face to face, and forever love Thee.
    O Mary, Mother of God, come to their assistance with thy powerful intercession.
    And pray for us also who are still in danger of eternal damnation.

## 5. The Closing Prayers

The publisher directs that these prayers follow the day's meditation and prayer on each of the nine days.

**One Our Father and one Hail Mary** for the Holy Souls. (Texts: [Foundational Daily Prayers](foundational-prayers.md).)

**Versicle.**

    On Thy Spouses have compassion,
    on these suffering children Thine;
    make these Holy Souls partakers
    of Thy happiness Divine.

**Prayer to Our Suffering Savior for the Souls in Purgatory.**

    O most sweet Jesus, through the bloody sweat which Thou didst suffer
    in the Garden of Gethsemane, have mercy on these Blessed Souls.
    Have mercy on them.

    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

    O most sweet Jesus, through the pains which Thou didst suffer
    during Thy most cruel scourging, have mercy on them.
    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

    O most sweet Jesus, through the pains which Thou didst suffer
    in Thy most painful crowning with thorns, have mercy on them.
    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

    O most sweet Jesus, through the pains which Thou didst suffer
    in carrying Thy cross to Calvary, have mercy on them.
    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

    O most sweet Jesus, through the pains which Thou didst suffer
    during Thy most cruel Crucifixion, have mercy on them.
    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

    O most sweet Jesus, through the pains which Thou didst suffer
    in Thy most bitter agony on the Cross, have mercy on them.
    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

    O most sweet Jesus, through the immense pain which Thou didst suffer
    in breathing forth Thy Blessed Soul, have mercy on them.
    R. Have mercy on them, O Lord.

_Here the one praying may name the favour he hopes to obtain from God during the novena through the intercession of the Holy Souls._

**Invocation of the Holy Souls.**

    Blessed Souls, we have prayed for thee;
    we entreat thee, who are so dear to God,
    and who are secure of never losing Him,
    to pray for us miserable sinners,
    who are in danger of being damned, and of losing God forever. Amen.

**The _De Profundis_ (Psalm 129/130).**

    Out of the depths I cry to You, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice!
    Let Your ears be attentive to my voice in supplication:
    If You, O Lord, mark iniquities, Lord, who can stand?
    But with You is forgiveness, that You may be revered.
    I trust in the Lord; my soul trusts in His word.
    My soul waits for the Lord, more than sentinels wait for the dawn.
    More than sentinels wait for the dawn, let Israel wait for the Lord;
    for with the Lord is kindness and with Him is plenteous redemption;
    and He will redeem Israel from all their iniquities.

The same psalm, with notes on its private use, is given in [Prayers for the Dying and the Dead](prayers-for-the-dead.md) (§ 7.4).

**Attribution note.** The publisher attributes the **Prayer to Our Suffering Savior for the Souls in Purgatory** to **St. Alphonsus Liguori** (1696–1787) and notes that it is often prayed as a nine-day novena for a particular intention. The attribution is traditional and devotional; it is not a matter of magisterial definition. The nine daily meditations and prayers are presented by the publisher **without a named author** and are in the traditional nineteenth-century devotional register (thee/thou address, "spouses of Jesus," the "prison" of Purgatory); they should be read as pious meditation, not as doctrinal definition.

## 6. Indulgences and Suffrages

### 6.1 No indulgence is attached to the novena as such

Neither the publisher nor the Church attaches an indulgence to this nine-day exercise. As with all devotions, the fruit of the novena is the charity with which it is prayed, and its value for the dead lies in its being joined to the Church's one sacrifice ([CCC 1676](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1676); [Novenas and Novena Masses § 9](novenas.md#9-indulgences-and-novenas)).

### 6.2 The _De Profundis_ and its "partial indulgence"

The publisher's page prints the _De Profundis_ with the parenthetical note "(Carries a partial indulgence)." That note reflects an **older grant** and should not be repeated as current law without qualification:

- An **1887 rescript** of the Sacred Congregation of Indulgences recorded a grant of **fifty days** for devoutly reciting Psalm 130 with the versicle _Requiem aeternam dona eis, Domine_ ([Acta Sanctae Sedis XX, p. 477](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/8aa814d9-03a2-412d-b748-599586f5229f/ref/page477)).
- The **days-and-years reckoning was abolished** by Pope St. Paul VI in _Indulgentiarum Doctrina_ (1967); from that reform onward indulgences are designated simply as plenary or partial ([CCC 1471](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1471)).
- The current _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_ **does not list the _De Profundis_ as a separately indulgenced prayer**. It lists the **invocation _Requiem aeternam_** as carrying a partial indulgence.

The practical conclusion is simple: praying the _De Profundis_ for the dead is entirely praiseworthy, and when it accompanies a **devout cemetery visit with prayer for the dead** it falls under that grant — but the indulgence is attached to the visit and the prayer for the dead, not to Psalm 130 as such.

### 6.3 The indulgences actually available in this season

The current _Enchiridion_ ([Concessiones 29](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Concessiones.%2029)) grants the following, **all applicable only to the souls in Purgatory**:

| Grant       | Work                                                                   | When                        |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Plenary** | Devout visit to a cemetery with prayer for the dead                    | Each day, 1–8 November      |
| **Plenary** | Devout visit to a church or oratory, with one Our Father and the Creed | 2 November (All Souls' Day) |
| **Partial** | Devout visit to a cemetery with prayer for the dead                    | Any day                     |
| **Partial** | Devout recitation of Lauds or Vespers from the Office of the Dead      | Any day                     |
| **Partial** | Devout invocation _Requiem aeternam dona eis, Domine…_                 | Any day                     |

The plenary grants carry the **usual conditions**: sacramental confession (within about twenty days), reception of Holy Communion, prayer for the intentions of the Holy Father, and complete detachment from all sin, including venial sin ([CCC 1478](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1478)). It is fitting to fulfill them during the novena itself, so that the novena leads to Penance and the Eucharist rather than standing apart from them.

The full catalogue of means for aiding the dead — the funeral rites, the 3rd/7th/30th/40th-day commemorations, the Office of the Dead, and the November indulgences — is in [Prayers for the Dying and the Dead](prayers-for-the-dead.md) and [Purgatory § 6](../eschatology/purgatory.md#6-reducing-temporal-punishment-while-still-living).

## 7. "Blessed Souls, pray for us": Asking the Holy Souls to Intercede

The closing invocation of this novena asks the Holy Souls to pray for the living. The practice calls for precision.

| Proposition                                                                  | Standing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The living may and should **pray for** the dead, and those prayers help them | **Dogma** — Florence ([DH 1304](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1304)); Trent ([DH 1820](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1820)); [CCC 1032](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1032) |
| Prayer for the dead can make **their intercession for us** effective         | Taught by the Catechism ([CCC 958](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/para-958))                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| The faithful may **piously ask** the Holy Souls to pray for them             | **Permissible pious practice**, long established; not a defined obligation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| The precise manner in which the Holy Souls know our requests                 | **Theological opinion** — disputed among theologians                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

The theological tradition is not unanimous on the last point. St. Thomas Aquinas held that the souls in Purgatory do not pray for the living, since they are not yet in the state of the blessed; St. Robert Bellarmine, Francisco Suárez, and St. Alphonsus Liguori held that they may, and the devotional tradition follows them. What the Church has **not** done is define the question — and Trent expressly warned against presenting uncertain matters as settled ([DH 1820](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/17f50f07-de81-4bf5-997c-f41ee830c033/ref/1820)).

Two practical consequences follow:

- **Ask in hope, not with certainty of mechanism.** "If you are able, pray for me" is the theologically sober form of the request.
- **Keep the direction of the devotion clear.** The primary act is **prayer for the dead**; asking their prayers is a secondary and subordinate fruit of the communion of saints — and quite distinct from the **invocation of canonized saints**, whose intercession and public cult the Church has formally approved ([CCC 956, 2683](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/956)).

## 8. Cautions and Pastoral Notes

- **The imagery is not the doctrine.** Fire, darkness, "prison," the "hour of deliverance," and the "Purgatorial term" are the vocabulary of a devout tradition, not definitions. Trent left the nature and duration of the purgatorial pain undefined, and "days and years" have never measured it. Read the meditations as aids to compunction, not as a description of a place with a timetable ([Purgatory § 7 and § 10](../eschatology/purgatory.md#7-time-in-purgatory--a-necessary-clarification)).
- **No one on earth knows the state of a particular soul.** The Church canonizes saints; she has never declared any human being to be in hell, and she does not declare particular souls to be in Purgatory. Pray for all the faithful departed, named and unnamed, and entrust the judgment to God.
- **The Mass outranks every devotion.** The novena is an addition to the Church's suffrage, never a substitute for having Mass offered or for the funeral rites ([Mass Intentions](mass-intentions.md); [CCC 1371](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1371)).
- **Guard against superstition.** Nine days of prayer do not oblige God, and the devotion is not a mechanism that releases a soul on the ninth day. The number nine has no power apart from faith, hope, and charity ([Novenas and Novena Masses § 11](novenas.md#11-pastoral-notes-and-cautions)).
- **Do not let the novena replace the sacraments.** Its most fruitful form leads to Confession and Holy Communion, especially in the November octave.
- **Keep charity at the centre.** An unremembered soul, a soul for whom no one prays, and a soul one has wronged in life are all fitting intentions for this novena.

## 9. Eastern Catholic Perspective

Prayer for the departed is the common inheritance of **all 24 _sui iuris_ Churches**. What differs is the form of the suffrage and the theological vocabulary:

| Latin practice (this novena)                                   | Eastern Catholic analogue                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Nine-day private novena for the dead                           | The **Panikhida** (memorial service), served on the 3rd, 9th, 40th day, and anniversaries; the **40-day** memorial                                                                               |
| Novena before All Souls' Day                                   | The **Soul Saturdays** (Ψυχοσάββατο), especially the Saturday of Meatfare Week before Lent                                                                                                       |
| Indulgences applied to the dead by way of suffrage             | The offering of the **Divine Liturgy** for the departed; **koliva** (boiled wheat, [1 Corinthians 15:36–38](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1co&g=15&p=36)) blessed and distributed; almsgiving |
| Purification expressed as the remission of temporal punishment | Purification expressed as **healing, illumination, and growth into _theosis_** (_Christ – Our Pascha_ § 250; see [Eastern Catholic Churches](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md))    |

The **indulgence system is a discipline of the Latin Church**; the Eastern Catholic Churches do not as a rule grant indulgences, and their suffrages are liturgical and ascetical. A Latin-rite Catholic praying this novena for Eastern Catholic departed is simply exercising the communion of saints in the Latin form; an Eastern Catholic who wishes to pray it should feel free to substitute the Panikhida and the Divine Liturgy, which are the fuller and more proper expressions of the same faith. See [Eastern Catholic Churches](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md) and the comparison in [Purgatory § 9](../eschatology/purgatory.md#9-the-eastern-catholic-perspective).
