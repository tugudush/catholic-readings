# The Hour of Great Mercy (3:00 PM)

## Theological Foundation

The **Hour of Great Mercy** — also called the **Hour of Mercy** or the **Three O'Clock Prayer** — is a daily devotion revealed by **our Lord Jesus Christ** to **St. Maria Faustina Kowalska** (1905–1938). It calls the faithful to pause each afternoon at **3:00 p.m.** to meditate on the Passion of Christ and to implore Divine Mercy for the whole world.

The hour of 3:00 p.m. is sacred because it recalls the hour of our Lord's death on the Cross. As the Gospel of Mark records: _"It was the third hour when they crucified him"_ (Mark 15:25). At this hour, Christ offered His life in atonement for the sins of the world, and from His pierced side flowed Blood and Water — the fount of mercy and the source of the sacraments.

For the broader context of the Divine Mercy revelations — including the Image, the Chaplet, the Feast of Mercy, and the life of St. Faustina — see [miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md](../miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md). For the full text and method of the Chaplet, see [liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md](chaplet-divine-mercy.md).

## The Revelation to St. Faustina

Jesus gave detailed instructions regarding the 3:00 p.m. hour on several occasions in St. Faustina's _Diary: Divine Mercy in My Soul_:

### The Call to Prayer (Diary, § 1320)

> _"At three o'clock, implore My mercy, especially for sinners; and, if only for a brief moment, immerse yourself in My Passion, particularly in My abandonment at the moment of agony. This is the hour of great mercy. In this hour, I will refuse nothing to the soul that makes a request of Me in virtue of My Passion."_

### Three Ways to Observe the Hour (Diary, § 1572)

Jesus provided three options, adapted to the duties and circumstances of each person:

> _"As often as you hear the clock strike the third hour, immerse yourself completely in My mercy, adoring and glorifying it; invoke its omnipotence for the whole world, and particularly for poor sinners; for at that moment mercy was opened wide for every soul. In this hour you can obtain everything for yourself and for others for the asking; it was the hour of grace for the whole world — mercy triumphed over justice."_

> _"My daughter, try your best to make the Stations of the Cross in this hour, provided that your duties permit it; and if you are not able to make the Stations of the Cross, then at least step into the chapel for a moment and adore, in the Most Blessed Sacrament, My Heart, which is full of mercy; and should you be unable to step into the chapel, immerse yourself in prayer there where you happen to be, if only for a very brief instant."_

## How to Observe the Hour of Great Mercy

Our Lord's instructions allow for flexibility based on one's state in life and daily duties. The following table summarizes the three options He gave, in order of preference:

| Option | Practice                                                                                                       | Requirement                                    |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **1**  | Make the **Stations of the Cross**, meditating on the Lord's Passion                                           | Suitable when time and duties permit           |
| **2**  | Visit the **Blessed Sacrament** in the chapel and adore the Merciful Heart of Jesus                            | Suitable when a church or chapel is accessible |
| **3**  | **Brief mental prayer** wherever you happen to be — pause for a moment and turn your heart to Christ's Passion | Always possible; the minimum asked of everyone |

### Essential Elements in Every Case

Regardless of which option is chosen, every observance of the Hour of Great Mercy includes:

- **Turning the mind to Christ's Passion**, especially His abandonment in agony
- **Imploring Divine Mercy**, particularly for poor sinners
- **Offering a prayer of entreaty**, trusting in the promise that nothing asked in virtue of the Passion will be refused

## Short Prayers for the Hour of Mercy

For those unable to make the Stations of the Cross or visit the Blessed Sacrament, the following brief aspirations may be used (as suggested by the official Divine Mercy website):

    Jesus, Mercy.

    Jesus, for the sake of Your sorrowful Passion,
    have mercy on us and on the whole world.

Other traditional prayers suitable for this hour include the Chaplet of Divine Mercy (see [chaplet-divine-mercy.md](chaplet-divine-mercy.md) for the full text) or simply reciting the **Eternal Father** prayer:

    Eternal Father,
    I offer You the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity
    of Your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ,
    in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.

## The Three O'Clock Prayer (Popular Formula)

A widely used form of the 3:00 p.m. prayer — especially common in the Philippines and among Filipino Catholic communities worldwide — condenses elements of the Chaplet of Divine Mercy into a brief, structured prayer suitable for daily communal recitation. This version was popularized through the _3 O'Clock Prayer_ devotion promoted by the late Fr. Federico G. Villanueva, S.J., and has become a daily habit for many families.

The prayer follows this sequence:

    You died, Jesus, but the source of life flowed out for souls,
    and the ocean of mercy opened up for the whole world.
    O Fountain of Life, immeasurable Divine Mercy,
    cover the whole world and empty Yourself out upon us.

    O Blood and Water, which flowed out from the Heart of Jesus
    as a fountain of mercy for us, I trust in You!

    Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One,
    have mercy on us and on the whole world.

_(Repeat "Holy God" three times)_

    Amen.

    Jesus, King of Mercy, I trust in You.

This prayer may be prayed individually or as a group, and it fulfills our Lord's request that the faithful pause at 3:00 p.m. to implore Divine Mercy, even when a fuller observance (Stations of the Cross, adoration, or the complete Chaplet) is not possible.

## The Promises Attached to This Hour

St. Faustina recorded the following promises of our Lord concerning the Hour of Great Mercy:

- **Nothing refused**: _"In this hour, I will refuse nothing to the soul that makes a request of Me in virtue of My Passion."_ (Diary, § 1320)
- **Everything obtainable**: _"In this hour you can obtain everything for yourself and for others for the asking; it was the hour of grace for the whole world."_ (Diary, § 1572)
- **Mercy opened wide**: _"At that moment mercy was opened wide for every soul."_ (Diary, § 1572)
- **Mercy triumphs**: _"It was the hour of grace for the whole world — mercy triumphed over justice."_ (Diary, § 1572)

The graces of this hour are obtained **in virtue of the Passion of Christ** — that is, through the infinite merits of His suffering and death. The condition is a sincere turning to God in trust and the desire to obtain mercy for oneself and for others.

## Relationship to the Chaplet of Divine Mercy

The **Chaplet of Divine Mercy** is one of the recommended ways to observe the Hour of Great Mercy, but the two are not identical:

- The **Chaplet** is a specific prayer formula that may be prayed at **any time** for any intention.
- The **Hour of Great Mercy** is a **daily devotional hour** (3:00 p.m.) that can be observed by the Chaplet, the Stations of the Cross, adoration of the Blessed Sacrament, or a brief prayer — whichever one's duties permit.

In practice, praying the Chaplet at 3:00 p.m. combines both devotions and is widely encouraged, but those unable to pray the full Chaplet may still observe the Hour of Mercy with a simple aspiration.

## Scriptural Foundation

The 3:00 p.m. hour is deeply rooted in the Gospel accounts of the Passion:

- **Mark 15:25**: _"It was the third hour when they crucified him."_
- **Matthew 27:45–46**: _"From the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land until the ninth hour. And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, 'Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?' that is, 'My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?'"_
- **John 19:34**: _"One of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once there came out blood and water."_

The "third hour" in Jewish reckoning corresponds to about 9:00 a.m. (the hour of crucifixion), while the "ninth hour" (about 3:00 p.m.) is the hour of Christ's death. St. Faustina's revelation consistently refers to the hour of death (3:00 p.m.) as the Hour of Great Mercy.

## Eastern Catholic Practice

Eastern Catholics who observe the Hour of Great Mercy may incorporate Eastern-style prayers and hymns. The Byzantine tradition places strong emphasis on the _philanthrōpia_ (love for mankind) of God, which resonates with the Divine Mercy message. The **Trisagion** ("Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us") — already part of Eastern liturgy and also used in the Chaplet's closing doxology — is especially fitting for this hour.

Eastern Catholics may also observe the hour by praying the **Twelve Passion Gospels** or the _Akathist to the Passion of Christ_, or simply by making the Sign of the Cross with the Eastern formula and reciting the Jesus Prayer: _"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner."_
