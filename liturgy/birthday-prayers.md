# Birthday Prayers

A birthday is a natural occasion for a Christian to give thanks to God. Life itself is a gift, and the years granted to a person are a continuing proof of God's providence and mercy. The Church has no dedicated birthday liturgy, but she does provide official blessings of thanksgiving, a Mass of thanksgiving, and a rich treasury of Scriptural and traditional prayers that may be prayed on a birthday — with or without a priest present.

This document distinguishes what is **official** (a rite or blessing in the Church's liturgical books), what is **traditional** (long-standing devotional formulas and Scriptural prayers), and what is simply **popular piety** (pious customs that carry no liturgical obligation).

---

## 1. Is There an Official Catholic Prayer for a Birthday?

**Short answer:** there is no dedicated official birthday rite, but the Church does provide official blessings and a Mass of thanksgiving that can be used for a birthday.

### 1.1 What the official books do not contain

The Roman _Book of Blessings_ (_De Benedictionibus_, 1984) contains no "Order for the Blessing on a Birthday." It does provide blessings for other family occasions — such as a wedding anniversary — and prayers for the elderly, but a birthday itself has no single prescribed formula in the Church's ritual books.

### 1.2 The official blessing of thanksgiving

The closest official resource is **Chapter XL of the _Book of Blessings_**: the _Order of Blessing in Thanksgiving for Benefits Received_ (_Ordo Benedictionis in Gratiarum Actione Agenda pro Acceptis Beneficiis_, nn. 1226ff.). It is a general rite of thanksgiving that can be adapted to any occasion for which a person or family wishes to thank God — a birthday among them.

- It may be led by a **priest, a deacon, or even a layperson**, with the rites and formulas adapted to the persons and place (n. 1227).
- It is used appropriately where a custom exists of holding a celebration of thanksgiving at the end of a year (n. 1228).
- Its Scripture readings are drawn from Philippians 4:4–7, 1 Corinthians 1, Colossians 3:15–17, 1 Thessalonians 5:12–24, 1 Timothy 2:1–10, and the healing of the ten lepers in Luke 17:11–19 — the Gospel in which only one of the ten returns to give thanks.

Beyond this, a priest or deacon may simply give a **blessing on request**; blessings are sacramentals, and they dispose the faithful to receive grace and commend persons and things to God's care (CCC 1667–1673). A family may also ask for the _Order for the Blessing of a Family_, which can be celebrated in the home.

### 1.3 The Mass "For the Giving of Thanks"

The Roman Missal includes a Mass **For the Giving of Thanks** among the _Masses and Prayers for Various Needs and Occasions_ (n. 49 in the Italian typical edition; the official English translation is in the same section of the Missal). Its Collect reads:

    O Dio, che sempre ascolti con bontà
    la voce dei tuoi fedeli che sono nella tribolazione,
    ti rendiamo grazie per i tuoi benefici
    e ti supplichiamo umilmente
    perché, liberi da tutti i mali,
    possiamo sempre servirti nella gioia.
    Per il nostro Signore Gesù Cristo, tuo Figlio, che è Dio,
    e vive e regna con te, nell'unità dello Spirito Santo,
    per tutti i secoli dei secoli.

A rendering in English:

    O God, who always graciously hear the voice
    of your faithful who are in tribulation,
    we give you thanks for your benefits
    and humbly beseech you that,
    freed from every evil,
    we may always serve you in joy.
    Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son,
    who is God, and lives and reigns with you,
    in the unity of the Holy Spirit,
    one God, for ever and ever.

Like the other Masses in this series, it may be celebrated on days the Church permits (generally weekdays — not on Solemnities or on the Sundays of Advent, Lent, and Easter) at the direction of the local Ordinary or for a genuine pastoral benefit. The simplest way to use it is to ask the parish to offer a **Mass of thanksgiving** on or near the birthday; for how Mass intentions are requested, see [Mass Intentions](mass-intentions.md).

### 1.4 Why the Church's calendar keeps so few birthdays

The Church's own way of keeping a "birthday" is instructive. From the earliest centuries, the faithful commemorated a saint on the **dies natalis** — the "heavenly birthday," the day of the saint's passage to eternal life — rather than on the day of earthly birth. Thus the _Martyrdom of Polycarp_ (c. 155 A.D.) records that the faithful gathered "to celebrate the birthday of his martyrdom" (see [The Apostolic Fathers](../church-history/apostolic-fathers.md)).

For this reason the General Roman Calendar keeps only three nativities: the **Nativity of the Lord** (December 25), the **Nativity of St. John the Baptist** (June 24), and the **Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary** (September 8). Mary's nativity, the feast popularly called "Mary's birthday," is treated in [The Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary](nativity-of-the-blessed-virgin-mary.md).

The practical lesson is not that earthly birthdays are unimportant but that they are ordered toward the greater birth — the life of grace that begins in Baptism and is brought to perfection in heaven.

## 2. The Catholic Meaning of a Birthday

A birthday prayer is first of all a prayer of **thanksgiving**, the most fundamental Christian response to God.

- **Thanksgiving is a duty and a joy.** "In all circumstances give thanks, for this is the will of God for you in Christ Jesus" (1 Thessalonians 5:18). The Catechism teaches that "thanksgiving characterizes the prayer of the Church" and that "every event and need can become an offering of thanksgiving" (CCC 2637–2638; cf. Philippians 4:6–7).
- **Life is a gift.** "You formed my inmost being; you knit me in my mother's womb. I praise you, because I am wonderfully made" (Psalm 139:13–14). Pope Benedict XVI, meditating on this psalm, described the human being as "the loftiest, most marvellous reality of the entire universe," a "wonder" of God, upon whom the Creator turns his loving gaze even in the womb (General Audience, 28 December 2005, _Psalm 139 [138]: The wonder of my being_).
- **Thanksgiving is what the Eucharist _is_.** The very name "Eucharist" means thanksgiving (CCC 1328–1332). The Eastern Catholic Catechism _Christ – Our Pascha_ puts it plainly: "The mystery of the Eucharist takes its name from this prayer of thanksgiving in which we express gratitude for all that we have received from God" (§ 376).
- **A birthday is not magic.** No prayer on a birthday obtains a special blessing automatically, and no particular formula is required. The grace of God is received through faith, the sacraments, and charity — not through the recitation of a birthday formula.

## 3. A Prayer for One's Own Birthday

This prayer may be prayed alone or with family, at Mass, before a meal, or before the cake is cut. It can be followed by the _Our Father_, the _Hail Mary_, and the _Glory Be_ (see [Foundational Daily Prayers](foundational-prayers.md)).

    Lord God, Father of all life,
    I thank You for the gift of my life
    and for every grace You have given me
    in the years You have granted me.
    Thank You for Your mercy toward my sins,
    for Your providence in my needs,
    and for the people who have loved me.

    As I begin another year,
    keep me in faith, hope, and charity.
    Let me live this day and every day for Your glory
    and for the good of my neighbor.
    Grant that I may grow in wisdom and grace,
    and come at last to the joy of eternal life.

    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

Two psalms are especially fitting to accompany this prayer: **Psalm 103[102]** ("Bless the Lord, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits") and **Psalm 139[138]** ("I praise you, because I am wonderfully made"). The great hymn of praise, the **Te Deum**, is also appropriate (see [The Liturgy of the Hours](liturgy-of-the-hours.md)), as is the **Magnificat** of Our Lady.

---

## 4. A Prayer for Another Person's Birthday

When praying for someone else — a child, parent, spouse, friend, or benefactor — replace **N.** with that person's name. For a woman, "handmaid" and feminine pronouns may be used; for a man, "servant" and masculine pronouns.

    Almighty and eternal God,
    we thank You for N.,
    whom You have brought to this day.
    Bless N. with health of body and soul,
    with wisdom, peace, and joy in Your Spirit.
    Guard N. from sin and every evil,
    keep N. faithful in Your love,
    and lead N. at last to the joy of eternal life.

    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

A birthday is also a fitting time to have a **Mass offered in thanksgiving** for the person (see [Mass Intentions](mass-intentions.md)) and to give a gift of charity in that person's name.

## 5. Traditional and Scriptural Prayers for a Birthday

### 5.1 The priestly blessing (Numbers 6:24–26)

This is the most ancient blessing in Scripture and is used in the Church's liturgy. It is a natural blessing to pray over someone on a birthday.

    The Lord bless thee, and keep thee.
    The Lord shew his face to thee, and have mercy on thee.
    The Lord turn his countenance to thee, and give thee peace.

In Latin:

    Benedícat tibi Dóminus, et custódiat te.
    Illúminet Dóminus fáciem suam super te, et misereátur tui.
    Convérta Dóminus vultum suum ad te, et det tibi pacem.

### 5.2 Scriptural prayers of thanksgiving

| Prayer                                | Reference      | Why it suits a birthday                            |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Bless the Lord, O my soul             | Psalm 103[102] | Thanksgiving for all God's benefits                |
| I praise you, wonderfully made        | Psalm 139[138] | Thanksgiving for the gift of life                  |
| I thank you, Lord, with all my heart  | Psalm 138[137] | Thanksgiving answered by God                       |
| Sing joyfully to the Lord             | Psalm 100[99]  | Joyful praise and gratitude                        |
| I lift up my eyes to the mountains    | Psalm 121[120] | Trust in God's protection for the year ahead       |
| O God, you are my God, for you I long | Psalm 63[62]   | The soul's thirst for God                          |
| The Magnificat                        | Luke 1:46–55   | Our Lady's hymn of thanksgiving                    |
| The Te Deum                           | Hymn           | The Church's great hymn of praise and thanksgiving |

### 5.3 Traditional acts of thanksgiving

- The **Anima Christi** ("Soul of Christ, sanctify me") and the other traditional prayers of thanksgiving, gathered in [Prayer After Communion](prayer-after-communion.md), may be prayed on a birthday, especially after Mass.
- The **Holy Rosary** prayed in thanksgiving — particularly the Glorious Mysteries — is a traditional way of thanking God for blessings received (see [How to Pray the Rosary](rosary/how-to-pray.md)).
- **"Ad multos annos"** ("To many years!") is a long-standing Latin acclamation of congratulation. Its Eastern counterpart is the **Polychronion** ("Many years!"), sung in the Byzantine tradition for bishops and on great occasions.

## 6. How to Keep a Catholic Birthday

- **Attend Mass**, ideally a Mass of thanksgiving, and receive Holy Communion in a state of grace.
- **Make a good Confession** beforehand, so that the day begins with the grace of the sacrament.
- **Give thanks in prayer**, using one of the prayers above, a psalm, or the Rosary.
- **Perform a work of mercy** — almsgiving, a gift to the poor, a visit to the sick — in gratitude for the year past.
- **Ask for a blessing**, if convenient, from a priest or deacon.
- **Mark one's Baptism.** Some families celebrate the **baptismal anniversary** as well as the birthday; a renewal of baptismal promises, or a visit to the baptismal font of one's parish, is a fitting way to return thanks for the life of grace.
- **Remember the name day.** In many Catholic cultures — Italian, Polish, Greek, Latin American, and others — the **name day** (_onomastico_), the feast of one's patron saint, is kept alongside or even in place of the birthday, since it honors the saint whose intercession accompanies one's life.
- **Avoid superstition.** Birthday prayers and customs are acts of faith, not charms. Nothing is gained by candles, numbers, or formulas in themselves apart from faith, prayer, and charity.

## 7. Latin and Eastern Catholic Dimensions

The Latin Church blesses a birthday chiefly through the sacramental of a **blessing of thanksgiving** and the **Mass For the Giving of Thanks**.

The Eastern Catholic Churches, whose tradition the word "Eucharist" holds in common, have their own parallel forms of thanksgiving:

- The **Moleben of Thanksgiving** — a service of supplication and gratitude, often served by a priest, that can be requested for a family or personal occasion.
- The **Akathist "Glory to God for All Things,"** a beloved Eastern hymn of thanksgiving.
- The **Anaphora** of the Divine Liturgy, whose great prayer of thanksgiving precedes all petition (cf. _Christ – Our Pascha_ § 376). In the Byzantine tradition the Polychronion ("Many years!") is sung to wish long life, especially for hierarchs.
- The blessing of a **family** and of the **home**, which in Eastern practice is closely associated with the celebration of feasts and family occasions.

Eastern Catholics should follow the customs and pastoral guidance of their own eparchy and parish.

## 8. Levels of Authority

- **Divine Revelation / Dogma.** That life is a gift from God and that thanksgiving is owed to Him belongs to the deposit of faith (see 1 Thessalonians 5:18; CCC 2637–2638). The Eucharist as the Church's supreme act of thanksgiving is a matter of faith (CCC 1328–1332).
- **Ecclesiastical Discipline / Pastoral Practice.** The content of the _Book of Blessings_ and of the Missal's "Masses and Prayers for Various Needs and Occasions," and the days on which the Mass _For the Giving of Thanks_ may be celebrated, are governed by changeable liturgical law.
- **Popular Piety.** A birthday prayer is a devotional practice, not part of the Church's public worship. No particular birthday prayer is required, and no special indulgence is attached to birthdays as such. Catholics are free to use, adapt, or omit these prayers.
