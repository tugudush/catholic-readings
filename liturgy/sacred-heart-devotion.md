# The Devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus

## Theological Foundations

### The Heart of Christ: Love Made Visible

The devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus is a form of Catholic piety that contemplates **Christ's personal love** for the Father and for humanity — a love made physically visible in His **pierced side**, from which blood and water flowed (John 19:34). It is not devotion to a physical organ in isolation; rather, the Sacred Heart denotes the entire mystery of Christ, His Person considered in its most intimate essence, namely His **infinite charity** as the principal means of salvation and sanctification.[^1]

> _"The devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus is a cultic form of the prophetic and evangelic gaze of the Church on Him whom they have pierced."_[^2]

### Scriptural Roots

The devotion is anchored in several New Testament passages:

- **John 19:34–37**: The piercing of Christ's side, from which blood and water flow — the fountain of the sacraments and the visible sign of His love poured out for humanity.
- **John 7:37–39**: Christ's invitation — _"If any one thirst, let him come to me and drink"_ — interpreted by the Fathers as referring to the living water flowing from His side.
- **Matthew 11:29**: _"Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart"_ — the Heart of Christ as the model of interior disposition.
- **Ephesians 3:17–19**: St. Paul's prayer that Christ may dwell in our hearts through faith, and that we may _"know the love of Christ which surpasses knowledge."_

### Why the Heart?

The Heart is chosen as the symbol of Christ's love because in Sacred Scripture and human experience, the heart represents the **centre of the person** — the seat of intellect, will, emotions, and love. In Christ, the heart is the natural symbol of His **divine-human love** for the Father and for mankind. As Pope Pius XII teaches in _Haurietis Aquas_, one may "mount" from what is bodily (the Heart) to the love signified and then to adoration of divine love in the Word Incarnate, because the divine and human natures are united in the **one Person** of Christ.[^3]

The Sacred Heart is thus honoured as the **natural and expressive symbol** of Christ's abiding love, linked to His ongoing charity flowing through His Mystical Body, the Church.[^4]

## History of the Devotion

### Early and Medieval Precursors

While the devotion in its modern form emerged in the seventeenth century, its roots are patristic and medieval:

- **Church Fathers** (St. Augustine, St. Jerome, St. John Chrysostom) frequently commented on the wounded side of Christ as the fountain of grace and the door of salvation.
- **St. Bernard of Clairvaux** (1090–1153) wrote extensively on the "wounded Heart" of Christ as the refuge of sinners.
- **St. Francis of Assisi** (1181–1226) and **St. Bonaventure** (1221–1274) promoted contemplation of Christ's Passion and the wound in His side.
- **St. Gertrude the Great** (1256–1302) and **St. Mechtilde of Hackeborn** (1241–1298) recorded mystical experiences centred on the Heart of Jesus.
- **The Venerable Lanspergius** (1489–1539) and **St. Francis de Sales** (1567–1622) further developed the language of devotion to Christ's Heart, presenting His humility, gentleness, and mercy as a model.[^5]

### St. John Eudes (1601–1680)

St. John Eudes promoted the **liturgical cult** of the Sacred Heart[^5] and composed the first liturgical office in its honour. He is credited with unifying the scattered threads of the devotion into a coherent theological and liturgical form, and his work prepared the ground for the revelations at Paray-le-Monial.

### The Revelations at Paray-le-Monial (1673–1675)

Between 1673 and 1675, **St. Margaret Mary Alacoque** (1647–1690), a Visitation sister in Paray-le-Monial, France, received four major apparitions of Jesus Christ in which He revealed His Heart burning with flames, surrounded by a crown of thorns and surmounted by a cross. Christ expressed deep sorrow over humanity's ingratitude and coldness, particularly toward the Sacrament of the Holy Eucharist, and requested specific devotional practices (see below).

St. Margaret Mary's spiritual director, **St. Claude de la Colombière, S.J.** (1641–1682), recognized the authenticity of her mystical experiences and helped document and spread the devotion.

_For the full account of these apparitions, see [miracles/prophecies-visions/sacred-heart-st-margaret-mary.md](../miracles/prophecies-visions/sacred-heart-st-margaret-mary.md)._

### Response to Jansenism

The devotion flourished as an antidote to **Jansenism**, a rigorist theological movement that emphasized divine justice and human unworthiness to the point of discouraging frequent reception of Holy Communion. The Sacred Heart devotion, by contrast, aroused **love and trust** in Christ's infinite mercy and encouraged frequent, devout Communion.[^5]

## Magisterial Documents

### _Annum Sacrum_ (Pope Leo XIII, 1899)

On June 11, 1899, Pope Leo XIII solemnly consecrated the entire human race to the Sacred Heart of Jesus, declaring it the greatest act of his pontificate. The encyclical grounds the consecration in Christ's universal kingship: _"His empire extends not only over Catholic nations... but also over all those who are deprived of the Christian faith."_ The consecration was accompanied by the prayer _"Most sweet Jesus, Redeemer of the human race"_ (see below).

### _Miserentissimus Redemptor_ (Pope Pius XI, 1928)

This encyclical promoted the annual liturgical feast and the practice of the **Act of Reparation** to the Sacred Heart, calling the faithful to make amends for the offenses committed against Christ's love, especially by those who have consecrated themselves to Him.

### _Haurietis Aquas_ (Pope Pius XII, 1956)

The definitive theological encyclical on the Sacred Heart, issued on the centenary of the extension of the feast to the universal Church. Its key teachings include:

- The devotion is profoundly connected to the mystery of love — the "foundation and culmination" of the Incarnation and Redemption.[^6]
- The Heart is the **natural symbol** of Christ's divine-human love, and its adoration is an act of religion "of high order," calling for self-dedication to the divine Redeemer.[^7]
- The devotion fosters **consecration, reparation, and apostolic zeal**.
- Nothing prevents adoring the Sacred Heart as the natural and expressive symbol of Christ's abiding love, linked to His ongoing charity flowing through His Mystical Body.[^4]
- The devotion leads to a deeper participation in the **Eucharistic mystery** and the liturgical life of the Church.

### _Sacramentum Caritatis_ (Pope Benedict XVI, 2007)

The postsynodal apostolic exhortation on the Eucharist connects the Sacred Heart devotion to the Eucharistic mystery, presenting the pierced Heart of Christ as the source from which the sacraments flow and as the icon of God's love made sacramentally present in the Holy Eucharist.

### Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy (Congregation for Divine Worship, 2001)

This document provides pastoral guidelines for the Sacred Heart devotion, emphasizing:

- The devotion must be **harmonized with the liturgy**, with Sunday Mass remaining primary.[^8]
- First Friday devotion should not become mere credulity but should foster active faith.[^8]
- The devotion must be grounded in its **biblical origins** and connection to the "great mysteries of the faith," with emphasis on the primacy of the love of God and neighbour.[^9]

## The Solemnity of the Sacred Heart

The **Solemnity of the Sacred Heart of Jesus** is celebrated on the **Friday following the second Sunday after Pentecost** (the Friday after the Octave of Corpus Christi). It is a solemnity in the universal Roman Rite calendar, ranking liturgically above a feast or memorial.

The Mass of the Sacred Heart and the Divine Office draw on rich scriptural and patristic texts centering on the love of Christ symbolized by His Heart, the pierced side, and the saving mysteries of the Passion.

## Devotional Practices

### 1. The Nine First Fridays

The practice of receiving Holy Communion on the **First Friday of each month for nine consecutive months**, based on the "Great Promise" (the Twelfth Promise) revealed to St. Margaret Mary. Jesus promised the grace of **final perseverance** — that those who complete this devotion would not die in His disfavour or without receiving the sacraments.

The _Directory on Popular Piety_ notes that this practice historically helped renew the faithful's use of the Sacraments of Penance and the Eucharist, while also cautioning that it should be integrated into full liturgical life rather than practised as an isolated routine.[^8]

### 2. The Holy Hour

Keeping an hour of prayer in reparation every **Thursday night from 11:00 p.m. to midnight**, uniting oneself with Christ's agony in the Garden of Gethsemane. This practice was explicitly requested by Christ in the revelations to St. Margaret Mary and is an act of compassionate companionship with the suffering Saviour, seeking to console Him for the sins of the world.

### 3. The Enthronement of the Sacred Heart

A pastoral practice in which an image of the Sacred Heart is **solemnly installed** in a home, school, or parish as a sign that the household or institution is consecrated to Christ's kingship and love.[^10] The enthronement typically includes:

- A liturgical blessing of the image by a priest.
- The recitation of the Act of Consecration of the family or institution.
- The installation of the image in a place of honour.
- A commitment to live according to the Gospel and to foster family prayer.

The practice is not identical with the liturgy but is part of popular piety's way of giving visible expression to devotion — something the Church generally views as normal and beneficial when rightly ordered.[^10]

### 4. Acts of Consecration

The **personal consecration** to the Sacred Heart is identified by Pope Pius XI as "undoubtedly the principal devotional practice" regarding the Sacred Heart.[^8] Through consecration, the faithful entrust themselves entirely to Christ, acknowledging His Kingship and pledging love, fidelity, and reparation.

### 5. Acts of Reparation

Acts of reparation are prayers in which the faithful, mindful of Christ's goodness, implore mercy for offenses committed against His Sacred Heart.[^8] These respond to Christ's own lament to St. Margaret Mary over the ingratitude, coldness, and irreverence — particularly toward the Eucharist — that humanity returns for His love.

### 6. The Litany of the Sacred Heart

The **Litany of the Sacred Heart of Jesus** is a formal liturgical prayer approved for public and private use. It consists of invocations addressing Christ under titles drawn from Scripture and tradition (e.g., "Heart of Jesus, burning furnace of charity," "Heart of Jesus, source of all consolation," "Heart of Jesus, our peace and reconciliation"), each followed by the response _"Miserere nobis"_ (Have mercy on us). It concludes with a prayer for union with Christ's Heart.

### 7. Veneration of the Image

The faithful are encouraged to display and venerate an image of the Sacred Heart — typically depicting Christ with His Heart visible, encircled by flames (symbolizing consuming love), surmounted by a cross (symbolizing the Crucifixion), and crowned with thorns (symbolizing the crown placed on Christ's head and the wounds of sin). This image is a sacramental that directs the mind and heart to the reality of Christ's love.

## The Twelve Promises of the Sacred Heart

As recorded in the writings of St. Margaret Mary Alacoque, Jesus made the following promises to those who honour His Sacred Heart:

1. I will give them all the graces necessary for their state of life.
2. I will establish peace in their families.
3. I will comfort them in all their afflictions.
4. I will be their secure refuge during life, and above all, in death.
5. I will bestow abundant blessings upon all their undertakings.
6. Sinners shall find in my Heart the source and infinite ocean of mercy.
7. Lukewarm souls shall become fervent.
8. Fervent souls shall quickly mount to high perfection.
9. I will bless every place in which an image of my Heart is exposed and honoured.
10. I will give to priests the gift of touching the most hardened hearts.
11. Those who shall promote this devotion shall have their names written in my Heart.
12. **The Great Promise**: I promise you in the excessive mercy of my Heart that my all-powerful love will grant to all those who receive Holy Communion on the First Fridays in nine consecutive months the grace of final perseverance; they shall not die in my disfavour or without receiving the sacraments.

## The Act of Consecration to the Sacred Heart

The following classic formula, promulgated in connection with the consecration of the human race by Pope Leo XIII, is the standard Act of Consecration to the Sacred Heart:

> Most sweet Jesus, Redeemer of the human race, look down upon us humbly prostrate before Thine altar. We are Thine and Thine we wish to be; but to be more surely united with Thee, behold each one of us freely consecrates himself today to Thy Most Sacred Heart.
>
> Many indeed have never known Thee; many, too, despising Thy precepts, have rejected Thee. Have mercy on them all, most merciful Jesus, and draw them to Thy Sacred Heart.
>
> Be Thou King, O Lord, not only of the faithful who have never forsaken Thee, but also of the prodigal children who have abandoned Thee; grant that they may quickly return to their Father's house, lest they die of wretchedness and hunger.
>
> Be Thou King of those who are deceived by erroneous opinions, or whom discord keeps aloof, and call them back to the harbour of truth and unity of faith, so that soon there may be but one flock and one Shepherd.
>
> Be Thou King of all those who are still involved in the darkness of idolatry or of Islam, and refuse not to draw them into the light of Thy kingdom.
>
> Look, finally, with eyes of pity upon the children of that race, once Thy chosen people. Let them too, though formerly plead for mercy, at length in their turn acknowledge Thee, their Redeemer.
>
> Grant, O Lord, to Thy Church assurance of freedom and immunity from harm; grant to all nations a peaceful order; cause that from one end of the earth to the other there may be but one heart and one mind.
>
> Praise, honour and glory be to the divine Heart that wrought our salvation; to It be glory and honour for ever. Amen.
>
> — Pope Leo XIII, _Acta Apostolicae Sedis_ 17 (1925), p. 34

## The Sacred Heart and Eastern Catholic Tradition

While the devotion to the Sacred Heart in its modern form emerged in the Latin Church, its theological core — the contemplation of Christ's pierced side and the love of the Incarnate Word — is shared across the Catholic Church's 24 _sui iuris_ Churches.

In **Eastern Catholic theology**, the devotion finds resonance in:

- The **Byzantine hymnography** of the Passion, particularly the _Akathist to the Passion of Christ_ and the hymns of Holy Week, which repeatedly contemplate the lance piercing Christ's side and the blood and water flowing forth.
- The **Eastern iconographic tradition**, which depicts Christ with His side wounded, from which blood and water flow (John 19:34), and the _Nipsis_ (vigilance) tradition of guarding the heart as the seat of union with God.
- The **Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom** and **St. Basil the Great**, in which the prayer of the anaphora recalls the piercing of Christ's side and the institution of the sacraments.

The Solemnity of the Sacred Heart, however, is a specifically **Roman Rite** liturgical celebration and is not part of the Byzantine liturgical calendar, though Eastern Catholic Churches may observe it through local adaptations or devotional practices.

## The Sacred Heart and the Immaculate Heart of Mary

The Sacred Heart of Jesus and the Immaculate Heart of Mary are closely linked in Catholic piety, as Mary's heart is perfectly conformed to the Heart of her Son. The two devotions are often paired:

- The **First Saturday** devotion (requested by Our Lady of Fatima) echoes the First Friday devotion, with reparation offered to the Immaculate Heart.
- **Pope Pius XII** consecrated the Church to the Immaculate Heart in 1942; **Pope Leo XIII** had consecrated the world to the Sacred Heart in 1899.
- The **Enthronement of the Sacred Heart** in homes is often accompanied by the consecration to the Immaculate Heart.

_For the Fatima apparitions, see [miracles/marian-apparitions/fatima.md](../miracles/marian-apparitions/fatima.md)._

## Indulgences

The Church grants **partial indulgences** for various acts of piety in honour of the Sacred Heart, and a **plenary indulgence** (under the usual conditions: sacramental confession, Eucharistic communion, prayer for the Holy Father's intentions, and detachment from sin) for:

- The public recitation of the Act of Consecration or the Act of Reparation on the Solemnity of the Sacred Heart.
- The recitation of the Litany of the Sacred Heart in a church or oratory.
- The practice of the First Fridays devotion when completed with the prescribed conditions.

---

## In Summary

The devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus is a magisterially approved and deeply rooted form of Catholic piety that:

1. Contemplates **Christ's personal, burning love** for humanity, made visible in His pierced side.
2. Responds to that love with **consecration, reparation, gratitude, and faithful love**.
3. Fosters **frequent Communion** (especially First Fridays), **prayerful vigil** (the Holy Hour), and **domestic consecration** (Enthronement).
4. Is grounded in **Sacred Scripture** (John 19:34), **Christological dogma** (the hypostatic union), and **magisterial teaching** (_Haurietis Aquas_, _Annum Sacrum_).
5. Is ordered toward **Eucharistic life** and full participation in the Sacred Liturgy, especially Sunday Mass.[^9]

> _"Behold this Heart which has so loved men that it has spared nothing, even to exhausting and consuming itself, in order to testify its love."_
> — Words of Christ to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque
