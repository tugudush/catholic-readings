# The Devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus

## Theological Foundations

### The Heart of Christ: Love Made Visible

The devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus is a form of Catholic piety that contemplates **Christ's personal love** for the Father and for humanity — a love made physically visible in His **pierced side**, from which blood and water flowed (John 19:34). It is not devotion to a physical organ in isolation; rather, the Sacred Heart denotes the entire mystery of Christ, His Person considered in its most intimate essence, namely His **infinite charity** as the principal means of salvation and sanctification.[^1]

> _"The devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus is a cultic form of the prophetic and evangelic gaze of the Church on Him whom they have pierced."_[^2]

### Scriptural Roots

The devotion is anchored in several New Testament passages:

- **John 19:34–37**: The piercing of Christ's side, from which blood and water flow — the fountain of the sacraments and the visible sign of His love poured out for humanity.
- **John 7:37–39**: Christ's invitation — _"If any one thirst, let him come to me and drink"_ — interpreted by the Fathers as referring to the living water flowing from His side.
- **Matthew 11:29**: _"Learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart"_ — the Heart of Christ as the model of interior disposition.
- **Ephesians 3:17–19**: St. Paul's prayer that Christ may dwell in our hearts through faith, and that we may _"know the love of Christ which surpasses knowledge."_

### Why the Heart?

The Heart is chosen as the symbol of Christ's love because in Sacred Scripture and human experience, the heart represents the **centre of the person** — the seat of intellect, will, emotions, and love. In Christ, the heart is the natural symbol of His **divine-human love** for the Father and for mankind. As Pope Pius XII teaches in _Haurietis Aquas_, one may "mount" from what is bodily (the Heart) to the love signified and then to adoration of divine love in the Word Incarnate, because the divine and human natures are united in the **one Person** of Christ.[^3]

The Sacred Heart is thus honoured as the **natural and expressive symbol** of Christ's abiding love, linked to His ongoing charity flowing through His Mystical Body, the Church.[^4]

## History of the Devotion

### Early and Medieval Precursors

While the devotion in its modern form emerged in the seventeenth century, its roots are patristic and medieval:

- **Church Fathers** (St. Augustine, St. Jerome, St. John Chrysostom) frequently commented on the wounded side of Christ as the fountain of grace and the door of salvation.
- **St. Bernard of Clairvaux** (1090–1153) wrote extensively on the "wounded Heart" of Christ as the refuge of sinners.
- **St. Francis of Assisi** (1181–1226) and **St. Bonaventure** (1221–1274) promoted contemplation of Christ's Passion and the wound in His side.
- **St. Gertrude the Great** (1256–1302) and **St. Mechtilde of Hackeborn** (1241–1298) recorded mystical experiences centred on the Heart of Jesus.
- **The Venerable Lanspergius** (1489–1539) and **St. Francis de Sales** (1567–1622) further developed the language of devotion to Christ's Heart, presenting His humility, gentleness, and mercy as a model.[^5]

### St. John Eudes (1601–1680)

St. John Eudes promoted the **liturgical cult** of the Sacred Heart[^5] and composed the first liturgical office in its honour. He is credited with unifying the scattered threads of the devotion into a coherent theological and liturgical form, and his work prepared the ground for the revelations at Paray-le-Monial.

### The Revelations at Paray-le-Monial (1673–1675)

Between 1673 and 1675, **St. Margaret Mary Alacoque** (1647–1690), a Visitation sister in Paray-le-Monial, France, received four major apparitions of Jesus Christ in which He revealed His Heart burning with flames, surrounded by a crown of thorns and surmounted by a cross. Christ expressed deep sorrow over humanity's ingratitude and coldness, particularly toward the Sacrament of the Holy Eucharist, and requested specific devotional practices (see below).

St. Margaret Mary's spiritual director, **St. Claude de la Colombière, S.J.** (1641–1682), recognized the authenticity of her mystical experiences and helped document and spread the devotion.

_For the full account of these apparitions, see [miracles/prophecies-visions/sacred-heart-st-margaret-mary.md](../miracles/prophecies-visions/sacred-heart-st-margaret-mary.md)._

### Response to Jansenism

The devotion flourished as an antidote to **Jansenism**, a rigorist theological movement that emphasized divine justice and human unworthiness to the point of discouraging frequent reception of Holy Communion. The Sacred Heart devotion, by contrast, aroused **love and trust** in Christ's infinite mercy and encouraged frequent, devout Communion.[^5]

## Magisterial Documents

### _Annum Sacrum_ (Pope Leo XIII, 1899)

On June 11, 1899, Pope Leo XIII solemnly consecrated the entire human race to the Sacred Heart of Jesus, declaring it the greatest act of his pontificate. The encyclical grounds the consecration in Christ's universal kingship: _"His empire extends not only over Catholic nations... but also over all those who are deprived of the Christian faith."_ The consecration was accompanied by the prayer _"Most sweet Jesus, Redeemer of the human race"_ (see below).

### _Miserentissimus Redemptor_ (Pope Pius XI, 1928)

This encyclical promoted the annual liturgical feast and the practice of the **Act of Reparation** to the Sacred Heart, calling the faithful to make amends for the offenses committed against Christ's love, especially by those who have consecrated themselves to Him.

### _Haurietis Aquas_ (Pope Pius XII, 1956)

The definitive theological encyclical on the Sacred Heart, issued on the centenary of the extension of the feast to the universal Church. Its key teachings include:

- The devotion is profoundly connected to the mystery of love — the "foundation and culmination" of the Incarnation and Redemption.[^6]
- The Heart is the **natural symbol** of Christ's divine-human love, and its adoration is an act of religion "of high order," calling for self-dedication to the divine Redeemer.[^7]
- The devotion fosters **consecration, reparation, and apostolic zeal**.
- Nothing prevents adoring the Sacred Heart as the natural and expressive symbol of Christ's abiding love, linked to His ongoing charity flowing through His Mystical Body.[^4]
- The devotion leads to a deeper participation in the **Eucharistic mystery** and the liturgical life of the Church.

### _Sacramentum Caritatis_ (Pope Benedict XVI, 2007)

The postsynodal apostolic exhortation on the Eucharist connects the Sacred Heart devotion to the Eucharistic mystery, presenting the pierced Heart of Christ as the source from which the sacraments flow and as the icon of God's love made sacramentally present in the Holy Eucharist.

### _Dilexit Nos_ (Pope Francis, 2024)

The most recent magisterial treatment of the devotion, the encyclical _Dilexit Nos_ ("He Loved Us") was promulgated on **24 October 2024**. Its title echoes St. John's proclamation that "we love, because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19) and the Apostle Paul's certainty that "we are more than conquerors through him who loved us" (Romans 8:37). The encyclical presents the Sacred Heart as the privileged revelation of God's **personal, prior, and unconditional love** for every human being, and summons the faithful to a response of love and reparation.[^11]

Its principal teachings include:

- **The recovery of the "heart"**: The encyclical's first chapter returns to the biblical and theological meaning of the heart as the **inmost core of the human person** — the integrating centre of intellect, will, memory, and affections — against the reduction of the person to a single aspect or function in a technocratic and digital age.[^12]
- **The threefold love of Christ**: Contemplating the pierced Heart of Jesus, the faithful perceive both His human sentiments — meekness, gentleness, genuine friendship — and, in and through them, His **infinite divine love** for the Father and for humanity.
- **Reception of the earlier magisterium**: The encyclical explicitly gathers the teaching of Leo XIII (consecration to the Sacred Heart), Pius XI (the devotion as a "summa" of the Christian life), and Pius XII (adoration of the Sacred Heart as an outstanding expression of the worship due to Jesus Christ).[^13]
- **Reparation as "building on the ruins"**: The final chapter develops reparation as a hope-filled, concrete response to the wounds of the Church and of the world — grounded in the certainty that "every wound can be healed, however deep it may be" — following the example of St. John Paul II.[^14]
- **The world's need for the Heart of Christ**: The devotion is presented not as a private sentiment but as bearing **spiritual and social dimensions**, ordering persons and societies toward love, friendship, and solidarity in a culture that tends to dissolve human bonds.[^14]

### Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy (Congregation for Divine Worship, 2001)

This document provides pastoral guidelines for the Sacred Heart devotion, emphasizing:

- The devotion must be **harmonized with the liturgy**, with Sunday Mass remaining primary.[^8]
- First Friday devotion should not become mere credulity but should foster active faith.[^8]
- The devotion must be grounded in its **biblical origins** and connection to the "great mysteries of the faith," with emphasis on the primacy of the love of God and neighbour.[^9]

## The Solemnity of the Sacred Heart

The **Solemnity of the Sacred Heart of Jesus** is celebrated on the **Friday following the second Sunday after Pentecost** (the Friday after the Octave of Corpus Christi). It is a solemnity in the universal Roman Rite calendar, ranking liturgically above a feast or memorial.

The Mass of the Sacred Heart and the Divine Office draw on rich scriptural and patristic texts centering on the love of Christ symbolized by His Heart, the pierced side, and the saving mysteries of the Passion.

## Devotional Practices

### 1. The Nine First Fridays

The practice of receiving Holy Communion on the **First Friday of each month for nine consecutive months**, based on the "Great Promise" (the Twelfth Promise) revealed to St. Margaret Mary. Jesus promised the grace of **final perseverance** — that those who complete this devotion would not die in His disfavour or without receiving the sacraments.

The _Directory on Popular Piety_ notes that this practice historically helped renew the faithful's use of the Sacraments of Penance and the Eucharist, while also cautioning that it should be integrated into full liturgical life rather than practised as an isolated routine.[^8]

### 2. The Holy Hour

Keeping an hour of prayer in reparation every **Thursday night from 11:00 p.m. to midnight**, uniting oneself with Christ's agony in the Garden of Gethsemane. This practice was explicitly requested by Christ in the revelations to St. Margaret Mary and is an act of compassionate companionship with the suffering Saviour, seeking to console Him for the sins of the world.

Pope Pius XI explains that Christ recommended two things for this hour: approaching the altar with the purpose of **expiating sin** — a "Communion of Reparation" — and making "expiatory supplications and prayers prolonged for a whole hour, rightly called the 'Holy Hour.'"[^15]

#### When and Where

The hour is kept **Thursday night from 11:00 p.m. to midnight** because **Friday is the day of the Passion**, of which the Agony in the Garden was the night-time prelude. St. Margaret Mary herself kept the Holy Hour "from eleven till midnight on the eve of the First Friday of each month," praying lying **prostrate** to share in the mortal sadness Christ endured when abandoned by His Apostles in His Agony.[^18]

- The most traditional form is before the **Blessed Sacrament exposed** in a church or chapel, often concluded with Benediction.
- When this is not possible, the hour may be kept before a **Sacred Heart image** at home or in any quiet place, recollected in the presence of God.
- St. Margaret Mary prayed **prostrate**, as Christ lay in the Garden; **kneeling** is the more common practice. The interior disposition of love and reparation matters more than the posture.

#### The Order of the Holy Hour

The following is a traditional order for the hour, with the prayers to be prayed at each step. It is adaptable to circumstances.

**1. Preparation (11:00–11:05)**

Quiet the mind, kneel or prostrate, and begin with the Sign of the Cross. A suggested opening prayer, placing oneself in Gethsemane with Christ:

    In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

    My Lord Jesus Christ, in the Garden of Gethsemane Thou didst ask Thy Apostles
    to watch one hour with Thee. I come now to keep that hour with Thee,
    to console Thy Sacred Heart, and to make reparation for my sins
    and for the sins of the whole world. Amen.

**2. The Gospel of the Agony (11:05–11:15)**

Read the account of Christ's agony slowly and prayerfully, from the Gospel of St. Matthew:

    Then Jesus went with them to a place called Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples,
    "Sit here, while I go yonder and pray." And taking with him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee,
    he began to be sorrowful and troubled. Then he said to them,
    "My soul is very sorrowful, even to death; remain here, and watch with me."
    And going a little farther he fell on his face and prayed,
    "My Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as thou wilt."
    And he came to the disciples and found them sleeping; and he said to Peter,
    "So, could you not watch with me one hour? Watch and pray that you may not enter into temptation;
    the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak."
    Again, for the second time, he went away and prayed,
    "My Father, if this cannot pass unless I drink it, thy will be done."
    And again he came and found them sleeping, for their eyes were heavy.
    So, leaving them again, he went away and prayed for the third time, saying the same words.
    Then he came to the disciples and said to them,
    "Are you still sleeping and taking your rest? Behold, the hour is at hand,
    and the Son of man is betrayed into the hands of sinners. Rise, let us be going; see, my betrayer is at hand."
    (Matthew 26:36–46)

**3. Meditation on Gethsemane (11:15–11:30)**

Contemplate, point by point, in silence or spoken meditation:

- **Christ's threefold prayer** — He returns three times to the Father, submitting His will each time: "Father, if it be possible, let this chalice pass from Me. Nevertheless, let not My will be done, but Thine."
- **His sweat of blood** — "And being in an agony he prayed more earnestly; and his sweat became like great drops of blood falling down upon the ground" (Luke 22:44).
- **The sleeping Apostles** — and His gentle rebuke, "Could you not watch one hour with me?" (Matthew 26:40). In this hour you are the one who stays awake with Him.
- **The causes of His agony** — His clear foreknowledge of the Passion, the sins of the whole world, and men's ingratitude for the blessings of Redemption.

Pray, in reparation, the Our Father, the Hail Mary, and the Glory Be:

    Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be Thy name; Thy kingdom come;
    Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread;
    and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us;
    and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

    Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee; blessed art thou among women,
    and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God,
    pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

    Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.
    As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

**4. The Prayer of Reparation (11:30–11:40)**

The official Prayer of Reparation, published by Pope Pius XI in connection with the encyclical _Miserentissimus Redemptor_:[^16]

    O sweetest Jesus, whose overflowing charity towards men is most ungratefully repaid
    by such great forgetfulness, neglect and contempt,
    see, prostrate before Thy altars, we strive by special honour
    to make amends for the wicked coldness of men
    and the contumely with which Thy most loving Heart is everywhere treated.
    At the same time, mindful of the fact that we too have sometimes not been free from unworthiness,
    and moved therefore with most vehement sorrow, in the first place we implore Thy mercy on us,
    being prepared by voluntary expiation to make amends for the sins we have ourselves committed,
    and also for the sins of those who wander far from the way of salvation,
    whether because, being obstinate in their unbelief, they refuse to follow Thee as their shepherd and leader,
    or because, spurning the promises of their Baptism, they have cast off the most sweet yoke of Thy law.
    We now endeavour to expiate all these lamentable crimes together,
    and it is also our purpose to make amends for each one of them severally:
    for the want of modesty in life and dress, for impurities,
    for so many snares set for the minds of the innocent, for the violation of feast days,
    for the horrid blasphemies against Thee and Thy saints,
    for the insults offered to Thy Vicar and to the priestly order,
    for the neglect of the Sacrament of Divine love or its profanation by horrible sacrileges,
    and lastly for the public sins of nations which resist the rights and the teaching authority of the Church
    which Thou hast instituted.

    Would that we could wash away these crimes with our own blood!
    And now, to make amends for the outrage offered to the Divine honour,
    we offer to Thee the same satisfaction which Thou didst once offer to Thy Father on the Cross
    and which Thou dost continually renew on our altars,
    we offer this conjoined with the expiations of the Virgin Mother and of all the Saints,
    and of all pious Christians,
    promising from our heart that so far as in us lies, with the help of Thy grace,
    we will make amends for our own past sins, and for the sins of others,
    and for the neglect of Thy boundless love, by firm faith, by a pure way of life,
    and by a perfect observance of the Gospel law, especially that of charity;
    we will also strive with all our strength to prevent injuries being offered to Thee,
    and gather as many as we can to become Thy followers.
    Receive, we beseech Thee, O most benign Jesus, by the intercession of the Blessed Virgin Mary, the Reparatress,
    the voluntary homage of this expiation,
    and vouchsafe, by that great gift of final perseverance, to keep us most faithful until death
    in our duty and in Thy service,
    so that at length we may all come to that fatherland,
    where Thou with the Father and the Holy Ghost livest and reignest God for ever and ever. Amen.

— Pope Pius XI, _Miserentissimus Redemptor_ (1928), Prayer of Reparation

**5. The Act of Consecration (11:40–11:45)**

Pray the classic Act of Consecration to the Sacred Heart of Pope Leo XIII, given below in this document (see the Act of Consecration to the Sacred Heart). By it one gives and binds oneself to Jesus Christ, the King.

**6. The Act of Spiritual Communion (11:45–11:50)**

When one cannot receive the Blessed Sacrament sacramentally, unite oneself to Christ by an act of spiritual communion:

    My Jesus, I believe that Thou art present in the Most Holy Sacrament of the Altar.
    I love Thee above all things, and I desire to receive Thee into my soul.
    Since I cannot now receive Thee sacramentally, come at least spiritually into my heart.
    I embrace Thee as if Thou wert already there, and unite myself wholly to Thee.
    Never permit me to be separated from Thee. Amen.

— after St. Alphonsus Liguori

**7. The Anima Christi (11:50–11:55)**

A classic prayer of adoration, traditionally attributed to the fourteenth century and prayed before the Blessed Sacrament:

    Soul of Christ, sanctify me.
    Body of Christ, save me.
    Blood of Christ, inebriate me.
    Water from the side of Christ, wash me.
    Passion of Christ, strengthen me.
    O good Jesus, hear me.
    Within Thy wounds hide me.
    Suffer me not to be separated from Thee.
    From the malicious enemy defend me.
    In the hour of my death call me.
    And bid me come unto Thee,
    that with Thy Saints I may praise Thee
    for ever and ever. Amen.

**8. Closing (11:55–midnight)**

End the hour with an act of love and thanksgiving. Pray the _O Sacrum Convivium_ of St. Thomas Aquinas:

    O sacred Banquet, wherein Christ is received,
    the memory of His Passion renewed,
    the mind is filled with grace,
    and the pledge of future glory is given unto us.

And the Divine Praises:

    Blessed be God.
    Blessed be His Holy Name.
    Blessed be Jesus Christ, true God and true man.
    Blessed be the Name of Jesus.
    Blessed be His Most Sacred Heart.
    Blessed be His Most Precious Blood.
    Blessed be Jesus in the Most Holy Sacrament of the Altar.
    Blessed be the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete.
    Blessed be the great Mother of God, Mary most holy.
    Blessed be her holy and Immaculate Conception.
    Blessed be her glorious Assumption.
    Blessed be the name of Mary, Virgin and Mother.
    Blessed be St. Joseph, her most chaste spouse.
    Blessed be God in His angels and in His saints.

Conclude with the Sign of the Cross and a resolve to live the coming day — the day of the Passion — in union with Christ. If the Blessed Sacrament is exposed, the hour closes with **Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament**.[^17]

The hour must foster **active faith**, not mere routine; it may always be simplified — even a single heartfelt act of reparation prayed with Christ in the Garden fulfills its spirit.

### 3. The Enthronement of the Sacred Heart

A pastoral practice in which an image of the Sacred Heart is **solemnly installed** in a home, school, or parish as a sign that the household or institution is consecrated to Christ's kingship and love.[^10] The enthronement typically includes:

- A liturgical blessing of the image by a priest.
- The recitation of the Act of Consecration of the family or institution.
- The installation of the image in a place of honour.
- A commitment to live according to the Gospel and to foster family prayer.

The practice is not identical with the liturgy but is part of popular piety's way of giving visible expression to devotion — something the Church generally views as normal and beneficial when rightly ordered.[^10]

### 4. Acts of Consecration

The **personal consecration** to the Sacred Heart is identified by Pope Pius XI as "undoubtedly the principal devotional practice" regarding the Sacred Heart.[^8] Through consecration, the faithful entrust themselves entirely to Christ, acknowledging His Kingship and pledging love, fidelity, and reparation.

### 5. Acts of Reparation

Acts of reparation are prayers in which the faithful, mindful of Christ's goodness, implore mercy for offenses committed against His Sacred Heart.[^8] These respond to Christ's own lament to St. Margaret Mary over the ingratitude, coldness, and irreverence — particularly toward the Eucharist — that humanity returns for His love.

### 6. The Litany of the Sacred Heart

The **Litany of the Sacred Heart of Jesus** is a formal liturgical prayer approved for public and private use. It consists of invocations addressing Christ under titles drawn from Scripture and tradition (e.g., "Heart of Jesus, burning furnace of charity," "Heart of Jesus, source of all consolation," "Heart of Jesus, our peace and reconciliation"), each followed by the response _"Miserere nobis"_ (Have mercy on us). It concludes with a prayer for union with Christ's Heart.

### 7. Veneration of the Image

The faithful are encouraged to display and venerate an image of the Sacred Heart — typically depicting Christ with His Heart visible, encircled by flames (symbolizing consuming love), surmounted by a cross (symbolizing the Crucifixion), and crowned with thorns (symbolizing the crown placed on Christ's head and the wounds of sin). This image is a sacramental that directs the mind and heart to the reality of Christ's love.

### 8. The Scapular of the Sacred Heart

Among the Church's approved devotional scapulars is the **Scapular of the Sacred Heart of Jesus**, a small scapular of red cloth bearing the image of the Sacred Heart, enrolled and worn as an outward sign of consecration to Christ's Heart. Like the Brown Scapular, it serves as a visible pledge of the wearer's devotion and belongs to the Church's sacramentals. _For the theology of scapulars and the rite of enrollment, see [sacramentals/brown-scapular.md](../sacramentals/brown-scapular.md)._

## The Twelve Promises of the Sacred Heart

As recorded in the writings of St. Margaret Mary Alacoque, Jesus made the following promises to those who honour His Sacred Heart:

1. I will give them all the graces necessary for their state of life.
2. I will establish peace in their families.
3. I will comfort them in all their afflictions.
4. I will be their secure refuge during life, and above all, in death.
5. I will bestow abundant blessings upon all their undertakings.
6. Sinners shall find in my Heart the source and infinite ocean of mercy.
7. Lukewarm souls shall become fervent.
8. Fervent souls shall quickly mount to high perfection.
9. I will bless every place in which an image of my Heart is exposed and honoured.
10. I will give to priests the gift of touching the most hardened hearts.
11. Those who shall promote this devotion shall have their names written in my Heart.
12. **The Great Promise**: I promise you in the excessive mercy of my Heart that my all-powerful love will grant to all those who receive Holy Communion on the First Fridays in nine consecutive months the grace of final perseverance; they shall not die in my disfavour or without receiving the sacraments.

## The Act of Consecration to the Sacred Heart

The following classic formula, promulgated in connection with the consecration of the human race by Pope Leo XIII, is the standard Act of Consecration to the Sacred Heart:

    Most sweet Jesus, Redeemer of the human race, look down upon us humbly prostrate before Thine altar.
    We are Thine and Thine we wish to be; but to be more surely united with Thee,
    behold each one of us freely consecrates himself today to Thy Most Sacred Heart.

    Many indeed have never known Thee; many, too, despising Thy precepts, have rejected Thee.
    Have mercy on them all, most merciful Jesus, and draw them to Thy Sacred Heart.

    Be Thou King, O Lord, not only of the faithful who have never forsaken Thee,
    but also of the prodigal children who have abandoned Thee;
    grant that they may quickly return to their Father's house, lest they die of wretchedness and hunger.

    Be Thou King of those who are deceived by erroneous opinions, or whom discord keeps aloof,
    and call them back to the harbour of truth and unity of faith,
    so that soon there may be but one flock and one Shepherd.

    Be Thou King of all those who are still involved in the darkness of idolatry or of Islam,
    and refuse not to draw them into the light of Thy kingdom.

    Look, finally, with eyes of pity upon the children of that race, once Thy chosen people.
    Let them too, though formerly plead for mercy, at length in their turn acknowledge Thee, their Redeemer.

    Grant, O Lord, to Thy Church assurance of freedom and immunity from harm;
    grant to all nations a peaceful order;
    cause that from one end of the earth to the other there may be but one heart and one mind.

    Praise, honour and glory be to the divine Heart that wrought our salvation;
    to It be glory and honour for ever. Amen.

— Pope Leo XIII, _Acta Apostolicae Sedis_ 17 (1925), p. 34

## The Litany of the Sacred Heart

The Litany of the Sacred Heart of Jesus, approved for public recitation, consists of the following structure:

    Lord, have mercy. Christ, have mercy. Lord, have mercy.
    Christ, hear us. Christ, graciously hear us.
    God the Father of Heaven, _have mercy on us._
    God the Son, Redeemer of the world, _have mercy on us._
    God the Holy Spirit, _have mercy on us._
    Holy Trinity, One God, _have mercy on us._

    Heart of Jesus, Son of the Eternal Father, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, formed by the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Virgin Mother, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, substantially united to the Word of God, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, of infinite majesty, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, sacred temple of God, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, tabernacle of the Most High, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, house of God and gate of heaven, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, burning furnace of charity, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, abode of justice and love, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, full of goodness and love, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, abyss of all virtues, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, most worthy of all praise, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, king and centre of all hearts, _have mercy on us._
    Heart of Jesus, in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, _have mercy on us._

## The Sacred Heart and Eastern Catholic Tradition

While the devotion to the Sacred Heart in its modern form emerged in the Latin Church, its theological core — the contemplation of Christ's pierced side and the love of the Incarnate Word — is shared across the Catholic Church's 24 _sui iuris_ Churches.

In **Eastern Catholic theology**, the devotion finds resonance in:

- The **Byzantine hymnography** of the Passion, particularly the _Akathist to the Passion of Christ_ and the hymns of Holy Week, which repeatedly contemplate the lance piercing Christ's side and the blood and water flowing forth.
- The **Eastern iconographic tradition**, which depicts Christ with His side wounded, from which blood and water flow (John 19:34), and the _Nipsis_ (vigilance) tradition of guarding the heart as the seat of union with God.
- The **Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom** and **St. Basil the Great**, in which the prayer of the anaphora recalls the piercing of Christ's side and the institution of the sacraments.

The Solemnity of the Sacred Heart, however, is a specifically **Roman Rite** liturgical celebration and is not part of the Byzantine liturgical calendar, though Eastern Catholic Churches may observe it through local adaptations or devotional practices.

## The Sacred Heart and the Immaculate Heart of Mary

The Sacred Heart of Jesus and the Immaculate Heart of Mary are closely linked in Catholic piety, as Mary's heart is perfectly conformed to the Heart of her Son. The two devotions are often paired:

- The **First Saturday** devotion (requested by Our Lady of Fatima) echoes the First Friday devotion, with reparation offered to the Immaculate Heart.
- **Pope Pius XII** consecrated the Church to the Immaculate Heart in 1942; **Pope Leo XIII** had consecrated the world to the Sacred Heart in 1899.
- The **Enthronement of the Sacred Heart** in homes is often accompanied by the consecration to the Immaculate Heart.

_For the Fatima apparitions, see [miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md](../miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md)._

## Indulgences

The Church grants **partial indulgences** for various acts of piety in honour of the Sacred Heart, and a **plenary indulgence** (under the usual conditions: sacramental confession, Eucharistic communion, prayer for the Holy Father's intentions, and detachment from sin) for:

- The public recitation of the Act of Consecration or the Act of Reparation on the Solemnity of the Sacred Heart.
- The recitation of the Litany of the Sacred Heart in a church or oratory.
- The practice of the First Fridays devotion when completed with the prescribed conditions.

## In Summary

The devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus is a magisterially approved and deeply rooted form of Catholic piety that:

1. Contemplates **Christ's personal, burning love** for humanity, made visible in His pierced side.
2. Responds to that love with **consecration, reparation, gratitude, and faithful love**.
3. Fosters **frequent Communion** (especially First Fridays), **prayerful vigil** (the Holy Hour), and **domestic consecration** (Enthronement).
4. Is grounded in **Sacred Scripture** (John 19:34), **Christological dogma** (the hypostatic union), and **magisterial teaching** (_Haurietis Aquas_, _Annum Sacrum_, and most recently _Dilexit Nos_).
5. Is ordered toward **Eucharistic life** and full participation in the Sacred Liturgy, especially Sunday Mass.[^9]

> — Words of Christ to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque
