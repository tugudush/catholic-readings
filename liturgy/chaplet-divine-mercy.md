# The Chaplet of Divine Mercy

## Theological Introduction

The Chaplet of Divine Mercy is a prayer of atonement and supplication revealed by **our Lord Jesus Christ** to **St. Maria Faustina Kowalska** (1905–1938), a Polish sister of the Congregation of the Sisters of Our Lady of Mercy. Jesus dictated the words of the Chaplet to St. Faustina on **September 13–14, 1935**, in Vilnius, as recorded in her _Diary: Divine Mercy in My Soul_ (§ 474–476).

The Chaplet is classified as a **private revelation** (see CCC 67). While belief in private revelations is not required for salvation, the Church has approved the Divine Mercy devotion as authentically Catholic, having thoroughly examined St. Faustina's writings and found them free from theological error. On April 15, 1978, the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith rescinded an earlier 1959 prohibition, and on April 30, 2000, St. John Paul II canonized St. Faustina and established **Divine Mercy Sunday** (the Second Sunday of Easter) for the universal Church.

Theologically, the Chaplet offers to **God the Father** the **Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity** of Jesus Christ — the infinite merits of His Passion — in atonement for sins and as a plea for mercy on the whole world. It may be prayed at any time but is especially associated with the **Hour of Mercy (3:00 p.m.)**, the hour of our Lord's death on the Cross.

_For the broader context of St. Faustina's revelations — including the Divine Mercy Image, the Feast of Mercy, and the Hour of Mercy — see [miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md](../miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md)._

## The Full Text of the Prayers

### Opening Prayers

Begin with the Sign of the Cross, then:

    You expired, Jesus, but the source of life gushed forth for souls,
    and the ocean of mercy opened up for the whole world.
    O Fount of Life, unfathomable Divine Mercy,
    envelop the whole world and empty Yourself out upon us.

_(The following three prayers are optional but customary:)_

    **Our Father** *(once)*

    Our Father, who art in heaven,
    hallowed be Thy name;
    Thy kingdom come;
    Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven.
    Give us this day our daily bread;
    and forgive us our trespasses
    as we forgive those who trespass against us;
    and lead us not into temptation,
    but deliver us from evil. Amen.

    **Hail Mary** *(once)*

    Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee;
    blessed art thou among women,
    and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus.
    Holy Mary, Mother of God,
    pray for us sinners,
    now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

    **The Apostles' Creed** *(once)*

    I believe in God, the Father almighty,
    Creator of heaven and earth,
    and in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord,
    who was conceived by the Holy Spirit,
    born of the Virgin Mary,
    suffered under Pontius Pilate,
    was crucified, died and was buried;
    He descended into hell;
    on the third day He rose again from the dead;
    He ascended into heaven,
    and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty;
    from there He will judge the living and the dead.
    I believe in the Holy Spirit,
    the holy catholic Church,
    the communion of saints,
    the forgiveness of sins,
    the resurrection of the body,
    and life everlasting. Amen.

### The Eternal Father Prayer (on each large bead)

    **Eternal Father,**
    **I offer You the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity**
    **of Your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ,**
    **in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world.**

### The Decade Prayer (on each of the ten small beads)

    **For the sake of His sorrowful Passion,**
    **have mercy on us and on the whole world.**

### Closing Doxology (after the five decades)

    **Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One,**
    **have mercy on us and on the whole world.**

_(Repeat three times)_

### Concluding Prayer

    **Eternal God, in whom mercy is endless**
    **and the treasury of compassion inexhaustible,**
    **look kindly upon us and increase Your mercy in us,**
    **that in difficult moments we might not despair nor become despondent,**
    **but with great confidence submit ourselves to Your holy will,**
    **which is Love and Mercy itself. Amen.**

## How to Pray the Chaplet (Step by Step)

The Chaplet is prayed on a standard set of rosary beads (a five-decade Rosary). It follows a repeating pattern of one large bead (Our Father bead) followed by ten small beads (Hail Mary beads), repeated five times.

| Step   | Bead                               | Prayer/Action                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**  | **Crucifix / Cross**               | Make the Sign of the Cross. Recite the **Opening Prayers** (see above).                                                                                                                                              |
| **2**  | **First large bead**               | Pray the **Eternal Father** prayer: _"Eternal Father, I offer You the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of Your dearly beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in atonement for our sins and those of the whole world."_ |
| **3**  | **First decade (10 small beads)**  | On each bead, pray: _"For the sake of His sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world."_                                                                                                              |
| **4**  | **Second large bead**              | Repeat the **Eternal Father** prayer.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **5**  | **Second decade (10 small beads)** | Repeat the **For the sake of His sorrowful Passion** prayer ten times.                                                                                                                                               |
| **6**  | **Third large bead**               | Repeat the **Eternal Father** prayer.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **7**  | **Third decade (10 small beads)**  | Repeat the **For the sake of His sorrowful Passion** prayer ten times.                                                                                                                                               |
| **8**  | **Fourth large bead**              | Repeat the **Eternal Father** prayer.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **9**  | **Fourth decade (10 small beads)** | Repeat the **For the sake of His sorrowful Passion** prayer ten times.                                                                                                                                               |
| **10** | **Fifth large bead**               | Repeat the **Eternal Father** prayer.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **11** | **Fifth decade (10 small beads)**  | Repeat the **For the sake of His sorrowful Passion** prayer ten times.                                                                                                                                               |
| **12** | **After the fifth decade**         | Recite the **Closing Doxology**: _"Holy God, Holy Mighty One, Holy Immortal One, have mercy on us and on the whole world"_ (three times).                                                                            |
| **13** | **Conclude**                       | Recite the **Concluding Prayer** (see above). Make the Sign of the Cross.                                                                                                                                            |

## When to Pray the Chaplet

- **Any time**: The Chaplet may be prayed at any hour for any intention — for oneself, for sinners, for the sick, for the dying, or for the whole world.
- **The Hour of Mercy (3:00 p.m.)**: Jesus asked St. Faustina that this Chaplet be prayed especially at the hour of His death, promising: _"In this hour, you can obtain everything for yourself and for others for the asking; it was the hour of grace for the whole world."_ (Diary, § 1572)
- **For the Dying**: Jesus promised that when the Chaplet is recited by the bedside of a dying person, His mercy envelops the soul and His anger is placated (Diary, § 1565, § 687).
- **Divine Mercy Sunday (Second Sunday of Easter)**: The Chaplet is especially fitting on this solemnity established by St. John Paul II.

## Promises and Conditions

St. Faustina recorded several promises of our Lord concerning the Chaplet:

    _"Say unceasingly the chaplet that I have taught you. Whoever will recite it will receive great mercy at the hour of death."_ (Diary, § 687)

    _"The souls that say this chaplet will be embraced by My mercy during their lifetime and especially at the hour of death."_ (Diary, § 754)

The graces of the devotion are connected to:

- **Trust** in God's goodness and mercy
- **Active love of neighbor** (works of mercy)
- **The state of sanctifying grace** — having received the Sacraments of Confession and Holy Communion, especially worthily on Divine Mercy Sunday

  _"No soul will be justified until it turns with confidence to My mercy."_ (Diary, § 177)

## Eastern Catholic Practice

The Chaplet of Divine Mercy, while originating in the Latin Rite tradition, is also prayed by Eastern Catholics. The prayers are the same, though Eastern Catholics typically make the Sign of the Cross using the Eastern formula (right thumb, index, and middle fingers joined, touching forehead, chest, right shoulder, left shoulder) and may add Eastern-style introductory prayers or the Trisagion before the Chaplet.

The theological focus on Divine Mercy resonates deeply with the Byzantine tradition's emphasis on the _philanthrōpia_ (love for mankind) of God, expressed in the repeated petitions of the Divine Liturgy: _"Have mercy on us, O Lord, according to Your great mercy."_
