# Images of the Sacred Heart and the Immaculate Heart

Source note for the pictures kept in this folder. These are historical sacred images, not photographs of the visions. Honour paid to an image goes to the person represented: latria to Christ, hyperdulia to the Blessed Virgin Mary, not to the paint or the file.

None of these pictures adds to public Revelation. The devotions they serve are approved. Belief in the private revelations connected with them is not required for the devotions themselves. See [sacred-heart-devotion.md](../sacred-heart-devotion.md) and [first-saturdays-devotion.md](../first-saturdays-devotion.md).

## Sacred Heart of Jesus

### The altarpiece

[sacred-heart-batoni-gesu-1767.jpg](sacred-heart-batoni-gesu-1767.jpg)

Pompeo Batoni, _Sacred Heart of Jesus_, oil on canvas, 1767, still the altarpiece of the north side chapel of the Church of the Gesù in Rome. Christ holds his Heart, flaming, crowned with thorns, and surmounted by a cross. The cartouche under the frame reads _Adveniat regnum tuum_. This is the painting that fixed the standard image of the devotion. It is not a portrait made during the apparitions to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque. Batoni was born eighteen years after her death.

The file here is a small public-domain photographic reproduction of that altarpiece. A larger photograph of the same chapel circulates with a Catholic News Service copyright mark in its metadata, so that larger file was not copied into this repository.

Source: [Wikimedia Commons, File:Batoni sacred heart.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Batoni_sacred_heart.jpg). Public domain: a faithful photographic reproduction of a two-dimensional work whose author died in 1787.

### The familiar devotional print

[sacred-heart-devotional.jpg](sacred-heart-devotional.jpg)

The picture most often hung in homes: the Heart on the breast, with rays, the cross, and the crown of thorns. It belongs to the same iconographic family as Batoni's altarpiece, but it is a later devotional print, not the 1767 canvas. Some online copies label this file as Batoni's painting. That label is not accurate.

Source: [Wikimedia Commons, File:SacredHeartBatoni.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SacredHeartBatoni.jpg). Dedicated to the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) by the uploader.

## Immaculate Heart of Mary

[immaculate-heart-kupelwieser.jpg](immaculate-heart-kupelwieser.jpg)

Leopold Kupelwieser, _The Heart of Mary_, in the chapel of St. Anthony, St. Peter's Church (Peterskirche), Vienna. Mary rests her hand on her Heart; the Heart is pierced by a sword and circled with roses, after Simeon's prophecy (Luke 2:35). There is no single Immaculate Heart canvas with the standing of Batoni's altarpiece. The public feast of the Heart of Mary is older than this painting: St. John Eudes kept it at Autun in 1648. This canvas is a nineteenth-century painted original of the figure the devotion uses.

Photograph by Diana Ringo, 22 September 2013, licensed [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Source: [Wikimedia Commons, File:Immaculate Heart of Mary.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary.jpg).

## The two Hearts together

### The Miraculous Medal

[miraculous-medal.jpg](miraculous-medal.jpg)

Obverse and reverse of the Medal of the Immaculate Conception. St. Catherine Labouré described the design in 1830 at the Rue du Bac in Paris. The first medals were struck in 1832. On the reverse, the Heart of Jesus is crowned with thorns and the Heart of Mary is pierced by a sword, under the cross and the letter M. This is the first image of both Hearts that the Church spread everywhere.

Photograph by Xhienne, 18 May 2007, licensed [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). Source: [Wikimedia Commons, File:Miraculous medal.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Miraculous_medal.jpg).

### López Portaña

[sacred-hearts-lopez-portana.jpg](sacred-hearts-lopez-portana.jpg)

Workshop of Vicente López Portaña, about 1829–1831, oil on canvas, now in the Museo Diocesano de Arte Sacro at Orihuela. It was given to the Royal Monastery of the Visitation by the Infanta María Francisca de Asís de Braganza y Borbón. The two Hearts are shown above: Christ's with the cross and the thorns, Mary's with roses.

Source: [Wikimedia Commons](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sagrados_corazones_de_Jes%C3%BAs_y_Mar%C3%ADa_(Sacred_Hearts_of_Jesus_and_Mary),_workshop_of_Vicente_L%C3%B3pez_Porta%C3%B1a.jpg>), and the [museum record](https://museodeartesacro.es/obras/sagrados-corazones-de-jesus-y-maria/). Public domain: the author died in 1850.

## What is not in this folder

St. Margaret Mary had a small image of the Heart venerated by her novices at Paray-le-Monial in 1685. No reliable public photograph of that object is kept here. Her own account remains the verbal original: a Heart like a furnace, transparent as crystal, with the wound, a crown of thorns, and a cross above it.

The standing "Immaculate Heart" familiar from Fátima — the Heart encircled by thorns, shown on 13 June 1917 and again at Pontevedra on 10 December 1925 — is a later type. The pilgrim statue that Sister Lúcia corrected, carved by José Ferreira Thedim in 1947, is not included here.
