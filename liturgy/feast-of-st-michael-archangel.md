# Feast of St. Michael the Archangel (Michaelmas)

> **Source note:** This article distinguishes the current Roman Rite liturgy, Eastern Catholic calendars, the Church's teaching on the holy angels, optional devotional practices, and regional Michaelmas customs. In the current Roman Rite, 29 September is the Feast of **Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, Archangels**, not a feast of Michael alone.

## 1. At a Glance

| Detail                                | Catholic observance                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Date in the Roman Rite**            | 29 September                                                                                                   |
| **Official Roman title**              | Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, Archangels                                                               |
| **Liturgical rank**                   | Feast in the current Roman General Calendar                                                                    |
| **Liturgical color**                  | White                                                                                                          |
| **Common English name**               | Michaelmas, meaning the Mass or feast of Saint Michael                                                         |
| **Universal Holy Day of Obligation?** | No. A diocese, parish, religious institute, or other particular calendar may give the day a higher local rank. |
| **Historical Roman setting**          | The dedication of an ancient basilica of Saint Michael on the Via Salaria, recalled by the Roman Martyrology   |

The day celebrates the missions of the three archangels named in Sacred Scripture and directs the faithful to the worship of the Most Holy Trinity. The archangels are honored as God's servants; they are not treated as divine powers alongside God.

## 2. Who Is Celebrated?

The Roman feast joins three biblical witnesses to God's saving work:

| Archangel   | Biblical witnesses                                | Liturgical and devotional association                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Michael** | Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1; Jude 9; Revelation 12:7-9 | The defender of God's people and the one whose name proclaims, "Who is like God?" |
| **Gabriel** | Daniel 8:16; 9:21; Luke 1:5-38                    | God's messenger who announces the births of John the Baptist and Jesus Christ     |
| **Raphael** | Tobit 3:17; 6:1-18; 12:15                         | God's healing messenger and companion of Tobias                                   |

The Church teaches that angels are spiritual, non-corporeal creatures created by God. They glorify God and serve His saving plan, including by assisting human beings. See the [Catechism of the Catholic Church §§328-336](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/328), especially §§329 and 336.

Veneration of the angels is not adoration. Catholics worship God alone and ask the angels for help and intercession as God's faithful ministers. The [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy §215](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/215) places this devotion within the Church's liturgical prayer, especially the Church's participation in the angelic praise of the Holy, Holy, Holy.

## 3. Roman Rite Observance

### 3.1 The Mass

The principal observance is participation in the Eucharistic celebration. Because 29 September is a feast, the Roman Missal provides a complete proper Mass, including the **Gloria** and proper prayers. The [Roman Missal, Proper of Saints](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/3f204803-62dd-4467-9edb-8b46db283e8a/ref/Proper%20of%20Saints) supplies the official texts.

The usual Roman lectionary choices are:

- **First reading:** Daniel 7:9-10, 13-14, or Revelation 12:7-12a;
- **Responsorial Psalm:** Psalm 138:1-5;
- **Gospel:** John 1:47-51, in which Christ promises that the disciples will see heaven opened and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man.

The proper texts use angelic imagery without making the angels the center of worship. The Entrance Antiphon draws from Psalm 103:20, the Communion Antiphon from Psalm 138:1, and the Collect asks that the faithful be defended on earth by those who serve God in heaven.

### 3.2 The Liturgy of the Hours

The feast may also be observed through the proper Office, especially Morning Prayer and Evening Prayer. The Church's praise is joined to the praise of the angels. The Directory on Popular Piety notes that the Church celebrates the Office of praise in the presence of the angels and entrusts the prayers of the faithful to their ministry.

### 3.3 Obligation and local calendars

29 September is not, simply by being the feast of the archangels, a universal Holy Day of Obligation in the Latin Church. The faithful should follow the obligations established by the universal law, their episcopal conference, and their particular diocese. A church or institution dedicated to Saint Michael may celebrate its patronal feast with a higher local rank and greater solemnity.

There is no universal fast, confession requirement, novena, chaplet, or special indulgence attached automatically to this feast. Confession, Eucharistic adoration, and additional prayer are fitting preparations or extensions of the celebration, but they remain optional unless a particular local observance provides otherwise.

## 4. History of the Feast

The [Roman Martyrology entry for 29 September](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/714191b4-50f3-4cf8-87a4-caafca5dbf2d/ref/September%2029) recalls the dedication of an ancient basilica of Saint Michael, built at the sixth milestone on the Via Salaria outside Rome. The Roman celebration developed around Saint Michael's cult and, in the current Roman calendar, commemorates Michael together with Gabriel and Raphael.

The name **Michaelmas** is the traditional English form of _Michael's Mass_. In English and northern European history, the date also became an autumn quarter day used for rents, contracts, terms, and hiring arrangements. Those civil uses explain why Michaelmas appears in legal, agricultural, and academic history, but they are not liturgical obligations.

The traditional Western commemoration of the Apparition of Saint Michael on **8 May**, associated especially with Monte Sant'Angelo in Apulia, is distinct from the current Roman feast on 29 September. Pope John Paul II's visit to Monte Sant'Angelo described the shrine as a place of silence, prayer, penance, pilgrimage, and invocation of Saint Michael's protection; see his [address of 24 May 1987](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/63617cdd-9458-4d51-ae15-88ec40085d7e/ref/2). The shrine's traditions should be distinguished from defined doctrine and from the universal Roman calendar.

## 5. Common Catholic Practices and Traditions

The practices below are common or fitting ways to observe the feast. Their status matters: the Mass is the Church's public liturgy, while the other practices are optional forms of popular piety or local custom.

| Practice                                | Common observance                                                                                                                                                                              | Status                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Attend Mass**                         | Participate in the feast-day Mass and receive Holy Communion when properly disposed.                                                                                                           | Principal liturgical observance; not a universal obligation merely because it is 29 September.                                                             |
| **Pray the Liturgy of the Hours**       | Pray Morning Prayer, Evening Prayer, or another Hour with the proper texts for the archangels.                                                                                                 | Official liturgical prayer; the extent of participation depends on one's state in life.                                                                    |
| **Pray the Prayer to Saint Michael**    | Ask Saint Michael to defend the Church and the faithful against evil and to lead the faithful toward God.                                                                                      | Traditional devotional prayer, not a universal requirement. The full prayer is included in the [St. Michael biography](../saints/st-michael-archangel.md). |
| **Make a novena**                       | Pray the [Nine-Day Novena to St. Michael and the Holy Archangels](novena-st-michael-archangels.md) from 20-28 September, or use another sound novena to Saint Michael or the three archangels. | Widespread optional devotion; there is no single universal novena mandated for the feast.                                                                  |
| **Pray a chaplet or litany**            | Use a reliable Catholic chaplet, litany, or short invocation of Saint Michael and the holy angels.                                                                                             | Optional popular piety; it is not part of the universal Mass.                                                                                              |
| **Go to Confession**                    | Receive the Sacrament of Penance as preparation when reconciliation is needed.                                                                                                                 | Always a fitting Christian practice, but not specifically required for this feast.                                                                         |
| **Spend time in Eucharistic adoration** | Pray before the Blessed Sacrament for the Church, families, the sick, the dying, and those facing spiritual or physical danger.                                                                | Optional parish or personal devotion.                                                                                                                      |
| **Keep a parish patronal feast**        | A church, school, hospital, military chaplaincy, or association under Saint Michael's patronage may celebrate with a solemn Mass, procession, talks, fellowship, or a charitable collection.   | Local observance; the particular calendar determines its rank.                                                                                             |
| **Make a pilgrimage**                   | Visit a shrine dedicated to Saint Michael, such as Monte Sant'Angelo, Mont-Saint-Michel, or a local sanctuary.                                                                                 | Longstanding devotional custom, not a requirement for the feast.                                                                                           |
| **Perform a work of mercy**             | Assist the poor, visit the sick, support a family in crisis, encourage someone in danger, or pray for the dying.                                                                               | A fitting Christ-centered response, not a prescribed ritual.                                                                                               |
| **Honor a sacred image**                | Place a properly blessed image or icon of Saint Michael in a prayer space, or decorate a parish image with flowers.                                                                            | Optional sacramental devotion; honor passes to God, not to the material image as a deity.                                                                  |

### 5.1 A simple family observance

A family does not need an elaborate program to keep the feast:

1. Read Revelation 12:7-12a or John 1:47-51 from an approved Catholic Bible.
2. Thank God for the service of the holy angels and ask for the grace to resist sin.
3. Pray the Prayer to Saint Michael or a short invocation such as:

       St. Michael the Archangel,
       defend us in battle.

4. Attend Mass if a convenient parish celebration is available.
5. Pray for the Church, the Pope and bishops, the clergy, the dying, and those who protect and serve others.
6. Perform one concrete work of mercy.
7. Share a simple meal or use seasonal flowers, explaining that these are family customs rather than required parts of the liturgy.

The essential pattern is prayer, participation in the Church's worship, and charity. Devotion to Saint Michael should lead to greater fidelity to Christ rather than to fear, superstition, or the search for magical protection.

## 6. Michaelmas Customs and Seasonal Culture

The following customs are associated with **Michaelmas** in English, Scottish, Irish, and northern European history. They may be meaningful cultural expressions, but they are not universal Catholic practice:

| Custom                                    | Historical or cultural meaning                                                                                                 | Catholic status                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Harvest meal**                          | A communal meal giving thanks for the harvest and marking the turn toward autumn.                                              | A fitting local or family custom when joined to thanksgiving and charity; not part of the Roman Missal. |
| **Michaelmas goose**                      | In parts of England and northern Europe, goose became associated with the date because of seasonal farming and market customs. | Regional food tradition, not a required feast-day dish.                                                 |
| **Michaelmas daisies**                    | Late-blooming asters became associated with the autumn feast in English usage.                                                 | Seasonal decoration, not a liturgical symbol required by the Church.                                    |
| **Hiring fairs and quarter-day business** | Michaelmas was historically used for rents, contracts, terms, and hiring arrangements.                                         | Civil and economic custom, not a religious obligation.                                                  |
| **Village or parish festival**            | Music, meals, processions, and communal gatherings may accompany a local patronal celebration.                                 | Permitted local custom when it supports the dignity of the feast and does not replace the liturgy.      |

Catholic families and parishes may adapt harmless local customs to their own culture. The custom should remain subordinate to worship, avoid superstition, and make room for prayer and concrete charity.

## 7. Eastern Catholic Context

The Eastern Catholic Churches follow their own proper liturgical calendars. The common Byzantine observance is not identical to the Latin feast:

| Tradition                     | Date and title                                                                                                                     | Notes                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Byzantine Catholic**        | **8 November**, or **21 November** where the Julian calendar is used: Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and all the Bodiless Powers | A collective feast of the holy angels, with Saint Michael prominent as _Archistrategos_, the chief commander of God's army.                                                     |
| **Byzantine local tradition** | **6 September**: Miracle of the Archangel Michael at Chonae                                                                        | Retained, ranked, or publicly celebrated according to the calendar of the particular Church. It should not be generalized as a universal obligation for every Eastern Catholic. |
| **Latin Church**              | **29 September**: Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, Archangels                                                                 | A feast of the three named archangels in the Roman General Calendar.                                                                                                            |

The [Catechism of the Ukrainian Catholic Church, _Christ - Our Pascha_ §143](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/5c0fb401-3c71-494e-bfce-ac8641dafd3b/ref/143) identifies 8/21 November as the feast of Saint Michael and the other bodiless powers and notes the Byzantine dedication of Mondays to the angels. The [Encyclopedic Dictionary of the Christian East](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/2cb86786-6b8e-4ddd-b5fe-d1ebaccb869c/ref/Angels) also records the Byzantine commemoration of Gabriel on 26 March and the joint commemoration of Michael and Gabriel on 8 November.

These are legitimate differences of liturgical tradition, not competing claims about which Church honors the angels correctly. The particular _sui iuris_ Church's approved calendar should always be followed.

## 8. Important Distinctions

- The current Roman feast is of **three archangels**, even though popular speech often calls it Saint Michael's feast.
- **2 October**, the Memorial of the Guardian Angels, is a separate Roman observance.
- **8 May**, the traditional Western commemoration of the Apparition of Saint Michael, is distinct from 29 September and does not turn every associated shrine tradition into dogma.
- A novena, chaplet, procession, pilgrimage, meal, goose, flower, or family custom is not universally required for Catholics.
- The feast does not teach that Saint Michael is a god, a magical protector, or an independent power. Christian protection comes from God; the faithful ask the angels to assist them as God's servants.
- The feast honors God's grace and saving work through the angels. Its center is the Triune God and the Paschal Mystery celebrated in the Eucharist.
