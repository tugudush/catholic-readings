# The First Saturdays Devotion (Devotion of Reparation to the Immaculate Heart of Mary)

## Doctrinal Classification and Level of Authority

The devotion of the **Five First Saturdays of the month** is an **approved form of popular piety** grounded in **private revelation**. Its proper theological classification must be stated at the outset:

- **It is not dogma.** The devotion flows from the Fatima message, which the Church classifies as private revelation: such revelations "do not belong, however, to the deposit of faith. It is not their role to improve or complete Christ's definitive Revelation, but to help live more fully by it in a certain period of history" (Catechism of the Catholic Church, 67).[^1]
- **It is not required for salvation.** Assent of faith is owed to public Revelation alone; belief in the Fatima message, though permitted and encouraged after ecclesial approval, is a matter of religious assent to a devotion the Church has judged "worthy of belief."
- **It is ecclesially received.** The Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments explicitly enumerates "the practice of the First Five Saturdays" among the approved devotions of popular piety to the Immaculate Heart of Mary in the _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (2002), §174.[^2]
- **Its promises are devotional, not contractual.** The promise of assistance at the hour of death is to be read as the language of assured graces of perseverance accompanying a lived spirituality of penance and reparation — never as a mechanical guarantee that permits a person to remain in mortal sin while treating the sacraments as a checklist.

The Second Vatican Council's teaching that the Church's devotion to the Blessed Virgin is "intrinsic to Christian worship" (CCC 971) provides the framework: Marian devotion, including the First Saturdays, is ordered toward a fuller communion with Christ and the sacred liturgy, never a parallel religion.

## Scriptural and Theological Foundations

### Reparation as an Act of Charity

The theological core of the First Saturdays is the Catholic doctrine of **reparation** — the loving effort, sustained by grace, to repair the offense that sin offers to God and to the hearts of God and His Saints. Reparation belongs to the work of conversion and penance (CCC 1434–1439) and rests upon the **communion of saints**: the charity of one member of the Mystical Body can be offered for the sins of others. Pope Pius XI, in his encyclical on reparation to the Sacred Heart, teaches that the "spirit of expiation or reparation" has always held "the first and foremost place" in this form of devotion, because Christ Himself complained of the injuries done to Him by ungrateful men and asked for acts of reparation — above all the "Communion of Reparation."[^3]

The devotion to the Immaculate Heart of Mary applies this same logic to the Mother of God. Because Mary's Heart is perfectly conformed to the Heart of her Son, the sins of men wound her Heart, not with a proper title to satisfaction (which belongs to Christ alone), but in the manner of her **maternal compassion** — the same compassion by which, according to the traditional meditation, she stood beneath the Cross (John 19:25–27;cf. Luke 2:34–35, Simeon's prophecy that a sword would pierce her soul).

### The Heart of Mary as the Symbol of Her Interior Life

Devotion to the "Heart" of a person is biblical symbolism for the interior life — the will, the affections, the seat of fidelity. The **Immaculate Heart of Mary** represents her perfect charity, her humility, her obedience of faith ("Behold the handmaid of the Lord," Luke 1:38), and her maternal intercession. Devotion to her Heart, as Pope John Paul II taught, is directed through her to Christ: "by dedicating ourselves to the heart of Mary we discover a sure way to the Sacred Heart of Jesus."[^4]

### Why Saturday? The Weekly Memorial of the Blessed Virgin Mary

The choice of **Saturday** is not arbitrary. The _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ explains that Saturdays "stand out among those days dedicated to the Virgin Mary" — a memorial that derives from Carolingian times (ninth century). Above all, Saturday commemorates "that great Saturday on which Our Lord lay in the tomb," when the Blessed Virgin Mary, "strengthened by faith and hope... was the only one of the disciples to hold vigil in expectation of the Lord's resurrection."[^5] Saturday is therefore the day of Our Lady's solitary fidelity and sorrowing hope — the day on which her maternal Heart most perfectly shared in the Passion of her Son. A devotion of reparation to her Immaculate Heart fittingly takes its place on this weekly memorial, just as the First Fridays of reparation to the Sacred Heart take their place on Friday, the weekly memorial of the Passion.

## History of the Devotion

### The Foundation Laid at Fatima (1917)

The First Saturdays devotion is the practical outgrowth of the request Our Lady of Fatima made during the apparitions of 1917:

- **June 13, 1917** — Our Lady showed the children her Heart: "In front of the palm of Our Lady's right hand was a Heart encircled by thorns which pierced it. We understood that this was the Immaculate Heart of Mary, outraged by the sins of humanity, and seeking reparation." She then declared: "My Immaculate Heart will be your refuge and the way that will lead you to God."
- **July 13, 1917** — In the second part of the Secret, Our Lady declared: "You have seen hell, where the souls of poor sinners go. To save them, God wishes to establish in the world devotion to my Immaculate Heart."[^6]

For the full account of the apparitions, their ecclesial approval, and the three parts of the Secret, see [miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md](../miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md).

### The Apparition at Pontevedra (December 10, 1925)

The explicit request for the First Saturdays was made on **December 10, 1925**, at **Pontevedra, Spain**, where Sister Lúcia dos Santos — the eldest of the three Fatima seers — was then a postulant with the Dorothean Sisters. The Blessed Virgin Mary appeared together with the Child Jesus, elevated on a cloud of light. Our Lady rested her hand on Lúcia's shoulder and showed her a Heart encircled by thorns. According to Sister Lúcia's written account:[^7]

> "The Most Holy Virgin said to me: 'My daughter, look at my Heart encircled with the thorns with which ungrateful men pierce it at every moment by their blasphemies and ingratitude. You, at least, try to console me, and say that I promise to assist at the hour of death, with all the graces necessary for salvation, all those who, on the first Saturday of five consecutive months, shall confess, receive Holy Communion, recite five decades of the Rosary, and keep me company for fifteen minutes while meditating on the mysteries of the Rosary, with the intention of making reparation to me.'"

The Child Jesus then spoke to Sister Lúcia, stressing the authority and maternal claim behind the request:

> "Have compassion on the Heart of your Most Holy Mother. Consider how she is pierced by the thorns of ungrateful men, and there is no one to remove them by an act of reparation."

### The Apparition at Tuy (February 15, 1926) — Clarifications on the Conditions

On **February 15, 1926**, the Child Jesus appeared again to Sister Lúcia (then in Tuy, Spain) and asked whether she had propagated the devotion. When Lúcia raised the practical difficulty that many souls were not able to fulfill everything at once, and asked whether a confession made several days beforehand would suffice, the Child Jesus replied that it would — **provided the soul receives Holy Communion on the First Saturday itself in the state of grace and with the intention of reparation**:

> "It is true, My daughter, that many souls begin but few persevere... The confession may be made within eight days, and even more, provided that when they receive Me on the first Saturday they are in the state of grace and have the intention of making reparation to the Immaculate Heart of Mary."

Sister Lúcia's questioner had also asked whether the Child Jesus meant that this confession could be made on the following Saturday, to which He answered that it could, provided the reception of Communion on the first Saturday of the month was made in the required dispositions. This clarification is the basis of the customary pastoral practice that the confession may be made **up to eight days before or after** the First Saturday (and, with a legitimate cause and right intention, even further in advance), so long as Communion itself is received on the day with a soul in the state of grace and the intention of reparation.[^7]

### The Revelation of the Five Blasphemies (May 29, 1930)

The deeper meaning of the **number five** was disclosed by Our Lord to Sister Lúcia in a locution at **Tuy on May 29, 1930**. The five consecutive First Saturdays correspond to the **five species of offenses and blasphemies** committed against the Immaculate Heart of Mary:

1. **Blasphemies against the Immaculate Conception** of the Blessed Virgin Mary;
2. **Blasphemies against her perpetual virginity**;
3. **Blasphemies against her divine maternity**, together with the refusal to recognize her as the Mother of all mankind;
4. **The implanting in children of indifference, contempt, and even hatred** toward this Immaculate Mother;
5. **Direct outrages against her sacred images** — the desecration and contempt shown to images of the Blessed Virgin.

Our Lord added: "Here, My daughter, is the reason why the Immaculate Heart of Mary inspired Me to ask for this little act of reparation; and, in consideration of it, to move My mercy to pardon those souls who have had the misfortune to offend her."[^7] The five Saturdays are thus a **substitutive reparation**: five acts of love offered in place of the fivefold blasphemy of ungrateful men.

### Diffusion and Ecclesial Reception

- **October 13, 1930** — Bishop José Alves Correia da Silva of Leiria declared the Fatima apparitions "worthy of belief" and authorized public devotion, opening the way for the pastoral diffusion of the whole Fatima message, including the reparatory devotions.
- **October 31, 1942** — Pope Pius XII, speaking by radio to the pilgrims at Fatima on the twenty-fifth anniversary of the last apparition, consecrated the Church and the human race to the Immaculate Heart of Mary; by the decree of May 4, 1944, he extended the Memorial of the Immaculate Heart of Mary to the universal Roman Rite.[^2]
- **March 25, 1984** — Pope St. John Paul II, in spiritual union with the bishops of the world, solemnly consecrated the world — and in the manner requested, Russia — to the Immaculate Heart of Mary, an act Sister Lúcia confirmed as fulfilling Our Lady's request.
- **May 13, 1982 and thereafter** — Pope St. John Paul II repeatedly taught at Fatima that Our Lady's message remains "more urgent" than ever: the maternal call "to penitence, to conversion" resounding from the Heart of Mary.[^8]
- **2002** — The _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ canonized the devotion's place in Catholic popular piety by expressly listing the practice of the First Five Saturdays among the devotions to the Immaculate Heart of Mary, alongside consecration and acts of reparation.[^2]

## The Conditions of the Devotion

On the **first Saturday of the month, for five consecutive months**, the faithful perform four acts, all offered **with the intention of making reparation to the Immaculate Heart of Mary**:

| #   | Condition                         | Practical Norms                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Sacramental Confession**        | May be made up to eight days before or after the First Saturday (and, with a legitimate cause and right intention, further in advance), provided one receives Holy Communion on the day itself in the state of grace and with the intention of reparation (cf. the apparition of February 15, 1926). |
| 2   | **Holy Communion of Reparation**  | Received on the First Saturday itself, in the state of grace, offered in reparation to the Immaculate Heart of Mary.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 3   | **The Rosary (five decades)**     | The customary third part of the Rosary — one set of five decades with the meditation on the corresponding mysteries.                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 4   | **Fifteen minutes of meditation** | Keeping Our Lady company for a quarter of an hour, meditating on the mysteries of the Rosary. This meditation is distinct from the recitation of the decades and may be made on one mystery or on several, using Scripture, mental prayer, or a meditation guide.                                    |

### Practical Notes on Fulfilling the Devotion

- **The intention of reparation** must accompany the practice: it is a month-by-month, act-by-act offering made for the five offenses against the Immaculate Heart (blasphemies against her Immaculate Conception, her virginity, her maternity, the corruption of children against her, and outrages against her images), for one's own sins, and for the conversion of sinners.
- **Parish practice.** In many places the devotion is fulfilled communally: a First Saturday Mass, confessions, the public recitation of the Rosary, and a fifteen-minute meditation before the Blessed Sacrament or on the mysteries. Communal fulfillment does not substitute for the personal intention of reparation.
- **The meditation can take many forms.** A slow, prayerful reading of the Gospel passages of a mystery; the use of a scriptural Rosary; a meditation before an image of Our Lady; or Eucharistic adoration centred on the mysteries of the Rosary — all are consonant with the request "keep me company for fifteen minutes." For the precise nature of this fourth condition and a worked fifteen-minute example, see [The Fifteen Minutes of Meditation in Detail](#the-fifteen-minutes-of-meditation-in-detail) below.
- **Interruption and resumption.** Those who begin the devotion and are interrupted are encouraged to resume it; the pastoral spirit of Our Lord's clarification in 1926 is that dispositions of grace, contrition, and the intention of reparation are decisive, not a rigid arithmetic of dates. Where the five consecutive months cannot be completed, many of the faithful adopt the custom of adding further First Saturdays with the intention of repairing the omissions.
- **Communion within the Eucharist.** The _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ cautions that the reception of Holy Communion on the Five First Saturdays should not be dominated by "overestimation of temporal factors" but should be re-contextualized "within the framework of the Eucharist": the First Saturdays are an opportunity "to live intensely the paschal Mystery celebrated in the Holy Eucharist, as inspired by the life of the Blessed Virgin Mary."[^2]

### The Fifteen Minutes of Meditation in Detail

The fourth condition — "keep me company for fifteen minutes while meditating on the mysteries of the Rosary" — is the most personal of the four acts and the one most easily reduced to a formality. The request made at Pontevedra is not for a longer Rosary: it is for a **separate quarter of an hour of accompaniment**. Just as Christ asked His disciples in Gethsemane, "Could you not watch with me one hour?" (Matthew 26:40), Our Lady asks for a deliberate, unhurried fifteen minutes spent in her company, considering the mysteries of her Rosary with the intention of consoling her Immaculate Heart.

#### Sister Lúcia's Clarifications (1927)

Because the 1925 formula might be read as though the meditation were simply the mental accompaniment of the decades themselves, Sister Lúcia set out the matter more precisely in a letter of **1927** to Fr. José Bernardo Gonçalves, S.J., her former confessor at Fatima. In substance, she explained three points:[^10]

- The fifteen minutes are **distinct from the recitation of the five decades**. The Rosary is the third condition; the meditation is the fourth. Piously reciting the decades while thinking of the mysteries does not of itself satisfy the fourth condition.
- The meditation may be made **on one mystery or on several**. A full quarter of an hour spent on the Annunciation alone is a complete fulfillment; so is fifteen minutes distributed over several mysteries.
- The meditation **need not be simultaneous** with the recitation of the decades. It may be made before or after the Rosary, or at another hour of the same First Saturday.

#### Ways of Making the Meditation

- **A slow, prayerful reading of the Gospel text** of one mystery, pausing over each verse (e.g., Luke 1:26–38 for the Annunciation).
- **Classical mental prayer**: a brief composition of place, two or three points for consideration, and a closing colloquy with Our Lady.
- **The scriptural Rosary format**: reading the Scripture verse appointed to each Hail Mary while keeping the mystery before the eyes.
- **Eucharistic adoration**: a quarter of an hour before the tabernacle or monstrance, with the mysteries of the Rosary unfolded interiorly.
- **A meditation guide or book**: a published meditation on the mysteries (e.g., from the Fatima pastoral literature or a Rosary commentary), read slowly and prayerfully rather than as study.
- **Family or communal meditation**: parents and children, or a First Saturday congregation, spending the quarter hour together on one mystery, each one holding the personal intention of reparation.

#### A Worked Example: Fifteen Minutes with the Annunciation

Suppose the fourth condition is fulfilled at home, before an image of Our Lady, on a First Saturday. One fruitful pattern:

| Minutes | Practice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–2     | Sign of the cross. State the intention: "I wish to make reparation to your Immaculate Heart." Place yourself in Our Lady's company, as one would sit quietly with a beloved mother.                                                                                       |
| 2–5     | Read Luke 1:26–38 slowly — the angel's greeting, Mary's troubling question, and her _fiat_: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done unto me according to thy word" (Luke 1:38).                                                                                      |
| 5–11    | Mental prayer. Consider her humility at the greeting; her faith in what exceeds every expectation; the silence in which she conceived the Word made flesh. Speak to her simply and directly, as a child speaks to a mother.                                               |
| 11–14   | Petition and reparation. Ask for a share in her humility and obedience; offer the quarter hour for the blasphemies against her divine maternity and for children raised in indifference or contempt toward her (the third and fourth blasphemies of the 1930 revelation). |
| 14–15   | Close with a short act of reparation (below) or the _Memorare_.                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

A closing act of reparation (one example):

    O Immaculate Heart of Mary, pierced by the blasphemies of ungrateful men,
    I offer You this quarter of an hour in reparation for the offenses committed against Your maternal love,
    and I beg You to teach me the humility and obedience of Your fiat at the Annunciation,
    that with You I may say "yes" to God in all things. Amen.

#### Example Prayers for the Fifteen-Minute Meditation

No particular wording is required for the meditation. The following are sample devotional formulas, not official liturgical texts; they may be adapted to the mystery being considered and to one's own circumstances. The prayer should make room for quiet reflection rather than replace it.

**Opening prayer**

    Immaculate Heart of Mary,
    I come to keep you company for these fifteen minutes.
    I offer this time in reparation for the offenses committed against your maternal Heart,
    and I ask you to lead me more deeply into the mysteries of your Son, Jesus Christ.
    Help me to pray with faith, humility, and love.
    Obtain for me the grace to live what I contemplate. Amen.

**The Annunciation: humility and obedience**

    O Mary, at the Annunciation you received God's word with a humble and obedient heart.
    Teach me to welcome God's will even when I do not understand it fully.
    I make reparation for the rejection of your Immaculate Conception and your divine maternity.
    Through your intercession, obtain for me a heart that can answer God with a faithful fiat.
    Lead me to Jesus, the Word made flesh. Amen.

**The Wedding at Cana: intercession and trust**

    O Mary, at Cana you noticed the need of the bride and bridegroom
    and brought it to Jesus with confidence.
    Teach me to see the needs of others and to entrust them to your Son.
    I make reparation for every contempt shown toward your maternal intercession.
    Help me to do whatever Jesus tells me,
    and to become an instrument of charity in my home and community. Amen.

**The Crucifixion: compassion and reparation**

    O Mary, standing beneath the Cross, you remained faithful while your Son offered Himself for the salvation of the world.
    Let me remain with you in reverence before the sacrifice of Jesus.
    I offer this meditation in reparation for the sorrows caused by sin
    and for every offense against your Immaculate Heart.
    Obtain for sinners the grace of repentance,
    and teach me to unite my sufferings to the Cross of Christ. Amen.

**The Resurrection: hope and perseverance**

    O Mary, your faith remained firm as you awaited the Resurrection of your Son.
    Strengthen my hope when prayer feels difficult and God's promises seem hidden.
    I make reparation for indifference toward you and for the neglect of your mother's love.
    Help me to seek the things that are above and to persevere in grace until death.
    May the risen Christ be the joy and center of my life. Amen.

**A prayer for reparation**

    Most sorrowful and Immaculate Heart of Mary,
    I am sorry for the offenses committed against your privileges and your maternal love.
    In union with the Heart of Jesus,
    I offer you my prayers, my sacrifices, and this time of meditation.
    For those who deny your Immaculate Conception or perpetual virginity,
    for those who reject your divine motherhood,
    for those who lead children to indifference or hatred toward you,
    and for those who dishonor your sacred images,
    I ask mercy, conversion, and the triumph of grace in every heart. Amen.

#### Where the Meditation May Be Made

In a church before the Blessed Sacrament; before a statue or image of Our Lady at home, in a shrine, or in a parish _pabellón_ ([cultural-practices/pabellon-saints-pavilion.md](../cultural-practices/pabellon-saints-pavilion.md)); during Eucharistic adoration; in the family; or anywhere one can pray recollectedly for fifteen minutes. What is essential is the **quarter hour itself, the meditation on the mysteries of the Rosary, and the intention of reparation** — the circumstances are free.

## The Promise Attached to the Devotion

The promise, as Our Lady spoke it at Pontevedra, is:

> "I promise to assist at the hour of death, with all the graces necessary for salvation, all those who, on the first Saturday of five consecutive months, shall confess, receive Holy Communion, recite five decades of the Rosary, and keep me company for fifteen minutes while meditating on the mysteries of the Rosary, with the intention of making reparation to me."[^7]

### How the Promise Is to Be Understood

The promise must be read within the Church's theology of grace and final perseverance, and not as a mechanical transaction:

1. **It is a promise of graces, not of salvation independently of one's cooperation.** "Assist at the hour of death, with all the graces necessary for salvation" means that Our Lady will obtain for those who complete the devotion with sincere dispositions the actual graces needed at death — among them contrition, the sacraments, peace of soul, and fidelity. The soul remains free to cooperate with or to reject these graces.
2. **It parallels the Twelve Promises of the Sacred Heart.** Just as the "Great Promise" of the Nine First Fridays (the grace of final perseverance) is to be read as devotional language about the ordinary means of salvation — the sacraments, prayer, perseverance — so the First Saturdays promise is to be read. The _Directory_ makes the identical caution for both devotions, warning against any "reduction of the practice to mere credulity" and insisting that the pious practice lead to an active, lived faith.[^9]
3. **It presupposes the sacramental life.** The promise builds upon Confession and Communion — it cannot be detached from the state of grace and the life of grace. A person who fulfilled the externals while living in unrepented mortal sin would not receive the promise's benefit, for the devotion's very first conditions are the sacraments.
4. **It directs the faithful to theology, not superstition.** The promise is best understood through the doctrine of the communion of saints: the intercession of the Mother of God at the hour of death is a fruit of the reparation rendered in love, in the same way that the saints' intercession obtains graces for the faithful (CCC 956, 1477).

## The First Saturdays and the Nine First Fridays

The First Saturdays devotion deliberately echoes the **Nine First Fridays of the Sacred Heart**, requested by Our Lord in the revelations to **St. Margaret Mary Alacoque** at Paray-le-Monial (1673–1675). The two devotions form a liturgical and theological diptych — the **Alliance of the Hearts of Jesus and Mary**:

| Element               | Nine First Fridays                                                                                                                                  | Five First Saturdays                                                                                    |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Object of reparation  | The Most Sacred Heart of Jesus                                                                                                                      | The Immaculate Heart of Mary                                                                            |
| Origin of the request | Apparitions to St. Margaret Mary Alacoque, Paray-le-Monial (1673–1675)                                                                              | Apparitions to Sister Lúcia at Pontevedra (1925), completing the Fatima message (1917)                  |
| Number and reason     | Nine months (nine months of Our Lord's hidden life; nine choirs of angels in traditional symbolism)                                                 | Five months, corresponding to the five blasphemies against the Immaculate Heart (revealed May 29, 1930) |
| Day of the week       | Friday — weekly memorial of the Passion                                                                                                             | Saturday — weekly memorial of Our Lady's vigil by the tomb                                              |
| Principal acts        | Confession, Communion of reparation                                                                                                                 | Confession, Communion of reparation, five decades of the Rosary, fifteen minutes of meditation          |
| Central promise       | Grace of final perseverance ("I will not refuse My love... I will give priests the power... My Heart will be their secure refuge at the last hour") | Assistance at the hour of death with all the graces necessary for salvation                             |
| Magisterial reception | _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_, §171                                                                                                  | _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_, §174                                                      |

The _Directory_ notes that the first-Friday devotion, at a time when sacramental Communion was rare among the faithful, "contributed significantly to a renewed use of the Sacraments of Penance and of the Holy Eucharist" — a judgment that applies equally to the First Saturdays, which in the twentieth century carried the same Eucharistic renewal into Marian piety.[^9]

## The First Saturdays and the Communion of Reparation to the Sacred Heart

Pope Pius XI, describing the Communion of Reparation requested by Our Lord of St. Margaret Mary, teaches that the faithful are to "approach the Altar with this purpose of expiating sin."[^3] The First Saturdays transpose this Eucharistic doctrine onto Marian soil: the Communion of each First Saturday is offered **through** the Immaculate Heart of Mary **to** the Sacred Heart of Jesus. In this way the devotion enacts what Pope St. John Paul II described at the International Symposium on the Alliance of the Hearts of Jesus and Mary: that consecration and devotion to the Heart of Mary "is practised essentially by a life of grace, of purity, of prayer, of penance that is joined to the fulfilment of all the duties of a Christian, and of reparation for our sins and the sins of the world."[^4]

Because the First Saturdays place Confession and Communion at their center, the devotion functions as a **regular rhythm of sacramental life** — a structure by which the faithful are led, five months at a time, to the regularity of confession, the fruitfulness of Communion, the meditative praying of the Rosary, and habits of reparation.

For the doctrine of the Rosary and its mysteries, see [liturgy/rosary/how-to-pray.md](rosary/how-to-pray.md).

## Reparation, Temporal Punishment, and the Last Things

The First Saturdays bear fruit in the economy of salvation described in the Church's teaching on **temporal punishment** and **indulgences** (CCC 1471–1479). Acts of reparation made in charity, together with the sacraments and prayer, remit temporal punishment due to sin; the devotion is therefore rightly numbered among the practical means by which the faithful prepare for the particular judgment and assist the Holy Souls in Purgatory — especially when the five Saturdays are offered for the dying and the departed. See [eschatology/purgatory.md](../eschatology/purgatory.md) for the complete doctrinal treatment.

The promises of Fatima, including the First Saturdays and the devotion to the Immaculate Heart, also belong to the Church's eschatological vigilance — the call to conversion, penance, and trust "amid the apocalyptic threats" of history, in Pope St. John Paul II's words.[^8] Contemporary catechesis on the end times, such as that of Fr. Chris Alar, MIC and Fr. Chad Ripperger, recommends the First Saturdays as a central element of the Fatima response. See [eschatology/alar-end-times-interview.md](../eschatology/alar-end-times-interview.md) and [eschatology/ripperger-our-times-part-iii-detachment-suffering-hope.md](../eschatology/ripperger-our-times-part-iii-detachment-suffering-hope.md).

## The First Saturdays in the Eastern Catholic Tradition

The First Saturdays devotion arises from a Latin-rite private revelation and has no exact structural equivalent in the Eastern Catholic Churches. The substance of the devotion, however, finds deep resonance in the **Byzantine and other Eastern traditions**:

- **Saturday as the day of the Theotokos.** In the Byzantine liturgical tradition, each Saturday carries a Marian character alongside its commemoration of the departed: the Canon to the Theotokos is sung at Saturday Orthros (Matins), and the Saturdays of Great Lent are marked by the **Akathist Hymn** ("To the Champion Leader," _Ti Ipermacho Stratego_) in honor of the Mother of God.
- **The Dormition Fast and the Paraklesis.** During the two-week Dormition Fast (August 1–14), the **Great Paraklesis** (Service of Consolation) to the Theotokos is sung daily — a sustained liturgical intercession closely analogous in spirit to the reparatory character of the First Saturdays.
- **Marian devotion ordered to the liturgy.** Both the Latin _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ and the Eastern liturgical books situate Marian devotion within — never apart from — the Eucharistic mystery and the liturgical year (cf. CCC 971; _Directory_, §§183–188).

The faithful of the Eastern Catholic Churches who wish to undertake the First Saturdays may do so in union with the Latin Church's devotion, ordering its acts (Confession, Communion, Rosary or an Akathist, meditation) to their own liturgical and spiritual tradition. The universal principle holds in both traditions: Marian devotion is _christocentric_ — it magnifies the Lord (Luke 1:46) and leads to her Son.

## The First Saturdays Within the Full Fatima Response

The First Saturdays are one element of the integrated Fatima message, which also includes:

- **The daily Rosary** — requested at every apparition in 1917 (see [liturgy/rosary/history.md](rosary/history.md)).
- **The Brown Scapular of Our Lady of Mount Carmel** — Our Lady's final apparition as Our Lady of Mount Carmel (October 13, 1917), and the traditional instruction of Sister Lúcia: "The Rosary and the Scapular are inseparable" (see [sacramentals/brown-scapular.md](../sacramentals/brown-scapular.md)).
- **The Consecration of Russia and of individuals to the Immaculate Heart** — fulfilled by Pius XII (1942, 1952), St. John Paul II (1984), and renewed by successive pontiffs (see [miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md](../miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md)).
- **The offering of daily duty and sacrifice** in reparation for sinners — "Pray, pray very much, and make sacrifices for sinners; for many souls go to hell because there are none to sacrifice themselves and to pray for them" (Our Lady to the children, 1917).

## In Summary

The devotion of the Five First Saturdays of reparation to the Immaculate Heart of Mary:

1. Is an **approved popular devotion** grounded in the private revelation of Fatima and its 1925 Pontevedra request — never dogma, and never required for salvation (CCC 67).[^1]
2. Rests upon the **theology of reparation**: love that repairs the offenses of ungrateful men against the Heart of the Mother of God (cf. Pius XI, _Miserentissimus Redemptor_).[^3]
3. Prescribes, on the first Saturday of five consecutive months: **Confession, Communion of reparation, five decades of the Rosary, and fifteen minutes of meditation** on the mysteries — all offered with the intention of reparation.
4. Carries the **promise of assistance at the hour of death** with the graces necessary for salvation — to be read as devotional assurance of graces of perseverance, not as a mechanical guarantee.
5. Echoes the **Nine First Fridays** of the Sacred Heart and embodies the **Alliance of the Hearts of Jesus and Mary**, leading through the Heart of Mary to the Heart of her Son.
6. Is ecclesially received in the _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (§§171, 174) and ordered toward the **Eucharist**, the state of grace, and the full sacramental life.[^2]

> _"My daughter, look at my Heart encircled with the thorns with which ungrateful men pierce it at every moment by their blasphemies and ingratitude. You, at least, try to console me."_
> — Our Lady to Sister Lúcia, Pontevedra, December 10, 1925
