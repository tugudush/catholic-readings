# The Golden Arrow: Reparation to the Holy Name and Devotion to the Holy Face of Jesus

The **Golden Arrow** is a short Catholic prayer of **reparation** that praises the Most Holy Name of God and offers it, together with the Holy Face of Jesus, to the Eternal Father for the conversion of sinners. It is inseparably connected with the devotion to the **Holy Face of Jesus** which arose at the **Carmel of Tours**, France, in the 1840s, and with the work of **Léon Dupont**, the "holy man of Tours."

Its reported origin is a set of interior communications ("locutions") received by the Discalced Carmelite **Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre** (born Perrine Éluère, 1816–1848), who was told, as she recorded it, that **blasphemy against the Holy Name is a "poisoned arrow"** aimed at the Heart of Christ, and that the prayer of praise she was given in return — the "Golden Arrow" — would both console that wounded Heart and draw down graces for the conversion of sinners.

The devotion has two objects, both drawn from the Decalogue:

1. **Reparation for blasphemy against the Holy Name of God** (the Second Commandment); and
2. **Reparation for the profanation of Sunday** and the holy days of obligation (the Third Commandment).

> **Doctrinal status.** The revelations to Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre are **private revelation**: they may be believed as human testimony, but they add nothing to the deposit of faith and belief in them is **not** required for salvation ([CCC 66–67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/66)).[^1] The **devotion itself**, by contrast, has been received and approved by the Church — above all in the 1885 approval of the devotion to the Holy Face under Pope Leo XIII and the 1958 institution of the Feast of the Holy Face on Shrove Tuesday under Pope Pius XII.

## 1. The Prayer

As the devotion has traditionally been handed down:

    May the most holy, most sacred, most adorable,
    most incomprehensible and ineffable Name of God
    be forever praised, blessed, loved, adored and glorified,
    in Heaven, on earth and under the earth,
    by all the creatures of God,
    and by the Sacred Heart of Our Lord Jesus Christ
    in the Most Holy Sacrament of the Altar.
    Amen.

The prayer is a verbal echo of the act of praise in [Philippians 2:9–11](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=phi&g=2&p=9) — _"at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those that are in heaven, on earth, and under the earth"_ — and an answer to the First Petition of the Our Father, that the **Name** of God be hallowed.

Variants of the text exist. Some versions render "ineffable" as **"unutterable,"** and some older translations read "in heaven, on earth, and **in the hells**" for "under the earth." A shorter related ejaculation, also attributed to the same communications, is:

    Admirable is the Name of God!

This brief invocation — _"Oh if you only knew what great merit you acquire by saying even once, 'Admirable is the Name of God,' in a spirit of reparation for blasphemy"_ — is recorded among Sister Marie's interior locutions and is easily memorized for habitual use.

## 2. Origin: Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre of the Carmel of Tours

**Perrine Éluère** was born on **4 October 1816** at **Rennes** in Brittany, the daughter of Peter and Frances Portier Éluère, and was baptized in the Church of Saint-Germain. Her mother died when she was twelve, and she was sent to train as a dressmaker with two paternal aunts.

On **13 November 1839** she entered the **Discalced Carmelite monastery of Tours** — a house with a particular devotion to the Sacred Heart — and on **8 June 1841** she was professed under the name **Marie de Saint-Pierre et de la Sainte Famille** (Mary of Saint Peter and of the Holy Family). She died on **8 July 1848**, at the age of thirty-one, without seeing the devotion she had been given spread beyond her cloister.

Sister Marie was careful to insist that her communications were **not** visions or apparitions: she described them as interior illuminations in which the truths shown her were "given her to understand," and she was neither shown an image nor did she physically hear a voice.

The chronology of the Golden Arrow is best given carefully, because popular accounts compress it:

| Date                      | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **8 August 1843**         | Pope Gregory XVI promulgated a brief erecting a confraternity under the patronage of Saint Louis IX for reparation of blasphemy against the Holy Name of God.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **26 August 1843**        | Léon Dupont distributed among several religious communities of Tours a prayer in honour of the Holy Name of God; the Carmelites, despite their friendship with him, were overlooked.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **c. mid-September 1843** | Sister Marie reported that, beginning her evening prayer, she understood that Our Lord would give her a prayer of reparation — a "golden dagger" against blasphemy of His Holy Name. The **Golden Arrow** itself is popularly dated to **26 August 1843**; her biographer, the Abbé Janvier, places the communication some eighteen days after Dupont's distribution.[^2] |
| **16 March 1844**         | The locution recommending the ejaculation "Admirable is the Name of God" in a spirit of reparation for blasphemy.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **1844–1847**             | Communications concerning the spread of devotion to the **Holy Face** in reparation for sacrilege and blasphemy, which Our Lord likened to a **"poisoned arrow."**                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

Our Lord reportedly told her that the two sins which offend Him most grievously are **blasphemy** and the **profanation of Sunday**, and that this prayer of praise would "pierce Him delightfully" even as it healed the wounds inflicted by blasphemers — with "torrents of graces for the conversion of sinners" flowing from the Heart so honored.

## 3. The Two Wounds: Blasphemy and the Profanation of Sunday

The two objects of the devotion are the two sins against the **first table of the Law** that discourse most directly against God's honour:

- **Blasphemy against the Holy Name** is forbidden by the **Second Commandment**, _"Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain"_ (Exodus 20:7). The Catechism teaches that this precept "prescribes respect for the Lord's name," forbidding the abuse of God's name and every use of it that is contemptuous, hateful, or mocking ([CCC 2142–2149](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2142)).[^6]
- **Profanation of Sunday** and the holy days of obligation is the violation of the **Third Commandment**, _"Remember that thou keep holy the sabbath day"_ (Exodus 20:8), which the Church applies to the Lord's Day and to the feasts of obligation ([CCC 2168–2188](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2168)).[^7] Sunday is not merely a day of rest but the weekly memorial of the Resurrection, on which the faithful are bound to participate in the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass ([CCC 2181](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2181)).

The devotion is therefore not an eccentric private addition to Catholic prayer but an **act of reparation** offered for two sins already condemned by divine law. It belongs to the wider theology of reparation — the truth that the members of Christ, by virtue of the **Communion of Saints**, can "fill up those things that are wanting of the sufferings of Christ" ([Colossians 1:24](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=cl&g=1&p=24)) and make satisfaction for sins both their own and others' ([Catholic Encyclopedia, "Reparation"](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/f2c979a8-871d-4f46-a059-320d3b837a76/ref/Reparation)).[^3] For the confession of these specific sins, see the treatment of the Second Commandment in [sacraments/examination-of-conscience.md](../sacraments/examination-of-conscience.md).

## 4. The Devotion to the Holy Face of Jesus (1844–1847)

From 1844 to 1847 Sister Marie reported further interior communications urging the spread of devotion to the **Holy Face of Jesus** in reparation for the insults Christ suffered in His Passion — and above all for sacrilege and blasphemy, which she understood as adding to those outrages in every generation.

The devotion is rooted in the **Veil of Veronica**: the tradition that a woman of Jerusalem wiped the blood and sweat — and, in Sister Marie's understanding, the spit and mud — from the Face of Jesus on the way to Calvary, and that His image was imprinted on the cloth. That scene is commemorated in the **Sixth Station of the Cross**; see [liturgy/stations-of-the-cross.md](stations-of-the-cross.md).

Two features distinguish the devotion as Sister Marie was given to understand it:

1. **The Holy Face is offered, not merely contemplated.** The remedy for blasphemy is an act of praise and offering: the Holy Face is presented to the **Eternal Father** as a "coin of infinite value" in exchange for the pardon of sinners and the ingratitude of souls dedicated to God. This is the theological heart of the Golden Arrow's connection with the Holy Face — the reparation is not a new doctrine but the offering of the One whom the Father always hears.
2. **Suffering with Christ's Face is ordered to glory.** A locution of 1844 records Our Lord as saying: _"Those who will contemplate the wounds on My Face here on earth shall contemplate it radiant in heaven."_

Two related sacramentals also descend from these communications:

- The **"Little Gospel"** or **Little Sachet** (1847) — a small leaflet bearing the short Gospel of the Circumcision ([Luke 2:21](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=2&p=21)), which names Jesus, together with the Holy Name monogram **IHS** and the motto _"When Jesus was named, Satan was disarmed."_ The leaflet is folded into a fabric pouch and worn; its bearer is encouraged to repeat the aspiration _"Blessed be the Most Holy Name of Jesus without end."_
- The **Scapular of the Holy Face**, approved by **Pope Leo XIII in 1885**, worn in honour of the Holy Face. See [sacramentals/other-approved-scapulars.md](../sacramentals/other-approved-scapulars.md) for the theology of scapular devotion and the rite of enrollment.

## 5. Léon Dupont and the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face

The man chiefly responsible for the spread of the devotion was **Léon Papin Dupont** (24 January 1797 – 18 March 1876), a lawyer and layman of Tours, remembered as the **"holy man of Tours"** and the "apostle of the Holy Face." Born in Martinique, he had settled in Tours in 1834; he helped establish nightly Eucharistic adoration in the city, founded charitable works with the Society of Saint Vincent de Paul and the Little Sisters of the Poor, and was a tireless promoter of the Saint Benedict Medal (see [sacramentals/medal-of-st-benedict.md](../sacramentals/medal-of-st-benedict.md)).

Having heard of Sister Marie's communications — he managed the temporal affairs of the Carmel, and she, as portress, supplied him with copies of the Little Gospel to distribute — Dupont began to keep a **lamp burning continuously before an image of the Holy Face**, at that time painted after the likeness of the **Veil of Veronica** (the image on the Shroud of Turin was not discernible to the naked eye until Secondo Pia's 1898 photographic negative). He invited visitors to pray with him before the image, and from the lamp's oil came a long stream of reported cures; the testimony cited in the _Dublin Review_ of 1885 records Mgr. Paul Guérin's attestation to having seen **more than 6,000 certificates of cures** attributed to the oil.[^5]

| Year          | Development                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

| **1847**      | A distinct **Archconfraternity of Reparation for blasphemy and the neglect of Sunday** was founded at Saint-Martin-de-La-Noue in Saint-Dizier, France, by Mgr. Parisis, Bishop of Langres — not the same institution as the devotion at Tours.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **1851**      | The **Archconfraternity of the Holy Face** was established at Tours through Dupont's piety.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **1874–1876** | Archbishop Charles-Théodore Colet of Tours examined the documents on Sister Marie's life, gave permission in 1876 for their publication, and encouraged the devotion — shortly before Dupont's death.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **1876**      | After Dupont's death his house on the Rue Saint-Étienne was purchased and his oratory made a chapel, dedicated **29 June 1876**; the **Confraternity of the Holy Face** was canonically erected and the **Priests of the Holy Face** established to serve the pilgrims. The oratory — the **Oratory of the Holy Face** — is today at 8 Rue Bernard Palissy, Tours, in the care of the Dominican Fathers of the French Province. |
| **1939**      | Dupont's cause for beatification was formally introduced, granting him the title **Servant of God**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

## 6. Ecclesiastical Approvals and Canonical Status

| Date             | Act                                                                                                                                                                  | Authority                                  |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **1843 (8 Aug)** | Papal brief erecting a confraternity of reparation for blasphemy against the Holy Name under Saint Louis IX                                                          | Pope Gregory XVI                           |
| **1851**         | Archconfraternity of the Holy Face established at Tours                                                                                                              | Church authority at Tours                  |
| **1876**         | Publication of Sister Marie's life and encouragement of the devotion                                                                                                 | Archbishop Charles-Théodore Colet of Tours |
| **1885**         | Approval of the Scapular of the Holy Face and elevation of the Confraternity of the Holy Face to an **archconfraternity**; approval of the devotion to the Holy Face | Pope Leo XIII                              |
| **1936–1945**    | Devotion of the Holy Face Medal, based on the Holy Face of the Shroud of Turin, promoted by Bl. Maria Pierina De Micheli                                             | Approved by Pope Pius XII                  |
| **1958**         | Formal declaration of the **Feast of the Holy Face of Jesus** on **Shrove Tuesday** (the Tuesday before Ash Wednesday) for all Roman Catholics[^9]                   | Pope Pius XII                              |

The decretal tradition numbers the **Holy Face** among the well-known archconfraternities of the Church, alongside those of the Holy Name, the Blessed Sacrament, the Sacred Heart, the Precious Blood, and the Holy Rosary ([Catholic Encyclopedia, "Archconfraternity"](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/f2c979a8-871d-4f46-a059-320d3b837a76/ref/Archconfraternity)).[^4]

Two clarifications follow from these facts:

- The **revelations** to Sister Marie remain private revelation; the **devotion**, the **scapular**, the **archconfraternity**, and the **feast** are acts of the Church's pastoral and liturgical authority, and are therefore **ecclesiastical and mutable** — they could be modified, as all disciplines can.
- The Feast of the Holy Face on Shrove Tuesday is a **liturgical discipline**, not a dogma; its institution neither canonizes the private revelations nor adds them to the deposit of faith.

## 7. Thérèse of Lisieux and the Holy Face

The most famous heir of the Tours devotion is **St. Thérèse of Lisieux** (1873–1897), who took the religious title **"of the Child Jesus and of the Holy Face."** Her parents, **Louis and Zélie Martin**, were enrolled in the Confraternity of the Holy Face in April 1885, and Thérèse was introduced to the devotion by her sister Pauline (Mother Agnès of Jesus).

Thérèse's writings gave the devotion a lasting voice. In her **"Canticle to the Holy Face"** (August 1895) she wrote: _"Jesus, Your ineffable image is the star which guides my steps."_ She also composed a **Holy Face prayer for sinners**:

    Eternal Father, since Thou hast given me for my inheritance
    the adorable Face of Thy Divine Son,
    I offer that face to Thee and I beg Thee,
    in exchange for this coin of infinite value,
    to forget the ingratitude of souls dedicated to Thee
    and to pardon all poor sinners.

Note how the offering of the Holy Face to the Father — the very structure of the Golden Arrow — is taken up explicitly by Thérèse. For her life and doctrine, see [saints/st-therese-lisieux.md](../saints/st-therese-lisieux.md).

## 8. Praying the Golden Arrow Today

The Golden Arrow is a **devotional formula**, not a liturgical text, and may be prayed privately at any time. It is especially fitting:

- **In reparation for blasphemy** — after hearing God's Name profaned, or as a habitual counterweight to a culture of casual blasphemy;
- **In reparation for the profanation of Sunday** — on Sundays and holy days, or when one is unable to assist at Mass;
- **Before the Blessed Sacrament**, in [Eucharistic adoration](eucharistic-adoration.md), or immediately after Holy Communion, when the Sacred Heart is present in the Most Holy Sacrament of the Altar that the prayer itself names;
- **Together with the Divine Praises**, whose line _"Blessed be His Holy Name"_ belongs to the same family of acts of reparation;
- **As a brief offering for the conversion of sinners**, joined to the Rosary, the [Chaplet of Divine Mercy](chaplet-divine-mercy.md), or an [act of spiritual communion](spiritual-communion.md).

It may be repeated freely and needs no special permission, enrollment, or ceremony. No specific indulgence is attached to the Golden Arrow as such; it is an act of **latria** (the worship due to God alone) offered in a spirit of praise and reparation. As with all devotions, it should be ordered to and never substituted for the Church's sacred liturgy.

## 9. Eastern Catholic Resonance

Although the Golden Arrow and the Tours devotion are Latin in origin, their substance has deep Eastern Catholic analogues:

- The **Jesus Prayer** — _"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner"_ — is the Byzantine tradition's continuous invocation of the Holy Name, and is likewise understood as a prayer that hallows the Name and heals the soul.
- The **veneration of the Holy Face** finds an ancient iconographic counterpart in the Eastern _acheiropoieta_ ("images not made by hands") — the **Mandylion** of Edessa and the _Image of the Saviour Not Made by Hands_ — which are venerated with the same theology of the Incarnation that underlies the Western devotion.
- The **monogram IHS** and the **feast of the Holy Name of Jesus** (3 January in the Roman calendar; the Circumcision and naming of Christ, 1 January, in the Byzantine calendar) express the same reverence for the divine Name.
- The Eastern Catholic and Orthodox traditions know the **Akathist** and **Paraklesis** as extended hymns of praise that, like the Golden Arrow, are acts of veneration offered on behalf of the whole Church.

## In Summary

1. The **Golden Arrow** is a prayer of praise and reparation for **blasphemy** against the Holy Name and for the **profanation of Sunday**, reported to have been given to **Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre** of the Carmel of Tours in 1843.
2. It is inseparable from the **devotion to the Holy Face of Jesus**, which the same communications urged in reparation for the outrages of the Passion, and which offers the Holy Face to the Eternal Father as a "coin of infinite value" for sinners.
3. The devotion was promoted by **Léon Dupont**, the "holy man of Tours," and embodied in the **Archconfraternity of the Holy Face** (1851), the **Oratory of the Holy Face**, and the **Scapular of the Holy Face**.
4. It was **approved by Pope Leo XIII (1885)** and given a liturgical feast on **Shrove Tuesday by Pope Pius XII (1958)**; the private revelations themselves remain **optional belief**.
5. It was carried forward, above all, by **St. Thérèse of Lisieux**, "of the Child Jesus and of the Holy Face."
