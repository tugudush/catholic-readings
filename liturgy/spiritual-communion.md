# Spiritual Communion

**Spiritual Communion** is an act of faith, love, and desire in which a person longs to receive Jesus Christ in the Most Holy Eucharist and unites himself or herself to Christ when sacramental reception is not possible or is not prudent.

It is a genuine Catholic devotional practice, but it is not the same as receiving the consecrated Host. The desire for the Eucharist should lead a person toward repentance, deeper faith, and worthy sacramental reception when that becomes possible.

---

## 1. Spiritual Communion and Sacramental Communion

The Council of Trent distinguished three ways of receiving the Eucharist: sacramentally only, spiritually only through faith and desire, and both sacramentally and spiritually. The third is the fullest reception: the person receives the sacrament while approaching with faith, charity, and the proper dispositions (Council of Trent, Session XIII, chapter 8).

| Form of reception                       | What it means                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sacramental Communion**               | Actually receiving the consecrated Eucharist, which is truly the Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity of Jesus Christ (CCC 1374). |
| **Spiritual Communion**                 | Desiring to receive Christ sacramentally and uniting oneself to Him through faith, love, repentance, and prayer.             |
| **Sacramental and spiritual Communion** | Receiving the Eucharist sacramentally with faith, charity, and a rightly disposed heart.                                     |

Spiritual Communion does not consecrate bread, cause the physical reception of the Eucharist, or remove the need for Confession. It is not a substitute that makes sacramental Communion unnecessary. The Church strongly recommends that the faithful receive the Eucharist frequently when they are properly disposed (CCC 1389).

---

## 2. When to Make a Spiritual Communion

A person may make a spiritual Communion:

- during Mass when he or she should not receive sacramentally;
- when illness, travel, or lack of access prevents sacramental reception;
- before the tabernacle or during Eucharistic adoration;
- at home while praying with the day's Scripture readings;
- after an act of repentance, while preparing to return to sacramental Communion.

A Catholic who is conscious of grave sin must receive the Sacrament of Penance before receiving Holy Communion, except in the exceptional circumstances described by canon law (CCC 1385; Code of Canon Law, canon 916). Spiritual Communion may still be offered as a prayer of repentance and desire for Christ, but it does not itself absolve mortal sin or replace sacramental Confession.

---

## 3. How to Make a Spiritual Communion at Mass

1. Remain prayerfully present during the Liturgy of the Eucharist and the Communion Rite.
2. When others approach for Communion, remain in your place if you are not receiving.
3. Make an act of faith in the Real Presence of Christ.
4. Express love for Jesus and a sincere desire to receive Him sacramentally when you can.
5. Ask for repentance, perseverance, and the grace to live in communion with Him.
6. Remain in silence for a moment of thanksgiving and continue participating in the Mass.

Some parishes invite those not receiving Communion to approach for a blessing; others do not. Follow the practice of the local parish and do not present yourself in a way that could be mistaken for receiving the Eucharist.

---

## 4. A Simple Act of Spiritual Communion

This contemporary devotional form may be prayed during Mass or at any other time:

    Jesus, I believe that You are truly present in the Most Holy Sacrament.
    I love You and desire to receive You.
    Since I cannot receive You sacramentally now,
    unite me spiritually to Yourself.
    Give me grace to repent, live faithfully,
    and receive You worthily when I can.
    Never let me be separated from You. Amen.

The traditional prayer associated with St. Alphonsus Liguori is also presented in the [Act of Spiritual Communion in the Sacred Heart devotion](sacred-heart-devotion.md).

---

## 5. The Interior Dispositions

A spiritual Communion should include the principal dispositions of Eucharistic devotion:

- **Faith** in the Real Presence of Jesus Christ;
- **Adoration** of Christ, truly present in the Sacrament;
- **Love** for Jesus and His Church;
- **Repentance** for sin and a desire for conversion;
- **Desire** to receive sacramentally when properly prepared;
- **Hope** in the grace and mercy of God;
- **Charity** expressed through obedience and service to others.

The prayer should not be treated as a way to minimize mortal sin or to decide a disputed canonical situation privately. When the reason for refraining from Communion involves marriage, canonical status, or another public matter, the person should follow the guidance of a confessor or pastor. For a civil marriage that requires regularization, see [Convalidation](../sacraments/convalidation.md).

---

## 6. Latin and Eastern Catholic Traditions

Spiritual Communion belongs to the shared Catholic treasury of Eucharistic devotion. The wording of private prayers may differ between the Latin Church and the Eastern Catholic Churches, but the essential act is the same: faith in Christ's Eucharistic presence, love for Him, repentance, and a sincere desire for sacramental communion.

Eastern Catholics who cannot receive the Divine Eucharist should follow the discipline and pastoral guidance of their own parish or eparchy. Blessed bread distributed in some Eastern traditions, such as antidoron, is not the Eucharist and should not be confused with sacramental Communion.

---

## 7. Levels of Authority

- The **Real Presence** of Christ in the Eucharist is a definitive teaching of the Catholic faith (CCC 1374; Council of Trent, Session XIII).
- The distinction between sacramental and spiritual reception is taught in the Council of Trent, Session XIII, chapter 8.
- The duty to be properly disposed before receiving Holy Communion is grounded in the Church's sacramental discipline (CCC 1385; Code of Canon Law, canon 916).
- The wording of the prayer above is a private devotional formula. Catholics are free to use it, adapt it, or use another approved prayer; no particular spiritual-Communion formula is required.
