# Litany of the Blessed Virgin Mary (Litany of Loreto)

The **Litany of the Blessed Virgin Mary**, commonly called the **Litany of Loreto** (_Litaniae Lauretanae_), is a Marian litany of praise and supplication. It invokes the Blessed Virgin Mary with biblical, patristic, and ecclesial titles and repeatedly asks for her intercession: **pray for us**.

The Litany of Loreto is an approved Catholic devotional and liturgical prayer. It is not a dogmatic definition, a sacrament, or a mandatory part of the Holy Rosary or the Mass. The Church's _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ identifies it as one of the two Marian litanies contained in the liturgical books and explicitly explains that litanies are independent acts of worship rather than an intrinsic appendix to the Rosary ([Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, no. 203](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/203)).

## 1. How to Pray the Litany

The Litany may be prayed privately or communally. In a public recitation, a leader normally proclaims each invocation and the people answer with the repeated response. The celebrant or leader may introduce and conclude the prayer according to the approved book or local custom.

1. Make the **Sign of the Cross**.
2. Proclaim the invocations to the Most Holy Trinity, responding with the appropriate petition.
3. Continue through the Marian invocations, responding **Pray for us** after each title.
4. Recite the invocations to the Lamb of God.
5. Pray the versicle and response:

        V. Pray for us, O holy Mother of God.
        R. That we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ.

6. Conclude with the collect printed below, or with the collect appointed in the approved liturgical or devotional book being used.

The Litany is especially associated with processions, celebrations of the Word of God, Marian feasts, parish devotions, and the Rosary in October. These associations do not change its status as an independent act of worship. Catholics should follow the liturgical and pastoral direction of the competent ecclesiastical authority when the Litany is used in a public celebration.

## 3. Meaning of the Marian Titles

The Litany's titles are not a collection of independent claims about a goddess or a source of divine power apart from God. They are a concentrated confession of the mystery of Christ and of Mary's place in the economy of salvation.

- **Holy Mother of God** expresses the Church's confession that the one Mary bore is truly the eternal Son made man. The title protects the truth of the Incarnation: Mary is Mother according to the humanity of the one divine Person, Jesus Christ.
- **Mother of Christ, Mother of the Church, Mother of Mercy, and Mother of Hope** unite Mary's relationship to Christ with her maternal spiritual care for the members of His Body. Her motherhood is entirely received from the grace and mission of her Son.
- **Mother of divine grace** and **Cause of our joy** direct the faithful toward God's grace and the joy of salvation. Mary is not the efficient source of grace; God alone gives grace, while Mary cooperates as the Mother of the Redeemer and intercedes for the faithful.
- **Mirror of justice, Seat of wisdom, and Mother of good counsel** praise the holiness, wisdom, and obedience by which Mary received God's word and kept it faithfully.
- **Spiritual vessel, Mystical rose, Tower of David, Ark of the covenant, and Gate of heaven** use poetic images drawn from Sacred Scripture and the Church's tradition. They should be read Christologically, especially in relation to the Incarnation, the New Covenant, and the Church's hope of eternal life.
- **Health of the sick, Refuge of sinners, Solace of migrants, Comforter of the afflicted, and Help of Christians** entrust human suffering and the needs of the Church to Mary's maternal intercession. Such prayer does not replace Christ's unique mediation, the sacraments, medical care, or the duties of justice and charity.
- **Queen of angels through Queen of peace** proclaim Mary's participation in the glory of her risen Son. Her queenship is a service of maternal intercession, not a rival sovereignty alongside the Kingship of Christ.

The Second Vatican Council teaches that Mary's maternal role neither diminishes nor obscures Christ's unique mediation; rather, her entire influence flows from the abundance of Christ's merits and leads the faithful to Him ([_Lumen Gentium_, 60, 62, 66-69](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html)). The Catechism likewise distinguishes authentic Marian devotion from adoration, which belongs to God alone (CCC 971, 2675-2679).

## 4. The Three Invocations Added in 2020

On June 20, 2020, the Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments issued a circular letter communicating Pope Francis' decision to insert three invocations into the Litany of Loreto. The circular specified their exact positions in the litany ([Acta Apostolicae Sedis, issue 7, July 2020, p. 28](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/be9ac8a9-8dee-4735-9f88-b12f5007281f/ref/page28)).

| Invocation             | Placement in the Litany          | Pastoral emphasis                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mother of Mercy**    | After **Mother of the Church**   | Entrusting the Church and the world to Mary's maternal intercession in the mercy of God.  |
| **Mother of Hope**     | After **Mother of divine grace** | Confessing Christian hope amid fear, uncertainty, and suffering.                          |
| **Solace of migrants** | After **Refuge of sinners**      | Commending migrants, refugees, and communities that receive them to Mary's maternal care. |

These additions are ecclesiastical developments in an approved liturgical and devotional formula. They do not add a new article to the deposit of faith or alter the Church's doctrine about Mary.

## 5. History and Ecclesial Status

The Litany of Loreto belongs to the wider medieval tradition of Marian litanies, which developed through a long history of invocations to the Blessed Virgin Mary. The exact origin of the Loreto form is not securely documented. The text became associated with the Shrine of the Holy House at Loreto and was printed in the sixteenth century; historical reference works identify 1587 as the year in which it received definitive ecclesiastical approval. The historical details of its early composition should therefore be distinguished from the Church's later approval and use of the text.

The Roman Pontiffs repeatedly recommended the Litany. In particular, the custom of concluding the Rosary with the Litany during October contributed to its widespread association with the Rosary. The Holy See's _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ preserves the important distinction: the Litany may accompany the Rosary or other acts of worship, but it remains an independent act of worship rather than a required fifth part of the Rosary.

The Litany is not the same as:

- the **Litany of the Saints**, which invokes the saints and asks them to intercede for the Church;
- the **Litany for the Coronation of Images of the Blessed Virgin Mary**, the other Marian litany identified in the _Directory_; or
- the **Litany of the Most Holy Name of Jesus** and other approved litanies dedicated to particular mysteries or saints.

## 6. Latin and Eastern Catholic Considerations

The Litany of Loreto is a Marian prayer of the Latin Church and is also available as a Catholic devotional prayer beyond the Latin Church. The twenty-three Eastern Catholic Churches possess their own liturgical traditions, including litanies, the Akathist Hymn, the Paraclesis, and other services honoring the Theotokos. These Eastern forms should not be replaced or treated as less Catholic simply because the Litany of Loreto is familiar in the West.

An Eastern Catholic may pray the Litany of Loreto as an approved personal or communal devotion, subject to the discipline of the proper Church and the direction of the local pastor. In every rite, Marian prayer is ordered to the worship of the Holy Trinity and to deeper conformity to Jesus Christ.

## 7. Doctrinal Classification

| Element                                           | Proper classification                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Invocation of Mary as **Mother of God**           | A Marian title expressing the dogma of the Incarnation and the Church's Christological faith. |
| The individual sequence and wording of the litany | An approved ecclesiastical prayer formula and devotional practice.                            |
| The 2020 additions                                | An authorized development in the liturgical and devotional text, not a new dogma.             |
| Asking Mary to **pray for us**                    | Catholic intercessory prayer, grounded in the communion of saints and Mary's maternal role.   |
| Use after the Rosary or in October                | A pastoral and devotional custom, not a universal obligation.                                 |

The faithful may therefore pray the Litany with confidence as an approved act of Marian devotion while remembering that belief in any particular private devotional association is not required for salvation. The definitive center of Catholic faith remains the public Revelation fulfilled in Jesus Christ and transmitted in Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition.
