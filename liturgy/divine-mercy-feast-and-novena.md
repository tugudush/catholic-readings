# The Feast of Divine Mercy and the Divine Mercy Novena

## Theological Introduction

The **Feast of Divine Mercy** is the title formally given to the **Second Sunday of Easter** (the Octave Day of Easter) for the universal Roman Rite by Pope St. John Paul II at the canonization of **St. Maria Faustina Kowalska** on **30 April 2000**, and confirmed by the **Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments** in its decree _Misericors et miserator_ of **5 May 2000**. It is not a new liturgical day with new proper texts: it is the Octave Day of Easter itself — the day of the Risen Lord's second appearance to the Apostles and of the institution of the ministry of the forgiveness of sins (John 20:19–31) — now bearing a name that proclaims the very content of the Easter mystery.

The **Divine Mercy Novena** is the nine-day prayer dictated by our Lord Jesus Christ to St. Faustina, to be prayed from **Good Friday** through the **eve of the Feast of Mercy** (the Octave of Easter), in which a different group of souls is offered to God the Father each day through the merits of Christ's Passion.

Two levels of doctrinal authority must be carefully distinguished throughout this document:

- The **doctrine of God's mercy poured out in Christ** is divine Revelation and irreformable doctrine (CCC 1846–1848; 210–211; _Dives in Misericordia_); the institution of the ministry of the forgiveness of sins on Easter evening is dogma (CCC 976; 1441).
- The **specific requests of our Lord recorded in the Diary of St. Faustina** — the Feast of Mercy, the Novena, the promises — constitute an **approved private revelation** (CCC 67). The Church has judged the devotion free from doctrinal error and has embraced it in her magisterial documents, but belief in the revelations themselves is not required for salvation, and they add nothing to the definitive public Revelation of Jesus Christ.

_For the broader context of the Divine Mercy revelations — the Image, the Chaplet, and the Hour of Mercy — see [miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md](../miracles/prophecies-visions/divine-mercy-st-faustina.md). For the full text and method of the Chaplet, see [liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md](chaplet-divine-mercy.md); for the 3:00 p.m. Hour of Great Mercy, see [liturgy/hour-of-great-mercy.md](hour-of-great-mercy.md). The separate autumn novena asking St. Faustina's intercession is documented in [liturgy/novena-st-faustina.md](novena-st-faustina.md)._

## 1. Biblical and Liturgical Foundation: The Octave Day of Easter

The Gospel appointed for the Second Sunday of Easter in all three liturgical years is **John 20:19–31**, which narrates two appearances of the Risen Christ:

1. **On the evening of the day of the Resurrection** (John 20:19–23): Jesus stands among the frightened Apostles in the Upper Room, says _"Peace be with you"_, shows them His hands and His side, breathes on them, and says: _"Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained."_
2. **"Eight days later"** (John 20:24–31): Jesus appears again with Thomas present, invites him to touch His wounds, and receives Thomas's confession of faith — _"My Lord and my God!"_ — before pronouncing the beatitude that anticipates the faith of every Christian: _"Blessed are those who have not seen and yet believe."_

The Church has celebrated Easter as an **eight-day feast (an Octave)** from the earliest centuries, precisely because John's Gospel marks the eighth day as the day of the Church's own birth experience: each Sunday is a "little Easter," and the Octave Day of Easter is the crowning of the Mother of all Sundays. Historically, this day bore several names:

| Historical Name            | Origin                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Octava Paschae**         | The Octave Day of Easter in the ancient Roman tradition                                                                                       |
| **_in albis (depositis)_** | From the medieval Latin name _Dominica in albis depositis_ — the Sunday on which the newly baptized laid aside their white baptismal garments |
| **Quasimodo Sunday**       | From the first word of the traditional Introit, _Quasi modo geniti infantes_ (1 Peter 2:2)                                                    |
| **Low Sunday**             | The English name, contrasting the "high" solemnity of Easter with the octave's conclusion                                                     |
| **Thomas Sunday**          | The Byzantine name, from the Gospel of the incredulity and confession of St. Thomas                                                           |

The decree _Misericors et miserator_ grounds the new title precisely in the Gospel of the day, quoting John 20:19–23: the Risen Christ shows His hands and His side, grants peace, and institutes the forgiveness of sins. Divine Mercy Sunday therefore does not import an alien devotion into the liturgy; it names the mystery the day already celebrates — **the mercy that flows from the pierced side of the Risen Christ**.

## 2. The Request for the Feast in the Revelations to St. Faustina

St. Maria Faustina Kowalska (1905–1938), a Sister of Our Lady of Mercy in Poland, recorded in her _Diary: Divine Mercy in My Soul_ our Lord's repeated request that the first Sunday after Easter be solemnly celebrated as the **Feast of Mercy**:

> _"My daughter, tell the whole world about My inconceivable mercy. I desire that the Feast of Mercy be a refuge and shelter for all souls, and especially for poor sinners. On that day the very depths of My tender mercy are open. I pour out a whole ocean of graces upon those souls who approach the Fount of My Mercy. The soul that will go to Confession and receive Holy Communion shall obtain complete forgiveness of sins and punishment. On that day all the divine floodgates through which grace flow are opened. Let no soul fear to draw near to Me, even though its sins be as scarlet. My mercy is so great that no mind, be it of man or of angel, will be able to fathom it throughout all eternity. Everything that exists has come forth from the very depths of My most tender mercy. Every soul in its relation to Me will contemplate My love and mercy throughout eternity. The Feast of Mercy emerged from My very depths of tenderness. It is My desire that it be solemnly celebrated on the first Sunday after Easter. Mankind will not have peace until it turns to the Fount of My Mercy."_ (Diary, § 699)

Our Lord also connected the Feast with the veneration of the Divine Mercy Image (first seen by St. Faustina on 22 February 1931 at Płock), asking that the image be solemnly blessed on the first Sunday after Easter and venerated throughout the world (Diary, § 88). The signature beneath the image — _"Jesus, I trust in You"_ — became the summary aspiration of the entire devotion. Pope St. John Paul II would later say:

> _"How many souls have been consoled by the prayer 'Jesus, I trust in you', which Providence intimated through Sr Faustina! This simple act of abandonment to Jesus dispels the thickest clouds and lets a ray of light penetrate every life."_
> (Homily at the Canonization of St. Mary Faustina Kowalska, 30 April 2000)

And on the first anniversary of the canonization (22 April 2001), the Holy Father quoted our Lord's words to St. Faustina: _"Humanity will never find peace until it turns with trust to Divine Mercy"_ (Diary, p. 132), adding: _"Divine Mercy! This is the Easter gift that the Church receives from the risen Christ and offers to humanity at the dawn of the third millennium."_

### The Theological Reading of the Promise

The Diary's promise — _"complete forgiveness of sins and punishment"_ — is a statement of **private revelation** and must be read in the light of the Church's sacramental and disciplinary teaching, not as a magical guarantee independent of the sacraments:

- The forgiveness of **guilt (eternal punishment)** occurs ordinarily through the **Sacrament of Penance**; perfect contrition joined to the resolve to confess obtains it apart from, or in anticipation of, the sacrament (CCC 1451–1452; 1490).
- The **temporal punishment** due to sin is remitted through **indulgences**, granted by the Church's authority from the treasury of the satisfactions of Christ and the saints (CCC 1471–1473; 1478).
- The Apostolic Penitentiary's decree of 2002 (below) is the Church's authoritative reception of the Diary's promise: it attaches a **plenary indulgence** to the observance of Divine Mercy Sunday under defined conditions — sacramental Confession, Eucharistic Communion, prayer for the Pope's intentions, and detachment from every sin.

Thus the Church neither ignores nor literalistically transposes the private-revelation promise; she translates it into the ordinary grammar of the sacramental life and of indulgenced devotion.

## 3. The Magisterial Institution of the Feast

| Date              | Document / Act                                                              | Content                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **30 April 2000** | Homily of Pope St. John Paul II at the canonization of St. Faustina         | Declares the Second Sunday of Easter "from now on throughout the Church will be called 'Divine Mercy Sunday'"                          |
| **5 May 2000**    | Decree of the Congregation for Divine Worship, _Misericors et miserator_    | Formally assigns the name "Divine Mercy Sunday" ( _Dominica Divinae Misericordiae_ ) to the Second Sunday of Easter for the Roman Rite |
| **22 April 2001** | Meditation of Pope St. John Paul II (first anniversary of the canonization) | Presents the feast as "the Easter gift that the Church receives from the risen Christ and offers to humanity"                          |
| **29 June 2002**  | Decree of the Apostolic Penitentiary                                        | Grants a perpetual plenary indulgence for Divine Mercy Sunday (see § 5 below)                                                          |
| **2001–present**  | _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_, § 154                         | Receives the Divine Mercy devotion within the framework of the Easter Octave                                                           |

The canonization homily of 30 April 2000 situated the declaration within the Easter Gospel itself:

> _"It is important then that we accept the whole message that comes to us from the word of God on this Second Sunday of Easter, which from now on throughout the Church will be called 'Divine Mercy Sunday.'"_

The **Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy** (§ 154) then fixed the ecclesial hermeneutic of the devotion:

> _"In connection with the octave of Easter, recent years have witnessed the development and diffusion of a special devotion to the Divine Mercy based on the writings of Sr. Faustina Kowalska who was canonized 30 April 2000. It concentrates on the mercy poured forth in Christ's death and resurrection, fount of the Holy Spirit who forgives sins and restores joy at having been redeemed. Since the liturgy of the Second Sunday of Easter or Divine Mercy Sunday — as it is now called — is the natural locus in which to express man's acceptance of the Redeemer's mercy, the faithful should be taught to understand this devotion in the light of the liturgical celebrations of these Easter days."_

The institution of the feast stands within the wider "mercy pontificate" of St. John Paul II: his first encyclical, _Dives in Misericordia_ (1980), had already presented the mercy of God as the central truth of Revelation; the Extraordinary Jubilee of Mercy proclaimed by Pope Francis culminated in the apostolic letter _Misericordia et Misera_ (2016). Providence itself seemed to underline the connection: Pope St. John Paul II died on **2 April 2005**, the **vigil of Divine Mercy Sunday**; he was beatified on **1 May 2011** and canonized on **27 April 2014**, both on Divine Mercy Sunday (see [saints/st-john-paul-ii.md](../saints/st-john-paul-ii.md)).

## 4. The Liturgy of Divine Mercy Sunday

Because the decree of 5 May 2000 **added a name, not new proper texts**, the liturgy of Divine Mercy Sunday remains the liturgy of the Second Sunday of Easter:

- **Gospel**: John 20:19–31 in all three years — the appearances of the Risen Lord, the institution of the ministry of forgiveness, and the confession of St. Thomas.
- **Character**: The day is still the **Octave Day of Easter** — a Sunday of the highest rank within the brightest season of the year, celebrated in white, with the _Gloria_, the Creed, and the Easter Sequence (_Victimae paschali laudes_) in the years it is prescribed.
- **Devotions after the liturgy**: The Apostolic Penitentiary decree directs priests to lead the faithful in **prayers to merciful Jesus after Mass or Vespers or during a pious exercise in honor of Divine Mercy**; parishes commonly add the veneration of the Divine Mercy Image, the recitation of the [Chaplet of Divine Mercy](chaplet-divine-mercy.md), and a period of Eucharistic adoration.
- **Sacramental emphasis**: Pastoral practice on this day rightly stresses the **Sacrament of Penance** (with abundant confessors made available) and worthy reception of Holy Communion, since the whole message of the feast is the mercy of the Risen Lord mediated through the forgiveness of sins (CCC 976; 1441–1445).

## 5. The Plenary Indulgence for Divine Mercy Sunday (Decree of 29 June 2002)

On the Solemnity of Saints Peter and Paul, **29 June 2002**, the **Apostolic Penitentiary** issued a decree — promulgated in the _Acta Apostolicae Sedis_ (vol. 94, 2002) — by which the acts of devotion in honor of Divine Mercy are enriched with indulgences, **perpetually valid** ("in perpetuum valiturum"). The decree was signed by Msgr. Aloisius De Magistris, titular Archbishop of Novae and Pro-Penitentiary Major, and Fr. John Francis Girotti, O.F.M. Conv., Regent.

### 5.1 The Ordinary Plenary Indulgence

A **plenary indulgence** is granted, under the usual conditions — **sacramental Confession, Eucharistic Communion, and prayer for the intentions of the Supreme Pontiff** — to any member of the faithful who, on the Second Sunday of Easter (Divine Mercy Sunday), with a soul **completely detached from affection for any sin, even venial**, either:

1. **participates in a pious exercise in honor of Divine Mercy** in any church or oratory; **or**
2. **at least** recites **before the Most Blessed Sacrament** — publicly exposed or even reserved in the tabernacle — the **Our Father** and the **Creed**, adding **some pious invocation of merciful Jesus** (for example, _"Merciful Jesus, I trust in You!"_).

### 5.2 The Partial Indulgence

A **partial indulgence** is granted to the faithful who, with a contrite heart at least, devoutly pour out any legitimately approved prayer to merciful Jesus.

### 5.3 The Indulgence for Those Impeded

Sailors serving on the open sea; the many brothers and sisters made exiles from their homeland by wars, political upheavals, the inclemency of places, and similar causes; **the sick and those who serve them**; and all who cannot leave their homes for a legitimate reason or whose service to the community cannot be delayed — these can obtain the **plenary indulgence** on Divine Mercy Sunday if, with **total detestation of every sin** as above, and with **the intention of fulfilling the three usual conditions as soon as possible**, they recite **before a pious image of Christ crucified** the **Our Father** and the **Creed**, adding a pious invocation of merciful Jesus (for example, _"Merciful Jesus, I trust in You!"_).

If even this is not possible, they may obtain the plenary indulgence by **joining themselves in spirit** to those who carry out the prescribed work in the ordinary way, **offering to the merciful God their prayers together with the hardships and sufferings of their own lives**, always with the intention of fulfilling the three conditions when it becomes possible.

### 5.4 The Duty of Pastors

The decree directs priests with pastoral care — especially parish priests — to **make this salutary disposition of the Church known** to their faithful, to be **promptly and generously available to hear their confessions**, and on Divine Mercy Sunday **solemnly to direct the prescribed prayers to merciful Jesus after the celebration of Holy Mass or Vespers, or during a pious exercise in honor of Divine Mercy**. Because _"Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy"_ (Matthew 5:7), pastors are to draw the faithful, in catechesis, to the **frequent performance of works of charity or mercy**, following the example and command of Christ Jesus as proposed in General Concession II of the _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_.

> **Summary of the conditions**
>
> | Requirement                      | Plenary (ordinary)                                                      | Partial                               | Plenary (impeded)                                              |
> | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
> | Sacramental Confession           | Yes                                                                     | —                                     | Intended (asap)                                                |
> | Eucharistic Communion            | Yes                                                                     | —                                     | Intended (asap)                                                |
> | Prayer for the Pope's intentions | Yes                                                                     | —                                     | Intended (asap)                                                |
> | Detachment from every sin        | Complete                                                                | Contrition                            | Complete detestation                                           |
> | Prescribed devotion              | Exercise / prayer before the Tabernacle + Our Father, Creed, invocation | Any approved prayer to merciful Jesus | Our Father, Creed, invocation before a crucifix or pious image |

## 6. The Divine Mercy Novena

### 6.1 Origin

The Divine Mercy Novena was **dictated by our Lord Jesus Christ to St. Faustina** and is recorded in her _Diary_ (§ 796 ff.); it was first circulated in a printed novena pamphlet in **1937**. Our Lord asked that it be prayed **from Good Friday through the eve of the Feast of Mercy** — that is, through the nine days of the Paschal Octave:

> _"During these days, I will lead you through the desert, so that you may learn My ways..."_ (Diary, § 739)

> _"Prepare yourself for the Feast of Mercy..."_

On **Good Friday**, at the beginning of the first novena, our Lord said to St. Faustina:

> _"I desire that during these nine days you lead souls to the fountain of My mercy, that they may draw therefrom strength and refreshment and whatever grace they need in the hardships of life and, especially, at the hour of death. On each day you will bring to My Heart a different group of souls, and you will immerse them in this ocean of My mercy; and I will bring all these souls into the house of My Father. You will do this in this life and in the next. I will deny nothing to any soul whom you will bring to the Fount of My mercy. On each day you will beg My Father, on the strength of My bitter Passion, for graces for these souls."_

### 6.2 The Nine Days: The Souls Offered Each Day

| Day | Liturgical Day       | Souls Offered to the Father's Mercy                                              |
| --- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Good Friday**      | All mankind, especially all sinners                                              |
| 2   | **Holy Saturday**    | The souls of priests and religious                                               |
| 3   | **Easter Sunday**    | All devout and faithful souls                                                    |
| 4   | **Easter Monday**    | Those who do not believe in God and those who do not yet know Him                |
| 5   | **Easter Tuesday**   | The souls of separated brethren (heretics and schismatics, in the Diary's words) |
| 6   | **Easter Wednesday** | The meek and humble souls and the souls of little children                       |
| 7   | **Easter Thursday**  | The souls who especially venerate and glorify God's mercy                        |
| 8   | **Easter Friday**    | The souls detained in Purgatory                                                  |
| 9   | **Easter Saturday**  | The souls who have become lukewarm                                               |

The novena thus ends on the **eve of Divine Mercy Sunday**. The progression is deliberate: from the whole world of sinners on the day of the Crucifixion, through the Church and those far from her, to the least visible and least loved groups — the suffering souls in Purgatory and the lukewarm — so that no state of soul is left outside the reach of the Divine Mercy.

### 6.3 The Structure of Each Day

Each day consists of:

1. **The day's offering** — naming the group of souls brought to the Heart of Jesus;
2. **The day's prayer to "Most Merciful Jesus"** — the petition proper to that group;
3. **The "Eternal Father" petition** — asking God the Father, on the strength of Christ's bitter Passion, for graces for that day's group (the wording changes each day);
4. Traditionally, the **[Chaplet of Divine Mercy](chaplet-divine-mercy.md)** is then prayed (see [liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md](chaplet-divine-mercy.md) for the full method).

### 6.4 The Full Text of the Novena

#### First Day — Good Friday: All Mankind, Especially All Sinners

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me all mankind, especially all sinful souls, and immerse them in the ocean of My mercy. In this way you will console Me in the bitter grief into which the loss of souls plunges Me."_

    Most Merciful Jesus, whose very nature it is to have compassion on us
    and to forgive us, do not look upon our sins
    but upon our trust in Your endless goodness,
    and receive us all into the embrace of Your inconceivable mercy,
    and make us render You worthy praise forever. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon all mankind
    and especially upon poor sinners, all enfolded in the Most Compassionate Heart of Jesus.
    For the sake of His sorrowful Passion show us Your mercy,
    that we may praise the omnipotence of Your mercy forever and ever. Amen.

#### Second Day — Holy Saturday: The Souls of Priests and Religious

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the souls of priests and religious, and immerse them in My unfathomable mercy. It was they who gave Me strength to endure My bitter Passion. Through them, as through channels, My mercy flows out upon mankind."_

    Most Merciful Jesus, from whom comes all that is good,
    increase Your grace in us, men and women consecrated to Your service,
    that we may perform worthy works of mercy,
    and that all who see us may glorify the Father of Mercy who is in heaven. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon the company of chosen souls in Your vineyard —
    upon the souls of priests and religious —
    and endow them with the strength of Your blessing.
    For the love of the Heart of Your Son, in which they are enfolded,
    impart to them Your power and light,
    that they may be able to comfort others and save them from sin,
    and that they may praise Your mercy forever and ever. Amen.

#### Third Day — Easter Sunday: All Devout and Faithful Souls

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me all devout and faithful souls, and immerse them in the ocean of My mercy. These souls brought Me consolation on the Cross. Every drop of agony and sorrow accepted and offered up with devotion and love eased for Me the dreadful weight of My debt of justice. These souls are the living image of My wounded Heart. They redeemed not only themselves but also other souls. The wicked soul will profit from their merits and be converted."_

    Most Merciful Jesus, from the treasury of Your mercy,
    You impart Your graces in great abundance to each and all.
    Receive us into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart
    and never let us escape from It.
    We beg this of You by that most wondrous love
    for the heavenly Father with which Your Heart burns so fiercely. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon faithful souls,
    as upon the inheritance of Your Son.
    For the sake of His sorrowful Passion grant them Your blessing
    and surround them with Your constant protection.
    Thus may they never fail in love or lose the treasure of the holy faith,
    but rather, with all the hosts of Angels and Saints,
    may they glorify Your mercies for endless ages. Amen.

#### Fourth Day — Easter Monday: Those Who Do Not Believe in God and Those Who Do Not Yet Know Him

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the pagans and those who do not yet know Me. I was also thinking of them during My last agony in the Garden, and their bitter tears of repentance were a comfort to My Heart."_

    Most Compassionate Jesus, You are the Light of the whole world.
    Receive into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart
    all the souls of those who do not believe in God
    and of those who as yet do not know You.
    Let the rays of grace enlighten them
    so that they, too, together with us, extol Your wonderful mercy;
    and do not let them escape from the abode which is Your Most Compassionate Heart. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon the souls of those who do not believe in You,
    and of those who as yet do not know You, but who are enclosed in the Most Compassionate Heart of Jesus.
    Draw them to the light of the Gospel.
    These souls do not know what great happiness it is to love You.
    Grant that they, too, may extol the generosity of Your mercy for endless ages. Amen.

#### Fifth Day — Easter Tuesday: The Souls of Separated Brethren

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the souls of heretics and schismatics, and immerse them in the ocean of My mercy. During My bitter Passion they tore at My Body and Heart; that is, My Church, as they returned to the unity of the fold, became part of My Body."_

    Most Merciful Jesus, Goodness Itself,
    You do not refuse light to those who seek it of You.
    Receive into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart
    the souls of those who have separated themselves from Your Church.
    Draw them by Your light into the unity of the Church,
    and do not let them escape from the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart;
    but bring it about that they, too, come to glorify the generosity of Your mercy. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon the souls of those
    who have separated themselves from Your Son's Church,
    who have squandered Your blessings and misused Your graces
    by obstinately persisting in their errors.
    Do not look upon their errors, but upon the love of Your own Son
    and upon His bitter Passion, which He underwent for their sake,
    since they, too, are enclosed in His Most Compassionate Heart.
    Bring it about that they also may glorify Your great mercy for endless ages. Amen.

#### Sixth Day — Easter Wednesday: The Meek and Humble Souls and the Souls of Little Children

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the meek and humble souls and the souls of little children, and immerse them in My mercy. These souls most closely resemble My Heart. They strengthened Me during My bitter agony. I saw them as earthly Angels, who would keep vigil at My altars. I pour out upon them whole torrents of grace. Only the humble soul is able to receive My grace. I favor humble souls with My confidence."_

    Most Tender Jesus, Meek and Humble of Heart,
    draw me to Yourself from the midst of sinners,
    and receive into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart
    all meek and humble souls and the souls of little children.
    These souls send all heaven into ecstasy,
    and they are the heavenly Father's favorites.
    They are a sweet-smelling bouquet before the throne of God;
    God Himself takes delight in their fragrance.
    These souls have no dwelling place here on earth,
    and every day they sow the seeds of eternal life there. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon meek and humble souls,
    and upon the souls of little children, who are enfolded
    in the abode which is the Most Compassionate Heart of Jesus.
    These souls bear the closest resemblance to Your Son.
    Their fragrance is from His Passion.
    These souls are a living host,
    singing forever a hymn of mercy and love. Amen.

#### Seventh Day — Easter Thursday: The Souls Who Especially Venerate and Glorify God's Mercy

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the souls who especially venerate and glorify My mercy, and immerse them in My mercy. These souls sorrowed most bitterly over My Passion and entered most deeply into My Spirit. They are living images of My Compassionate Heart. These souls will shine like a bright light in the world of darkness, and I will clothe them in the brilliance of My radiance. Each of these souls will be presented to My Father like a precious offering from among the last days of the world."_

    Most Merciful Jesus, whose Heart is Love Itself,
    receive into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart
    the souls of those who particularly extol and venerate the greatness of Your mercy.
    These souls are mighty with the very power of God Himself.
    In every way they go forward, defended by Your mercy.
    They perform deeds of mercy in union with You,
    and they bear with patience and courage
    the trials and sufferings that life sends them,
    doing penance for their own sins and for those of others.
    They firmly trust in the abode which is the Most Merciful Heart of Jesus.
    These souls will shine as a bright light in the world of darkness.
    After this life of exile, they will repose in Your Heart. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon the souls who glorify and venerate
    Your greatest attribute — that of Your fathomless mercy —
    and who are enclosed in the Most Compassionate Heart of Jesus.
    These souls are a living Gospel;
    their hands are full of deeds of mercy,
    and their hearts, overflowing with joy, sing a canticle of mercy to You, O Most High!
    I beg You, O God: show them Your mercy
    according to the hope and yearning of their souls.
    May God, who poured out a great light into their hearts,
    grant them, through His mercy, the hope of participating in His eternal glory. Amen.

#### Eighth Day — Easter Friday: The Souls Detained in Purgatory

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the souls who are detained in purgatory, and immerse them in the abyss of My mercy. Let the torrents of My Blood cool down their scorching flames. All these souls will greatly repay My mercy and will glorify it. I especially love these souls. The fruits of their trials are immense."_

    Most Merciful Jesus, You Yourself have said that You desire mercy;
    so I bring into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart
    the souls in Purgatory, souls who are very dear to You,
    and yet who must make retribution to Your justice.
    May the streams of Blood and Water which gushed forth from Your Heart
    put out the flames of Purgatory,
    that there too the power of Your mercy may be celebrated. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon the souls suffering in Purgatory,
    who are enfolded in the Most Compassionate Heart of Jesus.
    I beg You, by the sorrowful Passion of Jesus Your Son
    and by all the bitterness with which His most sacred Soul was flooded:
    Manifest Your mercy to the souls who are under Your just scrutiny.
    Look upon them in no other way than through the Wounds of Jesus, Your dearly beloved Son;
    for we firmly believe that there is no limit to Your goodness and compassion. Amen.

_On the efficacy of intercession for the souls in Purgatory, see [eschatology/purgatory.md](../eschatology/purgatory.md) and [liturgy/prayers-for-the-dead.md](prayers-for-the-dead.md)._

#### Ninth Day — Easter Saturday: The Souls Who Have Become Lukewarm

**Jesus' instruction**: _"Today bring to Me the souls who have become lukewarm, and immerse them in the abyss of My mercy. These souls wound My Heart most painfully. My soul suffered the most dreadful loathing in the Garden of Olives because of lukewarm souls. They were the reason I cried out: 'Father, take this cup away from Me, if it be Your will.' For them, the last hope of salvation is to flee to My mercy."_

    Most Compassionate Jesus, You are Compassion Itself.
    I bring lukewarm souls into the abode of Your Most Compassionate Heart.
    In this fire of Your pure love,
    let these tepid souls, who like corpses filled You with such deep disgust,
    be once again set aflame.
    O Most Compassionate Jesus, exercise the omnipotence of Your mercy
    and draw them into the very ardor of Your love,
    and bestow upon them the gift of holy love,
    for there is nothing beyond Your power. Amen.

    Eternal Father, turn Your merciful gaze upon lukewarm souls
    who are nonetheless enfolded in the Most Compassionate Heart of Jesus.
    Father of Mercy, I beg You by the bitter Passion of Your Son
    and by His three-hour agony on the Cross:
    Let them, too, glorify the abyss of Your mercy. Amen.

### 6.5 How and When to Pray the Novena

- **The revealed window**: Good Friday through the eve of Divine Mercy Sunday. The _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (§ 154) teaches that this devotion should be understood **in the light of the liturgical celebrations of these Easter days** — the novena is a personal prolongation of the Paschal Mystery.
- **At other times**: The novena may also be prayed at any other time and for the same nine intentions, although the Church connects it most closely with the Easter Octave and the devotions of Divine Mercy Sunday. Many of the faithful pray the [Chaplet](chaplet-divine-mercy.md) throughout the year (especially at the [Hour of Great Mercy](hour-of-great-mercy.md)) and reserve the novena in its full form for the Paschal window.
- **With the sacraments**: The novena culminates on Divine Mercy Sunday, when the faithful are encouraged to receive the Sacrament of Penance and Holy Communion in accord with the indulgence conditions of § 5 above (see [sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md](../sacraments/penance-reconciliation.md)).
- **Authoritative status**: The novena is an **approved private-revelation devotion**, not a liturgical rite; it may be freely used but never imposed, and its promises are received with trust insofar as they accord with the Church's faith and discipline.

## 7. The Novena to St. Faustina (Novena for Her Intercession)

The autumn novena asks for St. Faustina's intercession and is distinct from the Divine Mercy Novena in § 6. It is customarily prayed from **27 September through 5 October**, her feast day.

The complete daily guide — with preparation, order of prayer, Scripture links, original reading summaries, meditations, and prayers for all nine days — is in [liturgy/novena-st-faustina.md](novena-st-faustina.md). This is a private devotion, not a liturgical rite or obligatory formula; no decree attaches an indulgence to it (CCC 956; 2683).

## 8. Latin and Eastern Catholic Perspectives

The Feast of Divine Mercy belongs, as a liturgical institution, to the **Roman Rite**: the name "Divine Mercy Sunday" was added to the calendar of the Roman Church by the decree of the Congregation for Divine Worship in 2000, and the indulgence decree of 2002 is addressed to the whole Latin Church.

For the wider ecclesiology of the Catholic communion of Churches, see [church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md).

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Divine Mercy Sunday a new feast?**
No. It is the Second Sunday of Easter — the Octave Day, celebrated since apostolic times — given a new title in 2000. Its proper texts did not change; what changed is the name, and with it the Church's explicit invitation to contemplate the mercy celebrated in the Easter liturgy.

**Does the Diary's promise of "complete forgiveness of sins and punishment" mean that Confession and Holy Communion on that day automatically wipe out all punishment for sin?**
The promise is private revelation; the Church has received it through the grammar of her own discipline: the 2002 decree attaches a **plenary indulgence** — the full remission of temporal punishment — to the day's devotions under the usual conditions (Confession, Communion, prayer for the Pope's intentions, detachment from sin). One should trust our Lord's generosity while observing the conditions the Church has laid down.

**Must the Divine Mercy Novena begin on Good Friday?**
Our Lord asked that it be prayed from Good Friday to the eve of the Feast of Mercy, and this is the normative and recommended practice, integral to the Paschal Octave. The novena may also be prayed at other times, but the Church directs that the devotion always be understood in the light of the Easter liturgy (DPL § 154).

**What is the difference between the Divine Mercy Novena and the novena to St. Faustina?**
The **Divine Mercy Novena** is the nine-day prayer dictated by Jesus to St. Faustina, offered for nine groups of souls; it ends on the eve of Divine Mercy Sunday. The separate autumn **novena to St. Faustina** asks her intercession; its full guide is at [liturgy/novena-st-faustina.md](novena-st-faustina.md).

**How is the novena to St. Faustina for her intercession prayed?**
It is customarily prayed from **27 September through 5 October**. The order of prayer, linked readings with original summaries, meditations, and daily prayers are in [liturgy/novena-st-faustina.md](novena-st-faustina.md).

**Is the Divine Mercy devotion obligatory?**
No. As a devotion founded on approved private revelation, it is entirely optional (CCC 67); no private revelation adds to the deposit of faith or binds the faithful. It is nevertheless warmly commended by the modern Popes as a faithful echo of the Gospel's mercy.
