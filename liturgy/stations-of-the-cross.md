# The Stations of the Cross

The Via Crucis, or Stations of the Cross, is a devotional exercise that follows the Passion of Jesus through the traditional sequence of fourteen stations. The Church's [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, no. 134](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/134) retains this as the typical form while recognizing approved alternatives, and recommends concluding with hope in the Resurrection.

The traditional sequence includes Gospel events as well as scenes from devotional tradition. Where the Gospels do not narrate a scene, the Scripture reference below is offered for meditation, not as a biblical account of that event. The leader-and-assembly petitions in this guide are original devotional texts; local prayer books may use other wording.

## How to Pray

1. Begin with the Sign of the Cross and the opening prayer below.
2. At each station, announce its title and read the cited Scripture passage. Where a meditation passage is suggested instead, read that passage and remember that the traditional scene itself is not narrated in the Gospels.
3. Pause briefly, then pray the station's leader-and-assembly petition.
4. After each station, say the common response. In a church, move from station to station when possible; at home, follow the sequence in order.
5. After the fourteenth station, read the optional Resurrection passage and closing prayer.

The Stations are a pious devotion, not a single universally fixed liturgical script. The familiar response and petitions below may be adapted to local custom. For the indulgence attached to the devotion, see the Church's [Enchiridion of Indulgences, Concession 13](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Concessiones.%2013), which specifies additional norms.

## Opening

    Leader: In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.
    All: Amen.

    Leader: Lord Jesus Christ, as we follow the way of Your Cross, turn our hearts toward You.
    All: Teach us faith, repentance, and love for all who suffer.

## Common Response

After each station, the leader and assembly may use this familiar acclamation:

    Leader: We adore You, O Christ, and we bless You.
    All: Because by Your holy Cross You have redeemed the world.

## The Fourteen Stations

### First Station: Jesus Is Condemned to Death

**Scripture:** Matthew 27:11-26; Mark 15:1-15; Luke 23:1-25; John 18:28-19:16.

    Leader: Jesus, You endured an unjust sentence.
    All: Give us courage to speak the truth and defend the vulnerable.

### Second Station: Jesus Takes Up the Cross

**Scripture:** John 19:16-17; Matthew 27:31-32; Mark 15:20-21; Luke 23:26.

    Leader: You freely embraced the Cross for our salvation.
    All: Teach us to carry our daily burdens in love.

### Third Station: Jesus Falls the First Time

**Meditation Scripture:** No canonical Gospel narrates this fall. Read Isaiah 53:4-6, the prophecy of the suffering Servant.

    Leader: In Your suffering, You shared the weight of our human weakness.
    All: When we stumble, raise us by Your mercy.

### Fourth Station: Jesus Meets His Mother

**Meditation Scripture:** The Gospels do not narrate a meeting on the road. John records Mary at the Cross in John 19:25-27; see also Luke 2:34-35.

    Leader: You saw Your Mother in sorrow, and she remained near You.
    All: Comfort those who accompany their loved ones through suffering.

### Fifth Station: Simon of Cyrene Helps Jesus Carry the Cross

**Scripture:** Matthew 27:32; Mark 15:21; Luke 23:26.

    Leader: You accepted Simon's help on the road to Calvary.
    All: Make us ready to bear one another's burdens.

### Sixth Station: Veronica Wipes the Face of Jesus

**Meditation Scripture:** No canonical Gospel narrates Veronica's act. For meditation on compassionate service, read Matthew 25:35-40.

    Leader: Christian tradition remembers compassion shown to You on the road.
    All: Open our eyes to Your presence in those who suffer.

### Seventh Station: Jesus Falls the Second Time

**Meditation Scripture:** No canonical Gospel narrates this fall. For meditation on perseverance, read Hebrews 12:1-3.

    Leader: You continued toward Calvary despite the weight of the Cross.
    All: Give strength to those who are weary and in trial.

### Eighth Station: Jesus Meets the Women of Jerusalem

**Scripture:** Luke 23:27-31.

    Leader: You turned to the women who wept for You.
    All: Lead us to repentance and to faithful works of mercy.

### Ninth Station: Jesus Falls the Third Time

**Meditation Scripture:** No canonical Gospel narrates this fall. For meditation on the suffering of the just, read Psalm 22:14-18.

    Leader: You endured suffering to the end.
    All: Keep us faithful when hardship returns.

### Tenth Station: Jesus Is Stripped of His Garments

**Scripture:** Matthew 27:35; Mark 15:24; John 19:23-24.

    Leader: You were stripped and exposed to shame.
    All: Defend the dignity of the humiliated and exploited.

### Eleventh Station: Jesus Is Nailed to the Cross

**Scripture:** Luke 23:33; John 19:18. John 20:25-27 later refers to the marks of the nails.

    Leader: You were nailed to the Cross for love of us.
    All: Forgive our sins and reconcile us to one another.

### Twelfth Station: Jesus Dies on the Cross

**Scripture:** Matthew 27:45-50; Mark 15:33-37; Luke 23:44-46; John 19:28-30.

    Leader: You gave Your life for the salvation of the world.
    All: Remember the dying, comfort those who mourn, and give us hope.

### Thirteenth Station: Jesus Is Taken Down from the Cross

**Scripture:** Matthew 27:57-59; Mark 15:42-46; Luke 23:50-53; John 19:38-40.

    Leader: Your friends received Your Body with reverence.
    All: Comfort all who mourn and care for the dead.

### Fourteenth Station: Jesus Is Laid in the Tomb

**Scripture:** Matthew 27:60-66; Mark 15:46-47; Luke 23:53-56; John 19:41-42.

    Leader: You were laid in the tomb and entered the silence of death.
    All: Keep us steadfast in hope of the Resurrection.

## Closing in Resurrection Hope

The Directory recommends that the devotion conclude in expectation of the Resurrection. Read Luke 24:1-8 or John 20:1-18, then pray:

    Leader: Risen Lord, You have conquered death and opened the way to life.
    All: Keep us in faith, hope, and charity, and lead us to the joy of Your Kingdom.
