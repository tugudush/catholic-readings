# Mass Intentions: Doctrine, Practice, and Pastoral Guidance

> "At the Last Supper Christ instituted the Eucharistic sacrifice of his Body and Blood. He did this in order to perpetuate the sacrifice of the Cross throughout the ages until he should come again, and so to entrust to his beloved spouse, the Church, a memorial of his death and resurrection: a sacrament of love, a sign of unity, a bond of charity, a Paschal banquet in which Christ is eaten, the mind is filled with grace, and a pledge of future glory is given to us." ([CCC 1323](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1323); citing _Sacrosanctum Concilium_ §47)

This document treats the Catholic doctrine and practice of **Mass intentions** — what they are, how to request them, their canonical and theological foundation, and their particular application for the faithful departed.

---

## 1. What Is a Mass Intention?

A **Mass intention** is the **specific purpose** for which a priest offers the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass. Every Mass, being the same one sacrifice of Christ on Calvary perpetuated on the altar, may be applied by the celebrating priest for a particular spiritual benefit — for the living, for the dead, for a special need of the Church, for thanksgiving, or for any pious cause ([Council of Trent, Session XXII, Chapter 2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

The intention does not change the essence of the Mass (which always remains the perfect offering of Christ to the Father). Rather, it expresses **for whom** and **for what purpose** the fruits of the Eucharistic sacrifice are being applied. Because the Church is the communion of saints — the living union across heaven, earth, and purgatory — the benefits of the Mass can be applied to any member of the faithful, living or deceased, who is in a state capable of receiving them ([CCC 1371](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1371); [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, §251](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/251)).

### 1.1 The Threefold Fruit of the Mass

Scholastic theology distinguishes the fruits of the Mass:

- **General fruit**: the benefit that accrues to the whole Church from every valid celebration of the Eucharist.
- **Ministerial fruit**: the benefit applied by the priest to the specific intention for which he offers the Mass.
- **Special fruit**: the benefit received by those who participate in the Mass (the celebrant, the servers, and the congregation).

The **Mass intention** concerns the **ministerial fruit** — the application of the Mass by the priest for a determined person or purpose.

## 2. Theological Foundation

### 2.1 The Mass as Propitiatory Sacrifice

The Council of Trent solemnly defined that the Mass is a **true and proper sacrifice** — the same sacrifice of the Cross, offered sacramentally under the appearances of bread and wine — and that it is **propitiatory**, meaning that it avails for the remission of sins and the satisfaction of temporal punishment due to sin:

> "If anyone says that the sacrifice of the Mass is one only of praise and thanksgiving, or that it is a mere commemoration of the sacrifice consummated on the Cross, but not a propitiatory sacrifice; or that it profits him only who receives communion, and ought not to be offered for the living and the dead for sins, punishments, satisfactions, and other necessities: let him be anathema." ([Council of Trent, Session XXII, Canon 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/))

Because the Mass is propitiatory, its fruits can be applied to the souls in Purgatory, aiding in their purification and hastening their entry into the beatific vision.

### 2.2 The Communion of Saints

The doctrine of the communion of saints (CCC 946–962) provides the theological basis for offering Mass for the dead. The Church is one body in Christ, and the bond between the Church Militant (the faithful on earth), the Church Suffering (the souls in Purgatory), and the Church Triumphant (the saints in heaven) is not broken by death. The faithful on earth can intercede for the souls in Purgatory, and the most powerful intercession is the Eucharistic sacrifice ([CCC 1371](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1371); [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, §251](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/251)).

## 3. How to Request a Mass Intention

### 3.1 Contacting a Parish or Priest

Requesting a Mass intention is a simple process available to any Catholic:

1. **Contact the parish office** of a Catholic church, either in person, by phone, or by email.
2. **Specify the intention clearly**. Common formulations include:

   > "For the repose of the soul of [Name]"
   > "For the special intention of [Name]"
   > "For the healing of [Name]"
   > "In thanksgiving for [a specific grace]"
   > "For the intentions of the Holy Father"

3. **Indicate any preferred date** (the parish will schedule the Mass at the next available date or on the date requested if possible).
4. **Offer the customary stipend** (see §3.2 below). This is a donation to the parish and the support of the clergy, not a "purchase" of the Mass.

The parish secretary or priest will then schedule the Mass for the intention, and you may attend if you wish (or the priest may offer it privately if the date and time do not coincide with a public Mass).

### 3.2 The Mass Stipend (Offering)

Canon law governs the practice of Mass offerings:

> **Canon 945 §1**: "In accordance with the practice received in the Church, any priest celebrating or concelebrating a Mass may receive an offering to apply the Mass for a specific intention."

> **Canon 945 §2**: "Even if he receives no offering, a priest is earnestly recommended to celebrate Mass for the intention of the Christian faithful, especially the needy."

> **Canon 946**: "The Christian faithful who give an offering to apply the Mass for their intention contribute to the good of the Church and by that offering share its concern for the support of its ministers and its works."

Key points about the stipend:

- It is a **customary donation**, not a payment for the grace of the Mass. The value of the Mass is infinite; the stipend is a material contribution to the support of the clergy and the Church's mission.
- The amount is **set by each diocese** or parish and varies by region. There is no set universal amount.
- The priest is **obligated** to accept Mass intentions even if a stipend cannot be offered. The Church "earnestly recommends" that Masses be offered for the needy who cannot give an offering.
- A priest may accept only **one stipend per Mass** (Canon 948). If he celebrates several Masses in a day, he may accept an offering for each, but with restrictions on the amount he may retain (Canon 951).

### 3.3 Practical Guide: Preparing the Envelope or Intention Slip

When submitting a Mass intention — whether at the parish office counter or through a drop-box — it helps to prepare a small envelope or slip with the necessary information clearly written.

**What to write on the envelope (outside):**

Here is a clear template for your specific case — offering a Mass for the repose of the soul of your deceased mother:

> **FOR:** Mass Intention
>
> **INTENTION:** For the repose of the soul of [your mother's full name]
>
> **DATE REQUESTED:** [leave blank if no preference, or write a specific date]
>
> **REQUESTED BY:** [your name]
>
> **CONTACT:** [your phone number or email, optional but helpful]

**What to place inside the envelope:**

1. **The stipend** — cash or check in the customary amount (check your parish's standard offering)
2. **A small slip of paper** with the intention clearly written, in case the envelope exterior is not opened

**Sample interior slip:**

> I respectfully request a Mass to be offered for the repose of the soul of
>
> **_[Your Mother's Full Name]_**
>
> May she rest in peace. Amen.
>
> Stipend enclosed: ₱*** / $***

**Short version (for a drop-box where space is limited):**

You may write directly on a small piece of paper:

> Mass Intention — For the repose of the soul of [full name]
> Stipend enclosed. Thank you.

**For the drop-box at the Pabellón:**

In many Filipino parishes, a locked box for Mass intentions is provided in the [Pabellón (Saints' Pavilion)](../cultural-practices/pabellon-saints-pavilion.md) or near the church entrance. The procedure is the same: prepare the envelope as above and drop it in. The box is collected by the parish staff and processed exactly like in-person requests. This is a perfectly valid way to request a Mass intention without needing to speak to anyone at the office.

> **Note:** If you need a Mass on a very specific date (e.g., a birthday or anniversary), it is better to contact the parish office directly to confirm availability. The drop-box provides no opportunity to verify the date.

### 3.4 Multiple Mass Intentions

The faithful may request:

- **A single Mass** for a particular intention
- **A series of Masses** (e.g., a novena of Masses, or Gregorian Masses — thirty consecutive Masses for the dead)
- **Perpetual Mass associations** (enrolling a deceased person in a society that provides for regular Masses)

**Gregorian Masses** are a series of **thirty consecutive Masses** celebrated on thirty successive days without interruption for a **specific deceased person**. They are considered one of the most powerful forms of suffrage (intercessory prayer) for the faithful departed, based on the ancient tradition that the soul in Purgatory may be granted release through this continuous chain of Eucharistic sacrifices.

**Origin and Tradition:**

The practice traces back to **Pope St. Gregory the Great** (590–604), one of the four great Latin Doctors of the Church. In Book IV, Chapter 55 of his _Dialogues_, St. Gregory recounts the story of a monk named Justus, who died in possession of three gold coins (a violation of the monastic vow of poverty). After his death, St. Gregory ordered that thirty Masses be offered for his soul on thirty consecutive days. At the end of the thirty days, the deceased monk appeared to his brother and revealed that he had been delivered from Purgatory.

From this account arose the pious tradition known as the **"Gregorian Thirty"** or simply **Gregorian Masses**. The practice was historically so esteemed that many religious orders and pious associations offered this series of Masses as a standard form of suffrage for the dead.

**Theological Basis:**

The efficacy of Gregorian Masses does not lie in any "magical" property of the number thirty, but in the accumulated application of the fruits of the Eucharistic sacrifice — the most perfect prayer the Church can offer. Each Mass, being the same sacrifice of Christ made present on the altar, applies the infinite merits of Christ to the soul of the deceased. The thirty Masses, offered in an unbroken sequence, constitute an intensified and sustained plea for God's mercy.

**How to Request Gregorian Masses:**

1. **Contact a parish or a religious community** that offers Gregorian Masses. Not all parishes offer this service, as it requires a priest available to celebrate Mass for thirty consecutive days.
2. **Specify the intention**: "I would like to request Gregorian Masses for the repose of the soul of [name]."
3. **The stipend** is generally higher than for a single Mass, reflecting the series of thirty celebrations. In the Philippines, this often ranges from ₱3,000 to ₱6,000 total (see §5.2). In other countries, the amount varies.
4. **The series begins on a fixed date** and continues for thirty days without interruption. If a day is missed for a serious reason, the series may need to be restarted.

**Important Note:** The Church has never made a dogmatic pronouncement that thirty consecutive Masses always and infallibly obtain the release of a particular soul from Purgatory. The tradition is a **pious belief**, not a defined doctrine. The effect of the Masses depends on the mercy of God and the disposition of the soul. Nevertheless, the practice is highly commended by the Church and has been the source of great consolation for the faithful for over fourteen centuries.

## 4. Mass Intentions for the Faithful Departed

### 4.1 Offering Mass for the Dead

The Church has always taught that Mass may be offered for the dead. This practice is grounded in Sacred Scripture:

> "Therefore he [Judas Maccabeus] made atonement for the dead, that they might be delivered from their sin." (2 Maccabees 12:46, deuterocanonical)

The Mass is the most perfect form of suffrage (intercessory prayer) for the faithful departed. The _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ (2002) states:

> "The Church's suffrages for the dead are an urgent supplication that God may have mercy, purify by the fire of His charity, and bring the souls to the kingdom of light and life. This suffrage consists primarily in the celebration of the holy sacrifice of the Eucharist, together with other pious practices such as prayers, almsgiving, works of mercy, and indulgences for the faithful departed." ([Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, §251](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/251))

### 4.2 Mass for the Dead Beyond 40 Days — A Clear Answer

**Yes, you may offer a Mass intention for a deceased loved one who passed away more than 40 days ago.** There is no time limit on offering the Mass for the dead. The Church explicitly provides for Mass intentions for the deceased:

- On the **third, seventh, and thirtieth day** after death
- On the **fortieth day**
- On **anniversaries**
- At **any time** the faithful wish to pray for the deceased ([Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, §255](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/255))

The General Instruction of the Roman Missal provides that Masses for the dead may be celebrated:

> "upon receiving news of a death, for the final burial, or the first anniversary," and that "other Masses for the dead" (i.e., daily Masses for the dead) may be celebrated on weekdays in Ordinary Time "provided they are actually applied for the dead." ([GIRM §381](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/6c499d17-83df-470e-8558-6c60ebcf2814/ref/381))

The 40-day tradition is a **cultural and popular piety practice** — particularly strong in Filipino Catholic culture and many Eastern Catholic traditions — marking a special period of mourning and prayer. However, it is **not** a doctrinal limitation. The Church encourages ongoing prayer for the dead, especially through the Eucharistic sacrifice, on anniversaries and at any time throughout the year.

> "Prayer for the dead is a Christian duty and a continuation of the communion of saints. Suffrages for the dead are not restricted only to those whose faith is known in detail by us; it includes the dead 'whose faith is known to God.'" ([Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, §250](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/250))

### 4.3 How to Request a Mass for a Deceased Loved One

You may do the following:

1. **Visit or call your local Catholic parish** and ask to schedule a Mass intention. You can say:

   > "I would like to request a Mass for the repose of the soul of [name of the deceased]."

2. If you prefer, you may specify a date that is meaningful — a birthday, the anniversary of death, or any date that is convenient for you and the parish.

3. You may offer the customary stipend, but this is not required — the priest is obligated to accept the intention regardless.

4. You may attend the Mass if it is a public Mass, or you may unite yourself spiritually to the Mass if it is offered privately.

5. You may request **multiple Masses** — one, or a series — at different parishes or at the same parish over time.

### 4.4 Every Mass Can Be Offered for the Dead

You do **not** need to wait for a specially scheduled "Mass for the dead." When you attend any Mass:

- During the **offertory**, silently offer your intention for the repose of the deceased's soul
- During the **Eucharistic Prayer**, unite your intention with the priest's offering of the sacrifice
- When receiving **Holy Communion**, offer your communion for the repose of the deceased's soul

The entire Mass — from the Introit to the final blessing — is the same sacrifice of Christ. By consciously offering your participation for the deceased, you are applying the fruits of the Mass to their soul.

## 5. The Mass Stipend in Detail

### 5.1 Diocesan Guidelines

Each diocese establishes the **diocesan stipend amount** for a Mass intention. In many jurisdictions, the amount is modest (e.g., $10–$20 USD, or its equivalent). Parishes may also accept larger offerings, but the priest may not demand more than the diocesan stipend for the application of the Mass (Canon 952).

### 5.2 Philippine Practice: The Mass Offering

There is **no single national amount** set by the Catholic Bishops' Conference of the Philippines (CBCP) for Mass intentions. Each diocese, and often each parish, sets its own customary amount. The following are **typical ranges** — always voluntary, never a fixed fee:

| Type of Mass Intention                     | Typical Offering (PHP) | Notes                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Regular weekday Mass**                   | ₱50 – ₱200             | Most common: ₱100              |
| **Sunday Mass**                            | ₱300 – ₱500            | Some parishes may suggest ₱500 |
| **Special occasion / anniversary**         | ₱500 – ₱1,000          | Discretionary                  |
| **Gregorian Masses (30 consecutive days)** | Varies widely          | Often ₱3,000 – ₱6,000 total    |

**Key points for the Philippine faithful:**

- **Archdiocese of Manila**: Since 2001, has operated on a **voluntary donation** system — no set fee. Parishes post intentions on a bulletin board and accept free-will offerings. This model was adopted to ensure that the poor are not excluded.
- **Provincial and rural parishes**: Typically lower amounts (₱50–₱100), reflecting local economic conditions.
- **Urban Metro Manila parishes**: Tend toward ₱200–₱500.
- **No one is turned away**: If you cannot afford the customary amount, the intention is accepted regardless — the priest is bound by canon law to celebrate Mass for the needy without any offering (Canon 945 §2).

> "Catholics pay 50–100 pesos for a Mass intention. A priest usually receives a 500-peso stipend for saying a Sunday Mass." — UCA News (2001), reporting on the Manila Archdiocese's shift to voluntary donations under Cardinal Jaime Sin.

The 2025 Vatican decree from the Dicastery for the Clergy reaffirmed that Mass offerings must never take on a mercantile character, and that collective intentions (one Mass offered for multiple intentions) require transparency and the consent of the donors.

### 5.3 Bination and Trination

A priest who celebrates Mass more than once on a given day ("bination" = two Masses; "trination" = three Masses) may accept a stipend for each Mass, but:

- On **Sundays and holy days of obligation**, a priest who celebrates multiple Masses for the pastoral good of the faithful must apply one of those Masses for the people (the _Missa pro populo_).
- Any additional stipends beyond the first are generally remitted to the diocese or to charitable purposes (Canon 951).

### 5.4 Mass Offerings and the Poor

> "It is earnestly recommended that priests celebrate Mass for the intention of the Christian faithful, especially the needy, even if they receive no offering." (Canon 945 §2)

The faithful should never feel that their financial situation prevents them from having a Mass offered. The priest's obligation to accept intentions is independent of the stipend.

## 6. Latin Rite vs. Eastern Catholic Practice

| Aspect                                 | Latin (Roman) Rite                                                                     | Eastern Catholic Churches                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Term for the offering**              | Mass stipend / Mass intention                                                          | Prosphora offering / commemorations during the Proskomedia                                                                                                                       |
| **How intentions are presented**       | Verbally at parish office, often with a written intention slip                         | Names written on a list (_pomynnyk_) and given to the priest before the Divine Liturgy                                                                                           |
| **When the intention is commemorated** | During the Eucharistic Prayer (especially in the _Memento_ of the living and the dead) | During the **Proskomedia** (preparation of the gifts), where particles are removed from the prosphora loaves for each name, and again during the Great Entrance and the Anaphora |
| **Memorial services**                  | Requiem Mass, Office of the Dead                                                       | Panikhida, Parastas, 40-day memorial liturgies                                                                                                                                   |
| **Customary offering**                 | Monetary stipend                                                                       | Prosphora bread, wine, and/or a monetary offering; the "prosfora" list is a deeply integrated part of the preparation rite                                                       |
| **Periodic commemorations**            | 3rd, 7th, 30th, 40th day, anniversary                                                  | 3rd, 9th, 40th day, anniversary; the 40th day (_Sorokoust_) is especially significant                                                                                            |
| **Emphasis**                           | The Mass as propitiatory sacrifice applied for the dead                                | The Divine Liturgy as a participation in the heavenly worship, with the dead remembered in the Communion of Saints                                                               |

In the **Byzantine tradition**, the offering of the Divine Liturgy for the dead is expressed through the **Proskomedia** (Liturgy of Preparation). The priest removes particles from the prosphora bread while commemorating each name, and these particles are placed on the _diskarion_ (paten) beside the Lamb (the central particle representing Christ). This act powerfully symbolizes the inclusion of the faithful departed in the one sacrifice of Christ. After the consecration, these particles are placed into the chalice with the words:

> "Wash away, O Lord, the sins of all those here commemorated, by Your precious Blood, through the prayers of Your saints."

## 7. Common Questions

### 7.1 Can I offer Mass for a non-Catholic?

The Church permits Mass to be offered for the dead **only for baptized Christians**, since it is through Baptism that one becomes a member of the Body of Christ and shares in the communion of saints. However, the Church's prayer for the dead "includes the dead 'whose faith is known to God'" ([Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, §250](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/250)). A priest may apply the Mass for a non-Catholic in certain circumstances, especially when there is evidence of good faith and the intention is for God's mercy. The prudent judgment of the pastor should be sought.

### 7.2 Do I need to attend the Mass I requested?

You are **encouraged** but **not required** to attend. If the Mass is scheduled at a time when you cannot be present, or if the priest offers it privately, you may unite yourself spiritually to the Mass by praying at the appointed time. The value of the Mass for the intention does not depend on your physical presence.

### 7.3 Can I request Mass intentions at multiple parishes?

**Yes.** You may request Mass intentions at as many parishes as you wish. Many people especially do this for the dead, requesting Masses at multiple churches or even in different cities or countries.

### 7.4 Is there a limit to how many Mass intentions I can request?

Practically, the only limit is the availability of priests and the parish schedule. Many parishes have more requests than available Mass slots; in such cases, Mass intentions are scheduled in order. You may request Masses at multiple parishes simultaneously.

### 7.5 What is the difference between a "public" and "private" Mass intention?

- **Public Mass intention**: The Mass is celebrated in a public church with the congregation present, and the intention is announced (or at least known to the priest).
- **Private Mass intention**: The priest offers the Mass privately (without a congregation) for the specified intention. This is less common today but still possible. The grace of the Mass is the same regardless of the number of participants.

### 7.6 Does the 40-day tradition apply only in certain cultures?

The 40-day commemoration is observed in many Catholic cultures, particularly:

- **Filipino Catholic tradition**: The 40-day mourning period (_Waksi_) culminates with a Mass offered on the fortieth day (see [cultural-practices/filipino-funeral-traditions.md](../cultural-practices/filipino-funeral-traditions.md)).
- **Eastern Catholic tradition**: The fortieth-day memorial (_Sorokoust_ in Slavic usage; the "forty-day liturgy") is of great importance. The soul is traditionally believed to complete its particular judgment and initial purification by the fortieth day, after which it enters its state of rest until the general resurrection.
- **Italian, Polish, Spanish, and Latin American traditions**: The fortieth day is also observed in various forms.

In all these traditions, **Masses may continue to be offered after the fortieth day without any restriction**. The anniversary Mass and Masses on other occasions are normal and encouraged.

## 8. Summary: How to Offer a Mass Intention for a Deceased Loved One

Here is a clear, step-by-step guide:

| Step   | Action                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1.** | **Contact a Catholic parish** — by phone, email, or in person.                                                                                                            |
| **2.** | **State your intention** clearly: "I would like to have a Mass offered for the repose of the soul of [name of the deceased]."                                             |
| **3.** | **Request a preferred date** if you have one (e.g., a birthday, the anniversary of death, or any date). The parish will schedule the Mass at the earliest available date. |
| **4.** | **Offer the customary stipend** if you are able. If you cannot afford it, the intention will still be accepted.                                                           |
| **5.** | **Attend the Mass** if it is a public Mass and you are able. If not, unite yourself to it spiritually.                                                                    |
| **6.** | **Continue to pray** — You may request additional Masses at any time, on anniversaries, or simply whenever you wish to pray for the deceased.                             |

You may also request **multiple Masses** — for example, you could ask for a Mass on her anniversary each year, or have a series of Masses offered at different parishes.
