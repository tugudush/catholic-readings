# The Chaplet of the Holy Face of Jesus

The **Chaplet of the Holy Face of Jesus** — called in its classic printed source the **Little Chaplet of the Holy Face** — is a Catholic devotional prayer of **reparation** offered in honour of the five senses of Our Lord Jesus Christ and for the **triumph of His holy Church**. It is prayed on a small string of **thirty-nine beads** (six large and thirty-three small) with a **medal of the Holy Face**, and it belongs to the family of devotions that arose at the **Carmel of Tours**, France, in the 1840s through the interior communications reported by **Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre** (Perrine Éluère, 1816–1848).

The chaplet's prayers are almost entirely **verses of the Psalms** and short ejaculations:

- the opening versicle of the Divine Office, _"O God, come to my assistance"_ ([Psalm 69:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=69));
- the war-cry of the Church against the powers of evil, _"Arise, O Lord, and let Thy enemies be scattered"_ ([Psalm 67:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=67));
- the ejaculation _"My Jesus, mercy";_
- the _Gloria Patri_, said seven times in honour of the seven words spoken from the Cross and the seven sorrows of Our Lady;
- and the closing petition of the psalmist, _"Behold, O God our protector: and look on the face of thy Christ"_ ([Psalm 83:10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=83)).

Its purpose is stated in the 1887 _Manual of the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face_: _"It is well to recite it every day, in order to obtain from God, by means of the Face of His well-beloved Son, the triumph of our mother the Catholic Church, and the downfall of her enemies."_[^1]

> **Doctrinal status.** The communications to Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre are **private revelation**: they may be believed as human testimony, but they add nothing to the deposit of faith, and belief in them is **not** required for salvation ([CCC 66–67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/66)).[^2] The **chaplet itself** is a form of **popular piety** — an approved but entirely optional devotional practice of the Latin Church. It has no liturgical standing, imposes no obligation, and is not a liturgical rite. The **devotion to the Holy Face** that it serves was approved and indulgenced by the Church — notably by Pope Leo XIII in 1885, who erected the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face and recommended the recitation of the Rosary or the Chaplet, and by Pope Pius XII, who extended the Feast of the Holy Face to the whole Latin Church on Shrove Tuesday in 1958.

## 2. The Object of the Chaplet

The 1887 _Manual_ states the chaplet's double object in a single sentence: _"the honouring of the five senses of our Lord Jesus Christ, and of entreating God for the triumph of his Church."_ Each half of that sentence rests on a distinct theological foundation.

### 2.1 Reparation for blasphemy and the profanation of Sunday

The devotion to the Holy Face was given to the Church, as Sister Marie understood it, as a remedy for two sins against the **first table of the Decalogue**:

- **Blasphemy against the Holy Name of God**, forbidden by the **Second Commandment** — _"Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain"_ (Exodus 20:7). The Catechism teaches that this precept "prescribes respect for the Lord's name" and forbids every use of it that is contemptuous, hateful, or mocking ([CCC 2142–2149](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2142)).
- **Profanation of Sunday and the holy days**, the violation of the **Third Commandment** — _"Remember that thou keep holy the sabbath day"_ (Exodus 20:8), which the Church applies to the Lord's Day and the feasts of obligation ([CCC 2168–2188](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2168)).

Reparation is not a pious sentiment but a work of the **Communion of Saints**. Christ's sacrifice on Calvary is complete and sufficient; the Church does not add to it. But by His grace the members of His Body can be associated with it, "filling up those things that are wanting of the sufferings of Christ" ([Colossians 1:24](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=cl&g=1&p=24)) — not supplying a deficiency in the Cross, but participating in its application to themselves and to others ([CCC 961](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/961), [1475](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1475); Pope John Paul II, _Salvifici Doloris_ 24).[^3] The theology of reparation was given a classical modern expression in Pope Pius XI's _Miserentissimus Redemptor_ (1928), and it underlies every Holy Face prayer.[^4]

For the confession of these specific sins, see the treatment of the Second and Third Commandments in [sacraments/examination-of-conscience.md](../sacraments/examination-of-conscience.md). For the related prayer of praise that answers blasphemy, see [liturgy/golden-arrow-holy-face-devotion.md](golden-arrow-holy-face-devotion.md).

### 2.2 The five senses of the Holy Face

The chaplet is divided so that its thirty-three small beads correspond to the **thirty-three years of Our Lord's mortal life**. Thirty of them recall His **hidden life** and are arranged in five groups of six, each group honouring one of the **five senses** — touch, hearing, sight, smell, and taste — "which have their seat principally in His Holy Face," so as "to render homage to all the sufferings which our Lord endured in His Face, through each one of these senses." The remaining three beads recall His **public life** and honour "all the wounds of His adorable Face."[^1]

The theological warrant for this scheme is the reality of the Incarnation. The Son of God assumed a complete human nature, and "in suffering and death his humanity became the free and perfect instrument of his divine love" ([CCC 609](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/609)). Because He truly saw, heard, touched, smelled, and tasted, He could truly be wounded in each of those senses — and those wounds can be contemplated, loved, and offered. The chaplet is therefore a meditation on the **bodily** character of the Passion, not on an abstraction.

### 2.3 The triumph of the Church

The second object is intercession: _"entreating God for the triumph of his Church."_ This is the same petition as the second clause of the _Our Father_ — _"Thy kingdom come"_ — and the same confidence as the psalm the chaplet repeats: that God arises, and His enemies are scattered. The Church's enemies, in the Church's own understanding, are not flesh and blood but "the spirits of wickedness in the high places" ([Ephesians 6:12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=eph&g=6&p=12)), and the victory she begs is the victory of truth and grace, of the conversion of sinners, and of the perseverance of the faithful.

The chaplet's warfare is thus **spiritual**, and it is ordered to the salvation of those who oppose God, not their destruction. That is the consistent sense of the tradition in which it stands: the same communications that speak of "enemies" record Our Lord's desire for the conversion of blasphemers and His promise to pardon them.

## 3. The Beads

The chaplet is not a rosary and should not be confused with one. Its structure is:

| Part                   | Number | Meaning                                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cross**              | 1      | The mystery of the Redemption; the opening versicle and the first _Gloria_ are said on it |
| **Large beads**        | 6      | One before each of the five groups of six, and one before the final group of three        |
| **Small beads**        | 33     | The thirty-three years of the mortal life of Our Lord                                     |
| — first 30 small beads | 5 × 6  | The thirty years of His hidden life, honouring the five senses of the Holy Face           |
| — last 3 small beads   | 3      | The three years of His public life, honouring the wounds of His adorable Face             |
| **Medal**              | 1      | A medal of the Holy Face, on which the closing prayer is said                             |
| **Total beads**        | **39** | 6 large + 33 small                                                                        |

The _Manual_ describes the arrangement precisely: _"Each of these sixes is preceded by a large bead in honour of the sense which it is intended to honour, and is followed by a Gloria Patri. The three small beads recall to mind the public life of the Savior and have for their object the honouring of all the wounds of His adorable Face; the large bead which precedes them, has the same object in view and is followed by a Gloria Patri."_[^1]

In practice the chaplet is often made of **knotted brown cord** by Carmelite communities, and a medal of the Holy Face is attached at the join. Any string of beads with the right count may be used. The **prayer** is not a sacramental; the **beads and medal**, once blessed, are sacramentals in the sense of blessed objects ([CCC 1671](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1671)), and the Church attaches no power to the material object apart from the faith and devotion of the one who uses it. For the theology of medals and devotional objects, see [sacramentals/README.md](../sacramentals/README.md) and [sacramentals/sacred-images.md](../sacramentals/sacred-images.md).

## 4. How to Pray the Chaplet

### 4.1 At a glance

| Step | Where        | Prayer                                                                                       |
| ---- | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Cross        | Sign of the Cross; _"O God, come to my assistance…"_; _Glory Be_                             |
| 2    | Large bead 1 | _"My Jesus, mercy!"_; _Glory Be_ — intention: the sense of **touch**                         |
| 3    | 6 small      | _"Arise, O Lord…"_ ×6                                                                        |
| 4    | Large bead 2 | _"My Jesus, mercy!"_; _Glory Be_ — intention: the sense of **hearing**                       |
| 5    | 6 small      | _"Arise, O Lord…"_ ×6                                                                        |
| 6    | Large bead 3 | _"My Jesus, mercy!"_; _Glory Be_ — intention: the sense of **sight**                         |
| 7    | 6 small      | _"Arise, O Lord…"_ ×6                                                                        |
| 8    | Large bead 4 | _"My Jesus, mercy!"_; _Glory Be_ — intention: the sense of **smell**                         |
| 9    | 6 small      | _"Arise, O Lord…"_ ×6                                                                        |
| 10   | Large bead 5 | _"My Jesus, mercy!"_; _Glory Be_ — intention: the sense of **taste**                         |
| 11   | 6 small      | _"Arise, O Lord…"_ ×6                                                                        |
| 12   | Large bead 6 | _"My Jesus, mercy!"_; _Glory Be_ — intention: the **public life** and the wounds of the Face |
| 13   | 3 small      | _"Arise, O Lord…"_ ×3                                                                        |
| 14   | Medal        | _"Behold, O God our protector, and look upon the face of Thy Christ"_                        |

The _Gloria Patri_ is therefore said **seven times**: once on the cross after the opening versicle, and once after each of the six large beads. The _Manual_ explains the number: _"The Gloria is repeated seven times, in order to honour the seven words of Jesus upon the Cross, and the seven dolours of the Immaculate Virgin."_[^1]

### 4.2 Step by step

1. **Begin with the Sign of the Cross**, holding the cross of the chaplet.
2. **On the cross**, say the opening versicle of the Divine Office and the _Gloria_:

        O God, come to my assistance;
        O Lord, make haste to help me.
        Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost.
        As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be,
        world without end. Amen.

3. **On the first large bead**, in honour of the sense of touch, say _"My Jesus, mercy!"_ and the _Gloria_.
4. **On the next six small beads**, say six times: _"Arise, O Lord, and let Thy enemies be scattered, and let them that hate Thee flee from before Thy Face."_
5. **Repeat** steps 3–4 for hearing, sight, smell, and taste.
6. **On the sixth large bead**, in honour of Our Lord's three years of public life and the wounds of His Holy Face, say _"My Jesus, mercy!"_ and the _Gloria_.
7. **On the last three small beads**, say the "Arise, O Lord" verse three times.
8. **On the medal**, conclude:

        Behold, O God our protector:
        and look on the face of thy Christ.

### 4.3 Variants in use

The chaplet has been handed down in several recensions, and none of the differences touches the substance of the devotion:

- **Naming the sense.** The 1887 _Manual_ assigns the intention implicitly, by the large bead "in honour of the sense which it is intended to honour." Modern Carmelite usage makes it explicit, saying before the _"My Jesus, mercy!"_: _"In honour of the sense of touch of the Holy Face."_
- **Where the closing prayer is said.** The _Manual_ places it **on the medal**; some Carmelite houses place it **on the cross** at the end. Either is acceptable.
- **Additional prayers.** An _Apostles' Creed_, an act of contrition, or other prayers may be added before or after the chaplet; these are pious additions that do not alter its substance.
- **Concluding prayers of the modern apostolate.** Contemporary Holy Face devotional material often adds, after the medal, a prayer offering the instruments of the Passion and three invocations for the conversion of sinners (see §5.7). These are **later additions**: the 1887 _Manual_'s chapter on the Little Chaplet ends with the medal prayer, and the additional formulas should be presented as optional devotional supplements rather than as part of the historical chaplet.

## 5. The Full Text of the Prayers

### 5.1 The Sign of the Cross

    In the name of the Father, and of the Son,
    and of the Holy Ghost. Amen.

### 5.2 The opening versicle (on the cross)

    O God, come to my assistance;
    O Lord, make haste to help me.

Latin: _Deus, in adjutorium meum intende; Domine, ad adjuvandum me festina._

This is [Psalm 69:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=69) in the Vulgate's numbering (Psalm 70:2 in the Hebrew), and it is the versicle with which the Church begins the Liturgy of the Hours each day — a fitting opening for a prayer whose object is the glory of God and the triumph of His Church. See [liturgy/liturgy-of-the-hours.md](liturgy-of-the-hours.md).

### 5.3 On each large bead

    My Jesus, mercy!

This short ejaculation is repeated six times in the chaplet. In the 1887 _Manual_ it carries the note "(100 days of indulgence)" — a grant that belonged to the pre-1967 discipline of indulgences and is no longer in force (see §8).

### 5.4 On each small bead

    Arise, O Lord,
    and let Thy enemies be scattered,
    and let them that hate Thee flee from before Thy Face.

This is [Psalm 67:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=67) almost verbatim: _"Let God arise, and let his enemies be scattered: and let them that hate him flee from before his face."_ It is the verse with which the Church has long prayed against the powers of darkness; the tradition recorded by the Carmelites holds that Saint Athanasius valued it above every other verse of Scripture for putting the demons to flight. It is said **thirty-three times** in the chaplet — once for each year of Our Lord's life.

### 5.5 The _Gloria Patri_

    Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost.
    As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be,
    world without end. Amen.

Said **seven times**, in honour of the seven words spoken from the Cross and the seven sorrows of the Blessed Virgin Mary.

### 5.6 The closing prayer (on the medal)

    Behold, O God our protector:
    and look on the face of thy Christ.

This is [Psalm 83:10](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=83) — in the Vulgate, _Protector noster, aspice, Deus, et respice in faciem christi tui_. The 1887 _Manual_ renders it: _"God, our Protector, look on us, and cast Thy eyes upon the Face of Thy Christ."_ The prayer gathers the whole chaplet into one petition: it asks the Father to look upon the Face of His Son — the Face the chaplet has been honouring — and, for the sake of that Face, to look mercifully upon us.

### 5.7 Optional concluding prayers (modern Holy Face devotion)

The following prayers are found in modern Holy Face devotional material. They are **not** part of the 1887 _Manual_'s chaplet and may be omitted:

    Eternal Father, I offer Thee the Cross of our Lord Jesus Christ
    and all the other instruments of His Holy Passion,
    that Thou mayest put division in the camp of Thine enemies;
    for as Thy beloved Son hath said,
    a kingdom divided against itself shall fall.

    May the thrice holy name of God overthrow all their plans.
    May the name of the living God split them up by disagreements.
    May the terrible name of the God of eternity stamp out all their godlessness.

    Lord, I do not desire the death of the sinner,
    but I want him to be converted and to live.
    Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.

The last line is drawn from [Luke 23:34](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=23&p=34) and [Ezekiel 33:11](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=eze&g=33&p=11); the middle invocations are a devotional paraphrase of the Holy Name's power. They should be prayed in the spirit of the Church's intercession for sinners, never as an imprecation against persons.

### 5.8 The Archconfraternity's daily invocation

Members of the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face were enrolled with a simple daily obligation: to recite, for the intentions of the Archconfraternity, one _Our Father_, one _Hail Mary_, one _Glory Be_, and the invocation:

    Lord, show us Thy Face, and we shall be saved.

This is [Psalm 79:4](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=79) (_"Convert us, O God: and shew us thy face, and we shall be saved"_), repeated at verses 8 and 20 of the same psalm. The obligation was explicitly **not binding under pain of sin**: the _Manual_ notes that the associates' engagements "do not impose an obligation of conscience, that is to say, do not oblige under penalty of sin."[^1]

## 6. The Five Senses of the Holy Face: Meditations

The chaplet's five groups of six beads invite a sustained meditation on what the Passion cost the Redeemer in each of His senses. The following meditations follow the Gospel narratives and the prophecy of Isaiah; they are offered as aids, not as a fixed scheme. The chapter of Isaiah on the Suffering Servant is the Church's own commentary on the Holy Face: _"there is no beauty in him, nor comeliness"_ ([Isaiah 53:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=isa&g=53&p=2)); _"Despised, and the most abject of men, a man of sorrows"_ ([Isaiah 53:3](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=isa&g=53&p=3)); _"so shall his visage be inglorious among men"_ ([Isaiah 52:14](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=isa&g=52&p=14)).

### 6.1 Touch

Contemplate the hands laid upon the Face of Jesus: the blows of the soldiers, the buffeting, the palm-strokes, the crown of thorns pressed down, the reed struck against His head.

> _"Then did they spit in his face, and buffeted him: and others struck his face with the palms of their hands"_ ([Matthew 26:67](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=26&p=67)).
>
> _"And they struck his head with a reed: and they did spit on him"_ ([Mark 15:19](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mrk&g=15&p=19)).

_Reparation._ For every blow of the hand, and for the hardness of heart that strikes Christ again in His members — in the poor, the weak, and the innocent.

### 6.2 Hearing

Contemplate what the Sacred Ears of Jesus heard in His Passion: the blasphemies of the passers-by, the mockery of the priests and elders, the jeers of the soldiers, the denial of Peter, and the silence of friends.

> _"And they that passed by, blasphemed him, wagging their heads"_ ([Matthew 27:39](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=27&p=39)).
>
> _"Saying: Prophesy unto us, O Christ, who is he that struck thee?"_ ([Matthew 26:68](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=26&p=68)).

_Reparation._ For blasphemy — the sin the devotion was given to repair — and for every word that wounds the Name of God or the reputation of a neighbour.

### 6.3 Sight

Contemplate what the Eyes of Jesus saw: the spitting and the blindfolding, the mob, the soldiers, the sorrow of His Mother, the flight of the disciples, and the darkness over the land.

> _"And some began to spit on him, and to cover his face, and to buffet him"_ ([Mark 14:65](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mrk&g=14&p=65)).
>
> _"And the Lord turning looked on Peter"_ ([Luke 22:61](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=22&p=61)).

_Reparation._ For every immodest look, every wilful exposure of the mind to what defiles, and for the indifference with which men pass before the Face of Christ in the Blessed Sacrament.

### 6.4 Smell

Contemplate what the Sacred Face of Jesus endured in the place of His Passion: the spittle, the dust, the blood, the reek of the hill of execution, and the vinegar held to His lips.

> _"I am poured out like water… my strength is dried up like a potsherd"_ ([Psalm 21:15–16](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=21&p=15)).
>
> _"They gave me also gall for my meat: and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink"_ ([Psalm 68:22](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=psa&g=68)).

_Reparation._ For sensuality and every indulgence of the senses contrary to the law of God, and for the loss of the sense of sin.

### 6.5 Taste

Contemplate what the Mouth of Jesus tasted: the bitter gall, the wine mingled with myrrh, and the vinegar offered on the sponge.

> _"And they gave him wine to drink mingled with gall. And when he had tasted, he would not drink"_ ([Matthew 27:34](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=27&p=34)).
>
> _"Jesus therefore, when he had taken the vinegar, said: It is consummated"_ ([John 19:30](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=19&p=30)).

_Reparation._ For gluttony, for drunkenness, and for every refusal to receive the chalice of the Lord's will.

### 6.6 The wounds of the Holy Face (the three final beads)

The last three beads recall the three years of the **public life** and honour "all the wounds of His adorable Face." Here the chaplet gathers up the whole Passion of the Face:

- the **crown of thorns** pressed upon His head ([Matthew 27:29](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=27&p=29));
- the **spitting** and the **buffeting** of the servants ([Matthew 26:67](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=mat&g=26&p=67));
- the **veil** offered by the woman of Jerusalem, in which — according to the tradition the Church has cherished — the image of His Face was left imprinted ([liturgy/stations-of-the-cross.md](stations-of-the-cross.md), Sixth Station).

The tradition of Veronica is not recorded in the canonical Gospels; it is a tradition of the Church's piety, and the chaplet does not require it. What the chaplet does require is the truth it embodies: that the Face of the incarnate Word was truly disfigured for our sake, and that the same Face is now "the brightness of his glory" ([Hebrews 1:3](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=heb&g=1&p=3)).

## 7. History

### 7.1 Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre and the Work of Reparation

**Perrine Éluère** was born at **Rennes** on **4 October 1816**. She entered the **Discalced Carmelite monastery of Tours** on **13 November 1839**, was professed on **8 June 1841** as **Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre et de la Sainte Famille**, and died on **8 July 1848**, at the age of thirty-one.

From **1843 to 1848** she reported a series of interior communications — not visions, she insisted, but interior illuminations — in which Our Lord made known to her:

- that **blasphemy against His Holy Name** is a "poisoned arrow" continually wounding His Heart;
- that He would give her a prayer of praise, the **"Golden Arrow,"** which would heal those wounds and draw down graces for the conversion of sinners;
- that the **profanation of Sunday** was the second great offence of the age;
- that the exterior object of the Work of Reparation was to be **His Holy Face**, and that those who honour it perform the office of the pious Veronica;
- and, in **1847**, that the atheistic movement then rising against the Church — **Communism** — was the enemy the Work was to oppose by prayer and penance.

The full chronology, texts, and doctrinal classification of these communications are treated in [liturgy/golden-arrow-holy-face-devotion.md](golden-arrow-holy-face-devotion.md); they need not be repeated here.

### 7.2 The 1887 _Manual_ and the "Little Chaplet"

The chaplet's classic printed source is the **_Manual of the Archconfraternity of the Holy Face_**, composed by the Abbé **Pierre-Désiré Janvier**, dean of the metropolitan chapter of Tours and a priest of the Holy Face. The French original was approved and recommended by **Guillaume-René Meignan**, Archbishop of Tours, on **15 April 1886**; the English translation by **Mrs A.-R. Bennett** was published at **Tours in 1887** by the Oratory of the Holy Face, 8 rue Bernard-Palissy (formerly rue Saint-Étienne).[^1]

The _Manual_ is explicit about the chaplet's origin, in a single line that has governed every later account:

> _"The little chaplet comes to us from Sister Saint-Pierre, and the little scapular from Mr. Dupont."_[^1]

Two cautions follow from the way the source actually reads:

1. The _Manual_ **attributes** the chaplet to Sister Marie but gives **no date and no separate locution** instituting it. It should therefore be described as a devotion **derived from her communications and attributed to her by the Archconfraternity**, not as a prayer whose exact origin is documented to a particular day. Popular claims that "Our Lord dictated the chaplet to Sister Marie on such-and-such a date" go beyond what the sources state.
2. The chaplet's structure is a **catechetical arrangement** of the mysteries of the Passion (33 years, five senses, three years of public life, seven words, seven sorrows). Its power lies in the mysteries it honours and the faith with which it is prayed, not in the arrangement considered as a formula.

### 7.3 The Archconfraternity and the papal approvals

What the popes approved is the **devotion and the confraternity**, with their indulgences and their feast. They did not canonically certify each locution, and the Church has never required belief in them.

### 7.4 The devotion after the Second Vatican Council

The days-and-years indulgences attached to the Archconfraternity were abolished by the post-conciliar reform of indulgences (see §8), and the Feast of the Holy Face is no longer in the general Roman calendar. The devotion itself, however, has never been suppressed. It has been carried forward by:

- **Carmelite communities** in France, the United States, and elsewhere, which make and distribute Holy Face chaplets and devotional kits;
- the **Holy Face Association** (Montreal, 1976) and other lay apostolates, which distribute images, medals, and leaflets;
- the **Holy Face Medal** of Blessed Maria Pierina De Micheli, approved under Pope Pius XII;
- and the continuing observance of **Shrove Tuesday** as a day of reparation in parishes and religious houses that keep the older devotion.

For the related devotions of reparation that grew up in the same centuries — the Nine First Fridays, the Five First Saturdays, and the Divine Mercy devotion — see [liturgy/sacred-heart-devotion.md](sacred-heart-devotion.md), [liturgy/first-saturdays-devotion.md](first-saturdays-devotion.md), and [liturgy/chaplet-divine-mercy.md](chaplet-divine-mercy.md).

## 8. Indulgences: What Was Granted, What Still Applies

The chaplet's history is entangled with indulgences, and the entanglement needs untangling, because most traditional handbooks still print grants that the Church has since reformed.

### 8.1 The grants as they stood

| Grant                                                                                                                                          | Source                                             | Status today                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **100 days** for each recitation of _"My Jesus, mercy!"_ in the chaplet                                                                        | Printed in the 1887 _Manual_                       | **Obsolete**                              |
| **100 days** for "every prayer said before an effigy of the Holy Face"                                                                         | Pius IX, 11 December 1876                          | **Obsolete**                              |
| **60 days** for each pious exercise at the seat of the confraternity, and for kissing the image with the invocation _"Lord, show us Thy Face"_ | Leo XIII's 1885 brief, as recorded in the _Manual_ | **Obsolete**                              |
| **Seven years and seven quarantines** for assistance at the monthly reunions                                                                   | Leo XIII's 1885 brief                              | **Obsolete**                              |
| **Plenary indulgences** on admission, at the hour of death, on pilgrimage, and on certain feasts                                               | Leo XIII's 1885 brief                              | **Obsolete** as an archconfraternal grant |
| A **partial indulgence** for the chaplet as such                                                                                               | —                                                  | **None currently attached**               |

### 8.2 Why the old grants no longer bind

Pope St. Paul VI's apostolic constitution **_Indulgentiarum doctrina_** (1 January 1967) reformed the whole discipline. Its **Norm 4** decreed: _"A partial indulgence will henceforth be designated only with the words 'partial indulgence' without any determination of days or years."_[^5] The old arithmetic of "days and quarantines" — which never meant days subtracted from Purgatory, but a share in the Church's ancient canonical penances — was abolished. The 1968 _Normae_ reissued the same rule as **Norm 5**. Grants expressed in days therefore no longer have any meaning in the Church's current discipline; they may be reported as history, never as present entitlements.

### 8.3 The current position

The **1999 _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_** (fourth edition) contains **no concession specifically for devotion to the Holy Face** and **no concession for the Holy Face chaplet**.[^6] Nor does the Church grant a blanket indulgence to "any pious prayer": the four general concessions of the _Enchiridion_ each attach a partial indulgence to a **defined act** performed in a **defined manner**, and the norm on performing "a work enriched with a partial indulgence" presupposes that the work is already indulgenced.[^7]

The honest conclusion is therefore:

- **No indulgence should be claimed today** for praying the Holy Face chaplet as such.
- The chaplet remains a **worthy act of reparation, praise, and intercession**, and a partial indulgence may be gained through the general concessions if the acts performed happen to fall within them (for example, a pious invocation raised to God amid daily duties, under the first general concession).
- The correct pastoral practice is to pray the chaplet for its own sake — for the glory of God, the conversion of sinners, and the triumph of the Church — and not for a quantified remission.

This is the same correction that must be applied to other pre-1967 grants; see the treatment of obsolete days-and-years language in [liturgy/pietate-tua-prayer.md](pietate-tua-prayer.md) and the analysis of the November indulgences in [eschatology/ewtn-vatican-november-indulgences-holy-souls.md](../eschatology/ewtn-vatican-november-indulgences-holy-souls.md).

## 9. The Promises Attributed to Our Lord

The _Manual_ collects nine promises "in favour of all those who honour His holy Face." They are not all of the same origin: two come from the medieval revelations of **St. Gertrude the Great** and **St. Mechtilde**, and the rest from the communications to **Sister Marie de Saint-Pierre**.

| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Those who honour the Holy Face "shall receive in themselves, by the impression of my humanity, a bright irradiation of my Divinity," and shall shine with a brightness surpassing many others in eternal life.                                                 | St. Gertrude, _Insinuations_, IV, 7        |
| 2   | "Not one of them shall be separated from me."                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | St. Mechtilde, _Of Spiritual Grace_, I, 13 |
| 3   | Our Lord "will imprint his divine likeness on the souls of those who honour his most holy countenance."                                                                                                                                                        | Sister Marie, 2 January 1847               |
| 4   | "By my Holy Face you shall work miracles."                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Sister Marie, 27 October 1845              |
| 5   | "By my Holy Face you will obtain the conversion of many sinners. Nothing that you ask in making this offering will be refused to you. If you knew how pleasing the sight of my Face is to my Father!"                                                          | Sister Marie, 22 November 1846             |
| 6   | "As in a kingdom you can procure all you wish for with a coin marked with the prince's effigy, so in the kingdom of Heaven you will obtain all you desire with the precious coin of my holy Humanity, which is my adorable countenance."                       | Sister Marie, 29 October 1845              |
| 7   | "All those who honour my Holy Face in a spirit of reparation will by so doing perform the office of the pious Veronica."                                                                                                                                       | Sister Marie, October 1845                 |
| 8   | "According to the care you take in making reparation to my Face disfigured by blasphemies, so will I take care of yours which has been disfigured by sin. | 9   | Those who defend His cause "by words, by prayers, or in writings" He will defend before His Father; at their death He will purify their souls and restore them to their primitive beauty.                                                                      | Sister Marie, 12 March 1846                |

### How these promises should be read

- They are **private revelation**. They may be believed as pious testimony; they may not be preached as dogma, and the Church does not require belief in them ([CCC 66–67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/66)).
- Their substance is **consonant with revealed truth**: that the justified are conformed to the image of the Son ([Romans 8:29](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=rom&g=8&p=29)), that "we shall be like to him: because we shall see him as he is" ([1 John 3:2](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=1jo&g=3&p=2)), that no one can snatch Christ's sheep from His hand ([John 10:28](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=joh&g=10&p=28)), and that the prayer of the just is powerful ([James 5:16](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=jas&g=5&p=16)).
- They are **not mechanical guarantees**. A promise attached to a devotion is a promise made to the devotion as the Church understands devotion — faith, humility, charity, and perseverance — not to the mere recitation of words. The same _Manual_ insists that the associate's engagements "do not oblige under penalty of sin," and the whole tradition of indulgences and promises excludes presumption ([CCC 2091–2092](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2091)).

## 10. "The Downfall of Her Enemies": Reading the Chaplet's Intention Today

The chaplet's stated object includes "the downfall of her enemies," and the communications to Sister Marie name **Communism** as the movement against which the Work of Reparation was to be offered. Two things must be kept together here: what the sources say, and what the Church's faith requires of the way such language is used.

**What the sources say.** In 1847 Sister Marie reported that Our Lord had told her that the evil spirit opposing God and His Church was rising once again under the name of **Communism**; that "it is the Communists who have dragged me from my tabernacles, profaned and despoiled my sanctuaries"; and that "their designs shall be frustrated." The _Manual_ itself, however, states the object of the chaplet in general terms: "the triumph of our mother the Catholic Church, and the downfall of her enemies."

**What the Church has taught.** The Church has indeed condemned atheistic Communism as a doctrine — definitively in Pope Pius XI's encyclical **_Divini Redemptoris_** (19 March 1937), which calls it "intrinsically wrong" and incompatible with the Christian faith.[^8] That condemnation is a **magisterial act** with its own authority; it does not depend on, and is not proved by, private revelation.

**How the chaplet should be prayed.** The enemies the chaplet opposes are, in the Church's own language, spiritual: "our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers, against the rulers of the world of this darkness, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places" ([Ephesians 6:12](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=eph&g=6&p=12)). The "downfall" the chaplet begs is therefore:

- the **overthrow of falsehood** and the conversion of those held by it;
- the **defeat of sin** in the world and in the praying soul;
- the **preservation of the Church** in faith, worship, and charity.

The chaplet is not a political instrument, and it must never be turned into an imprecation against persons, classes, or nations. The last recorded instinct of the devotion itself is Christ's own: _"Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do"_ ([Luke 23:34](https://www.drbo.org/x/d?b=drb&c=luk&g=23&p=34)).

## 11. Praying the Chaplet Today

Because the chaplet is popular piety, it may be prayed:

- **At any time and in any place**, alone or in common. No permission, enrollment, or ceremony is required. The _Manual_'s recommendation to recite it daily is counsel, not obligation.
- **Before the Blessed Sacrament**, or in [Eucharistic adoration](eucharistic-adoration.md), where the Face the chaplet honours is present in the Most Holy Sacrament.
- **After hearing blasphemy**, as a habitual act of reparation — the purpose for which the devotion was given.
- **On Sundays and holy days**, in reparation for their profanation, and especially on **Shrove Tuesday**, the Feast of the Holy Face in the older calendar for those who keep it.
- **On Tuesdays**, following the practice promoted by Blessed Maria Pierina De Micheli, who united the Holy Face devotion to a weekly visit of reparation before the Blessed Sacrament.
- **In preparation for Confession**, using the chaplet as an act of contrition and reparation, with the [examination of conscience](../sacraments/examination-of-conscience.md).
- **For the dying and for sinners**, joining it to the [Rosary](rosary/how-to-pray.md), the [Chaplet of Divine Mercy](chaplet-divine-mercy.md), or [spiritual Communion](spiritual-communion.md).

A complete recitation takes only a few minutes; the thirty-three "Arise, O Lord" verses can be said slowly, with the meditations of §6, or quickly, as a repeated aspiration. The chaplet is a **devotion**, not a liturgy: it is ordered to the Mass and to the sacraments, and it never substitutes for them.

## 12. Doctrinal Cautions and Common Misunderstandings

1. **It is not a sacrament or a liturgical rite.** The chaplet is a devotional prayer. Its beads and medal may be blessed — and are then sacramentals — but the prayer's efficacy depends on grace, faith, and charity, not on the object ([CCC 1667–1670](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1667)).
2. **It is not a "weapon" with automatic effect.** The tradition's military language (the "Arise, O Lord" verse, the "downfall of her enemies") is the language of spiritual combat under God's sovereignty. It is not an incantation, and it does not coerce God.
3. **The "100 days" indulgence is obsolete.** Do not repeat it as if it were current (see §8).
4. **No indulgence is attached to the chaplet today.** Absence from the _Enchiridion_ does not diminish the devotion's worth; it only removes a quantified claim.
5. **The promises are private revelation.** They are not articles of faith, and no one is bound to believe them.
6. **The chaplet is not the Rosary**, and the five "senses" are not five "mysteries" in the Rosary's sense. The two prayers have different structures and different objects.
7. **It does not replace the Mass.** The Council of Trent teaches that the Mass is the same sacrifice as Calvary offered unbloodily, and no devotion adds to it (Session XXII; [CCC 1367](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1367)). The chaplet is a way of uniting oneself to that one oblation.
8. **The Veronica tradition is not in the canonical Gospels.** It is a tradition of the Church's piety, commemorated in the Sixth Station; the chaplet's worth does not depend on resolving historical questions about the veil.
9. **The chaplet is not a badge of a faction.** It belongs to the whole Church. Its use should be free of political or nationalist overtones, and its "enemies" should be understood spiritually.
10. **The "Holy Face" is not a separate Christ.** The Face honoured is the Face of the one Lord Jesus Christ, true God and true man; the devotion is a way of contemplating His one Person, not a distinct object of worship.

## 13. Eastern Catholic Resonance

The Holy Face devotion is Latin in its historical form, but the mystery it honours is the common inheritance of the whole Church, and the Eastern Catholic and Orthodox traditions possess ancient counterparts:

- **The Mandylion and the Image Not Made by Hands.** The Eastern tradition's _acheiropoieton_ — the image of Christ "not made by hands," associated with Edessa and with the Saviour's imprint on a cloth — is the Byzantine counterpart of the Veil of Veronica. Both express the same theology: that the Face of the incarnate Word can be depicted, venerated, and loved. See [sacramentals/sacred-images.md](../sacramentals/sacred-images.md).
- **The Sunday of Orthodoxy.** The First Sunday of Great Lent in the Byzantine tradition commemorates the restoration of the holy images in 843 — the "Triumph of Orthodoxy" — a feast that vindicates the same principle the Holy Face devotion presupposes.
- **The Jesus Prayer.** The continuous invocation _"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner"_ is the East's own prayer of the Holy Name and Holy Face — an unceasing act of praise and compunction, closely parallel in spirit to the chaplet's repeated _"My Jesus, mercy"_ and _"Arise, O Lord"_ (see [liturgy/golden-arrow-holy-face-devotion.md](golden-arrow-holy-face-devotion.md), §9).
- **The Akathist and Paraklesis.** The Eastern Catholic and Orthodox traditions honour Christ and His Mother with extended hymns — the _Akathist to Our Sweetest Lord Jesus Christ_ among them — which are acts of veneration and reparation offered on behalf of the whole Church.
- **The Eastern Catholic practice of the chaplet.** An Eastern Catholic may pray the Holy Face chaplet privately without any difficulty; the chaplet is a devotion, not a liturgical rite, and the Eastern Churches' own prayer books contain analogous short devotional forms. In the Byzantine tradition, the veneration of icons and the Jesus Prayer normally take its place, but there is no incompatibility.
- **The feast and the calendar.** The Feast of the Holy Face on Shrove Tuesday is a Latin observance. Eastern Catholics keep the same mystery within their own liturgical year — above all on the First Sunday of Great Lent and on Good Friday, when the holy images are venerated and the Passion is contemplated.

For the Eastern Catholic Churches generally, see [church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md](../church-history/eastern-catholic-churches.md) and [church-history/eastern-orthodox-church.md](../church-history/eastern-orthodox-church.md).
