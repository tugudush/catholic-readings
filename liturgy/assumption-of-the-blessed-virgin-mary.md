# The Solemnity of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary

The **Solemnity of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary** is celebrated on **August 15**. It commemorates the dogma that the Immaculate Mother of God, having completed the course of her earthly life, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory. Pope Pius XII defined this truth as a dogma of divine and Catholic faith in _Munificentissimus Deus_ §44.[^1]

In the Roman Rite, August 15 is a **solemnity**, not merely a feast. Its public celebration is the Eucharistic liturgy and the Liturgy of the Hours. The Rosary, novenas, processions, Marian hymns, and household customs are fitting forms of popular piety, but no single private prayer formula is universally required.

The Assumption honors God first: the Father who glorified Mary, the Son whose Resurrection is the source of her glorification, and the Holy Spirit who made her the Mother of the Saviour. Mary is honored as the first member of the Church to share fully in the resurrection of the body and as a sign of the destiny promised to the faithful.

---

## 1. Dogmatic and Theological Foundation

The Church teaches that:

- Mary is the **Immaculate Mother of God** and ever-Virgin.
- At the completion of her earthly life, she was assumed **body and soul** into heavenly glory.
- Her Assumption is a participation in the victory of Christ over sin and death, not an independent power belonging to Mary.
- She is the **first fruits and image of the Church** in glory, and therefore a sign of sure hope for the pilgrim Church.[^2]

The dogmatic definition does not settle whether Mary died before being assumed. The Church's faith concerns the reality of her bodily glorification, while the precise manner and moment of her passage from earthly life remain distinct theological questions.

The New Testament does not narrate the Assumption as a historical scene. The Church reads the mystery in the light of Mary's unique union with Christ, her divine motherhood, the Resurrection, and the promise of bodily glory. The principal biblical texts associated with the solemnity include:

- **Luke 1:39-56**, especially the Magnificat: "All generations will call me blessed."
- **Revelation 11:19-12:1**, the woman clothed with the sun and the ark of God's covenant.
- **1 Corinthians 15:20-27**, Christ as the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep and the destruction of death.
- **Psalm 45**, a royal and nuptial psalm used in the Roman liturgy for the feast.

The Catechism presents the Assumption as the anticipation of the resurrection of other Christians: Mary "anticipates the resurrection of all members of his Body" (CCC 966).[^3]

## 2. Roman Rite Liturgical Observance

### 2.1 The Vigil and the Daytime Solemnity

Because the Assumption is a solemnity, its celebration begins liturgically with **First Vespers** on the evening of August 14. Where provided by the local calendar, a **Vigil Mass** may be celebrated that evening. The principal celebration is the Mass during the day on August 15.

| Moment                 | Usual observance                                                     | Character                                                 |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Evening of August 14   | First Vespers and, where offered, the Vigil Mass                     | The solemnity begins with praise and expectation          |
| August 15              | Mass during the day                                                  | Eucharistic celebration of Mary's glorification in Christ |
| August 15              | Office of Readings, Morning Prayer, Evening Prayer, and Night Prayer | The official prayer of the Church throughout the day      |
| Parish or local custom | Rosary, procession, Benediction, hymns, or patronal celebration      | Optional popular devotion                                 |

The liturgical color is **white or gold**, expressing joy, purity, and glory. The Gloria and Creed are used at the solemnity. The precise Mass readings and English wording of the prayers should be taken from the approved Missal and lectionary of the local church.

### 2.2 The Proper Mass Prayers

The Roman Missal provides proper texts for the Vigil and for the Mass during the day. The principal prayers and antiphons include:

- **Entrance Antiphon:** the woman clothed with the sun and crowned with twelve stars (Revelation 12:1), or an antiphon inviting the Church to rejoice in Mary's Assumption.
- **Collect:** asks that, as Mary has been assumed body and soul into heavenly glory, the faithful may remain attentive to the things above and share in her glory.
- **Prayer over the Offerings:** asks, through Mary's intercession, that the hearts of the faithful may burn with love and continually long for God.
- **Preface:** proclaims Mary as a sign of sure hope and consolation for the pilgrim Church and as the image and beginning of the Church's future glory.
- **Communion Antiphon:** "All generations will call me blessed, for he who is mighty has done great things for me" (Luke 1:48-49).
- **Prayer after Communion:** asks, through Mary's intercession, that the faithful may attain the glory of the resurrection.

The complete texts should be prayed from the current approved edition of the [Roman Missal](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/3f204803-62dd-4467-9edb-8b46db283e8a/ref/Proper%20of%20Saints), since translations and local lectionary arrangements can differ.

### 2.3 Mass Readings

The Roman Missal provides a Vigil and a daytime form. Common Roman lectionary arrangements include:

| Celebration         | Readings commonly associated with the form                                            |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vigil Mass          | 1 Chronicles 15:3-4, 15-16; 16:1-2; Psalm 132; 1 Corinthians 15:54b-57; Luke 11:27-28 |
| Mass during the day | Revelation 11:19a; 12:1-6a, 10ab; Psalm 45; 1 Corinthians 15:20-27; Luke 1:39-56      |

Local editions may provide alternate readings. The daytime Gospel of the Visitation and the Magnificat is especially fitting because Mary herself proclaims that the Almighty has done great things for her.

### 2.4 Holy Day of Obligation

Canon 1246 §1 lists the Assumption among the universal holy days of obligation in the Latin Church. Canon 1246 §2 permits episcopal conferences, with the approval of the Holy See, to suppress or transfer certain days. Therefore, the obligation to attend Mass must be checked in the law and calendar of the particular country or diocese.[^4]

## 3. Common Traditions and Popular Piety

The Congregation for Divine Worship describes August 15 as a feast deeply rooted in popular piety and notes that in many places it is simply called **Our Lady's Day**. Popular customs differ by country and parish.

### 3.1 The Assumption Novena

A common preparation is a nine-day novena from **August 6 through August 14**, ending with First Vespers or the Vigil Mass. There is no single universally mandated Assumption novena. Parish booklets and devotional traditions use different prayers.

A sound novena should include:

- the Sign of the Cross;
- a short reading from Sacred Scripture;
- the day's petition and a period of silence;
- an Our Father, three Hail Marys, and a Glory Be;
- one decade or five decades of the Rosary;
- the Litany of Loreto or another approved Marian prayer;
- a closing prayer and personal intentions.

### 3.2 The Rosary and Marian Prayers

The **Glorious Mysteries** are especially appropriate because the fourth mystery is the Assumption and the fifth is Mary's Coronation. A complete Rosary may be prayed, or a single decade may be used as part of a shorter family observance. The basic prayers and the complete structure are available in [How to Pray the Rosary](rosary/how-to-pray.md).

Other commonly associated prayers include:

- the **Magnificat** (Luke 1:46-55);
- the **Hail, Holy Queen** after the Rosary;
- the **Litany of Loreto**;
- the ancient Marian antiphon **Sub tuum praesidium**;
- the **Memorare**;
- Marian hymns such as _Ave maris stella_ and _Salve Regina_.

These prayers ask Mary's intercession and praise God's work in her. They do not replace the Eucharistic liturgy, and they do not constitute worship owed to God alone. Foundational prayers and their Latin and Eastern forms are collected in [Catholic Prayers](foundational-prayers.md).

### 3.3 Processions, Images, and Parish Celebrations

In many Catholic communities, the solemnity includes a Marian procession, the decoration of a statue or icon with flowers, the singing of Marian hymns, and a parish feast or patronal celebration. These customs are expressions of popular piety and should lead the faithful toward Mass, the sacraments, and works of charity.

### 3.4 Blessing of Herbs and Flowers

The blessing of herbs on August 15 is especially associated with German-speaking countries. The [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy §181](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/181) describes this custom as a Christian blessing of God's created gifts, not as a magical practice. In other regions, flowers, crops, or garden produce may be brought for a similar blessing according to local custom and the approved ritual.

The blessing should direct thanksgiving to God the Creator and ask that the faithful use created goods wisely and gratefully. It should not be treated as a guarantee of healing or protection apart from divine providence.

### 3.5 Preparation and Works of Mercy

Many Catholics prepare through Confession, additional prayer, fasting according to personal discipline, and acts of charity. The Latin Rite has no universal fast specifically required before the Assumption, although Eastern Catholic Churches may observe the Dormition Fast and local communities may retain older customs.

## 4. Suggested Sets of Prayer

There is no single official private prayer set for the Assumption. The following arrangements are customary and doctrinally sound.

### 4.1 Short Household Set

This arrangement takes approximately ten to fifteen minutes:

1. Sign of the Cross.
2. Read Luke 1:46-55, the Magnificat.
3. Pray one decade of the Glorious Mysteries, meditating on the Assumption.
4. Pray the Hail, Holy Queen.
5. Offer personal intentions through Mary's intercession.
6. Conclude with the Collect of the Assumption from the approved Roman Missal.

### 4.2 Full Marian Devotional Set

This arrangement is suitable for a parish group or family gathering:

1. Opening hymn or Marian antiphon.
2. Reading of Luke 1:39-56.
3. Five decades of the Glorious Mysteries.
4. The Magnificat.
5. The Litany of Loreto.
6. A Marian hymn or _Sub tuum praesidium_.
7. Intercessions for the Church, families, the sick, the dying, and the departed.
8. Closing prayer and blessing, when led by an authorized minister.

### 4.3 Liturgical Set

The fullest official form is:

1. First Vespers on August 14.
2. The Vigil Mass, where available, or the Mass during the day on August 15.
3. Morning Prayer and Evening Prayer from the proper of the solemnity.
4. The Office of Readings for deeper meditation on the mystery.
5. Night Prayer at the conclusion of the day.

The general structure and theology of the Hours are explained in [The Liturgy of the Hours](liturgy-of-the-hours.md).

### 4.4 Suggested Private Prayer

The following is a private devotional prayer, not a replacement for the official Collect:

    Father of mercies,
    in the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary
    you show the destiny prepared for the members of Christ.
    Through her motherly intercession,
    keep us faithful to the Gospel,
    attentive to the things above,
    and hopeful in the promise of the resurrection.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

## 5. Byzantine and Other Eastern Catholic Observance

The Byzantine Catholic Churches generally celebrate August 15 as the **Dormition of the Theotokos**, one of the Twelve Great Feasts. The term Dormition means "falling asleep" and expresses the Eastern liturgical contemplation of Mary's passage from earthly life and glorification by God.

Common Byzantine observances include:

- the **Dormition Fast**, ordinarily August 1-14;
- the **Paraklesis**, or Supplication Service, to the Theotokos;
- Great Vespers with Litiya;
- Matins and the Divine Liturgy on August 15;
- processions with an icon or an epitaphios representing the burial of the Theotokos, where local custom provides for it;
- the singing of Byzantine hymns that praise Mary as the Mother of God and the image of the Church's future glory.

The Byzantine tradition's liturgical language of Dormition and the Roman Church's dogmatic language of Assumption are not competing doctrines. They are distinct liturgical expressions within the one Catholic faith. The particular customs of the other Eastern Catholic Churches should be consulted in their own liturgical books, since the Catholic Church includes twenty-four Churches _sui iuris_ with distinct traditions and calendars.

## 6. Doctrinal and Pastoral Distinctions

The Assumption should be presented with the following distinctions:

| Category                                                  | Status                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mary's bodily Assumption                                  | Dogma of divine and Catholic faith                                   |
| August 15 as a solemnity                                  | Universal Roman Rite liturgical discipline                           |
| Mass obligation                                           | Universal canon law as adapted by the competent episcopal conference |
| Rosary, novena, procession, and Marian hymns              | Approved or customary forms of popular piety, varying by place       |
| Blessing of herbs or flowers                              | Regional sacramental or devotional custom                            |
| The exact manner of Mary's death before her glorification | Not defined by the dogmatic formula                                  |

All authentic Assumption devotion leads to Christ, the Eucharist, the hope of the resurrection, fidelity to the Gospel, and charity toward the living and the dead.
