# Novena to St. Faustina for Her Intercession

## Purpose and Status

This nine-day devotion asks **St. Maria Faustina Kowalska** to intercede for the intentions entrusted to God through her prayers. It is distinct from the **Divine Mercy Novena** in [liturgy/divine-mercy-feast-and-novena.md](divine-mercy-feast-and-novena.md), which was given through St. Faustina and offers nine groups of souls to the mercy of God.

The invocation of the saints is part of Catholic practice: those who are united with Christ in glory intercede for the faithful (CCC 956; 2683). This particular sequence, however, is a **private devotional arrangement**, not a liturgical rite or a prescribed formula of the Church. It may be prayed freely and is not required for salvation.

St. Faustina's Optional Memorial is observed on **5 October** in the Roman Rite, the anniversary of her death in 1938. The customary autumn novena runs **27 September through 5 October**, with the feast as its ninth day. It may also be prayed at any time for a serious intention.

## Scripture Readings

The passages below are quoted from the **Douay-Rheims 1899 American Edition (DRA)**, a public-domain text. Each citation links to the source passage for verification. The brief reading-focus notes are original summaries.

The Psalm numbering differs: **Psalm 62:8–9** in common numbering is **Psalm 61:8–9** in the Douay-Rheims/Vulgate numbering. For John 6, the common citation **John 6:51, 53–54** corresponds to **DRA John 6:51–52, 54–55**; the DRA divides common John 6:51 across two verses and numbers the following verses differently.

## Preparation

- An image of the Divine Mercy, signed _"Jesus, I trust in You,"_ may be placed where you pray; a candle is optional.
- A copy of St. Faustina's _Diary_ may be used for further meditation, but is not necessary.
- State the intention before God. In the prayers below, **N.** may be replaced with a person's name or a short intention.
- The novena is a fitting time for the Sacrament of Penance and, when possible, Holy Communion. It does not replace either sacrament.
- The [Chaplet of Divine Mercy](chaplet-divine-mercy.md) or a Rosary may be added, but neither is required for this novena.

## Daily Order

1. Make the **Sign of the Cross**.
2. State the intention of the novena.
3. Read the day's linked Scripture passage.
4. Consider the short reading focus and meditation.
5. Pray the day's prayer.
6. Pray the daily invocation to St. Faustina.
7. Pray one **Our Father**, one **Hail Mary**, and one **Glory Be** for the intention.
8. (Optional) Pray the Chaplet of Divine Mercy or a decade of the Rosary.
9. On the ninth day, conclude with the **Prayer of Thanksgiving** below.

### Prayer for the Intention

    Merciful Father,
    I commend to You N. and this intention:

    [state the intention]

    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    grant what is according to Your will,
    and give me the grace to receive Your answer
    with faith, hope, and charity.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Daily Invocation

    St. Faustina, apostle of Divine Mercy,
    secretary of the mercy of God,
    pray for us and for the intentions of our hearts,
    that we may trust in Jesus without limit. Amen.

## The Nine Days

| Day | Theme                           | Petition                                                                 |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Trust in God's mercy            | For confidence in Christ's mercy and help in trusting Him                |
| 2   | Conversion and repentance       | For sincere repentance, a good confession, and the conversion of sinners |
| 3   | Priests and consecrated persons | For holiness and perseverance among clergy and religious                 |
| 4   | The sick and suffering          | For the sick, caregivers, and all who bear trials                        |
| 5   | The souls in Purgatory          | For the faithful departed undergoing purification                        |
| 6   | Humility and littleness         | For humility, obedience, and openness to grace                           |
| 7   | Works of mercy                  | For charity toward neighbors in need                                     |
| 8   | The Eucharist and Penance       | For reverence and love for the sacraments                                |
| 9   | A merciful death                | For the dying and for perseverance at the hour of death                  |

### Day 1 — Trust in God's Mercy

**Scripture:** [Psalm 62:8–9 (DRA Psalm 61:8–9)](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2061%3A8-9&version=DRA); [John 20:27–29](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2020%3A27-29&version=DRA).

> **Psalm 61:8–9 (Douay-Rheims numbering)**
>
> **8.** In God is my salvation and my glory: he is the God of my help, and my hope is in God.
>
> **9.** Trust in him, all ye congregation of people: pour out your hearts before him. God is our helper for ever.
>
> **John 20:27–29**
>
> **27.** Then he saith to Thomas: Put in thy finger hither, and see my hands; and bring hither thy hand, and put it into my side; and be not faithless, but believing.
>
> **28.** Thomas answered, and said to him: My Lord, and my God.
>
> **29.** Jesus saith to him: Because thou hast seen me, Thomas, thou hast believed: blessed are they that have not seen, and have believed.

**Reading focus:** The psalm directs the heart to rely on God; in the Gospel, Thomas recognizes the risen Jesus, and Jesus speaks of the faith of those who will believe without seeing Him.

**Meditation:** Trust is the signature of the Divine Mercy devotion. The first movement of this novena is to place fear, doubt, and self-reliance before the goodness of God. St. Faustina's witness points beyond herself to Christ, the source of mercy.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, You revealed to St. Faustina
    the ocean of Your mercy open to every soul,
    and You sealed Your image with the words, "Jesus, I trust in You."
    Through her intercession,
    grant me unshakable trust in Your goodness.

    Where I am afraid, give me confidence;
    where I doubt Your forgiveness, give me hope;
    where I measure Your love by my weakness,
    let me measure it by Your Cross.
    May I trust You without limit
    and lead others to trust You likewise.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 2 — Conversion and Repentance

**Scripture:** [Luke 15:17–24](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2015%3A17-24&version=DRA).

> **17.** And returning to himself, he said: How many hired servants in my father's house abound with bread, and I here perish with hunger!
>
> **18.** I will arise, and will go to my father, and say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee:
>
> **19.** I am not worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants.
>
> **20.** And rising up he came to his father. And when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and was moved with compassion, and running to him fell upon his neck, and kissed him.
>
> **21.** And the son said to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee, I am not now worthy to be called thy son.
>
> **22.** And the father said to his servants: Bring forth quickly the first robe, and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet:
>
> **23.** And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it, and let us eat and make merry:
>
> **24.** Because this my son was dead, and is come to life again: was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry.

**Reading focus:** The younger son recognizes the ruin of his choices, returns to his father, and is received with compassion and restored to the household.

**Meditation:** The parable of the two sons shows the Father's welcome of the sinner who returns. Ask not only for personal conversion, but also for those who have become distant from God to receive the grace to turn back and seek forgiveness.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Father, You welcome every sinner who returns to You.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    give me the grace of true conversion:
    light to see my sins honestly,
    sorrow to detest them,
    courage to confess them humbly.

    Deliver me from despair and from presumption,
    from hiding my wounds and excusing my faults.
    Bring me — and sinners everywhere —
    to the embrace of Your forgiveness
    in the Sacrament of Penance.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 3 — Priests and Consecrated Persons

**Scripture:** [John 20:21–23](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2020%3A21-23&version=DRA).

> **21.** He said therefore to them again: Peace be to you. As the Father hath sent me, I also send you.
>
> **22.** When he had said this, he breathed on them; and he said to them: Receive ye the Holy Ghost.
>
> **23.** Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them: and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained.

**Reading focus:** The risen Christ sends His disciples, gives them the Holy Spirit, and entrusts to them the ministry concerning the forgiveness of sins.

**Meditation:** On Easter evening Christ entrusted the apostolic ministry of reconciliation to His Church. Pray with gratitude for priests and consecrated persons, asking that they faithfully serve the people entrusted to them and remain close to Christ.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, on the evening of Your Resurrection
    You sent Your Apostles into the world
    and gave them the ministry of reconciliation.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    pour out Your grace on Your priests
    and on all consecrated souls.

    Keep them holy, courageous, and faithful;
    make them true channels of Your mercy
    in preaching, in the sacraments, and in their daily lives.
    Grant perseverance to those who serve,
    light to those who waver,
    and to those who have wandered,
    the grace to return.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 4 — The Sick and the Suffering

**Scripture:** [Colossians 1:24](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians%201%3A24&version=DRA).

> **24.** Who now rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up those things that are wanting of the sufferings of Christ, in my flesh, for his body, which is the church:

**Reading focus:** Paul describes his sufferings for the Church, Christ's Body, and the ministry entrusted to him.

**Meditation:** St. Paul presents his hardships as service to the Church. St. Faustina, who suffered from tuberculosis, also offered her trials for others. Pray for the sick and suffering, for those who care for them, and for grace to unite unavoidable trials to Christ's saving work.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, You united human suffering to Your own
    and made it a participation in Your saving Passion.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    who offered her long illness for the conversion of sinners,
    receive the sufferings of the sick, N.,
    and of all who suffer.

    Give them patience in weakness,
    peace in anxiety,
    and the grace to offer their trials with Yours.
    Comfort those who care for them,
    and let every pain accepted with love
    become a channel of mercy for the world.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 5 — The Souls in Purgatory

**Scripture:** [2 Maccabees 12:44–46 (DRA: 2 Machabees)](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Machabees%2012%3A44-46&version=DRA).

> **44.** (For if he had not hoped that they that were slain should rise again, it would have seemed superfluous and vain to pray for the dead,)
>
> **45.** And because he considered that they who had fallen asleep with godliness, had great grace laid up for them.
>
> **46.** It is therefore a holy and wholesome thought to pray for the dead, that they may be loosed from sins.

**Reading focus:** Judas makes an offering for fallen soldiers in hope of the resurrection; the passage commends prayer for the dead.

**Meditation:** The Church prays for the faithful departed and offers suffrages for them. St. Faustina's Divine Mercy Novena also includes a day devoted to the souls in Purgatory. Remember the departed who have no one to pray for them, and entrust them to God's mercy.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Father, merciful even in Your justice,
    look upon the souls detained in Purgatory
    through the Wounds of Your dearly beloved Son.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    who taught us to pray for them with confidence,
    grant them refreshment, light, and peace.

    Hasten the day of their happiness in Your presence;
    let our prayers, sacrifices, and Masses assist them;
    and when we too must pass through that purification,
    purify us quickly and receive us into Your glory.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

_For more on suffrage for the faithful departed, see [eschatology/purgatory.md](../eschatology/purgatory.md) and [liturgy/prayers-for-the-dead.md](prayers-for-the-dead.md)._

### Day 6 — Humility and Littleness

**Scripture:** [Matthew 18:3–4](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2018%3A3-4&version=DRA).

> **3.** And said: Amen I say to you, unless you be converted, and become as little children, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.
>
> **4.** Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, he is the greater in the kingdom of heaven.

**Reading focus:** Jesus places a child among the disciples and teaches that conversion and humility are necessary for entrance into the Kingdom.

**Meditation:** God chose a hidden, obedient religious sister to bear witness to His mercy. Humility is not self-contempt; it is the willingness to receive God's gifts and serve without seeking recognition.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, Meek and Humble of Heart,
    You hide the secrets of Your Kingdom from the wise
    and reveal them to little ones.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    humble, hidden, and obedient,
    give me the grace of true humility.

    Free me from the need to be seen,
    from rivalry and self-importance,
    from the pride that blocks Your grace.
    Make me small enough to receive Your mercy
    and to pass it on without claiming it as my own.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 7 — The Works of Mercy

**Scripture:** [Matthew 25:34–40](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2025%3A34-40&version=DRA).

> **34.** Then shall the king say to them that shall be on his right hand: Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess you the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world.
>
> **35.** For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat: I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink: I was a stranger, and you took me in:
>
> **36.** Naked, and you covered me: sick, and you visited me: I was in prison, and you came to me.
>
> **37.** Then shall the just answer him, saying: Lord, when did we see thee hungry and fed thee: thirsty and gave thee drink?
>
> **38.** Or when did we see thee a stranger and took thee in? Or naked and covered thee?
>
> **39.** Or when did we see thee sick or in prison and came to thee?
>
> **40.** And the king answering shall say to them: Amen I say to you, as long as you did it to one of these my least brethren, you did it to me.

**Reading focus:** In the judgment scene, the King identifies Himself with people in need and regards acts of mercy toward them as acts offered to Him.

**Meditation:** Mercy received is meant to become mercy given. St. Faustina's spirituality calls the faithful to concrete charity; the Church likewise teaches the corporal and spiritual works of mercy (CCC 2447). Ask for one practical opportunity to serve a neighbor today.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, You ask us to show mercy
    through our actions, our words, and our prayers.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    make my charity sincere and practical.

    Open my eyes to the poor, the sick, the lonely, the grieving;
    open my mouth to forgive and to encourage;
    open my hands to give;
    open my heart to pray for those no one prays for.
    Let me not pass by on the other side,
    but be mercy for someone today.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 8 — The Eucharist and Penance

**Scripture:** [John 6:51, 53–54 (DRA: 6:51–52, 54–55)](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%206%3A51-55&version=DRA).

> **51.** I am the living bread which came down from heaven.
>
> **52.** If any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever: and the bread that I will give is my flesh, for the life of the world.
>
> **54.** Then Jesus said to them: Amen, amen, I say unto you: except you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, you shall not have life in you.
>
> **55.** He that eateth my flesh and drinketh my blood hath everlasting life: and I will raise him up in the last day.

**Reading focus:** Jesus speaks of Himself as the living bread from heaven and teaches that communion in His flesh and blood is ordered to eternal life. The DRA verse divisions differ from the common numbering.

**Meditation:** The pierced Heart of Christ is the source of sacramental life. The Divine Mercy devotion should lead the faithful toward the Sacrament of Penance and the Eucharist, received with faith and reverence.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, You left us the sacraments
    as channels of Your life and mercy.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    give me a deeper love for the Eucharist and Penance.

    Prepare my heart to receive You in Holy Communion;
    give me sorrow for my sins and joy in Your forgiveness;
    let me approach Your altar with reverence
    and leave it transformed.
    May every Communion unite me more closely to You
    and make me an instrument of Your mercy.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 9 — A Merciful Death

**Scripture:** [Luke 23:42–43](https://classic.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2023%3A42-43&version=DRA).

> **42.** And he said to Jesus: Lord, remember me when thou shalt come into thy kingdom.
>
> **43.** And Jesus said to him: Amen I say to thee, this day thou shalt be with me in paradise.

**Reading focus:** One of the crucified men turns to Jesus, entrusting himself to His Kingdom; Jesus promises him communion with Himself that very day.

**Meditation:** The repentant thief turns to Christ at the end of his life and receives a promise of salvation. St. Faustina died on 5 October 1938, the feast day on which this novena concludes. Pray for the dying and for perseverance in faith, hope, and charity at the hour of death.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful Jesus, You heard the repentant thief
    who turned to You at the end of his life.
    Through the intercession of St. Faustina,
    who spent her life proclaiming Your mercy,
    grant N. and all the dying a merciful death.

    Keep them firm in faith, in hope, and in love;
    deliver them from the attacks of the evil one;
    send Your holy angels to guard them;
    and receive them into the house of Your Father.
    When my own hour comes,
    let me die trusting in Your mercy.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

## On the Feast Day — 5 October

- Assist at Holy Mass where her Optional Memorial is celebrated. If a higher-ranking day takes precedence in the local calendar, the novena may still be prayed privately.
- Give thanks for graces received and entrust any unanswered intention to God's will.
- Pray the concluding prayer below; the [Chaplet of Divine Mercy](chaplet-divine-mercy.md) or a Rosary may also be added.
- Perform a work of mercy for someone in need.

## Concluding Prayer of Thanksgiving

    Merciful Father, You chose Your servant Maria Faustina
    as a witness to Your boundless mercy toward sinners
    and a messenger of the mercy of Your Son.
    Through her intercession, grant us the grace she preached with her whole life:
    trust in Your goodness,
    sincere repentance,
    charity that becomes works of mercy,
    and confidence in Your forgiveness,
    especially at the hour of our death.
    Grant also, through her prayers,
    the favors we now entrust to her intercession, N.,
    if they accord with Your holy will
    and serve our salvation and Your greater glory.
    We ask this through Christ our Lord. Amen.

Replace **N.** with the name of the person or the intention for whom the novena is offered.

## Pastoral Notes

- This is a private devotion. No decree of the Church attaches an indulgence to this novena; its value lies in prayer, the intercession of the saint, and the dispositions of the person praying.
- The novena does not replace the Church's liturgy, the sacraments, or works of mercy.
- If offered for someone deceased, it may be prayed as suffrage for the faithful departed; see [liturgy/prayers-for-the-dead.md](prayers-for-the-dead.md).
- This autumn novena and the Divine Mercy Novena are distinct devotions: the latter belongs to the days from Good Friday through the eve of Divine Mercy Sunday.
