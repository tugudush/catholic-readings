## 1. Overview

The **Prayer to St. Joseph for Purity** is a short but powerful prayer of **spiritual warfare against sins of the flesh** — lust, immoral desires, and impure actions. It was composed by **Fr. Donald Calloway, MIC** (b. 1972), a priest of the Congregation of Marian Fathers of the Immaculate Conception and one of the foremost contemporary promoters of devotion to St. Joseph as **spiritual father** and **terror of demons**.

The prayer is intended for those **tempted by lust** — whether through the imagination, the passions, media, or circumstance — and invokes the intercession of St. Joseph, the pure and most chaste spouse of the Blessed Virgin Mary, whose own life was consecrated entirely to God and to the Holy Family. It is frequently shared and prayed in the context of the modern devotion of **Consecration to St. Joseph**, popularized by Fr. Calloway through his book _Consecration to St. Joseph: The Wonders of Our Spiritual Father_ (2020).

For the comprehensive biography of St. Joseph — his biblical portrait, the theology of his fatherhood, his titles, and his patronage — see [saints/patron-st-joseph.md](../saints/patron-st-joseph.md).

## 2. The Prayer

    Saint Joseph, strong spiritual father,
    defend me against sins of the flesh.
    Jesus said: "Blessed are the pure of heart,
    for they shall see God."
    Saint Joseph, Terror of Demons,
    protect me from lust, immoral desires in my heart,
    and impure actions in my body.

    Help me not to offend God.
    Here and now, I chain myself to you
    and sacrifice everything for the good, the true, and the beautiful.
    I love you, St. Joseph,
    and I thank you for being my spiritual father.
    Amen.

    Saint Joseph, pure and most chaste spouse
    of the Blessed Virgin Mary,
    protect us from all impurity and intercede for us!
    Amen.

_(Prayer by: Fr. Donald Calloway, MIC)_

## 3. The Author: Fr. Donald Calloway, MIC

**Fr. Donald H. Calloway, MIC** (b. 1972, New Britain, Connecticut) is a priest of the **Congregation of Marian Fathers of the Immaculate Conception** (M.I.C.). A convert to Catholicism from a troubled youth, he was drawn into the Church through the intercession of the Blessed Virgin Mary and the example of the Marian Fathers. He holds degrees in philosophy and theology and has served in parish ministry, as a seminary professor, and as a prolific author of over a dozen books on Marian and Josephite spirituality.

He is best known internationally for his book **_Consecration to St. Joseph: The Wonders of Our Spiritual Father_** (2020), which revived and systematized the devotion of consecrating oneself to St. Joseph as a **spiritual father**, following the model of St. Louis de Montfort's consecration to Jesus through Mary. Fr. Calloway has also published _St. Joseph Novena_, _Under the Mantle_, _Consoling the Heart of Jesus_, and numerous works on the Divine Mercy devotion.

His prayer for purity, reproduced above, is part of this wider program of Josephite devotion: just as St. Joseph protected and defended the Holy Family at Nazareth, so the consecrated soul asks him to protect and defend the soul's chastity and purity in the spiritual combat of daily life.

## 4. Theological Foundation

### 4.1 St. Joseph, Model of Chastity and Purity

The prayer's appeal rests on the Church's constant teaching that **St. Joseph was a virgin**, living in perfect chastity as the spouse of the Blessed Virgin Mary. St. Jerome (c. 347–420), in his treatise _Against Helvidius_ (383 A.D.), defended the perpetual virginity of Mary and taught that Joseph, like Mary, remained a virgin throughout his life — establishing Joseph as the model of **chastity** and the **Guardian of the Virgin**. St. Augustine (354–430) added that the marriage of Joseph and Mary was a true marriage possessing the three goods of matrimony: _proles_ (offspring — Jesus), _fides_ (fidelity — mutual chastity), and _sacramentum_ (indissolubility). See [saints/patron-st-joseph.md](../saints/patron-st-joseph.md) for the fuller treatment.

The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that chastity "means the successful integration of sexuality within the person and thus the inner unity of man in his bodily and spiritual being" (CCC 2337), and that "all the baptized are called to chastity" (CCC 2348) — the married in conjugal chastity, the unmarried and celibate in continence. The Church's liturgy invokes St. Joseph precisely as **"most chaste spouse"** (_sponse castissime_) in the Litany of St. Joseph, and the iconographic lily in his hand has for centuries symbolized his purity.

### 4.2 "Terror of Demons"

The prayer invokes St. Joseph as **"Terror of Demons,"** one of the titles given him in the **Litany of St. Joseph**, approved for public use by Pope St. Pius X in 1909. St. John Henry Newman, in his _Meditations on the Litany of St. Joseph_, taught that the demons fear St. Joseph because he is the protector of the Holy Family and the guardian of the Redeemer, whose authority over the household of Nazareth — and by extension over the household of God, the Church — the fallen spirits cannot abide.

The title is profoundly apt for a prayer against lust. In Catholic theology, impurity and sexual sin are among the principal instruments by which the devil enslaves souls, clouding the intellect, weakening the will, and defiling the temple of the Holy Spirit (cf. 1 Corinthians 6:19–20). By invoking St. Joseph as Terror of Demons, the prayer seeks the protection of the very saint whose chastity and fidelity repelled the assaults of the enemy, and who once saved the Child Jesus from the murderous designs of Herod (Matthew 2:13–15). Pope St. John Paul II recalled the Church's prayer that St. Joseph, "just as once you saved the Child Jesus from mortal danger, so now defend God's holy Church from the snares of her enemies and from all adversity" (_Redemptoris Custos_, 31).

### 4.3 "Strong Spiritual Father"

The prayer addresses St. Joseph as **"strong spiritual father"** — the title at the heart of Fr. Calloway's consecration devotion. Pope Francis, in his apostolic letter **_Patris Corde_** (8 December 2020), on the 150th anniversary of the declaration of St. Joseph as Patron of the Universal Church, taught that "the greatness of Saint Joseph is that he was the spouse of Mary and the father of Jesus" and that he placed himself "at the service of the entire plan of salvation" (_Patris Corde_, 1). Pope Leo XIII, in the encyclical **_Quamquam Pluries_** (15 August 1889), declared that Joseph "was the guardian, the administrator, and the legal defender of the divine house" and that he holds "a paternal authority" over the whole Church (_Quamquam Pluries_, 3).

It is this paternal authority — exercised in the hidden life of Nazareth in the perfect service of Jesus and Mary — that the praying soul invokes as a defense "against sins of the flesh." The act of filial self-surrender, "Here and now, I chain myself to you," echoes the language of total consecration familiar from the Marian tradition of St. Louis de Montfort and applied by Fr. Calloway to St. Joseph: the soul entrusts itself wholly to its spiritual father, that he may lead it in purity to Jesus and Mary.

### 4.4 "Blessed Are the Pure of Heart"

The prayer quotes our Lord's sixth Beatitude: **"Blessed are the pure of heart, for they shall see God"** (Matthew 5:8). The Catechism teaches that "the sixth beatitude proclaims, 'Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God'" and that "purity of heart is the precondition of the vision of God" (CCC 2518). Purity of heart refers not only to sexual chastity but to the whole interior ordering of the heart toward God: "the heart is the dwelling-place where I am, or where I take refuge... The heart is the place of decision, deeper than our psychic drives" (CCC 2517).

The prayer thus links the gift of **chastity** (the moral virtue ordering bodily integrity and the passions) with the gift of **purity of heart** (the theological and moral disposition to see God). By praying for defense against lust — "immoral desires in my heart, and impure actions in my body" — the soul asks St. Joseph to cooperate with the grace of Christ in restoring that purity of heart which is the condition of the Beatific Vision, now by faith and finally in heaven (cf. CCC 2519, 2520).

### 4.5 "The Good, the True, and the Beautiful"

The prayer's pledge to "sacrifice everything for the good, the true, and the beautiful" reflects the classical **transcendentals** — the properties of Being that are convertible with Being itself and ultimately identical with God. Lust and impurity are, by contrast, disordered attachments that substitute the fleeting pleasure of the creature for the true, the good, and the beautiful, which is God. The sacrifice promised in the prayer is thus an act of **reparation and reordering**: the soul renounces the counterfeit goods of sin in order to possess the authentic Good, Truth, and Beauty — God Himself — after the example of St. Joseph, who sacrificed everything for the Holy Family.

## 5. How and When to Pray It

The prayer may be used whenever the soul is **tempted by lust or impurity**, and is especially suited to:

- **The moment of temptation** — prayed immediately and briefly as an act of resistance and recourse
- **Morning and evening prayer** — as a daily petition for the grace of chastity and purity
- **Days of special devotion to St. Joseph** — Wednesdays, the month of March, and the Solemnity of St. Joseph (19 March), traditionally dedicated to him
- **The preparation for Consecration to St. Joseph** — as part of the 33-day preparation popularized by Fr. Calloway
- **The Sacrament of Penance** — as an act of thanksgiving and resolve after sacramental absolution

In the spiritual combat against sins of the flesh, the Church also recommends frequent recourse to the **Sacraments** (especially Penance and the Most Holy Eucharist), prayer to the **Blessed Virgin Mary**, the **St. Michael the Archangel Prayer** (see [saints/patron-st-michael-archangel.md](../saints/patron-st-michael-archangel.md)), and the prayer to one's **Guardian Angel**. Purity of heart is first of all a gift of God received through grace, and is strengthened by the practice of the virtues, custody of the eyes and imagination, and the avoidance of near occasions of sin (cf. CCC 2520–2523).

## 6. Eastern Catholic Dimension

In the **Byzantine** and other Eastern Catholic liturgical traditions, St. Joseph is likewise venerated with great honor. The Byzantine liturgical year commemorates **St. Joseph the Betrothed** on the **Sunday after the Nativity of Christ** (the Sunday of the Holy Ancestors of Christ, which includes Joseph), and also on the synaxis of the Holy Family. Eastern iconography depicts Joseph as an elderly man, often holding the Child Jesus or a **lily** — the ancient symbol of his purity and chastity — and sometimes a flowering staff, recalling the tradition that his staff blossomed to indicate God's choice of him as Mary's spouse.

While the specific formula of Fr. Calloway's prayer is a Latin-Rite devotional composition, its theological content — St. Joseph as chaste spouse, protector of the Holy Family, and powerful intercessor — is fully shared by the Eastern Catholic Churches, and the prayer may be prayed with equal devotion by Eastern Catholics in their own tradition.
