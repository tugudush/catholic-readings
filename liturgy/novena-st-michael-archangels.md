# Nine-Day Novena to St. Michael and the Holy Archangels

> **Devotional status:** This is a Catholic devotional arrangement prepared for this repository. It is not a universal official novena, a sacramental, or a substitute for the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass. The prayers are directed to God and ask St. Michael, St. Gabriel, St. Raphael, and the holy angels to intercede as God's servants.

This nine-day novena may be prayed to **St. Michael the Archangel** alone or, especially in preparation for the Roman feast on 29 September, to **Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, Archangels** together. The nine days may be prayed at any time. A common Roman Rite schedule is **20-28 September**, concluding before the Feast of the Archangels on 29 September. A Byzantine Catholic parish may adapt the schedule to its own calendar before the **Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and all the Bodiless Powers** on 8 November.

The intention of the novena may be stated in the blank below. The intention should be morally good and entrusted to God's wisdom rather than treated as a guaranteed result of completing nine days.

## 1. Who Is Invoked?

### 1.1 St. Michael

St. Michael's name means **"Who is like God?"** Sacred Scripture presents him as a chief prince who protects God's people (Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1), as the archangel who says, **"The Lord rebuke you"** when contending with the devil (Jude 9), and as the leader of the faithful angels in the vision of the heavenly battle (Revelation 12:7-9).

The faithful ask St. Michael to intercede for the Church, the Pope and bishops, clergy, families, the dying, those who protect others, and all who are facing temptation or grave danger. He is not an independent divine power. Christian victory over sin and death comes from God through Jesus Christ.

### 1.2 St. Gabriel and St. Raphael

The Roman feast joins St. Michael with the two other archangels named in Sacred Scripture:

| Archangel   | Biblical witness                                  | Principal devotional theme                                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Michael** | Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1; Jude 9; Revelation 12:7-9 | Fidelity, spiritual combat, and protection of God's people  |
| **Gabriel** | Daniel 8:16; 9:21; Luke 1:26-38                   | Proclamation of God's word, vocation, and obedient faith    |
| **Raphael** | Tobit 3:17; 6:1-18; 12:15                         | Healing, guidance, safe travel, and God's providential care |

The Church officially recognizes these three names. Devotion should not invent or promote additional names for angels. The [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy §§215-217](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/215) provides the Church's principles for devotion to the holy angels.

## 2. When to Pray the Novena

The novena may be prayed:

- **20-28 September**, as preparation for the Roman Feast of Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael on 29 September;
- **30 October-7 November**, as a possible preparation for the Byzantine Catholic observance on 8 November, subject to the calendar of the particular Church;
- for nine consecutive days before a parish patronal celebration, pilgrimage, or apostolic undertaking; or
- at any other time when a person or community wishes to commend a serious intention to God.

The date does not give the prayer a magical force. The value of the novena lies in persevering prayer, conversion of life, confidence in God's providence, and charity toward one's neighbor.

## 3. Daily Order of Prayer

The following order may be used each day. A parish may add approved hymns, a homily, the Rosary, the Litany of the Saints, Eucharistic adoration, or another suitable devotion.

1. Make the **Sign of the Cross**.
2. State the intention of the novena.
3. Read the day's Scripture passage slowly.
4. Offer the day's meditation and novena prayer.
5. Pray one **Our Father**, one **Hail Mary**, and one **Glory Be**, or another suitable Trinitarian prayer.
6. Pray the **Prayer to St. Michael** below.
7. End with the concluding prayer.

### 3.1 Intention

The person praying may say:

    Almighty and merciful God,
    I place before You this intention:

    [state the intention]

    Through the intercession of St. Michael,
    St. Gabriel, St. Raphael,
    and all Your holy angels,
    grant what is according to Your will
    and give me the grace to receive Your answer
    with faith, hope, and charity.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### 3.2 Prayer to St. Michael

This traditional formula is associated with Pope Leo XIII. It is a devotional prayer, not a replacement for the Church's liturgy and not a formula of exorcism for private use.

    Saint Michael the Archangel,
    defend us in battle;
    be our protection against the wickedness and snares of the devil.

    May God rebuke him, we humbly pray;
    and do thou, O Prince of the heavenly host,
    by the power of God,
    cast into hell Satan
    and all the evil spirits
    who prowl about the world
    seeking the ruin of souls.
    Amen.

### 3.3 Concluding Prayer

    O God, who in Your loving providence
    appoint angels and archangels
    to serve Your saving plan,
    grant that we may be defended by their protection
    and rejoice with them in Your presence.

    Through the intercession of St. Michael,
    St. Gabriel, and St. Raphael,
    keep us faithful to Your Son,
    obedient to the Holy Spirit,
    and steadfast in charity.

    May our prayer lead us to the sacraments,
    to repentance from sin,
    and to greater love of You and our neighbor.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

## 4. The Nine Days

### Day 1 - The Glory of God and the Ministry of the Angels

**Scripture:** Psalm 103:20-22; Catechism of the Catholic Church §§328-336.

**Meditation:** The holy angels are creatures of God. They glorify Him, obey His word, and serve His saving plan. The first movement of this novena is therefore adoration: God alone is worshipped, while the angels are honored as His ministers and invoked as intercessors.

**Prayer:**

    Almighty God,
    You created the visible and invisible world
    and call all Your creatures to glorify Your name.
    Through the intercession of St. Michael
    and all the holy angels,
    free me from pride and self-reliance.

    Teach me to recognize Your presence,
    to obey Your commandments,
    and to place every desire beneath Your will.
    May this novena lead me to worship You more faithfully
    and to serve my neighbor with humility.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 2 - The Humility of St. Michael

**Scripture:** Daniel 10:10-14, 20-21.

**Meditation:** St. Michael's name is a confession that no one is like God. His strength is not self-glorification but faithful service. In every spiritual struggle, the Christian must resist the temptation to make the self, a created power, or a private experience the center of life.

**Prayer:**

    Lord God, source of all holiness,
    You have given St. Michael a name
    that proclaims Your incomparable majesty.
    Through his intercession,
    deliver me from vanity, resentment,
    and the desire to control what belongs to You.

    Give me the humility to say,
    in thought, word, and deed,
    that You alone are God.
    May I use every gift for Your glory
    and for the good of the people You entrust to me.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 3 - Protection of the Church

**Scripture:** Daniel 12:1; Acts 20:28-32.

**Meditation:** The Church belongs to Christ and is sustained by His grace. The faithful may ask St. Michael to intercede for the Pope, bishops, priests, deacons, religious, missionaries, families, and all the baptized. This prayer should produce fidelity, courage, and charity rather than fear or suspicion.

**Prayer:**

    O God, Shepherd of Your people,
    protect Your holy Church in every trial.
    Through the intercession of St. Michael,
    strengthen the Pope and the bishops,
    sanctify priests and deacons,
    encourage religious and missionaries,
    and keep every Christian faithful to the Gospel.

    Heal division, expose falsehood,
    defend the vulnerable,
    and make Your Church a clear witness
    to the mercy and truth of Jesus Christ.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 4 - Victory in Spiritual Combat

**Scripture:** Revelation 12:7-12a; Jude 9.

**Meditation:** Revelation uses the imagery of battle to proclaim God's triumph over the dragon. Jude teaches that even St. Michael invokes the Lord's judgment rather than relying on his own authority. The Christian response to evil is faith in Christ, repentance, prayer, the sacraments, and perseverance in truth.

**Prayer:**

    Lord Jesus Christ,
    victorious over sin and death,
    strengthen me in the battle against temptation.
    Through the intercession of St. Michael,
    help me reject hatred, deception,
    impurity, injustice, and despair.

    Guard my mind from falsehood,
    my heart from malice,
    and my will from deliberate sin.
    When I fall, lead me quickly to repentance
    and to the healing mercy of the Sacrament of Penance.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 5 - The Annunciation and the Message of Gabriel

**Scripture:** Luke 1:26-38.

**Meditation:** St. Gabriel announces the Incarnation of the Son of God to the Blessed Virgin Mary. His ministry directs attention away from himself and toward the saving action of God. With Gabriel's help, the faithful may ask for courage to receive God's word and to answer it with obedient faith.

**Prayer:**

    O God, who sent the archangel Gabriel
    to proclaim the Incarnation of Your Son,
    open my heart to Your word.
    Through the intercession of St. Gabriel,
    give me courage to discern my vocation,
    strength to accept my responsibilities,
    and humility to say yes to Your grace.

    May the mystery of Christ's Incarnation
    shape my choices, my speech,
    and my service to others.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 6 - Healing and Guidance through Raphael

**Scripture:** Tobit 12:6-15.

**Meditation:** The Book of Tobit reveals Raphael as a servant sent by God. The Church may ask for the intercession of St. Raphael for the sick, travelers, caregivers, medical workers, families, and those seeking wise guidance. This prayer does not promise a particular physical result; it entrusts every need to God's providence and supports the use of appropriate medical and pastoral care.

**Prayer:**

    Merciful God, healer of body and soul,
    through the intercession of St. Raphael,
    bring consolation to the sick,
    wisdom to caregivers and physicians,
    safety to travelers,
    and peace to families burdened by illness.

    Grant healing according to Your will,
    and give every suffering person
    patience, practical help, and Christian hope.
    Teach me to accompany the afflicted
    with compassion rather than empty promises.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 7 - The Guardian Angels and the Little Ones

**Scripture:** Matthew 18:10; Psalm 91:9-12.

**Meditation:** Christ teaches that the little ones are not forgotten before the Father. The Church teaches that human life is surrounded by the protecting care of the angels. This day is an opportunity to pray for children, families, the elderly, the unborn, the persecuted, and all who are vulnerable.

**Prayer:**

    Father of mercies,
    protect the little ones and all who are vulnerable.
    Through the intercession of the holy angels,
    guard children, families, the elderly,
    the unborn, the poor, and the persecuted.

    Make my home a place of prayer and safety.
    Give me eyes to notice those in need
    and the courage to defend human dignity
    through truthful speech and concrete charity.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 8 - The Armor of God

**Scripture:** Ephesians 6:10-18.

**Meditation:** St. Paul names the armor of God as truth, righteousness, faith, salvation, the word of God, and persevering prayer. The Christian does not fight spiritual evil through superstition or private rituals, but by abiding in Christ and living the grace of Baptism. St. Michael's intercession should lead to a deeper sacramental life.

**Prayer:**

    Holy Spirit, strengthen me with Your grace.
    Through the intercession of St. Michael
    and all the holy angels,
    clothe me with truth, justice, faith,
    and the hope of salvation.

    Help me to pray without ceasing,
    to forgive those who injure me,
    to resist every form of occult practice,
    and to seek the help of the Church
    when serious spiritual or pastoral danger arises.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

### Day 9 - Perseverance in Christ and the Hope of Heaven

**Scripture:** John 1:47-51; Hebrews 1:5-14.

**Meditation:** Christ is the Lord of the angels. The Gospel presents heaven opened and the angels ascending and descending upon the Son of Man. The novena ends where it began: in the worship of God. Ask for perseverance in grace, a holy death, and the joy of praising God with the angels and saints.

**Prayer:**

    Eternal Father,
    through Your Son Jesus Christ,
    You have opened the way to eternal life.
    Through the intercession of St. Michael,
    St. Gabriel, St. Raphael,
    and all the holy angels,
    keep me faithful until death.

    Strengthen the dying,
    console those who mourn,
    purify Your Church,
    and gather Your people into the joy of heaven.
    May my life become an offering of praise
    and may I one day behold Your glory
    with all the angels and saints.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

## 5. The Novena and the Holy Mass

### 5.1 The novena is not the Mass

A private or parish novena prayer is not the Eucharistic liturgy. It must not be inserted into the Eucharistic Prayer, substituted for the approved readings, or presented as though it were a text from the Roman Missal or an Eastern Catholic Divine Liturgy.

The **Holy Mass** is the sacramental re-presentation of Christ's one sacrifice and the summit of Christian worship. A novena is a form of popular piety that disposes the faithful to pray, repent, and participate more fruitfully in the Church's liturgy. The [Catechism of the Catholic Church §§1322-1419](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1322) and the [Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy §90](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4fba701f-fda0-47a5-a2de-2e210d81f770/ref/90) provide the relevant framework.

### 5.2 Requesting nine Mass intentions

A person or parish may ask a priest to offer a series of Masses for the intention of the novena, subject to the parish schedule, the liturgical calendar, and the priest's obligations. The faithful may use wording such as:

    For the intention of [name or group],
    that Almighty God may grant what is truly good
    through the intercession of St. Michael
    and the holy archangels,
    according to His merciful will.

A Mass intention does not purchase grace, and a stipend is not a fee for the Mass. For the canonical principles governing Mass intentions, offerings, and the support of the clergy, see [Mass Intentions: Doctrine, Practice, and Pastoral Guidance](mass-intentions.md), especially its discussion of Code of Canon Law canons 901 and 945-958.

The person who requests the Mass is encouraged to attend when possible and to receive Holy Communion when properly disposed. If a requested Mass cannot be celebrated on a particular day, the parish should explain the available schedule and any alternative arrangement.

### 5.3 A Roman Rite Mass on 29 September

In the current Roman General Calendar, 29 September is the **Feast of Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, Archangels**. The Roman Missal provides the proper Mass for the feast, including the Gloria and proper prayers. The Roman Lectionary gives the following options:

- **First reading:** Daniel 7:9-10, 13-14, or Revelation 12:7-12a;
- **Responsorial Psalm:** Psalm 138:1-2ab, 2cde-3, 4-5;
- **Gospel:** John 1:47-51.

The [Roman Missal, Proper of Saints](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/3f204803-62dd-4467-9edb-8b46db283e8a/ref/Proper%20of%20Saints) is the authority for the proper texts. A parish should follow the approved liturgical books and the rank of the day rather than replacing the Mass with a private novena formula.

29 September is not, by that fact alone, a universal Holy Day of Obligation in the Latin Church. The obligations of a particular country, diocese, parish, or institution must be followed. See the [Feast of St. Michael the Archangel](feast-of-st-michael-archangel.md) for the feast's Roman, Eastern Catholic, and local devotional context.

### 5.4 Eastern Catholic observance

Eastern Catholic Churches follow their own approved calendars and liturgical books. In the Byzantine tradition, the **Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and all the Bodiless Powers** is commonly celebrated on **8 November**; in communities using the Julian calendar, that liturgical date corresponds to 21 November on the Gregorian civil calendar. A parish novena should use the calendar and texts of its own _sui iuris_ Church.

The [Catechism of the Ukrainian Catholic Church, _Christ - Our Pascha_ §143](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/5c0fb401-3c71-494e-bfce-ac8641dafd3b/ref/143) describes this Byzantine observance and the Church's veneration of the bodiless powers. In an Eastern Catholic setting, the appropriate companion to the novena is the Divine Liturgy, not an improvised Roman Mass formulary.

### 5.5 Optional devotional conclusion after Mass

With the pastor's permission, a parish may pray the novena's daily prayer or the Prayer to St. Michael after the dismissal. This is a devotional addition, not part of the Roman Missal or the Divine Liturgy. The priest or deacon should not alter the approved Eucharistic or sacramental texts to accommodate the novena.

## 6. Levels of Authority

| Element                                                                    | Status                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| God alone is worshipped; angels are creatures who serve God's saving plan  | Catholic doctrine grounded in Sacred Scripture and the Catechism, especially CCC §§328-336                                                                             |
| The Roman Feast of Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael on 29 September    | Official Roman liturgy according to the General Roman Calendar and Roman Missal                                                                                        |
| The Byzantine Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and all the Bodiless Powers | Official observance in the calendars of the relevant Eastern Catholic Churches                                                                                         |
| A series of nine days of prayer                                            | Optional popular devotion; no single universal formula is required                                                                                                     |
| The prayers and meditations in this document                               | Devotional composition, to be used freely and prudently; not revealed doctrine or liturgical text                                                                      |
| A nine-day series of Mass intentions                                       | A legitimate parish request when arranged according to canon law, local policy, and the liturgical calendar; it is not automatically a universal liturgical observance |
| Promises that a novena guarantees a requested outcome                      | Not Catholic teaching; prayer remains subject to God's will and must reject superstition                                                                               |

## 7. Pastoral Safeguards

- Ask the archangels to intercede **with God**; do not treat them as gods, magical forces, or independent sources of power.
- Do not use this novena as a private exorcism or attempt to command demons. In serious cases of spiritual distress, contact a Catholic priest and follow diocesan guidance.
- Prayer for healing should accompany, not replace, appropriate medical and psychological care.
- A novena does not replace Sunday Mass, the Sacrament of Penance, the Anointing of the Sick, or other obligations and means of grace proper to one's state in life.
- If one day is missed, simply resume the novena. There is no magical penalty and no need to repeat the entire series unless a person freely chooses to do so.
- A parish may adapt the music, language, Scripture readings for the private service, and charitable activity while preserving the distinction between popular devotion and the liturgy.
