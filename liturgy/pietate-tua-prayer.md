# Pietate Tua: Historical Prayer and Indulgence Claim

## Verification Summary

The Facebook post is based on a genuine historical Catholic prayer entry, but its devotional commentary treats an old indulgence attribution as though it were a current, measurable guarantee for souls in Purgatory. The historical claim is substantially corroborated; the present-day claims require qualification.

| Claim in the post                                                          | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The prayer is known as _Pietate tua_                                       | **Verified.** It appears in the 1857 _Raccolta_.                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Pope Leo XII granted an indulgence on 9 July 1828                          | **Historically attested.** The 1857 _Raccolta_ reports a handwritten rescript attributed to Leo XII and says it was deposited in the archive of the Vatican Basilica. The original rescript was not independently inspected for this entry. |
| Forty days for each recitation and 100 years plus quarantines on Saturdays | **Verified as historical wording in the 1857 _Raccolta_, not as a current measure.** The post is reproducing an obsolete days-and-years system.                                                                                             |
| Four consecutive Saturdays                                                 | **Approximate.** The historical wording says “every Saturday for a month,” not specifically “four consecutive Saturdays.”                                                                                                                   |
| Souls may have remained in Purgatory for hundreds of years                 | **Unsupported and misleading.** The Church does not define Purgatory by earthly calendar duration.                                                                                                                                          |
| Reciting the prayer can finally release countless souls with certainty     | **Overstated.** Indulgences are applied to the departed by way of suffrage, not as an automatic or mechanically guaranteed release.                                                                                                         |
| The Leo XII grant can be advertised as a current indulgence                | **Not established.** The post does not provide evidence of confirmation under the current discipline of indulgences.                                                                                                                        |

## The Post Examined

The post was published by [Messages of Our Mother Mary to the World](https://www.facebook.com/permalink.php?story_fbid=pfbid02e9txmpu6YGjD6VMb2V32JGiXi3iA1mKGdLKjsDb6tALN6Jxmjn7ESSeCkhxomazpl&id=100068591042501), displayed as dated 13 September 2026, and inspected on 16 September 2026. It presents the prayer under the heading “PIETATE TUA,” recommends Saturday recitation, and attributes the two indulgence grants to Pope Leo XII.

The post also adds a premise that describes the prayer as a means of freeing souls who may have suffered in Purgatory for hundreds of years. That latter explanation is not part of the historical _Raccolta_ entry and should not be treated as Catholic doctrine.

## Historical Source

The [1857 _Raccolta_ entry for “Pietate tua”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Raccolta_%281857%29/Prayer%E2%80%94%22Pietate_tua%22), a scanned historical collection published under the Sacred Congregation of Indulgences and Holy Relics, contains the same prayer in Latin and English. Its heading reports that Pope Leo XII granted:

1. A forty-day indulgence whenever the faithful devoutly recited the prayer.
2. An indulgence of 100 years and as many quarantines when the prayer was said every Saturday for a month.

The entry attributes these grants to a rescript in Pope Leo XII's own hand, dated 9 July 1828, and says that it was deposited in the _Archivium_ of the Sacred Vatican Basilica. This is good evidence that the claim was printed in an authoritative nineteenth-century indulgence collection. It is not, by itself, a direct examination of the 1828 rescript or proof that the old grant remains in force today.

The Facebook version is also not a word-for-word transcription of the 1857 English translation. For example, the historical translation says “all stain of sin” and “all virtue,” while the post says “all vices” and “all virtues.” The historical translation reads “prelates, princes”; the post reads “prelates, priests.” These are textual variants in a devotional translation, not evidence that the prayer is a new revelation.

## The Prayer

### Latin Text

The following is the Latin text printed in the 1857 _Raccolta_:

    Pietate tua, quæsumus Domine, nostrorum solve vincula peccatorum,
    et intercedente Beata, semperque Virgine Dei Genitrice Maria,
    cum Beatis Apostolis tuis Petro, et Paulo, et omnibus Sanctis
    nos famulos tuos, et loca nostra in omni sanctitate custodi:
    omnes consanguinitate, affinitate, ac familiaritate nobis conjunctos
    a vitiis purga, virtutibus illustra;
    pacem et salutem nobis tribue;
    hostes visibiles, et invisibiles remove;
    carnalia desideria repelle;
    aerem salubrem indulge;
    amicis et inimicis nostris charitatem largire;
    Urbem tuam custodi;
    Pontificem nostrum N. conserva;
    omnes Praelatos, Principes, cunctumque Populum Christianum
    ab omni adversitate defende.
    Benedictio tua sit super nos semper;
    et omnibus Fidelibus defunctis requiem aeternam concede.
    Per Christum Dominum nostrum. Amen.

### English Text

The following is a lightly lineated form of the English prayer reproduced in the Facebook post. “N.” is a placeholder for the reigning Supreme Pontiff.

    Loosen, O Lord, we pray Thee, in Thy pity, the bonds of our sins,
    and by the intercession of the blessed Mary,
    ever Virgin Mother of God,
    the blessed Apostles Peter and Paul, and all saints,
    keep us Thy servants and our abodes in all holiness;
    cleanse us, our relations, kinsfolk, and acquaintances, from all vices;
    adorn us with all virtues;
    grant to us peace and health;
    repel our enemies visible and invisible;
    curb our carnal desires;
    vouchsafe us healthful seasons;
    bestow Thy charity upon our friends and our enemies;
    guard Thy holy city;
    preserve our Sovereign Pontiff N.,
    and defend all prelates, priests, and all Christian people from all adversity.
    Let Thy blessing be ever upon us,
    and grant to all the faithful departed eternal rest.
    Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

The prayer is a traditional petition for forgiveness, holiness, peace, protection, charity, the Pope and the Church, and the eternal rest of the faithful departed. Its devotional use does not depend on accepting every historical claim circulated with it.

## Current Catholic Teaching on the Indulgence Claim

### What an indulgence is

The Catechism defines an indulgence as the remission before God of temporal punishment due to sins whose guilt has already been forgiven ([CCC 1471](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1471)). An indulgence is therefore not the forgiveness of an unforgiven sin, and it is not a purchase of salvation.

Pope Paul VI's [_Indulgentiarum doctrina_, no. 12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/8b6ff07d-4c17-48fa-9202-88944fc52d28/ref/12), abolished the former determination of partial indulgences by days and years. The current norms state that a partial indulgence is designated simply as a “partial indulgence,” without a number of days or years ([_Indulgentiarum doctrina_, Norms 1-7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/8b6ff07d-4c17-48fa-9202-88944fc52d28/ref/Norms)). The current _Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_ repeats this rule ([_Enchiridion_, Normae](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Normae)).

Paul VI's transitional norms further stated that indulgences not confirmed under the revised discipline would become null and void two years after the 1967 constitution ([_Indulgentiarum doctrina_, Transitional Norms](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/8b6ff07d-4c17-48fa-9202-88944fc52d28/ref/Transitional%20Norms)). For that reason, the 1857 printing of the Leo XII grant cannot be presented as a presently operative indulgence without evidence of later confirmation.

### Application to the faithful departed

The Church does teach that partial and plenary indulgences can be applied to the faithful departed by way of suffrage ([_Indulgentiarum doctrina_, Norm 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/8b6ff07d-4c17-48fa-9202-88944fc52d28/ref/Norms); [CCC 1479](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1479)). “By way of suffrage” means that the living ask God to apply the spiritual benefit to the departed; it does not give the person praying a timetable or a guaranteed human calculation of the soul's purification.

The current _Enchiridion_ does contain specific indulgences for the faithful departed, including visits to a cemetery with prayer for the dead and the recitation of _Requiem aeternam_ ([_Enchiridion Indulgentiarum_, Concessio 29](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/b4e30e90-7ed9-4cc5-be4b-305ed07d425b/ref/Concessiones.%2029)). I did not find a current specific entry for _Pietate tua_ in the current indulgence material consulted here.

### Purgatory is not measured in calendar years

The Catechism teaches that the purification called Purgatory may occur after death, but it does not assign that purification an earthly calendar duration ([CCC 1472](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1472)). The Church's _Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy_ describes suffrage for the dead as an urgent supplication for God's mercy and lists the Eucharistic Sacrifice, prayers, works of mercy, and indulgences among the ways the Church assists the departed ([Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, 251](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/5a23f665-2c67-4148-9925-221dc116f288/ref/251)).

The old expressions “forty days” and “100 years” were categories in a former indulgence discipline. They were never a chart showing that a soul would remain in Purgatory for forty days or a hundred earthly years. The post's suggestion that particular souls have remained there for “hundreds of years” therefore goes beyond defined Catholic teaching and should be omitted.

## Responsible Devotional Use

The prayer may be recited as a traditional Catholic prayer for personal conversion, protection, the needs of the Church, and the faithful departed. It is appropriate to pray it on Saturday if that custom helps devotion, but the day itself should not be advertised as a guaranteed way to obtain the old Leo XII grant.

For the departed, Catholics may also:

- offer the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass for their repose;
- pray _Requiem aeternam_ and other approved prayers for the dead;
- visit a cemetery and pray for the departed, especially during the indulgenced period specified by the current _Enchiridion_;
- perform works of mercy and charity in communion with the Church's prayer.

The most accurate summary is: _Pietate tua_ is a genuine historical prayer preserved in the nineteenth-century _Raccolta_, and the Leo XII attribution is historically recorded there. The old “forty days” and “100 years” language is not the current measure of indulgences, and the prayer should not be presented as an automatic release from a calculable duration in Purgatory.
