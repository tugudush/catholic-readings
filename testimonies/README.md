# Faith Testimonies & Interviews

The [testimonies/](./) directory documents **first-person accounts of faith** — conversion narratives, celebrity and public-figure interviews, and lay testimonies — in the same source-critical style used elsewhere in this repository: the speaker's own claims are reported and attributed, the machine transcript is distinguished from verified fact, and every substantive theological statement is graded against Catholic doctrine.

## Scope

- **What belongs here.** Documented testimonies and interviews in which a named person narrates their own religious experience, practice, and moral judgments — especially public figures whose account reaches a large audience.
- **What does not belong here.** Catechesis, papal and conciliar documents, saints' biographies (see [saints/](../saints/README.md)), comparative studies of other ecclesial communities (see [protestantism/](../protestantism/README.md)), and the documentation of claimed private revelations (see [miracles/](../miracles/README.md) and [eschatology/](../eschatology/README.md)).

## Principles Applied

1. **Testimony is evidence of experience, not of doctrine.** A person's sincerity does not make their theology correct, and a person's theological imprecision does not make their experience false.
2. **Attribute, then assess.** The speaker's position is stated from the interview itself before any Catholic evaluation is offered.
3. **Grade the claims.** Each substantive assertion is weighed as dogma, doctrine, ecclesiastical discipline, or theological/personal opinion — including claims that lie outside the Church's competence altogether, such as conspiracy theories.
4. **Note the silences.** A Catholic assessment records what a testimony omits (the sacraments, the Church's teaching authority, Our Lady and the saints) as carefully as what it affirms.
5. **Neither credulity nor contempt.** Popular testimonies are neither endorsed wholesale nor dismissed; the Second Vatican Council's teaching on the elements of sanctification and truth that exist outside the Church's visible boundaries (_Lumen Gentium_ 8; _Unitatis Redintegratio_ 3) governs the reading.
