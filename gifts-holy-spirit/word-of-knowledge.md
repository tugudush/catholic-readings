# The Word of Knowledge (λόγος γνώσεως)

The second of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another the **utterance of knowledge** according to the same Spirit." This gift is a Spirit-given capacity to manifest knowledge of divine things through the created order, strengthening faith and communicating truth for the common good.

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To one is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom, and to another the **utterance of knowledge** according to the same Spirit."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:8 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek λόγος γνώσεως (_logos gnōseōs_) pairs the term for "word, utterance, discourse" (_logos_) with _gnōsis_ — knowledge. St. Paul deliberately distinguishes this from σοφία (_sophia_, wisdom). The two are paired as complementary intellectual charisms: wisdom contemplates divine things in themselves; knowledge apprehends divine things as they are reflected in creation.

### 1.2 Knowledge in the Pauline Corpus

St. Paul uses γνῶσις (_gnōsis_) extensively. In [1 Corinthians 8:1–3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), he warns that "knowledge puffs up, but love builds up" — a caution that applies equally to the charism: the "word of knowledge" must be exercised in charity, not as a pretext for spiritual pride. In [1 Corinthians 13:2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), he relativizes even prophetic powers and all knowledge before the supremacy of love.

In [Colossians 2:2–3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), Paul prays that believers attain "the full assurance of understanding and the knowledge of God's mystery, that is, Christ himself, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge." Here wisdom and knowledge are again paired — and both are ultimately found in Christ.

### 1.3 Relation to the Old Testament

The Old Testament concept of _da'at_ (דַּעַת, "knowledge") often denotes relational, experiential knowledge of God — not mere intellectual cognition. The "word of knowledge" as a New Testament charism brings this Hebrew sense of intimate, lived knowledge into the charismatic life of the Church.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term                       | Greek                           | Meaning                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Word / Utterance**       | λόγος (_logos_)                 | Speech, discourse, reasoned utterance; a communication that is intelligible and ordered                                                                      |
| **Knowledge**              | γνῶσις (_gnōsis_)               | In the Pauline sense: knowledge of divine things as manifested through creatures and through the mysteries of salvation history                              |
| **Utterance of Knowledge** | λόγος γνώσεως (_logos gnōseōs_) | The Spirit-given ability to articulate knowledge of God's truth persuasively, especially as discerned through creation, Scripture, and the Church's teaching |

Like λόγος σοφίας, the phrase λόγος γνώσεως is unique to 1 Corinthians 12:8 in the New Testament — a distinct Pauline formulation for a specific charismatic gift. It should not be confused with the gift of knowledge among the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit (Isaiah 11:2).

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom treats the "word of knowledge" as a distinct gift related to the **communication** of what one knows. He observes a pastoral reality: some possess genuine spiritual knowledge but lack the ability to convey it effectively, while others are gifted by the Spirit to articulate knowledge in a way that edifies the hearers.

This distinction is pastorally significant. The charism is not merely the possession of knowledge but the **grace of its communication** — the Spirit empowers the recipient to speak about divine things in a way that is clear, convincing, and conducive to faith.

### 3.2 St. Augustine of Hippo (354–430)

St. Augustine's distinction between _sapientia_ (wisdom, contemplation of eternal realities) and _scientia_ (knowledge, the right use and understanding of temporal things) in [_De Trinitate_ XII–XIV](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) provides the classic Western framework for understanding the difference between the "word of wisdom" and the "word of knowledge":

- **_Sapientia_** (wisdom): contemplates eternal, unchanging divine realities — the _ratio aeterna_ of things in God.
- **_Scientia_** (knowledge): engages with temporal, created realities as they relate to the faith — the _ratio temporalis_ — and applies divine truth to concrete situations.

Augustine's framework illuminates why St. Paul lists them as distinct yet paired gifts.

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:8

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 12.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas Aquinas distinguishes the "word of knowledge" from the "word of wisdom" by their objects:

- **Wisdom** concerns **divine things in themselves** — the highest contemplative knowledge, ordered to the judgment of all things according to the highest causes.
- **Knowledge** enables one to manifest things of God **through creatures** — it discerns the reflection of the Creator in the created order and articulates the intelligibility of the faith as it intersects with human experience and the natural world.

Aquinas frames the "word of knowledge" as strengthening faith rather than producing merely "curious" or speculative knowledge. Its purpose is **edification**, not intellectual display. The charism serves to build up the hearers in the truth of the faith by helping them see how divine revelation illuminates every dimension of reality.

### 4.2 Charismatic Knowledge vs. the Gift of Knowledge

|                | **Charism of the Word of Knowledge**                             | **Gift of Knowledge (Isaiah 11:2)**                                   |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Category**   | _Gratia gratis data_ (for the benefit of others)                 | One of the seven gifts — a permanent disposition for sanctification   |
| **Purpose**    | To communicate knowledge of divine things to others persuasively | To enable the soul to judge created things rightly in relation to God |
| **Mode**       | Extraordinary, occasional, communicative                         | Abiding, habitual, interior                                           |
| **Recipients** | Given variously as the Spirit wills                              | Given to every baptized person in the state of grace                  |

### 4.3 Knowledge Ordered to Charity

Aquinas stresses, with St. Paul, that charismatic knowledge without charity is worthless ([_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 13.1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)). The "word of knowledge" is a **service** — it exists to build up the Body of Christ in faith, hope, and love, not to inflate the ego of the gifted individual.

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism teaches that charisms are "graces of the Holy Spirit which directly or indirectly benefit the Church" ([CCC 799](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/799)). The "word of knowledge" falls within this general affirmation — it is a particular manifestation of the Spirit for ecclesial service.

Pope St. John Paul II, in [_Christifideles Laici_ 24](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), describes charisms as ordered to the Church's needs and subject to discernment by those who preside in the Church. The "word of knowledge" is not a license for private speculation or doctrinal innovation but a grace exercised within and for the ecclesial communion.

The 2016 CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) reaffirms the complementarity of hierarchical and charismatic gifts: the "word of knowledge" is authentically charismatic only when it serves the Church's unity and is exercised in harmony with apostolic teaching.

### 5.2 Manifestations Today

In the Church's pastoral experience, the "word of knowledge" may manifest as:

- **Catechetical insight**: the ability to explain the faith clearly and compellingly, connecting doctrine to lived experience.
- **Apopologetic articulation**: the grace to defend and explain the faith persuasively in dialogue with non-believers and those of other traditions.
- **Spiritual direction**: insight into the movements of the soul and the ability to articulate how God is working in a person's life.
- **Pastoral application**: the capacity to apply the Church's moral and social teaching to concrete, complex situations with clarity and compassion.
- **Biblical exposition**: the grace to unfold the meaning of Sacred Scripture in a way that strikes the hearers as illuminating and authoritative.

### 5.3 Discernment

The "word of knowledge" is subject to the same ecclesial discernment as all charisms. The test is threefold: does the knowledge communicated conform to apostolic doctrine? Does it build up the Church in charity? Does it bear the fruits of the Holy Spirit ([Galatians 5:22–23](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/))?

The gift of [discernment of spirits](discernment-of-spirits.md) exists precisely to distinguish authentic charismatic knowledge from counterfeit claims and from the merely human opinion that presents itself as spiritual insight.

## 6. Distinction from Related Concepts

| Concept                             | Distinction                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Gift of Knowledge (Isaiah 11:2)** | A permanent disposition for personal sanctification; the charism is an occasional gift for communicating knowledge to others.                                                                                                              |
| **Word of Wisdom (1 Cor 12:8)**     | Wisdom contemplates divine things in themselves; knowledge apprehends divine things through creatures and applies truth to concrete realities.                                                                                             |
| **Prophecy (1 Cor 12:10)**          | Prophecy is direct divine illumination, often spontaneous and future-oriented; the word of knowledge typically involves the articulation of truths already revealed, applied to present circumstances.                                     |
| **Theological Learning**            | The word of knowledge is a supernatural charism, not reducible to academic study — though grace builds on nature and may elevate natural intellectual gifts.                                                                               |
| **Gnosticism**                      | Unlike the heretical _gnōsis_ of ancient Gnosticism (which claimed secret, saving knowledge for an elite), the charismatic "word of knowledge" serves the **common good** of the whole Church and conforms to public apostolic revelation. |

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- The word of knowledge is given **for edification**, not self-promotion. St. Paul's warning stands: "knowledge puffs up, but love builds up" ([1 Cor 8:1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- The charism operates within the **deposit of faith** (_depositum fidei_). It does not reveal new doctrines but illuminates, applies, and communicates the faith once delivered to the saints ([Jude 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- Those who exercise a teaching office in the Church (bishops, priests, catechists, theologians) should pray for this charism — not to replace study, but to **elevate** it, that their words may carry the Spirit's power.
- The supreme model of the word of knowledge is Christ, who "knew what was in man" ([John 2:25](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) and whose teaching revealed the Father perfectly.
