# Gifts of Healing (χαρίσματα ἰαμάτων)

The fourth of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:9](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another **gifts of healing** by the one Spirit." Notably, both "gifts" and "healings" appear in the plural in the Greek — suggesting a diversity of healing graces distributed by the one Spirit for the building up of the Church.

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To another faith by the same Spirit, to another **gifts of healing** by the one Spirit."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:9 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek χαρίσματα ἰαμάτων (_charismata iamatōn_) uses the plural for both words — literally, "charisms of healings." This double plural is significant: it suggests that healing is not a single, undifferentiated gift but a **category of diverse graces** — healing of different kinds of infirmities, through different modes, for different persons.

### 1.2 Healing in the Ministry of Jesus

The healing charism is rooted in the healing ministry of Christ himself, who "went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil" ([Acts 10:38](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). The Gospels record numerous healings:

- **Physical healings**: the blind ([Mark 10:46–52](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), the lame ([Mark 2:1–12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), lepers ([Luke 17:11–19](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), the deaf and mute ([Mark 7:31–37](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Deliverance from demons**: the Gerasene demoniac ([Mark 5:1–20](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), the Canaanite woman's daughter ([Matthew 15:21–28](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Resuscitations**: Jairus's daughter ([Mark 5:35–43](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), the widow's son at Nain ([Luke 7:11–17](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

Jesus explicitly commissioned the apostles to heal: "Cure the sick, raise the dead, cleanse the lepers, cast out demons" ([Matthew 10:8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

### 1.3 Healing in the Apostolic Church

The Acts of the Apostles records the continuation of healing through the apostles and other believers:

- **St. Peter** heals a man lame from birth at the Beautiful Gate ([Acts 3:1–10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **St. Peter's shadow** falling on the sick brings healing ([Acts 5:15–16](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **St. Paul** heals a crippled man at Lystra ([Acts 14:8–10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) and raises Eutychus from the dead ([Acts 20:9–12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Handkerchiefs and aprons** that had touched Paul's skin were carried to the sick, and diseases left them ([Acts 19:11–12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

### 1.4 The Sacrament of Anointing of the Sick

St. James provides the apostolic mandate for sacramental healing: "Are any among you sick? They should call for the elders of the church and have them pray over them, anointing them with oil in the name of the Lord. The prayer of faith will save the sick, and the Lord will raise them up" ([James 5:14–15](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). This is the scriptural foundation of the [Sacrament of the Anointing of the Sick](../sacraments/anointing-of-the-sick.md). The charismatic gifts of healing are distinct from — but related to — the sacramental economy of healing.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term                  | Greek                                    | Meaning                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Charisms**          | χαρίσματα (_charismata_)                 | Gratuitous gifts of grace; plural here emphasizes the diversity of healing graces                                                  |
| **Healings**          | ἰαμάτων (_iamatōn_)                      | Cures, restorations to health; from ἰάομαι (_iaomai_), "I heal"                                                                    |
| **Gifts of Healings** | χαρίσματα ἰαμάτων (_charismata iamatōn_) | A category of charismatic graces directed toward the restoration of bodily, mental, or spiritual health through the Spirit's power |

The double plural — "gifts" and "healings" both plural — is unique in the list of 1 Corinthians 12:8–10 and suggests a rich diversity: healing of different diseases, through different modes, for different persons, as the Spirit wills.

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom distinguishes the "gift of healing" from the broader charism of "working of miracles." Healing, he observes, is specifically focused on **cures** — the restoration of the sick to health — whereas the working of miracles encompasses a wider range of extraordinary divine acts.

Chrysostom notes that the healing charism was widely operative in the apostolic Church and served a **persuasive** function: miracles of healing confirmed the truth of the Gospel and drew unbelievers to faith. The healings were never an end in themselves but signs pointing to the divine power of Christ and the truth of the apostolic preaching.

### 3.2 St. Augustine of Hippo (354–430)

In _The City of God_ (Book XXII, Chapter 8), St. Augustine records numerous healings that occurred in his own diocese — including the restoration of sight to the blind and the healing of a fistula through prayer at the shrine of St. Stephen. Augustine testifies that such miracles continued in the Church beyond the apostolic age, though he notes they were less widely publicized than the apostolic miracles because they had become, in a sense, routine.

Augustine's witness is important: it refutes the claim that charismatic healing ceased with the apostolic generation. The Church has always recognized that the Spirit continues to bestow gifts of healing as He wills.

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:9

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 12.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas describes healing as a divine sign that persuades by **benefit** — the conferral of a tangible good, namely the restoration of health. Unlike the "working of miracles," which persuades by the **greatness** of the deed (cosmic phenomena, raising the dead), the "gifts of healings" persuade by the **kindness** and **mercy** of the act.

Aquinas distinguishes the healing charism from the sacramental economy. While the [Sacrament of Anointing of the Sick](../sacraments/anointing-of-the-sick.md) is the ordinary means of spiritual and (when God wills) physical healing, the charismatic gifts of healing are **extraordinary** and **gratuitous** — given not through the sacramental rite but through the direct operation of the Holy Spirit through a gifted individual.

### 4.2 Healing as a Sign

For Aquinas, the healing charism is classified among the _gratiae gratis datae_ — graces freely given for the benefit of others. Its primary purpose is **confirmation of the faith**:

1. It demonstrates the power of God to those who witness or receive the healing.
2. It manifests the compassion of Christ, who continues to heal through His Body, the Church.
3. It serves as a sign of the ultimate healing — the resurrection of the body and the restoration of all things in Christ.

### 4.3 The Plural: "Gifts of Healings"

Aquinas notes the significance of the double plural: there are diverse kinds of healings — of the body, of the mind, of the spirit — and diverse modes through which the Spirit works them. Healing charisms are not monolithic; the Spirit distributes them as He wills for the manifold needs of the Church.

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism teaches that "the Holy Spirit gives to some a special charism of healing so as to make manifest the power of the grace of the risen Lord" ([CCC 1508](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1508)). However, the Catechism also counsels that "the most intense prayers do not always obtain the healing of all illnesses," and that St. Paul must teach us that "my grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" ([2 Corinthians 12:9](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [CCC 1508](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1508)).

The CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) reaffirms that healing charisms are exercised within the Church's life and for the common good, always in harmony with ecclesial authority.

### 5.2 The Relationship to the Sacrament of Anointing

The charismatic gifts of healing are **not a substitute** for the Sacrament of the Anointing of the Sick — nor is the sacrament a substitute for the charism. They operate in different orders:

|                 | **Gifts of Healing (Charism)**                    | **Anointing of the Sick (Sacrament)**                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Institution** | Given by the Spirit as He wills                   | Instituted by Christ, entrusted to the Church ([James 5:14–15](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/))                                     |
| **Minister**    | Any believer to whom the Spirit gives the charism | A priest (or bishop)                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Mode**        | Extraordinary, occasional                         | Ordinary, sacramental — available to any seriously ill Catholic                                                                                                                 |
| **Effect**      | Physical healing (primary), spiritual consolation | Spiritual strengthening, peace, forgiveness of sins; physical healing if God wills ([CCC 1520](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1520)) |
| **Necessity**   | Not necessary for salvation                       | Not absolutely necessary, but the ordinary means of grace for the seriously ill                                                                                                 |

### 5.3 Manifestations Today

In the Church's pastoral experience, the gifts of healing may manifest as:

- **Physical healing**: the restoration of bodily health through prayer, often instantaneous or unusually rapid, beyond medical explanation.
- **Inner healing**: the healing of emotional wounds, traumatic memories, and psychological afflictions through the Spirit's power.
- **Deliverance**: liberation from demonic oppression or obsession — a specialized form of spiritual healing.
- **Healing of relationships**: the restoration of broken communion between persons through the reconciling power of the Spirit.

### 5.4 Discernment, Prudence, and the Medical Profession

The Church insists on several principles regarding healing charisms:

- **Healing prayer does not replace medical care**. The Church honors the medical profession as a participation in Christ's healing ministry and encourages the faithful to seek appropriate medical treatment ([CCC 2288–2291](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2288)).
- **Not all sickness is healed in this life**. The cross of illness remains a mystery of participation in Christ's passion. Healing charisms do not eliminate the scandal of suffering but manifest God's power within it.
- **Healing ministries must be subject to ecclesial oversight**. The Church's pastors have the duty of testing claims of healing and ensuring that healing ministries do not exploit the vulnerable or promote superstition.

## 6. Distinction from Related Concepts

| Concept                               | Distinction                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Working of Miracles (1 Cor 12:10)** | Miracles encompass acts beyond cures (e.g., nature miracles, raising the dead); healing is a specific subset of miraculous action focused on restoration of health. |
| **Charismatic Faith (1 Cor 12:9)**    | Charismatic faith is often the **interior disposition** through which healing is effected; the gifts of healing are the **external manifestation**.                 |
| **Sacrament of Anointing**            | The sacramental, ordinary means of grace for the sick; healing charisms are extraordinary and not sacramentally mediated.                                           |
| **Medical Science**                   | Medicine operates through natural means; healing charisms operate through supernatural divine power. They are complementary, not contradictory.                     |

---

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- Those who believe they have received a gift of healing should exercise it in **humility, obedience to ecclesial authority, and submission to medical prudence**. No healing minister should discourage medical treatment.
- Healing prayer should always be offered with the disposition: **"Your will be done"** ([Matthew 6:10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). God is not obligated to heal; the charism is a gift, not a mechanism.
- The ultimate healing is the **resurrection of the body**. All charismatic healings in this life are temporary signs pointing toward the definitive healing in the age to come.
- The Church's healing ministry includes both the **sacramental** (Anointing of the Sick, Penance, Eucharist) and the **charismatic** (gifts of healing). Neither should be neglected in favor of the other.
