# The Working of Miracles (ἐνεργήματα δυνάμεων)

The fifth of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another the **working of miracles**." This charism encompasses extraordinary divine acts that transcend the powers of nature — signs that persuade by the sheer greatness of the deed — given by the Spirit for the confirmation of the faith and the building up of the Church.

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To another the **working of miracles**, to another prophecy, to another the discernment of spirits."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:10 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek ἐνεργήματα δυνάμεων (_energēmata dynameōn_) is a rich phrase. Ἐνεργήματα (_energēmata_) derives from ἐνεργέω (_energeō_), "I work, I am active, I operate" — it denotes effective, powerful **operations** or **workings**. Δυνάμεων (_dynameōn_) is the plural genitive of δύναμις (_dynamis_), "power, might, miraculous power." Together, the phrase means "operations of powers" or "workings of mighty deeds."

### 1.2 Miracles in the Ministry of Jesus

The working of miracles is preeminently manifested in the life of Christ. The Gospels record nature miracles, exorcisms, healings, and resuscitations — all of which manifest divine power and confirm Christ's identity and authority:

- **Nature miracles**: calming the storm ([Mark 4:35–41](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), walking on water ([Matthew 14:22–33](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), multiplying loaves ([Mark 6:30–44](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), turning water into wine ([John 2:1–11](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Resuscitations**: Jairus's daughter, the widow's son at Nain, Lazarus ([John 11:1–44](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

Christ's miracles are not mere wonders but **signs** (σημεῖα, _sēmeia_) — acts that reveal His divine identity and mission ([John 2:11](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [CCC 547–550](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/547)).

### 1.3 Miracles in the Apostolic Church

The Acts of the Apostles records the continuation of miracles through the apostles:

- **St. Peter** raises Tabitha (Dorcas) from the dead ([Acts 9:36–42](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **St. Paul** blinds Elymas the magician as a sign of divine judgment ([Acts 13:8–12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **St. Paul** survives a viper's bite without harm on Malta ([Acts 28:3–6](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

St. Paul himself appeals to "signs and wonders and mighty works" as authenticating marks of his apostleship ([2 Corinthians 12:12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [Romans 15:18–19](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term                      | Greek                                       | Meaning                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Workings / Operations** | ἐνεργήματα (_energēmata_)                   | Effective, powerful operations — from ἐνεργέω, "I work, I energize"                                    |
| **Powers / Mighty Deeds** | δυνάμεων (_dynameōn_)                       | Miraculous powers, mighty acts — from δύναμις, "power, might, miracle"                                 |
| **Working of Miracles**   | ἐνεργήματα δυνάμεων (_energēmata dynameōn_) | Operations of miraculous power — extraordinary divine acts that transcend the powers of created nature |

The plural δυνάμεων suggests a diversity of miraculous operations: not one kind of miracle but many, distributed by the one Spirit as He wills.

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom contrasts the "working of miracles" with the "gifts of healing." Healing, he notes, is specifically focused on cures — the restoration of bodily health — whereas the working of miracles encompasses a broader range of extraordinary acts beyond cures: casting out demons, nature miracles, raising the dead, and other manifestations of divine power.

Chrysostom emphasizes that both gifts are from the same Spirit and serve the common good. The diversity of miraculous operations demonstrates the richness of the Spirit's gifts and prevents any one believer from claiming self-sufficiency.

### 3.2 St. Augustine of Hippo (354–430)

St. Augustine, in _The City of God_ (Book XXII, Chapter 8), testifies that miracles continued in his own time — he catalogues healings, exorcisms, and even a resuscitation that occurred in his diocese. Augustine's witness is important for refuting the claim that the "working of miracles" ceased with the apostolic age.

Augustine distinguishes between the **purpose** of miracles in the apostolic era (to confirm the initial preaching of the Gospel to an unbelieving world) and their **continuing** purpose (to manifest God's power and mercy, to strengthen the faith of believers, and to draw unbelievers to Christ).

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:10

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 12.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas distinguishes the "working of miracles" from the "gifts of healing" by the **mode of persuasion**:

- **Gifts of healing** persuade by **benefit** — the conferral of a tangible good, the restoration of health. The emphasis is on the recipient's good.
- **Working of miracles** persuades by the **greatness of the deed** — the sheer magnitude of the divine act, which manifests God's power in an unmistakable way.

Aquinas gives scriptural illustrations of the "working of miracles": the dividing of the Red Sea ([Exodus 14](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), celestial phenomena such as the sun standing still ([Joshua 10:12–14](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), and other acts that transcend the ordinary course of nature.

### 4.2 The Theology of Miracle

For Aquinas, a miracle (_miraculum_) is an effect produced by God **beyond the order of all created nature** — an act that only God, as the Author of nature, can perform (_Summa Theologiae_ I, q. 105, a. 7). The charismatic "working of miracles" is a participation in this divine power: God uses a human instrument to effect what exceeds natural powers.

Aquinas classifies miracles into three degrees (_Summa Theologiae_ I, q. 105, a. 8):

| Degree      | Description                                       | Example                                                                                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Highest** | What nature can never do                          | The Incarnation; the glorification of a mortal body; two bodies occupying the same space |
| **Middle**  | What nature can do, but not in this order         | Raising the dead (nature can produce life, but not restore it to a corpse)               |
| **Lowest**  | What nature can do, but without the natural means | Healing instantly without medicine; the multiplication of loaves                         |

The charismatic "working of miracles" typically involves the second and third degrees.

### 4.3 Miracles and Faith

Aquinas insists that miracles are **signs** ordered to the confirmation of faith. They are never ends in themselves. The charism of working miracles exists to build up the Church — to manifest God's power, to confirm the truth of the Gospel, and to draw unbelievers to faith. Without charity, even the most spectacular miracle-working profits nothing for the worker's salvation ([_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 13.1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism teaches that the Holy Spirit gives "to some... the charism of miracles" for the common good of the Church ([CCC 2003](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2003)). The Church has always recognized that the Spirit continues to work miracles — especially through the intercession of the saints — as signs confirming the faith and manifesting God's power.

The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Holy Spirit "guides the Church in the way of all truth... and equips her with various hierarchical and charismatic gifts, directing her through them" ([_Lumen Gentium_ 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

The CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) teaches that charismatic gifts, including miracles, are not a basis for autonomy or self-authorization but are exercised within the Church and in harmony with ecclesial obedience.

### 5.2 Miracles in the Canonization Process

The Church requires **verified miracles** — typically medical healings that cannot be explained by natural causes — for the beatification and canonization of saints (except in the case of martyrs). These miracles are subjected to rigorous scrutiny by medical and theological experts. The recognition of a miracle is a solemn judgment that an event exceeds the powers of nature and is attributable to divine intervention through the intercession of the prospective saint.

This canonical practice testifies to the Church's living faith that the "working of miracles" continues in her midst.

### 5.3 Manifestations Today

The working of miracles may manifest as:

- **Eucharistic miracles**: the visible transformation of the Eucharistic species into flesh and blood (e.g., [Lanciano](../miracles/eucharistic-miracles/), [Bolsena-Orvieto](../miracles/eucharistic-miracles/)).
- **Marian apparitions**: supernatural appearances of the Blessed Virgin Mary, often accompanied by miraculous signs (e.g., the miracle of the sun at [Fatima](../miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/fatima.md), the tilma of [Guadalupe](../miracles/marian-apparitions/approved/guadalupe.md)).
- **Miracles through the intercession of saints**: extraordinary healings and interventions attributed to the prayers of canonized saints and blesseds.
- **Nature miracles**: events in which the ordinary course of nature is suspended — healings that exceed medical explanation, protection from natural disasters, and other signs of divine power.

### 5.4 Discernment

The Church exercises great caution in evaluating claims of miracles. The 2024 Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith norms for the discernment of alleged supernatural phenomena provide a framework for testing such claims. Key principles include:

- Miracles must be subjected to **scientific, medical, and theological scrutiny**.
- The **fruits** of the alleged miracle — conversion, growth in faith, charity — are essential criteria.
- No private revelation or alleged miracle can **add to or correct** the definitive public Revelation of Jesus Christ.
- The faithful are never **obliged** to believe in private revelations or alleged miracles, even those approved by the Church.

## 6. Distinction from Related Concepts

| Concept                            | Distinction                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gifts of Healing (1 Cor 12:9)**  | Healing is a subset of miraculous action focused on the restoration of health. The "working of miracles" encompasses broader extraordinary acts — nature miracles, raising the dead, exorcisms.                                                                                        |
| **Charismatic Faith (1 Cor 12:9)** | Charismatic faith is often the **interior disposition** through which miracles are worked; the "working of miracles" is the **external manifestation**.                                                                                                                                |
| **Magic / Sorcery**                | Miracles are works of **divine power** ordered to the confirmation of faith and the glory of God. Magic is the attempt to manipulate supernatural forces apart from God — gravely sinful ([CCC 2117](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/2117)). |
| **Scientific Anomaly**             | A miracle is not merely an unexplained event but a **divine sign** — an act of God that transcends the powers of nature and carries a spiritual meaning.                                                                                                                               |

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- The working of miracles is a **service**, not a spectacle. It is given to build up the Church, not to entertain or to elevate the miracle-worker.
- The supreme miracle is the **Eucharist** — Christ substantially present under the appearances of bread and wine. Every charismatic miracle is ordered toward this central mystery of the faith.
- The Church's pastors must **discern** claims of miracles with prudence, seeking medical and scientific expertise, and never promoting credulity.
- A miracle does **not** authenticate every teaching or practice of the person through whom it is worked. The test of doctrine is conformity to the apostolic faith, not the presence of signs and wonders ([Galatians 1:8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
