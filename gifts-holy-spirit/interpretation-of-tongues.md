# The Interpretation of Tongues (ἑρμηνεία γλωσσῶν)

The ninth and final charismatic gift enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another the **interpretation of tongues**." This gift is the essential complement to speaking in tongues — the Spirit-given ability to render intelligible what is spoken in an unknown tongue, so that the Church may be edified. Without interpretation, tongues remain "mysteries in the Spirit" that build up only the speaker; with interpretation, they become a vehicle of prophecy for the whole assembly.

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To another various kinds of tongues, to another the **interpretation of tongues**."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:10 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek ἑρμηνεία γλωσσῶν (_hermēneia glōssōn_) places the gift at the end of St. Paul's list — paired with speaking in tongues as its complement and completion. The term ἑρμηνεία (_hermēneia_) is related to the god Hermes, the messenger and interpreter of the gods in Greek mythology. In the New Testament context, it denotes the Spirit-given capacity to **translate, explain, or unfold the meaning** of an utterance spoken in tongues.

### 1.2 The Pauline Instructions in 1 Corinthians 14

St. Paul's instructions for the exercise of tongues and interpretation in the assembly are precise and binding:

> "If anyone speaks in a tongue, let there be only two or at most three, and each in turn; and let one interpret. But if there is no one to interpret, let them be silent in church and speak to themselves and to God."
> — [1 Corinthians 14:27–28 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

Key principles from this passage:

1. **Tongues in the assembly require interpretation** — without it, the tongue must remain silent in public worship.
2. **One interpreter suffices** for multiple tongue-speakers ("let **one** interpret").
3. **Orderly turn-taking**: tongues are exercised in sequence, not simultaneously.
4. **The purpose is edification**: the interpreted tongue becomes intelligible and thus profitable for the whole Church.

### 1.3 The Interpreted Tongue as Functional Prophecy

St. Paul explicitly equates interpreted tongues with prophecy in terms of ecclesial value:

> "Now I would like all of you to speak in tongues, but even more to prophesy. One who prophesies is greater than one who speaks in tongues, **unless someone interprets**, so that the church may be built up."
> — [1 Corinthians 14:5 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The phrase "unless someone interprets" is crucial. It establishes that tongues-with-interpretation are **equivalent in value to prophecy**, because interpretation restores intelligibility and allows the utterance to edify the hearers. The interpretation of tongues is therefore a **communicative bridge** between the transcendent mystery of the tongue and the rational understanding of the assembly.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term                          | Greek                                  | Meaning                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Interpretation**            | ἑρμηνεία (_hermēneia_)                 | Translation, explanation, unfolding of meaning — derived from ἑρμηνεύω (_hermēneuō_), "I interpret, I explain"              |
| **Tongues**                   | γλωσσῶν (_glōssōn_)                    | Languages — the charismatic utterances spoken under the Spirit's impulse                                                    |
| **Interpretation of Tongues** | ἑρμηνεία γλωσσῶν (_hermēneia glōssōn_) | The Spirit-given ability to render a tongue intelligible — to convey its meaning in the vernacular language of the assembly |

The word ἑρμηνεία appears elsewhere in the New Testament only at [1 Corinthians 14:26](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), where St. Paul says that when the church assembles, "each one has... an interpretation" (ἑρμηνείαν ἔχει). The verb form appears in [Luke 24:27](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), where Christ "interpreted" (διερμήνευσεν) the Scriptures concerning himself to the disciples on the road to Emmaus.

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom notes that the gift of interpretation is **complementary** to the gift of tongues: one may speak in tongues, another interpret. The two gifts are distributed separately by the Spirit — a person who speaks in tongues may not necessarily have the gift of interpreting their own utterance, and vice versa.

Chrysostom sees in this distribution a divine pedagogy: by separating the two gifts, the Spirit teaches the Corinthians their **mutual dependence**. No one possesses all the gifts; each needs the other. The tongue-speaker needs the interpreter; the interpreter needs the tongue-speaker. Together they serve the Church.

### 3.2 The Early Church's Practice

The [_Didache_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) (c. 50–120 A.D.), while not directly addressing interpretation of tongues, provides rules for testing prophets that would apply equally to interpreters: an authentic prophetic utterance (or interpretation) does not contradict the Gospel, does not ask for money, and is accompanied by a life of virtue.

As the Church settled into its post-apostolic existence, the gift of tongues and its interpretation became less prominent in the liturgical assembly — not because they were denied, but because the Church's public worship came to center on the **Eucharistic liturgy** with its own inspired language of Scripture and prayer. The need for charismatic interpretation diminished as the liturgy itself became the normative "interpretation" of the mysteries of God.

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 14

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 14.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas Aquinas explains the theological logic of interpretation:

1. **Interpretation restores intelligibility**: "When tongues are interpreted, they can edify the Church." The interpretation bridges the gap between the mysterious utterance and the rational understanding of the hearers.
2. **Interpreted tongues = prophecy**: Aquinas explicitly states that tongues-with-interpretation become comparable in value to prophecy because the message becomes intelligible and can build up others. The interpretation is the **means** by which an otherwise private spiritual experience becomes a public ecclesial good.
3. **Interpretation as a distinct charism**: Aquinas recognizes interpretation as a separate _gratia gratis data_ — not simply a natural linguistic skill but a supernatural gift of the Spirit. The interpreter does not deduce the meaning by study or analysis but receives it by charismatic illumination.

### 4.2 The Mind and the Spirit

Aquinas's treatment of interpretation is grounded in his larger concern for the engagement of the **intellect** in charismatic acts. "I will pray with the spirit, but I will pray with the mind also; I will sing praise with the spirit, but I will sing praise with the mind also" ([1 Cor 14:15](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

The interpretation of tongues serves precisely this end: it brings the charismatic utterance into the sphere of the **mind**, where it can be understood, evaluated, and assimilated. Without interpretation, the tongue remains in the sphere of the "spirit" alone — a genuine but incomplete charismatic act.

### 4.3 The Hierarchy of Communicative Charisms (Revisited)

| Charism                        | Mode                                        | Edification                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prophecy**                   | Direct intelligible divine communication    | Directly edifies all hearers                        |
| **Tongues + Interpretation**   | Mysterious utterance → intelligible meaning | Edifies all hearers through the interpreted message |
| **Word of Wisdom / Knowledge** | Intelligible teaching                       | Edifies through instruction                         |
| **Uninterpreted Tongues**      | Mysterious utterance without interpretation | Edifies only the speaker, and imperfectly           |

Interpretation is therefore the **key** that unlocks the edifying potential of tongues. Without it, tongues are a locked door; with it, the door opens to the full benefit of the assembly.

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catholic Church does not have an extensive body of magisterial teaching specifically on the interpretation of tongues. However, the general principles governing charisms apply:

- Interpretation is a genuine charism when exercised authentically ([_Lumen Gentium_ 12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).
- The interpretation must be tested against the apostolic faith — it cannot contradict Scripture or Tradition.
- The interpretation serves the **common good** and is subject to ecclesial discernment ([_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_ 5–7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

### 5.2 The Catholic Charismatic Renewal

In the Catholic Charismatic Renewal, the gift of interpretation is exercised in prayer groups and charismatic assemblies following the Pauline pattern: a tongue is spoken aloud, and then an interpretation is given (often by a different person). The interpretation typically takes the form of a **prophetic message** — a word of encouragement, exhortation, or consolation addressed to the assembly in the first person ("I, the Lord, say to you...").

The Renewal's practice reflects St. Paul's principles:

- The interpretation is **prophetic** in content — it builds up, encourages, and consoles ([1 Cor 14:3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- The interpretation is **intelligible** — spoken in the vernacular language of the assembly.
- The interpretation is **subject to discernment** — tested against Scripture and the Church's teaching, and evaluated by the community's leaders.

### 5.3 The Interpretation and the Liturgy

The Church's liturgical norms do not provide for charismatic interpretation within the Eucharistic liturgy. The liturgy has its own inspired language — the Word proclaimed in Scripture, the Eucharistic Prayer, the Church's sacred hymnody — which is the normative mode of the Spirit's speech in the assembly.

Charismatic interpretation, like tongues, is properly exercised in **paraliturgical** settings — prayer groups, retreats, and other gatherings outside the Mass — where it can be exercised with the freedom and order that St. Paul envisions.

### 5.4 Discernment of Interpretation

The gift of [discernment of spirits](discernment-of-spirits.md) applies with particular force to the interpretation of tongues. Because the interpretation is a **human articulation** of a mysterious divine impulse, it is susceptible to:

- **The interpreter's own thoughts and biases**: what is presented as "interpretation" may be contaminated by the interpreter's opinions, emotions, or desires.
- **Deliberate counterfeiting**: a person may fabricate an interpretation to appear spiritual.
- **Demonic interference**: as with all charisms, the enemy can counterfeit.

Criteria for testing an interpretation:

| Authentic Interpretation                                   | Inauthentic Interpretation                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Conforms to Scripture and Tradition                        | Contradicts or "improves upon" the deposit of faith             |
| Builds up, encourages, consoles                            | Causes confusion, fear, or division                             |
| Glorifies Christ and leads to Him                          | Draws attention to the interpreter or to novelty                |
| Consistent with the character of God as revealed in Christ | Portrays God as capricious, cruel, or inconsistent              |
| Accepted by mature believers after testing                 | Accepted only by the gullible; rejected by discerning believers |

## 6. The Relationship Between Tongues and Interpretation

St. Paul's distribution of tongues and interpretation as **separate gifts** given to different persons is theologically significant:

| Principle                  | Implication                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mutual Dependence**      | The tongue-speaker needs the interpreter; the interpreter needs the tongue-speaker. Neither is self-sufficient. This teaches the Body's interdependence ([1 Cor 12:21](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). |
| **Humility**               | The tongue-speaker cannot claim that their utterance is "too spiritual to be understood." The very existence of the separate gift of interpretation proves that the Spirit intends tongues to be rendered intelligible.                              |
| **Community Discernment**  | Because interpretation comes through another person, the assembly can evaluate both the tongue and the interpretation. The message is tested by the community, not imposed by a single individual.                                                   |
| **Christological Pattern** | The relationship mirrors the Incarnation: the transcendent Word (tongue) becomes intelligible through human mediation (interpretation), just as the eternal Logos became intelligible in the human nature of Christ.                                 |

### Can the Same Person Have Both Gifts?

St. Paul envisions a person having both gifts: "One who speaks in a tongue should pray for the power to interpret" ([1 Cor 14:13](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). While the gifts are distinct, the same person may receive both — and Paul encourages tongue-speakers to pray for the additional gift of interpretation so that their tongues may edify others.

However, Paul's rule for the assembly envisions the normal pattern as **different persons** exercising the two gifts — which provides the built-in safeguard of community discernment.

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- The interpretation of tongues is a **service**, not a platform. It exists to make the tongue intelligible for the edification of the Church — not to elevate the interpreter.
- Those who exercise this gift should do so with **humility and sobriety**, aware that the interpretation is a human articulation of a divine impulse and therefore subject to the limitations of the human instrument.
- Interpretations should be **brief, focused, and edifying** — following the pattern of prophetic speech in Scripture. Lengthy, rambling, or self-referential "interpretations" should be viewed with caution.
- The interpretation is always **subject to the judgment of the Church**. St. Paul's rule applies: "Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is said" ([1 Cor 14:29](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). Interpretations, like prophecies, must be weighed.
- Without the gift of interpretation, the tongue must remain silent in the assembly ([1 Cor 14:28](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). This is an apostolic command, not a suggestion. It protects the Church from disorder and ensures that all public charismatic expression serves intelligible edification.
