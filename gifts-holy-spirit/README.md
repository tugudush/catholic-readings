# The Nine Spiritual Gifts (Charisms) of the Holy Spirit

A comprehensive theological and historical study of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:8–10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), drawn from Sacred Scripture, the Church Fathers (especially St. John Chrysostom), St. Thomas Aquinas, the [_Catechism of the Catholic Church_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/), and the 2016 Letter _Iuvenescit Ecclesia_ of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith.

---

For the related catechetical handouts on the seven sanctifying gifts and the traditional twelve fruits of the Holy Spirit, see [The Seven Gifts and Twelve Fruits](seven-gifts-twelve-fruits.md). These are distinct from the nine Pauline charisms documented here.

## 1. Foundational Theology of the Charisms

### 1.1 What Is a Charism?

The term **charism** (Greek: χάρισμα, _charisma_) derives from χάρις (_charis_), "grace." In the New Testament, charisms are **gratuitous gifts of the Holy Spirit** given to individual believers **for the common good** of the Church — not primarily for personal sanctification, but for the building up of the Body of Christ ([1 Cor 12:7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

St. Thomas Aquinas explains that charisms are _gratiae gratis datae_ ("graces freely given") — distinct from sanctifying grace (_gratia gratum faciens_) — bestowed not to sanctify the recipient directly but to enable the recipient to cooperate in the sanctification of others ([_Summa Theologiae_ I-II, q. 111, a. 1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/); [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 12.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

The Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, in its 2016 Letter _Iuvenescit Ecclesia_, teaches that charisms are "particular gifts or promptings of grace" given by the Holy Spirit for the Church's needs in history and directed toward the building up of the ecclesial community. The CDF insists there is **no opposition** between hierarchical and charismatic gifts; rather, they are **harmonically connected and complementary**, with charisms exercised within the Church and without undermining obedience to legitimate authority ([_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_ 5–7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

### 1.2 The Biblical Text: 1 Corinthians 12:8–10

> "To one is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom, and to another the utterance of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith by the same Spirit, to another gifts of healing by the one Spirit, to another the working of miracles, to another prophecy, to another the discernment of spirits, to another various kinds of tongues, to another the interpretation of tongues."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:8–10 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

### 1.3 Distinction: Charisms vs. the Seven Gifts of the Holy Spirit

A crucial distinction must be maintained between the **nine charismatic gifts** of 1 Corinthians 12:8–10 and the **seven gifts of the Holy Spirit** enumerated in Isaiah 11:2:

|                | **Charisms (1 Cor 12:8–10)**                                                                           | **Seven Gifts (Isaiah 11:2)**                                               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nature**     | Gratuitous, extraordinary manifestations given **for specific tasks** in service of the Church         | **Permanent dispositions** that make the soul docile to divine inspirations |
| **Recipients** | Given variously to different members of the Body of Christ                                             | Given to **every baptized person** in the state of grace                    |
| **Purpose**    | For the **common good** — building up the Church, confirming the faith, manifesting the Spirit's power | For **personal sanctification** and docility to the Holy Spirit             |
| **Duration**   | May be temporary or occasional; given as the Spirit wills for particular needs                         | **Permanent** and abiding; grow with sanctifying grace                      |
| **Source**     | Same Holy Spirit (1 Cor 12:11)                                                                         | Same Holy Spirit, resting on the Messiah (Isaiah 11:2)                      |

The seven gifts are: **wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, piety, and fear of the Lord** ([CCC 1831](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/); [Compendium of the Catechism 389](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4ea2b0093/ref/)). While certain terms overlap (e.g., "wisdom" and "knowledge" appear in both lists), the **mode** differs: the Isaian seven are sanctifying dispositions; the Pauline nine are charismatic manifestations for ecclesial service.

## 3. Key Principles for Understanding the Charisms

### 3.1 All Charisms Are Ordered to Charity

St. Thomas Aquinas and St. Paul both stress that even the most striking charisms are ordered by **charity** — without it, they do not achieve their deepest purpose ([1 Cor 13:1–3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 13.1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

### 3.2 Charisms Serve the Common Good

"To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good" ([1 Cor 12:7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). No charism is given for private self-aggrandizement. Every authentic charismatic gift builds up the Church.

### 3.3 Charisms and Hierarchy Are Complementary

The Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith teaches that there is no opposition between hierarchical and charismatic gifts. Charisms are exercised within the Church's life and in harmony with ecclesial obedience ([_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_ 5–7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)). The same Spirit who gives charisms also established the apostolic hierarchy; they cannot be in contradiction.

### 3.4 Charisms Require Discernment

Not every claimed manifestation is authentic. St. John Chrysostom warns the Church to "prove" things rather than accept everything uncritically ([_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)). The Church's pastors have the duty of testing charisms and discerning their authenticity ([_Lumen Gentium_ 12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

### 3.5 Intelligibility and Edification

St. Paul's extended treatment in 1 Corinthians 14 makes clear that gifts exercised in the assembly must be **intelligible** and **edify** the hearers. Tongues without interpretation do not profit the Church; prophecy — because it communicates understandable divine truth — is "greater" than uninterpreted tongues ([1 Cor 14:5](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 14.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).
