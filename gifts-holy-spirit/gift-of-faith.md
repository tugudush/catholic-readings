# The Gift of Faith (πίστις)

The third of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:9](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another **faith** by the same Spirit." This charism is **not** the ordinary theological virtue of faith common to all Christians, but an extraordinary, Spirit-given confidence associated with miraculous divine action — the "faith that moves mountains."

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To another **faith** by the same Spirit, to another gifts of healing by the one Spirit."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:9 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek word πίστις (_pistis_) here appears without a qualifying adjective — simply "faith." Yet the context makes clear that St. Paul is not referring to the saving faith common to all believers (which he assumes every Christian possesses) but to a **distinct charismatic manifestation** of the Spirit given to some for the common good.

### 1.2 The "Faith That Moves Mountains"

The key to interpreting this charism is St. Paul's own earlier reference to a superlative degree of faith in [1 Corinthians 13:2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "If I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing." This "mountain-moving faith" echoes Christ's words in [Matthew 17:20](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "If you have faith the size of a mustard seed, you will say to this mountain, 'Move from here to there,' and it will move."

The charismatic "gift of faith" is therefore a **supernatural confidence** — a particular, Spirit-wrought certainty that God will act in an extraordinary way in a specific circumstance. It is faith not merely as assent to revealed truth (the theological virtue) but as a **charismatic power** that anticipates and, in some sense, activates divine intervention.

### 1.3 Faith in the Broader Pauline Corpus

St. Paul's theology of faith is rich and multidimensional:

- **Saving faith** (_fides qua creditur_): the personal act of entrusting oneself to God, by which the sinner is justified ([Romans 3:28](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [Galatians 2:16](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Doctrinal faith** (_fides quae creditur_): the content of revealed truth, the "faith once delivered to the saints" ([Jude 3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Charismatic faith**: the gift listed here — a special, Spirit-given confidence for miraculous action.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term                            | Greek             | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Faith**                       | πίστις (_pistis_) | In context: not the ordinary theological virtue but a charismatic gift — an extraordinary trust in God's power to act miraculously in a specific situation                                                    |
| **Theological Virtue of Faith** | _fides_ (Latin)   | The supernatural virtue by which we believe in God and all that He has revealed, on the authority of God Himself ([CCC 1814](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/1814)) |

The distinction between the theological virtue and the charism is critical: every Christian possesses the theological virtue of faith (infused at Baptism), but not every Christian receives the charismatic gift of faith.

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom explicitly states that St. Paul does **not** mean "the faith of doctrines" — the faith by which we believe the articles of the Creed — but rather a **"faith of miracles"**: the confidence associated with extraordinary divine action.

Chrysostom connects this charism to the faith that enabled the apostles and martyrs to perform signs and wonders. He observes that this gift is given to some but not all — it is among the diverse manifestations of the one Spirit distributed for the common good.

### 3.2 Other Patristic Voices

The patristic tradition consistently distinguishes between the faith that saves and the faith that works miracles:

- **St. Cyril of Jerusalem** (_Catechetical Lectures_ V) teaches that faith is one in kind but manifold in operation: one kind is dogmatic faith (assent to revealed truth), another is charismatic faith (the grace of working signs).
- **St. Augustine** (_De Trinitate_ XIII) distinguishes the faith by which we believe in God from the faith by which we perform mighty works through God's power — the latter being a gift, not a reward for merit.

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:9

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 12.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas explicitly warns that this is **not** the ordinary virtue of faith common to all Christians. Rather, it is the "utterance of faith" — meaning an exceptional ability to **propose** and present the faith's certainty, especially with a gift-like power that produces effects beyond ordinary human capacity.

Aquinas identifies this charism with a **supereminent certitude** — a confidence in God's promises and power that is so intense and Spirit-wrought that it can effect miraculous outcomes. He connects it to the "faith that moves mountains" of [1 Corinthians 13:2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/) and [Matthew 17:20](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/).

### 4.2 Charismatic Faith vs. Theological Faith

|                             | **Charismatic Faith (1 Cor 12:9)**                                                   | **Theological Faith (Virtue)**                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Category**                | _Gratia gratis data_ (for the benefit of others)                                     | Theological virtue — infused at Baptism for personal sanctification                                                                                                                                         |
| **Nature**                  | Extraordinary confidence in God's miraculous intervention in a specific circumstance | Habitual assent to all that God has revealed, on the authority of God revealing                                                                                                                             |
| **Recipients**              | Given variously as the Spirit wills                                                  | Given to **every baptized person**                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Object**                  | God's power to act miraculously here and now                                         | God's revealed truth — the _depositum fidei_                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Effect**                  | Moves mountains; works signs and wonders; strengthens the faith of onlookers         | Justifies the sinner; is the beginning of eternal life ([CCC 163](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/163))                                                           |
| **Necessity for Salvation** | Not necessary — a free gift of the Spirit                                            | Absolutely necessary ([Hebrews 11:6](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [CCC 161](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/161)) |

### 4.3 Faith, Miracles, and Charity

Aquinas insists, with St. Paul, that charismatic faith without charity is nothing ([_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 13.1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)). The charism of faith is not a measure of personal holiness; it is a gift of service. One may possess "all faith, so as to remove mountains" and yet lack charity — and in that case, the charism profits nothing for the possessor's salvation, though God may use it for the benefit of others.

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism treats the theological virtue of faith extensively ([CCC 142–184](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/142)) but does not separately enumerate the charismatic gift of faith. However, the general teaching on charisms applies: they are "graces of the Holy Spirit which directly or indirectly benefit the Church" ([CCC 799](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/799)).

The CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) teaches that charisms are exercised within the Church's life and for the common good, in harmony with ecclesial obedience. The charismatic gift of faith is not a basis for presumption or spiritual exhibitionism — it is a grace that builds up the Church by manifesting God's power and inspiring faith in others.

### 5.2 Manifestations Today

In the Church's pastoral experience, the charismatic gift of faith may manifest as:

- **Supernatural confidence in prayer**: a Spirit-given certainty that God will answer a specific petition in a particular way, often confirmed by the outcome.
- **Boldness in mission**: the grace to undertake seemingly impossible apostolic works with unshakable trust in God's provision and power.
- **Faith that inspires faith**: the capacity — through one's own evident trust in God — to awaken faith in others, especially in circumstances of doubt, crisis, or unbelief.
- **Miraculous intervention**: in conjunction with the [gifts of healing](gifts-of-healing.md) and [working of miracles](working-of-miracles.md), charismatic faith may be the interior disposition through which God works extraordinary signs.

### 5.3 Discernment and Caution

The charismatic gift of faith must be distinguished from:

- **Presumption**: the reckless expectation that God will act without regard to His will or the natural order He has established. Authentic charismatic faith is always submitted to God's sovereign will.
- **Psychological enthusiasm**: mere emotional intensity or wishful thinking dressed in religious language.
- **Spiritual pride**: the temptation to regard oneself as specially favored or more spiritual than others because of this gift.

The Church's pastors have the duty of testing charisms ([_Lumen Gentium_ 12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)). The gift of [discernment of spirits](discernment-of-spirits.md) is the proper charism for testing claims of charismatic faith.

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- The charismatic gift of faith is a **service**, not a status symbol. It exists to build up others, not to elevate the recipient.
- This charism often operates in **conjunction** with other gifts — particularly [gifts of healing](gifts-of-healing.md), [working of miracles](working-of-miracles.md), and [prophecy](prophecy.md) — as the interior disposition of confident trust through which God's power is manifested externally.
- The gift does **not** guarantee that every prayer of the recipient will be answered as desired. Charismatic faith operates under the sovereignty of God and in conformity with His will.
- Pastors should encourage the faithful to pray for an increase of **both** the theological virtue of faith (necessary for salvation) and the charismatic gift of faith (useful for the Church's mission), while clearly distinguishing the two.
