# The Word of Wisdom (λόγος σοφίας)

The first of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To one is given through the Spirit the **utterance of wisdom**." This gift is a Spirit-given capacity to communicate divine truths persuasively, especially in matters pertaining to salvation, for the building up of the Church.

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To one is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom, and to another the utterance of knowledge according to the same Spirit."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:8 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek λόγος σοφίας (_logos sophias_) is more precisely rendered "utterance of wisdom" or "word of wisdom" — it is not wisdom as a private intellectual possession, but wisdom **expressed**, wisdom **spoken** for the benefit of others. The _logos_ (word, utterance, discourse) is the mode; _sophia_ (wisdom) is the content.

### 1.2 Wisdom in the Broader Pauline Corpus

St. Paul's treatment of wisdom must be understood against the backdrop of his earlier contrast between the "wisdom of this age" and the "wisdom of God" in [1 Corinthians 1:18–2:16](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/). The "word of wisdom" in 1 Corinthians 12 is the Spirit's answer to the Corinthian fascination with Greek rhetorical wisdom: it is not the wisdom of the philosophers, but the wisdom of the Cross — divine truth communicated through the Spirit's power.

See also [Colossians 1:9](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/) ("that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding") and [Ephesians 1:17](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/) ("a spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the knowledge of him").

### 1.3 Relation to Old Testament Wisdom

The charism of the word of wisdom stands in continuity with the Old Testament wisdom tradition — Solomon's gift of wisdom to govern and judge ([1 Kings 3:9–12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)), the wisdom literature's portrayal of divine Wisdom as a personified attribute of God ([Proverbs 8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [Wisdom 7](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) — but is now a **charismatic manifestation** of the Holy Spirit distributed among the members of the Body of Christ for the common good.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term | Greek | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Word / Utterance** | λόγος (_logos_) | Speech, discourse, reasoned utterance; a communication that is intelligible and ordered |
| **Wisdom** | σοφία (_sophia_) | In the Pauline sense: knowledge of divine realities — the highest contemplative knowledge of God, His plan of salvation, and the mystery of Christ |
| **Utterance of Wisdom** | λόγος σοφίας (_logos sophias_) | The Spirit-given ability to articulate divine wisdom persuasively for the edification of the hearers |

The term λόγος σοφίας occurs **only here** in the New Testament — it is a distinct Pauline formulation to describe a specific charismatic manifestation. It should not be confused with the gift of wisdom among the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit (Isaiah 11:2), which is a **permanent disposition** making the soul docile to divine inspirations.

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom identifies the "word of wisdom" with the kind of spiritual wisdom found in apostolic proclamation and teaching. He connects this gift with the Apostles Paul and John — men who were endowed with a special capacity to penetrate and communicate the deep things of God.

Chrysostom emphasizes that the diversity of gifts serves to heal divisions in the Corinthian church: the Spirit distributes gifts variously so that believers recognize their mutual dependence. The "word of wisdom" is not given to elevate one believer above another but to remind all that every gift comes from the same Spirit ([_Homily 29.1_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

### 3.2 St. Augustine of Hippo (354–430)

St. Augustine, while not commenting directly on 1 Corinthians 12:8 in the same detail as Chrysostom, treats wisdom as the highest of the intellectual gifts — the contemplative knowledge of eternal things — and consistently subordinates all charismatic manifestations to charity. In his _De Trinitate_, Augustine distinguishes _sapientia_ (wisdom, the contemplation of eternal truths) from _scientia_ (knowledge, the right use of temporal things), a distinction that illuminates the difference between the "word of wisdom" and the "word of knowledge" in the Pauline list.

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:8

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 12.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas links the "word of wisdom" to **persuasive ability in matters pertaining to salvation**: wisdom enables one to persuade others regarding divine things. He notes that _sapientia_ concerns knowledge of divine realities — the highest and most excellent objects of knowledge.

Aquinas distinguishes the "word of wisdom" from the "word of knowledge" by their **objects**:

- **Wisdom** (_sapientia_): concerns divine things in themselves — the mysteries of the Trinity, the Incarnation, the economy of salvation.
- **Knowledge** (_scientia_): concerns divine things as manifested **through creatures** — the natural order as it reflects the Creator.

### 4.2 Charismatic Wisdom vs. the Gift of Wisdom

In the _Summa Theologiae_ (I-II, q. 111, a. 4), Aquinas distinguishes:

| | **Charism of the Word of Wisdom** | **Gift of Wisdom (Isaiah 11:2)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Category** | _Gratia gratis data_ (grace freely given for others) | One of the seven gifts — a permanent disposition for sanctification |
| **Purpose** | To communicate divine truth persuasively to others | To make the soul docile to the Holy Spirit's inspirations |
| **Mode** | Extraordinary, occasional, for ecclesial service | Abiding, habitual, given to every soul in the state of grace |
| **Effect** | Edification of the Church through teaching and proclamation | Personal union with God through contemplation and right judgment |

### 4.3 Wisdom and Charity

Aquinas stresses that the "word of wisdom" — like all charisms — is ordered to **charity**. Without charity, the most eloquent proclamation of divine wisdom is "a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal" ([1 Cor 13:1](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). The charism achieves its purpose only when it serves love of God and neighbor.

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 The Catechism and Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism of the Catholic Church, while not enumerating the nine charisms individually, affirms that charisms are "graces of the Holy Spirit which directly or indirectly benefit the Church, ordered as they are to her building up, to the good of men, and to the needs of the world" ([CCC 799](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/799)).

Pope St. John Paul II, in [_Christifideles Laici_ 24](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), describes charisms as "particular gifts or promptings of grace, called charisms," given in variety and ordered to the Church's needs, with their usefulness assessed by the ecclesial community.

The 2016 CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) insists on the harmonious complementarity of hierarchical and charismatic gifts: charisms are not a basis for autonomy or self-authorization but are exercised within the Church's life and for the common good, in obedience to legitimate authority.

### 5.2 Manifestations of the Word of Wisdom Today

In the Church's pastoral experience, the "word of wisdom" may manifest as:

- **Inspired preaching and teaching**: the capacity to communicate the truths of the faith with unusual clarity, penetration, and persuasive power.
- **Counsel in difficult circumstances**: the Spirit-given ability to offer wisdom that illuminates complex moral, spiritual, or pastoral situations.
- **Theological insight**: a particular grace given to theologians, catechists, and spiritual directors to penetrate and articulate the mysteries of the faith.
- **Prophetic witness**: wisdom that cuts through confusion and speaks truth to the Church and the world with apostolic boldness.

### 5.3 Discernment

Because the "word of wisdom" involves persuasive communication, the Church exercises vigilance to ensure that what is presented as spiritual wisdom is genuinely from the Holy Spirit and not merely human eloquence or — worse — deceptive teaching. The test is always: does the utterance build up the Church in charity, conform to apostolic doctrine, and bear the fruits of the Spirit ([Galatians 5:22–23](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/))?

St. John Chrysostom's warning remains pertinent: "Therefore, prove the spirits, whether they be of God" ([_Homily 29.5_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)). The gift of [discernment of spirits](discernment-of-spirits.md) exists precisely to test claims of charismatic wisdom.

## 6. Distinction from Related Concepts

| Concept | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| **Gift of Wisdom (Isaiah 11:2)** | A **permanent disposition** for personal sanctification; given to every baptized person in grace. The charism of the word of wisdom is an extraordinary, occasional gift for ecclesial service. |
| **Word of Knowledge (1 Cor 12:8)** | Knowledge concerns divine truths manifested through **creatures**; wisdom concerns divine truths **in themselves**. |
| **Prophecy (1 Cor 12:10)** | Prophecy is direct divine illumination, often spontaneous; the word of wisdom may involve more discursive, reasoned articulation of divine truth. Both are communicative charisms. |
| **Natural Eloquence** | The word of wisdom is a **supernatural** charism — not reducible to rhetorical skill or intellectual ability, though it may operate through natural faculties elevated by grace. |

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- The word of wisdom is given **for others**, not for private satisfaction. Those who believe they have received it should exercise it in humility and service.
- The charism operates within the **ecclesial communion**: it is subject to the discernment of the Church's pastors and must be exercised in harmony with the apostolic faith.
- The word of wisdom is **not incompatible with study and preparation** — grace builds on nature. The Spirit may use a person's natural intellect and learning as instruments for the charism.
- The supreme model of the word of wisdom is **Jesus Christ himself**, who is "the wisdom of God" ([1 Cor 1:24](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) and whose teaching astonished the crowds because "he taught them as one having authority, and not as their scribes" ([Matthew 7:28–29](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
