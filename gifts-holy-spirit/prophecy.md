# Prophecy (προφητεία)

The sixth of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another **prophecy**." St. Paul accords prophecy a privileged place among the charisms — in [1 Corinthians 14](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/) he ranks it above tongues because prophecy edifies the Church through intelligible divine communication.

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To another the working of miracles, to another **prophecy**, to another the discernment of spirits."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:10 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek προφητεία (_prophēteia_) derives from προ- (_pro-_, "before, forth") and φημί (_phēmi_, "I speak") — literally, "to speak forth." In the biblical sense, prophecy is not primarily **prediction** of the future (though it may include that) but **proclamation** of divine truth under the direct inspiration of the Holy Spirit.

### 1.2 Prophecy in 1 Corinthians 14

St. Paul devotes the entirety of 1 Corinthians 14 to the proper exercise of prophecy and tongues in the assembly. His key principles:

1. **Prophecy edifies the Church**: "Those who prophesy speak to other people for their upbuilding and encouragement and consolation" ([1 Cor 14:3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
2. **Prophecy is greater than uninterpreted tongues**: "I would rather speak five words with my mind, in order to instruct others also, than ten thousand words in a tongue" ([1 Cor 14:19](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
3. **Prophecy is intelligible**: Unlike uninterpreted tongues, prophecy communicates in a language the hearers understand, so that the Church is built up.
4. **Prophecy can convict and convert unbelievers**: "If all prophesy, an unbeliever or outsider who enters is reproved by all and called to account by all; the secrets of the unbeliever's heart are disclosed, and that person will bow down before God and worship him" ([1 Cor 14:24–25](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
5. **Prophets are subject to order**: "The spirits of prophets are subject to the prophets" ([1 Cor 14:32](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). Prophecy is not ecstatic loss of control but a gift exercised with sobriety and in good order.

### 1.3 Prophecy in the Old Testament

The charismatic gift of prophecy stands in continuity with the Old Testament prophetic tradition. The prophets of Israel — Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Twelve — were seized by the Spirit of God and compelled to speak His word to the people, often at great personal cost. Their prophecy was characterized by:

- **Divine commissioning**: "The word of the LORD came to me" ([Jeremiah 1:4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
- **Moral and spiritual exhortation**: calling Israel to repentance, justice, and fidelity to the covenant.
- **Messianic promise**: foretelling the coming of the Messiah and the establishment of the New Covenant.
- **Symbolic actions**: embodying the prophetic message through dramatic deeds.

### 1.4 The Fulfillment of Prophecy at Pentecost

St. Peter, in his Pentecost sermon, cites the prophet Joel: "In the last days it will be, God declares, that I will pour out my Spirit upon all flesh, and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" ([Acts 2:17](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/); [Joel 2:28](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). The charismatic gift of prophecy in the Church is the fulfillment of this promise — the Spirit now distributes the prophetic gift broadly among the members of the Body of Christ, not restricting it to a few chosen individuals.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term            | Greek                    | Meaning                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prophecy**    | προφητεία (_prophēteia_) | The gift of speaking forth divine truth under the direct inspiration of the Holy Spirit, for the upbuilding, encouragement, and consolation of the Church |
| **Prophet**     | προφήτης (_prophētēs_)   | One who speaks on behalf of God — the mouthpiece of the Spirit                                                                                            |
| **To Prophesy** | προφητεύω (_prophēteuō_) | To exercise the gift of prophecy — to speak under divine impulse for the edification of others                                                            |

The classical Greek προφήτης meant "one who speaks for a god" (e.g., the oracle at Delphi). The biblical usage transforms this: the Christian prophet speaks not ecstatic oracles but intelligible divine truth under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, always in conformity with the apostolic faith.

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom speaks of prophecy among the charismatic gifts and warns the Church to **beware false prophets** and to discern. He stresses that prophecy is a gift for edification — not for display — and that it must be exercised with humility and in subjection to the Church's order.

Chrysostom's pastoral concern is evident: the Corinthian church was disordered, and the charisms — including prophecy — were being misused for self-aggrandizement. He insists that prophecy serves the common good and that prophets must recognize their dependence on the whole Body.

### 3.2 The Apostolic Fathers

- **The Didache** (c. 50–120 A.D.) provides the earliest extra-biblical instructions for discerning true from false prophets. A true prophet does not order a meal "in the Spirit" and then eat it himself; does not ask for money; and practices what he preaches (_Didache_ 11).
- **St. Irenaeus of Lyons** (_Against Heresies_ V.6.1) testifies that the prophetic charism continued in the Church of his day and was a mark of the Spirit's presence among true believers, in contrast to the heretics who lacked authentic spiritual gifts.

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:10 and 14

In his [_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. Thomas treats prophecy as a gift related to **divine illumination** — a participation in God's own knowledge communicated to the human intellect for the benefit of others.

Aquinas devotes significant attention to 1 Corinthians 14, where he explains why prophecy is "greater" than tongues. His reasoning:

1. **Intelligibility**: Prophecy communicates in a language the hearers understand. "I would rather speak five words with my mind, in order to instruct others, than ten thousand words in a tongue" ([1 Cor 14:19](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
2. **Edification**: Prophecy builds up the Church directly, whereas uninterpreted tongues edify only the speaker (and even then imperfectly, since the mind is "unfruitful").
3. **Evangelization**: Prophecy can convict and convert unbelievers by disclosing the secrets of the heart ([1 Cor 14:24–25](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

When tongues are **interpreted**, however, they become comparable in value to prophecy — because the message becomes intelligible and can edify the hearers ([_Commentary on 1 Corinthians_ 14.2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

### 4.2 Aquinas's Broader Theology of Prophecy

In the _Summa Theologiae_ (II-II, qq. 171–174), Aquinas develops a comprehensive theology of prophecy:

- **Prophecy is a cognitive gift**: it involves the intellectual apprehension of truths exceeding natural knowledge.
- **Prophecy involves judgment**: the prophet does not merely receive images or words but judges, by a supernatural light, the divine truth communicated to him.
- **Prophecy is a _gratia gratis data_**: it is given primarily for the benefit of others, not for the sanctification of the prophet (though the prophet must be in a state of grace to exercise it fruitfully).

### 4.3 Prophecy and the Hierarchy

Aquinas recognizes that prophecy is not confined to the ordained hierarchy. The Spirit "blows where it wills" ([John 3:8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). Nevertheless, the exercise of prophecy is subject to ecclesial discernment: "Do not despise the words of prophets, but test everything; hold fast to what is good" ([1 Thessalonians 5:20–21](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism teaches that "the Holy Spirit is the interpreter of Scripture" and that "the charism of prophecy" — together with the whole of Scripture — must be read in the light of the same Spirit by whom it was written ([CCC 111](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/111), [137](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/137)).

The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Holy Spirit "distributes special graces among the faithful of every rank... [including] the grace of the word of wisdom and knowledge and prophecy," and that these charisms are to be received with thanksgiving and tested by the Church's pastors ([_Lumen Gentium_ 12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

Pope St. John Paul II, in [_Christifideles Laici_ 24](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), acknowledges the "new and flourishing rebirth of the charism of prophecy" in the Church and calls for its authentic exercise in communion with the hierarchy.

The CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) insists that the charismatic gift of prophecy must be exercised within the Church's life and in harmony with ecclesial obedience. Prophecy is not a basis for criticizing or undermining legitimate authority but a gift for strengthening faith and building up the Church.

### 5.2 Prophecy and the _Sensus Fidei_

The International Theological Commission, in its 2014 document [_Sensus Fidei in the Life of the Church_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), connects the prophetic charism to the _sensus fidei_ — the supernatural sense of the faith possessed by the whole People of God. The _sensus fidei_ enables the faithful to recognize authentic prophecy and to reject what is false.

### 5.3 Manifestations Today

In the Church's pastoral experience, the gift of prophecy may manifest as:

- **Inspired preaching**: words that strike the hearers as directly from God — penetrating, convicting, converting.
- **A word of knowledge about a person's hidden circumstances**: as described in [1 Corinthians 14:24–25](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/), the disclosure of "the secrets of the heart" that leads to worship.
- **Prophetic calling to repentance**: the Spirit-empowered proclamation of the Gospel that cuts through complacency and calls for conversion.
- **Revelation of God's will in a specific situation**: guidance for the Church or an individual, always conformable to Scripture and Tradition.

### 5.4 Discernment of Prophecy

The Church has always recognized the danger of **false prophecy**. Christ himself warned: "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves" ([Matthew 7:15](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

Criteria for discerning authentic prophecy:

| Criterion                   | Authentic Prophecy                                                                                                                   | False Prophecy                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Conformity to the Faith** | Consistent with Scripture and Tradition                                                                                              | Contradicts apostolic doctrine ([Galatians 1:8](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) |
| **Fruits**                  | Produces peace, conversion, charity                                                                                                  | Produces division, pride, sensationalism                                                                                     |
| **Humility**                | The prophet submits to ecclesial discernment                                                                                         | The prophet claims authority above the Church                                                                                |
| **Accuracy**                | Predictive prophecies come to pass ([Deuteronomy 18:22](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) | Predictions fail; prophecies are vague or self-serving                                                                       |
| **Christocentrism**         | Glorifies Christ and leads to Him                                                                                                    | Glorifies the prophet or leads away from Christ                                                                              |

## 6. Prophecy and Public Revelation

A crucial doctrinal distinction must be maintained:

- **Public Revelation** — the definitive self-disclosure of God in Jesus Christ, transmitted through Scripture and Apostolic Tradition — is **complete and closed**. "The Christian economy, therefore, since it is the new and definitive covenant, will never pass away; and no new public revelation is to be expected before the glorious manifestation of our Lord Jesus Christ" ([CCC 66](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/66); [_Dei Verbum_ 4](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).
- **Private prophecy** — the charismatic gift exercised in the Church — does **not** add to or correct Public Revelation. It serves to help the faithful live out the one definitive Revelation more fully in a particular time and place ([CCC 67](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/67)).

No prophetic utterance in the Church can contradict or surpass the apostolic faith. The test of all prophecy is its conformity to the Gospel once delivered.

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- "Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise the words of prophets, but test everything; hold fast to what is good" ([1 Thessalonians 5:19–21](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). The Church must neither suppress authentic prophecy nor accept every claim uncritically.
- Prophecy is exercised in **good order**: "The spirits of prophets are subject to the prophets, for God is a God not of disorder but of peace" ([1 Cor 14:32–33](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). Authentic prophecy does not manifest as uncontrollable ecstasy or disruption of the liturgy.
- The prophetic charism is **distributed broadly** in the Body of Christ — not confined to clergy or religious. The Second Vatican Council explicitly teaches that the laity share in Christ's prophetic office ([_Lumen Gentium_ 35](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).
- The goal of all prophecy is **charity**. Prophecy without love "is nothing" ([1 Cor 13:2](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).
