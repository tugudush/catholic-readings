# The Discernment of Spirits (διακρίσεις πνευμάτων)

The seventh of the nine charismatic gifts enumerated by St. Paul in [1 Corinthians 12:10](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/): "To another the **discernment of spirits**." This charism is the Spirit-given ability to distinguish between authentic and counterfeit spiritual manifestations — to know "who is spiritual and who is not," to "test the spirits to see whether they are from God."

---

## 1. Biblical Foundation

### 1.1 The Pauline Text

> "To another the working of miracles, to another prophecy, to another the **discernment of spirits**."
> — [1 Corinthians 12:10 (NRSV-CE)](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)

The Greek διακρίσεις πνευμάτων (_diakriseis pneumatōn_) uses the plural for both words — "discernings" or "distinguishings" of "spirits." This double plural suggests a **diversity of discerning operations** directed toward the manifold manifestations of spiritual reality.

### 1.2 The Broader Biblical Mandate for Discernment

The need for discernment runs throughout the New Testament:

- **1 Thessalonians 5:20–21**: "Do not despise the words of prophets, but **test everything**; hold fast to what is good." The verb δοκιμάζετε (_dokimazete_) means "test, examine, prove" — the same concept as discernment.
- **1 John 4:1**: "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but **test the spirits** to see whether they are from God; for many false prophets have gone out into the world." This is the most explicit New Testament command to exercise discernment of spirits.
- **Matthew 7:15–20**: "Beware of false prophets... You will know them by their fruits." Christ himself provides the criterion: authentic spiritual manifestations bear good fruit.
- **Hebrews 5:14**: "Solid food is for the mature, for those whose faculties have been trained by practice to **distinguish good from evil**."

### 1.3 The Context: Corinthian Disorder

The Corinthian church was awash in charismatic manifestations — prophecy, tongues, miracles — but lacked the discernment to distinguish genuine from counterfeit. St. Paul provides the gift of discernment of spirits precisely to address this need. Without discernment, the charisms become sources of confusion rather than edification.

## 2. Greek Terminology

| Term                              | Greek                                         | Meaning                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Discernings / Distinguishings** | διακρίσεις (_diakriseis_)                     | Acts of separating, distinguishing, judging between — from διακρίνω (_diakrinō_), "I separate, I judge, I discern"                                           |
| **Spirits**                       | πνευμάτων (_pneumatōn_)                       | Spiritual realities, manifestations, or beings — the Holy Spirit, angelic spirits, demonic spirits, and the human spirit                                     |
| **Discernment of Spirits**        | διακρίσεις πνευμάτων (_diakriseis pneumatōn_) | The charismatic grace to distinguish between spirits — to identify the source (divine, human, or demonic) of a spiritual manifestation, teaching, or impulse |

The double plural is significant: there is not one act of discernment but many — the Spirit gives varied graces of discernment for the manifold situations in which the Church must "test the spirits."

## 3. Patristic Commentary

### 3.1 St. John Chrysostom (c. 347–407)

In his [_Homily 29 on 1 Corinthians_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), St. John Chrysostom defines the discernment of spirits as knowing **"who is spiritual and who is not"** — recognizing the prophet versus the deceiver. He connects this gift directly to the need to "prove" things rather than accept everything uncritically.

Chrysostom's pastoral concern is that without discernment, the Church is vulnerable to wolves in sheep's clothing. The charismatic manifestations in Corinth — prophecy, tongues, miracles — could be counterfeited. A person might speak eloquently, perform wonders, and yet be motivated by an unclean spirit. Only the gift of discernment can penetrate the surface and identify the true source.

### 3.2 The Desert Fathers and the Discernment of Thoughts

The desert tradition — represented by St. Anthony the Great, Evagrius Ponticus, and St. John Cassian — developed a profound practical theology of the discernment of spirits, focused particularly on the **discernment of thoughts** (_discretio spirituum_ or _diakrisis logismōn_).

- **Evagrius Ponticus** (345–399) taught that thoughts (_logismoi_) arise from three sources: God, the devil, and the human mind itself. The spiritual athlete must learn to distinguish their origin.
- **St. John Cassian** (c. 360–435) called discernment "the mother of all virtues" and devoted an entire _Conference_ (the second) to its exposition. He taught that discernment is acquired through humility, obedience to a spiritual father, and purity of heart.

While the desert tradition's "discernment of thoughts" is not identical to the Pauline charism, it represents the application of the same spiritual principle to the interior life.

### 3.3 St. Ignatius of Loyola (1491–1556)

St. Ignatius's [_Rules for the Discernment of Spirits_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) in the _Spiritual Exercises_ represent the classic Catholic synthesis of the biblical and patristic tradition on discernment. Ignatius distinguishes:

- **Consolation**: interior movements that draw the soul toward God — increase of faith, hope, and charity; peace; tears of compunction.
- **Desolation**: interior movements that draw the soul away from God — darkness of soul, turmoil, inclinations to what is base, loss of faith, hope, and charity.

Ignatius's rules provide practical criteria: in consolation, the good spirit is at work; in desolation, the evil spirit. For those moving from mortal sin to mortal sin, the evil spirit proposes apparent pleasures; for those progressing in the spiritual life, the evil spirit "bites, saddens, and puts obstacles, disquieting with false reasons."

## 4. Thomistic Theology

### 4.1 St. Thomas Aquinas on 1 Corinthians 12:10

While St. Thomas's extant commentary on 1 Corinthians 12 does not treat the discernment of spirits in the same detail as the other charisms, his broader treatment of charisms underscores their ordering toward the Church's good and the necessity of proper use — which implies discernment.

Aquinas treats the _gratiae gratis datae_ as ordered to the communication of divine truth to others. Among these, the discernment of spirits serves a **gatekeeping** function: it ensures that the other communicative charisms (prophecy, word of wisdom, word of knowledge, tongues) are exercised authentically and not counterfeited.

### 4.2 Discernment as a Participation in God's Judgment

In the _Summa Theologiae_, Aquinas teaches that all judgment participates in God's own judgment. The charism of discernment of spirits is a particular participation in the divine light by which God knows and distinguishes the thoughts and intentions of the heart ([Hebrews 4:12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

Aquinas distinguishes:

| Level                                    | Operation               | Description                                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Natural**                              | Prudence and experience | The acquired ability to judge character and situations through reason and experience                                                                                      |
| **Supernatural (Gift of Counsel)**       | One of the seven gifts  | A permanent disposition enabling the soul to be docile to the Holy Spirit's guidance in particular situations                                                             |
| **Charismatic (Discernment of Spirits)** | _Gratia gratis data_    | An occasional, extraordinary gift for distinguishing the source of spiritual manifestations — given for the common good, not primarily for the recipient's sanctification |

## 5. Catholic Understanding Today

### 5.1 Magisterial Teaching

The Catechism teaches that the discernment of spirits is necessary for the authentic exercise of charisms ([CCC 801](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/801)). The Church's pastors have the duty "not to extinguish the Spirit, but to test everything and hold fast to what is good" ([CCC 801](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/0583c069-d4bf-42dd-97de-c19f0b80150f/ref/801), citing [1 Thessalonians 5:19–21](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)).

The Second Vatican Council teaches that "the judgment as to their genuineness and proper use [of extraordinary gifts] belongs to those who preside over the Church, to whose special competence it belongs, not indeed to extinguish the Spirit, but to test all things and hold fast to that which is good" ([_Lumen Gentium_ 12](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/)).

The CDF Letter [_Iuvenescit Ecclesia_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/) emphasizes that charisms are not meant to create disorder or independence from ecclesial obedience. Discernment is essential precisely because disordered or deceptive expressions can occur, and the gift of discernment — exercised by both individuals and the hierarchy — protects the Church from error.

### 5.2 The 2024 Norms for Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena

On May 17, 2024, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith issued new [_Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena_](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), which entered into force on May 19, 2024. These norms represent the most recent and authoritative magisterial framework for the discernment of spirits as applied to alleged apparitions, revelations, and other supernatural claims.

Key provisions:

- The Dicastery must approve the local bishop's judgment before it is published.
- Six possible conclusions replace the previous three (_constat de supernaturalitate_, _non constat_, _constat de non supernaturalitate_): _Nihil obstat_; _Prae oculis habeatur_; _Curatur_; _Sub mandato_; _Prohibetur et obstruatur_; _Declaratio de non supernaturalitate_.
- The _Nihil obstat_ does not declare an event supernatural, only that "nothing hinders" the promotion of devotion and pilgrimage.
- The faithful are never obliged to believe in private revelations, even those approved.

### 5.3 Discernment in the Life of the Believer

While the charism of discernment of spirits is a _gratia gratis data_ given to some for the common good, every Christian is called to the **acquired discernment** that comes through growth in virtue, knowledge of Scripture and Tradition, and docility to the Holy Spirit.

Pope Francis has made discernment a central theme of his pontificate, emphasizing that it is a necessary habit for navigating the complexities of modern life. In [_Gaudete et Exsultate_ 166](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/4a72892b-0a56-4371-a138-ff4aea2b0093/ref/), he writes: "Discernment is necessary not only at extraordinary times, when we need to resolve grave problems and make crucial decisions. It is a means of spiritual combat for helping us to follow the Lord more faithfully."

### 5.4 Criteria for Discernment

The Catholic tradition provides concrete criteria for discerning spirits:

| The Spirit of God                                                                                                                                                         | The Spirit of Error                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh ([1 John 4:2–3](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/))                                     | Denies the Incarnation or diminishes Christ                                                                                       |
| Leads to obedience and humility                                                                                                                                           | Leads to pride, rebellion, self-assertion                                                                                         |
| Produces the fruits of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness ([Galatians 5:22–23](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) | Produces the works of the flesh ([Galatians 5:19–21](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)) |
| Conforms to Scripture and Apostolic Tradition                                                                                                                             | Contradicts or "improves upon" the deposit of faith                                                                               |
| Builds up the Church in unity and charity                                                                                                                                 | Causes division, factions, and spiritual elitism                                                                                  |
| Operates in peace and order ([1 Cor 14:33](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/))                                                   | Produces disorder, confusion, compulsive behavior                                                                                 |
| Accepted by the Church's pastors after due testing                                                                                                                        | Rejected or ignored by legitimate authority                                                                                       |

## 6. The Discernment of Spirits and the Other Charisms

The gift of discernment of spirits functions as a **gatekeeper** among the charisms. Its relationship to the other gifts is intimate:

| Related Charism                 | How Discernment Serves It                                                              |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prophecy**                    | Tests whether a prophetic utterance is from God, the human spirit, or a demonic source |
| **Speaking in Tongues**         | Distinguishes authentic glossolalia from psychological or demonic phenomena            |
| **Interpretation of Tongues**   | Tests whether an interpretation is genuinely from the Spirit or a human fabrication    |
| **Word of Wisdom / Knowledge**  | Judges whether a teaching is divine wisdom or human opinion                            |
| **Gifts of Healing / Miracles** | Determines whether a healing or miracle is from God or from other sources              |
| **Gift of Faith**               | Distinguishes charismatic faith from presumption or enthusiasm                         |

Without the discernment of spirits, the entire charismatic economy is vulnerable to corruption. This is why St. Paul places it immediately after prophecy in the list — the two are paired: prophecy communicates, discernment judges.

## 7. Practical & Pastoral Considerations

- The discernment of spirits is **not suspicion or cynicism**. It is the charitable but sober judgment that protects the Church from error while remaining open to the Spirit's genuine gifts.
- Discernment is exercised **both by individuals** (who must "test the spirits" in their own lives) **and by the hierarchy** (who have the solemn duty of judging the authenticity of extraordinary phenomena).
- No one should trust their own discernment **absolutely**. The gift is exercised within the communion of the Church — in dialogue with pastors, spiritual directors, and the wisdom of the Tradition.
- The supreme model of discernment is the **Blessed Virgin Mary**, who "treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart" ([Luke 2:19](https://www.magisterium.com/docs/00a1de35-98f1-481a-956f-e98e2b52331d/ref/)). Her receptive, pondering discernment — without haste, without presumption — is the model for all Christian discernment.
